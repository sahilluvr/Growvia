Small business marketing in Los Angeles is hard for a simple reason: almost every category you can think of already has dozens of businesses with hundreds of reviews competing for the same few spots in Google Maps. A dentist in Glendale is not competing with "LA". They are competing with the 20 practices inside a short drive of the person searching.

The good news is that LA's size works in your favor once you stop marketing to the whole county. Searchers here rarely cross the 405 or the 10 for a cleaning, a plumber or a consultation. If you win your own few square miles, you can grow without outspending anyone downtown.

This playbook covers how Angelenos search, how to compete in a saturated Maps market, Spanish and Asian-language audiences, marketing sensitively after the January 2025 wildfires, and, in depth, the California rules that apply to your ads, reviews and data.

## The Los Angeles County market at a glance

Los Angeles County is home to about 10 million people (2020 Census), more than any other US county, according to the [US Census Bureau's QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/). It is not one city. It has 88 incorporated cities, from Los Angeles itself to Long Beach, Glendale, Pasadena, Santa Clarita, Torrance, Pomona, Burbank and Lancaster, plus large unincorporated areas like Altadena and East Los Angeles.

::stats Los Angeles County in three numbers | 10 million | residents (2020 Census) | 88 | incorporated cities | 4,000+ | square miles of land

The San Fernando Valley, the South Bay, the Westside, the San Gabriel Valley, the Antelope Valley and Long Beach each behave like separate markets, and many residents identify with their neighborhood (Boyle Heights, Koreatown, Leimert Park, Encino) more than with "Los Angeles".

### Who your customers are

- **Spanish-speaking households** are a huge share of the county, especially in East LA, South LA, the southeast cities and much of the San Fernando Valley. Many are bilingual and switch languages depending on the topic.
- **Asian-language communities** are concentrated and loyal: Korean in Koreatown, Chinese (Mandarin and Cantonese) in the San Gabriel Valley cities like Monterey Park, Alhambra and Arcadia, Armenian in Glendale and Burbank, Filipino in Eagle Rock and West Covina, Thai in East Hollywood.
- **Entertainment workers and commuters** with irregular schedules, who search near work as often as near home and expect online booking.

## Why your service radius matters more here than almost anywhere

Traffic is the hidden variable in LA local SEO. Google's local ranking considers relevance, distance and prominence, as explained on the [Google Business Profile help page about local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). In LA, "distance" effectively means drive time. Someone in Sherman Oaks will happily choose a dentist 2 miles away over a better-reviewed one 6 miles away across the hill.

So stop thinking about "ranking in Los Angeles" and map your real catchment instead.

1. Pull the zip codes of your last 100 customers or patients.
2. Draw the area that covers roughly 80% of them. For most service businesses in LA, it is far smaller than you expect.
3. Track your Google Maps position on a grid across that area, not from your office address alone. Rankings can swing from first to off the map within a couple of miles.
4. Build your content, reviews and outreach around the neighborhoods inside that zone.

::scale How crowded is your Maps category? (rule of thumb) | Good: you appear in the top 3 across most of your catchment grid | Needs work: top 3 only within a few blocks of your office | Poor: outside the top 10 even next door

## How Angelenos search, in practice

LA searchers lean heavily on Google Maps and "near me" queries, and many combine a neighborhood with a service: "Korean dentist Koreatown", "abogado de accidentes Van Nuys", "Armenian pharmacy Glendale". They also increasingly ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity for a shortlist before they ever open Maps.

::flow How an LA customer typically finds you | Asks Maps or an AI assistant | Compares 3 to 5 nearby options | Reads recent reviews and photos | Calls, books or messages

What this means for you:

- **Neighborhood language on your site.** Mention the neighborhoods and landmarks you actually serve ("two blocks from the Glendale Galleria"). Don't create dozens of thin city pages.

## Marketing a dental practice in Los Angeles

LA dental is fiercely competitive. Beverly Hills and Wilshire practices compete on cosmetic work. San Gabriel Valley and East LA practices often win on language, insurance acceptance (including Denti-Cal, California's Medi-Cal dental program) and family convenience.

- **Pick a lane per location.** "Implants and sedation for anxious patients in Torrance" beats "comprehensive dentistry in Los Angeles."
- **Show insurance and languages above the fold.** In LA, "do you take my plan" and "does anyone speak my language" decide most bookings.
- **Ask every patient for a review at checkout, and reply safely.** Never confirm someone is a patient or mention treatment in a reply. HHS has settled with a California practice, New Vision Dental, for $23,000 over Yelp review replies that disclosed patient information.
- **Follow California's health advertising law.** Business and Professions Code section 651 bars false or misleading advertising by licensed health professionals, and the [Dental Board of California](https://www.dbc.ca.gov/) oversees how dentists describe specialties and credentials.

## Marketing a law firm in Los Angeles

LA's legal market is enormous. Big PI firms own the freeway billboards, but most small firms win on Maps, referrals and speed of response. Learn California's rules, which differ from the ABA Model Rules in places, before you spend on ads.

- **California Rules of Professional Conduct 7.1 to 7.5** govern lawyer communications and advertising. Rule 7.1 bans false or misleading communications. Rule 7.2 requires advertising to include the name and address of at least one lawyer or firm responsible for its content. Rule 7.3 limits live solicitation. Rule 7.4 restricts calling yourself a "certified specialist" unless you hold a certification recognized by the State Bar. Rule 7.5 covers firm names and trade names.
- **The Business and Professions Code adds advertising provisions** (sections 6157 to 6159.2), including rules on guarantees, dramatizations and how past results are presented.
- **No "runners and cappers"** who steer accident victims to a firm.

Read the current text on [the State Bar of California's rules page](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Attorneys/Conduct-Discipline/Rules/Rules-of-Professional-Conduct) and compare states in our [lawyer advertising rules by state guide](/blog/lawyer-advertising-rules-by-state). Then put your energy into intake, because in LA the firm that answers first usually signs the case. Our [AI marketing for lawyers pillar](/blog/ai-marketing-for-lawyers) covers the complete system.

## Marketing after the January 2025 wildfires

The Palisades and Eaton fires in January 2025 destroyed thousands of homes in Pacific Palisades, Altadena and nearby areas. Rebuilding has created real demand for restoration, roofing, electrical, landscaping, architects, insurance-claim and property lawyers, and mental health and community services. It has also attracted predatory marketing, so how you market matters as much as what you offer.

::check Wildfire-area marketing done responsibly | Show your license number in every ad (required for California contractors) | Publish real rebuild projects, not stock photos | Explain permits and timelines plainly | Never pressure people to sign on the spot | Avoid "fire victims" language in ad targeting | Quote fair prices during any declared emergency

A few rules matter specifically here:

- **Contractors must be licensed and show their license number** in advertising. The [Contractors State License Board](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/) warns homeowners about disaster scams, and contracting without a license in a declared disaster area carries tougher penalties.
- **Price gouging is illegal during declared emergencies** under California Penal Code section 396, which generally limits price increases on many goods and services, including repair and reconstruction.
- **Lawyers must follow solicitation rules.** Direct, live solicitation of fire survivors is restricted under Rule 7.3. Educational content (insurance claim timelines, how to document losses) is the ethical and effective route.

## California's marketing rules in one table

This is the reference section for all our California pages. Most of these rules apply statewide, whether you are in Long Beach, Irvine, San Diego or Oakland.

| Rule | Who it affects | What it means for your marketing |
|---|---|---|
| CCPA/CPRA (California Consumer Privacy Act) | For-profit businesses over a revenue threshold (originally $25 million, now inflation-adjusted) or that buy, sell or share personal data of 100,000+ consumers or households | Privacy notice, opt-out of "selling or sharing" (including some ad-tracking), honoring Global Privacy Control signals |
| CA Rules of Professional Conduct 7.1 to 7.5 | Lawyers | No misleading claims, responsible lawyer named in ads, specialist limits, solicitation limits |
| Business and Professions Code 651 | Doctors, dentists, chiropractors and other healing arts licensees | No false or misleading health advertising |
| CSLB license rules | Contractors | License number in ads, licensed work only |
| Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA) | Healthcare providers and some health apps | California's own medical privacy law, alongside HIPAA |
| HIPAA (federal) | Covered healthcare providers | Written authorization before using patient information for marketing |
| FTC Consumer Reviews Rule (federal) | All businesses | No fake, bought or suppressed reviews |
| TCPA (federal) | Anyone texting customers | Prior express written consent for marketing texts |

Many small businesses fall under the CCPA thresholds, but a clear privacy policy is good practice either way. The [California Attorney General's CCPA page](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa) explains who must comply. Healthcare practices should be especially careful with tracking pixels on appointment and patient pages.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I rank in Google Maps in Los Angeles?
Focus on a small, realistic area around your location, keep your Business Profile complete, and earn steady reviews that mention your services and neighborhood. Distance weighs heavily in LA because of traffic.

### Does the CCPA apply to my small business?
Only if you meet one of its thresholds, such as revenue above the inflation-adjusted limit or handling personal data of 100,000+ California consumers or households. Keep a clear privacy policy either way.

### Do California lawyers have to file their ads with the State Bar?
California does not have a Texas or Florida style filing requirement for most ads, but ads must comply with Rules 7.1 to 7.5 and the Business and Professions Code, including naming a responsible lawyer. Check current rules before launching.

### Should I market in Spanish in Los Angeles?
If you can serve Spanish-speaking customers well, yes. Publish properly translated pages, list the language in your Business Profile, and don't advertise in a language your team can't support.

### How should businesses market to wildfire-affected neighborhoods?
Lead with education and proof of real work, show your license, price fairly and avoid pressure tactics. Lawyers must follow California's solicitation limits, and contractors should expect homeowners to verify licenses with the CSLB.

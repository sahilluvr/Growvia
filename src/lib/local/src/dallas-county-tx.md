In 2019, a Dallas dental practice paid $10,000 to settle with federal regulators after replying to online reviews in a way that revealed patient information. It's a small number with a big lesson for every local business in Dallas County: the way you show up online is now part of how you're judged, legally and commercially.

Dallas County has about 2.6 million residents (2020 Census, see [Census QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/)), plus a steady flow of people arriving through corporate relocations. Many of your future customers have never heard of you, don't know which neighborhoods are which, and will pick a dentist, roofer or lawyer from what Google Maps, reviews and AI assistants tell them.

This guide shows how to win those customers: reaching newcomers, handling the spring hail rush, replying to reviews safely, and staying inside Texas rules for lawyers and data privacy.

## Who you're selling to in Dallas County

The county covers the City of Dallas plus Irving, Garland, Mesquite, Grand Prairie (shared with Tarrant and Ellis counties), Richardson and Carrollton (both partly in neighboring counties), Rowlett, DeSoto, Cedar Hill, Duncanville, Lancaster, University Park and Highland Park.

Three customer groups dominate most local plans:

- **Relocating professionals.** Large employers keep moving offices and headquarters into North Texas, with clusters in Las Colinas (Irving), Uptown and along the LBJ and Dallas North Tollway corridors. These people arrive with no dentist, no pediatrician, no handyman and no favorite taco spot.
- **Established neighborhoods with strong identities.** Lakewood, Preston Hollow, Lake Highlands, Oak Cliff and the Bishop Arts District, Deep Ellum and the Park Cities each have loyal residents who search by neighborhood name.
- **Spanish-speaking families.** Large Hispanic communities live across West Dallas, Oak Cliff, Pleasant Grove, Irving and Grand Prairie. Spanish-language pages and staff are a real advantage here, not a nice-to-have.

::funnel Example: how a relocating family finds a dentist (illustrative) | Searches "dentist near Las Colinas": 1,000 | Opens a Maps profile: 300 | Visits the website: 90 | Calls or books: 25 | Becomes a patient: 15

The point of that example is the drop at each step. Newcomers compare several profiles before they call. Your photos, review count, recent replies and a clear "new patients welcome" message are what move them down the funnel.

## Reaching newcomers before they pick someone else

A relocating customer usually makes most of their service choices in the first few months. Get in front of them early.

1. **Name the places they're moving to.** "Family dentist in Irving near Las Colinas" helps both Google and a newcomer who doesn't know the area yet. Write location pages only for places you really serve.
2. **Answer the newcomer questions.** "Which neighborhoods have the best schools?" is for realtors, but "Do you take my employer's dental plan?" and "Can I get seen this week?" are yours. Put those answers on your site.
3. **Show up in AI assistants.** Transplants often ask ChatGPT or Perplexity "who's a good pediatrician in Richardson?" before opening Maps. Consistent business details, specific service pages and steady reviews make you easier to recommend. Our guide on [AI search visibility](/blog/get-recommended-by-chatgpt-gemini-perplexity) explains the mechanics.
4. **Partner with the relocation ecosystem.** Real estate agents, apartment leasing offices and HR teams hand out "welcome" lists. Ask to be on them. Agents can see our [real estate marketing pillar](/blog/ai-marketing-for-real-estate-agents) for the other side of this relationship.

## Hail season and the roofing rush

North Texas is one of the most hail-prone regions in the country. Spring storms, roughly March through June, can damage thousands of roofs in a single afternoon. Within hours, yard signs and door-knockers appear, many from out of town.

For legitimate local roofers, restoration firms and auto glass and body shops, this is both the biggest opportunity and the biggest reputation risk of the year.

::scale How Dallas homeowners judge a roofer after a hailstorm | Good: local address on GBP, years of reviews with photos, clear inspection process, written estimate | Needs work: few reviews, generic stock photos, vague "insurance specialist" claims | Poor: no local address, pressure to sign today, offers to "cover your deductible"

> Texas law bars contractors from waiving, absorbing or rebating a homeowner's insurance deductible, and roofing has no statewide license in Texas. That makes trust signals, like a verifiable local address, real job photos and reviews over several seasons, especially important. Check current Texas Insurance Code rules before you advertise anything about deductibles.

What to do before the next storm:

- **Build a hail-damage page now**, with real photos from local jobs, a plain explanation of how inspections and claims work, and your service area by city.
- **Keep your Google Business Profile current** during storm weeks: hours, a short post about current wait times, and fast replies to questions.
- **Ask for reviews with photos** after every completed roof. Our [roofing and storm-season guide](/blog/roofing-marketing-storm-season) has scripts and an ethical playbook.
- **Consider Local Services Ads** for roofing. Our guide to [Local Services Ads for contractors](/blog/google-local-services-ads-home-services) covers verification and lead disputes.

HVAC and plumbing firms face their own Dallas peaks: long, hot summers and occasional hard freezes like February 2021. The [home services marketing pillar](/blog/ai-marketing-for-home-services) has a seasonal plan.

The local lesson worth spending time on is review replies.

### The Elite Dental case, and what it means for your replies

In 2019, the HHS Office for Civil Rights announced a **$10,000 settlement with Elite Dental Associates in Dallas**. The practice had disclosed patients' information while replying to reviews on Yelp. In December 2022, OCR settled with New Vision Dental in California for $23,000 over the same kind of conduct. HHS lists these cases among its [HIPAA resolution agreements](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/index.html).

The rule of thumb: **never confirm that the reviewer is a patient, and never mention treatment, appointments or records in a public reply.** Here's a safer pattern for a negative review.

::chat A HIPAA-safe reply to an angry review | them: Worst visit ever. They botched my crown and overcharged me. | you: Thank you for sharing this. We take every concern seriously, and privacy rules keep us from discussing anyone's care online. | you: Please call our office manager directly at our main number so we can listen and help. | them: Fine, I'll call tomorrow.

Notice what the reply doesn't say: no "we reviewed your chart", no "your crown was placed on...", no "you missed two appointments". Our [dental reviews and HIPAA guide](/blog/dental-office-google-reviews-hipaa) has templates for positive, negative and fake reviews, and the [negative review response guide](/blog/professional-services-negative-review-responses) covers other professions.

The FTC's Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, effective October 21, 2024, adds another layer: no fake or bought reviews, no suppressing negative ones. The FTC's [reviews and endorsements guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews) has the details.

## Marketing a law firm in Dallas

Dallas is a major legal market, with large firms downtown and in Uptown and a dense field of personal injury, family, criminal defense, immigration and business law practices across the county. Corporate relocations also bring steady demand for employment, real estate and estate planning work.

Two Texas rules matter most:

- **Texas Rule 7.04** requires filing most lawyer advertisements with the State Bar's Advertising Review Committee within 10 days of first use, with a fee, or seeking pre-approval at least 30 days before. Exemptions are in Rule 7.05.
- **The TDPSA** governs how you use personal data, and law firm pages often touch sensitive topics like immigration status or health.

We explain both in depth, with a filing checklist, on our [Houston and Harris County page](/local-marketing/harris-county-tx). | Rule or law | Who it affects in Dallas | The short version |
|---|---|---|
| Texas Rule 7.04 (and 7.05) | Lawyers | File most ads within 10 days, or pre-clear 30 days ahead |
| Texas Data Privacy and Security Act | Most businesses; small businesses for sensitive data sales | Effective July 1, 2024; enforced by the Texas AG |
| HIPAA | Dentists, clinics, therapists | No patient details in review replies or marketing without authorization |
| FTC Consumer Reviews Rule | Every business | No fake, bought or suppressed reviews |
| Texas deductible rules | Roofers, restoration | Don't offer to waive or cover deductibles |

None of this is legal advice. Check the current rules for your profession.

## Frequently asked questions

### What happened in the Elite Dental HIPAA case in Dallas?

In 2019, Elite Dental Associates in Dallas agreed to pay $10,000 to settle with HHS after disclosing patient information in replies to Yelp reviews. The lesson is to never confirm someone is a patient in a public reply.

### When is hail season in Dallas?

Large hailstorms in North Texas are most common in spring, roughly March through June, though they can happen at other times. Roofers should build hail-damage pages and review flows before the season starts.

### How do I reach people relocating to Dallas for work?

Name the areas they move to on your site, answer newcomer questions clearly, get on relocation and realtor welcome lists, and keep reviews steady so you show up in Maps and AI assistants.

### Do Dallas lawyers need to file ads with the State Bar of Texas?

Most ads must be filed with the Advertising Review Committee within 10 days of first use under Rule 7.04, with exemptions in Rule 7.05. Our Houston page explains the process in detail.

### Should Dallas businesses market in Spanish?

Yes, for most consumer services. Large Spanish-speaking communities live across the county, and a well-written Spanish page often faces less competition than the English version.

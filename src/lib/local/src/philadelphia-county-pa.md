Philadelphia small business marketing starts with a quirk: the city and the county are the same place. Philadelphia County and the City of Philadelphia have shared the same borders since 1854, and about 1.6 million people live there ([2020 Census](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/)). So when a customer searches "Philadelphia," they mean a specific, well-defined area, and most locals describe where they live by neighborhood, not by city.

That makes Philly a neighborhood market with a strong sense of identity. Someone in Fishtown, South Philly or Chestnut Hill knows exactly where they are, and they want a business that knows too. Add one of the oldest housing stocks in the country, several major universities and hospital systems, and Pennsylvania's own lawyer advertising rules, and you have a market that rewards specific, local marketing.

This playbook covers who your customers are, how to win in Google Maps, how dentists, law firms and contractors should adjust, and a 90-day plan.

## Who you're marketing to in Philadelphia

Philadelphia isn't one audience. A quick map of the city's main customer groups:

- **Long-time residents** in row-home neighborhoods like South Philly, Port Richmond, Kensington and the Northeast, often loyal to businesses their families have used for years.
- **Newer arrivals and young professionals** in Fishtown, Northern Liberties, Graduate Hospital and East Passyunk, who find almost everything through Google and Instagram.
- **Students and university staff** around University City (Penn and Drexel) and North Philadelphia (Temple), who turn over every year.
- **Hospital and healthcare workers**, many working shifts at Penn Medicine, Jefferson, Temple Health and Children's Hospital of Philadelphia.
- **Multilingual communities**, including a large Puerto Rican and broader Latino community in North Philadelphia, Chinatown near Center City, and Vietnamese, Cambodian and Mexican communities in South Philadelphia.

Each group responds to different messages. Students care about price, walking distance and online booking. Long-time residents care about trust and reputation. Shift workers care about hours.

## Neighborhood SEO: Philly searches by name

Philadelphians use neighborhood names in searches all the time: "dentist Fishtown," "electrician Roxborough," "lawyer near City Hall," "pizza East Passyunk." Google ranks local results by relevance, distance and prominence ([Google's guide](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)), and your neighborhood mentions help with relevance.

**Build pages only for neighborhoods you truly serve.** A plumber based in Mayfair might build pages for Mayfair, Holmesburg, Tacony and Fox Chase, each with real details: the types of homes, common problems, typical response times. A thin page that swaps in a neighborhood name won't rank and can drag your site down.

**Mention landmarks and transit.** The Broad Street Line, the Market-Frankford El, Rittenhouse Square, the Italian Market. These help searchers and AI assistants understand where you are.

**Keep your details identical everywhere.** Your name, address and phone should match across Google, Yelp, Facebook, your website and industry directories. Our [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-small-business) covers the full list.

## Row homes and old houses: home services demand

Much of Philadelphia's housing is row homes and twins built long ago. That creates steady demand for plumbers, electricians, roofers and HVAC contractors, and a clear marketing angle: expertise with old houses.

| Common old-house issue | Who gets the call | Content idea |
|---|---|---|
| Outdated wiring (knob-and-tube, undersized panels) | Electricians | "Is your Philly row home's wiring safe? Signs it's time for a panel upgrade" |
| Old supply and sewer lines, basement backups | Plumbers | "Why row-home basements back up and what fixes it" |
| Flat roofs and shared party walls | Roofers | "Flat roof repair on attached homes, what neighbors should know" |
| Radiators, boilers and older heating systems | HVAC and boiler techs | "Boiler or heat pump? Upgrading heat in an older Philly home" |
| Older homes with possible lead paint or lead service lines | Contractors, plumbers | Plain-language explainers that point to official city and EPA guidance |

**Winter brings emergencies.** Frozen pipes, boiler failures and no-heat calls rise in cold snaps. Make sure your listing shows real hours, your phone is answered and your site has a clear emergency page.

**Attached homes affect your marketing.** On a block of row homes, one happy customer can lead to calls from the neighbors on either side. Yard signs (where allowed), door hangers and asking for referrals are especially effective here.

## Universities and hospitals shape demand

Philadelphia's universities and hospital systems bring a steady flow of new residents: students, residents and fellows, nurses, researchers and their families. Many of them arrive knowing no one locally, so they choose a dentist, gym, salon or lawyer almost entirely through search and reviews.

- **Time your marketing to the academic calendar.** Late August and early September bring move-ins, and May brings move-outs. Gyms, salons, movers, restaurants and dentists all see shifts.
- **Make it easy for newcomers.** "New to Philly?" content, clear directions from SEPTA stops and online booking all help.
- **Respect shift schedules.** Healthcare workers often need early, late or weekend appointments. Say so if you offer them.

AI assistants matter for this group, because a new arrival will often ask ChatGPT "good dentist near Penn" or "affordable gym in University City." See our guide on [getting recommended by AI assistants](/blog/get-recommended-by-chatgpt-gemini-perplexity).

## Marketing a dental practice in Philadelphia

Philly dentists compete block by block, and patients often choose based on insurance, convenience and reviews. Start with our [AI marketing guide for dentists](/blog/ai-marketing-for-dentists), then adjust for the city.

- **Lead with insurance.** Many Philadelphians have Medicaid managed-care plans, union plans or employer coverage. A clear list of accepted plans saves calls and builds trust.
- **Serve your neighborhood's languages.** If your staff speaks Spanish, Vietnamese or Chinese, say so on your site and Google profile, and build a page in that language.
- **Target students and hospital staff thoughtfully.** Emergency slots, evening hours and quick online forms win busy people.
- **Reply to reviews without revealing anything.** HHS has settled with dental practices over review replies that disclosed patient information ($10,000 with Elite Dental Associates in 2019, $23,000 with New Vision Dental in 2022). Keep replies general and invite the reviewer to call.

### Pennsylvania's lawyer advertising rules

Pennsylvania's rules on lawyer communications sit in Rules 7.1 through 7.3 of the Pennsylvania Rules of Professional Conduct. The Pennsylvania Supreme Court amended them by order in October 2024, effective November 14, 2024, according to the [Disciplinary Board of the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania](https://padisciplinaryboard.org/news-media/news-article/3866/supreme-court-adopts-amendments-to-advertising-and-solicitation-rules). The amendments consolidated the content of former Rules 7.4, 7.5 and 7.7 into the remaining rules. Key points from the current rules:

- **No false or misleading communications** (Rule 7.1), including misleading firm names.
- **Name a responsible lawyer.** Communications must include the name and contact information of at least one lawyer or law firm responsible for the content.
- **Paid endorsements must be disclosed,** and endorsements by celebrities or public figures are prohibited.
- **Dramatizations must be labeled.** Pictures or people that aren't authentic need a disclosure that the scene is a dramatization.
- **"No fee unless you win" ads** must disclose that the client may still owe certain expenses, if that's the case.
- **"Specialist" claims are restricted** to a short list of exceptions, such as certification by an accredited organization.
- **The old two-year ad retention requirement was repealed** in the 2024 amendments.
- **Solicitation** (Rule 7.3) now has a formal definition: a communication initiated by or for a lawyer, directed at a specific person, offering services for a known legal matter.

This is a summary, not legal advice. Read the current rules and comments before launching a campaign, and compare with other states in our [lawyer advertising rules by state](/blog/lawyer-advertising-rules-by-state) guide.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Philadelphia County the same as the City of Philadelphia?

Yes. The city and county have shared the same boundaries since the 1854 consolidation, so marketing to "Philadelphia" and "Philadelphia County" means the same area.

### Do Pennsylvania lawyers still have to keep copies of their ads?

The specific two-year retention requirement in the old Rule 7.2 was repealed in the amendments effective November 14, 2024. Keeping your own records is still wise, and you should check the current rules.

### Should I build a page for every Philadelphia neighborhood?

No. Build pages only for neighborhoods you actually serve and can describe with real details. Thin, copied pages tend to hurt rankings.

### When is the busiest season for Philadelphia home services?

Winter cold snaps bring frozen pipes and heating failures, and spring brings renovation and roofing work. Plan your content and staffing for both.

### Can Pennsylvania lawyers call themselves specialists?

Only under limited exceptions in the rules, such as certification by an accredited organization. Check the current text of the Pennsylvania rules before using the word.

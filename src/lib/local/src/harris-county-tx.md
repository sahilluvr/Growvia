Harris County is home to about 4.7 million people (2020 Census, see [Census QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/)), which makes it the most populous county in Texas and one of the largest in the country. For a local business, that size cuts both ways. There are more customers than you could ever serve, and more competitors than you can count, spread across a metro with no single downtown that everyone drives to.

This playbook covers what changes when you market a small business in Houston and Harris County: sizing your radius in a city without zoning, planning around storm seasons, reaching Spanish and Vietnamese speakers, and the two Texas rules that trip up local firms most: the lawyer advertising filing rule (Rule 7.04) and the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA).

This page isn't legal advice, and rules change, so check current texts.

### No zoning means your radius is a business decision

Houston is famous for having no traditional zoning code. Homes, strip centers, clinics and warehouses sit side by side, and the metro sprawls in every direction along the freeways. In practice, that means a Google Maps searcher in Cypress rarely sees a business in Pearland, and a dentist in the Heights should not expect patients from Kingwood.

Pick a service area based on rush-hour drive time, not miles. A plumber can cover more ground than a dental office, because patients won't cross the 610 Loop and Beltway 8 for a cleaning. ## How Houstonians search

Most local searches here happen on phones, in Google Maps or with "near me" phrasing. A few patterns are specific to this market.

- **Community names beat "Houston".** People type "dentist Cypress" or "AC repair Atascocita". Mention the communities you truly serve on your site and in Google Business Profile posts.
- **Spanish is a mainstream language here.** A large share of Harris County residents speak Spanish at home. A Spanish service page, written by a fluent speaker, often has far less competition than its English twin.
- **Vietnamese and Chinese matter in the southwest.** Houston has one of the largest Vietnamese-American communities in the US, centered around Bellaire Boulevard and Alief. Businesses in that corridor do well with Vietnamese-language pages, signage and staff names listed clearly.
- **AI assistants are a new front door.** Newcomers ask ChatGPT "best pediatric dentist near Katy" before opening Maps. See our guide to [getting recommended by AI assistants](/blog/get-recommended-by-chatgpt-gemini-perplexity).

## Storm seasons shape the calendar

Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30. Houston has lived through several defining weather events in the past decade: **Hurricane Harvey** in August 2017, which caused catastrophic flooding across the region, the **February 2021 winter storm** that knocked out power and burst pipes across Texas, and **Hurricane Beryl** in July 2024, which left a large share of the area without power for days.

Each event drove searches for water extraction, mold remediation, roofing, generators, tree removal, plumbing and, later, insurance and property lawyers. Plan for the surge before it comes.

## Marketing a dental practice in Houston

Dental competition is tight across Harris County, especially in the Katy and Cypress corridors where families keep moving in.

- **Lead with insurance and language.** Patients filter by "accepts my plan" and "speaks Spanish" (or Vietnamese). Show both above the fold and on your profile.
- **Near the Medical Center, be specific.** Patients with complex needs search for specialists and sedation. A dedicated page per treatment beats a single "services" page.

## Marketing a law firm in Houston

Houston is one of the most competitive legal markets in the US. Personal injury, maritime and offshore injury (thanks to the Port of Houston and the energy industry), immigration, family law and criminal defense all fight for the same Maps spots. ### Texas Rule 7.04, in plain English

Under the **Texas Disciplinary Rules of Professional Conduct, Rule 7.04**, most lawyer advertisements and solicitation communications must be filed with the State Bar of Texas **Advertising Review Committee** (ARC). The rule requires:

1. **A copy of the ad in its final form** (for a video or audio spot, the recording), along with a completed lawyer advertising and solicitation communication application.
2. **Filing no later than 10 days after the ad is first disseminated**, with the fee set by the State Bar's Board of Directors.
3. **Or pre-approval:** you can submit the ad at least **30 days before** first use and ask the committee for an advance opinion. A finding of compliance is binding in your favor if the final ad matches what you submitted.
4. **Substantiation on request:** you must promptly back up statements or claims in the ad if the committee asks.

Exemptions are listed in **Rule 7.05**. Whether a given website page, social post, directory profile or email must be filed depends on its content and channel, so check the current exemptions before assuming you're covered. The Texas Center for Legal Ethics publishes the [current Rule 7.04 text](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/filing-requirements-for-advertisements-and-solicitation-communications/).

::check Before a Houston law firm ad goes live | Is any past result shown with required context? | Are "expert" or "specialist" claims allowed under Texas rules? | Is the ad in its final form for filing? | Is the filing calendared within 10 days of first use? | Is the fee paid and the application complete? | Is substantiation for every claim on file?

### Practical habits for Houston firms

- **Keep a filing log.** Every billboard, radio spot, TV ad and paid social campaign gets a row: first run date, filing date, reference number, version.
- **Plan pre-approval for big buys.** If you're spending heavily on TV or outdoor, the 30-day pre-approval route reduces the risk of having to pull a paid campaign.
- **Translate carefully.** Review a Spanish version of an ad as carefully as the English one, make sure the translation doesn't add claims, and ask the committee whether it needs its own filing.
- **Watch solicitation after disasters and crashes.** Texas has strict barratry and solicitation rules. Our [personal injury marketing guide](/blog/personal-injury-lawyer-marketing) explains why chasing accident victims is a serious ethics risk.

## The Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA)

The TDPSA took effect on **July 1, 2024**. It gives Texas residents rights over their personal data, and it's enforced by the **Texas Attorney General**, which has made privacy a visible enforcement priority. The Attorney General's office has a [plain-English TDPSA overview](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/consumer-protection/file-consumer-complaint/consumer-privacy-rights/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act).

### Does it apply to a small Houston business?

The TDPSA uses the federal **SBA definition of a small business**, and small businesses are generally exempt. But there's one important exception: **a small business may not sell a consumer's sensitive data without that consumer's consent.** Sensitive data under the law includes things like health diagnoses, precise geolocation, biometric data, racial or ethnic origin, religious beliefs, sexuality, citizenship or immigration status, and data about known children.

Larger businesses that don't meet the SBA small business definition carry the full set of obligations.

| TDPSA topic | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Effective date | July 1, 2024 |
| Who enforces | Texas Attorney General only, no private lawsuits |
| Small business rule | Generally exempt, but selling sensitive data needs consent |
| Consumer rights | Access, correction, deletion, portability, opt out of targeted ads, sale and certain profiling |
| Required notices | If you sell sensitive or biometric data, specific notices are required, such as "We may sell your sensitive personal data" |
| Penalties | Civil penalties of up to $7,500 per violation |
| Cure period | Written notice and 30 days to fix before the AG sues |

### What this means for everyday marketing

- **Audit your tracking pixels.** Ad pixels can share visitor data with ad platforms, which may count as a sale or targeted advertising. This matters most for clinics, med spas and law firms whose pages reveal sensitive topics. Our guide to [healthcare tracking pixels](/blog/healthcare-tracking-pixels-privacy) explains safer setups.
- **Don't buy or sell lists with sensitive data.** A list of "people who visited our immigration page" or "patients interested in fertility care" is exactly the kind of data that needs consent.
- **Remember biometrics.** Texas also has a biometric privacy law (the Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act), so gyms and med spas should get advice before adding face or fingerprint check-in.
- **Health data has extra rules.** Texas medical privacy law reaches further than HIPAA in some respects, so clinics should keep PHI out of marketing tools entirely. Our [HIPAA-compliant marketing guide](/blog/hipaa-compliant-marketing-guide) covers the basics.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Houston lawyers have to file their websites with the State Bar?

It depends on the content. Rule 7.04 requires filing most advertisements within 10 days of first use, and Rule 7.05 lists exemptions. Check the current rules or ask the State Bar's Advertising Review Committee about your specific pages.

### Does the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act apply to my small business?

Businesses that meet the SBA small business definition are generally exempt, but they still can't sell sensitive data without consent. If you share sensitive data with ad platforms or list buyers, get advice.

### How big should my service area be in Houston?

Base it on rush-hour drive time, not distance. Most customers won't cross major freeways for routine services, so a smaller, well-covered area usually ranks better than a huge one.

### Should I market in Spanish in Harris County?

For most consumer businesses, yes. Write a real Spanish page with a fluent speaker, list Spanish-speaking staff and answer Spanish reviews in Spanish.

Las Vegas never closes, and neither does demand for local services. A casino dealer finishing a graveyard shift at 4 a.m. may book a dental cleaning on her phone before bed. A Henderson homeowner whose AC dies on a 115-degree afternoon wants a technician in hours, not days. If your business only answers between 9 and 5, a large share of Las Vegas customers will never reach you.

Clark County has about 2.3 million residents ([2020 Census](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/)), plus tens of millions of visitors a year who mostly stay on and around the Strip. This guide is about the residents: the people who live in Summerlin, Henderson, North Las Vegas and the unincorporated towns, and who need dentists, lawyers, plumbers, salons and contractors. It covers how they search, what a 24/7 economy means for your marketing, the Nevada rules that catch businesses off guard, and a plan you can start this month.

## Residents vs visitors: know which Las Vegas you serve

Most small businesses in the valley serve locals. Some, like restaurants, med spas, personal injury firms and tour operators, serve both. The two audiences search very differently.

| | Residents | Visitors |
|---|---|---|
| Where they search | Google Maps, "near me", AI assistants, neighborhood Facebook groups | Google Maps near their hotel, Yelp, TripAdvisor, hotel concierge |
| What they value | Proximity to home, insurance accepted, consistent reviews | Distance from the Strip, open now, rideshare access |
| Timing | Evenings, weekends, after shift work | Same day, often late at night |
| Who you compete with | Neighborhood rivals in Summerlin, Henderson, Centennial Hills | Every business near the resort corridor |

Much of what people call "Las Vegas" is actually unincorporated Clark County: Paradise (which includes most of the Strip), Spring Valley, Enterprise, Sunrise Manor, Whitney and Winchester. Customers use those names and neighborhood names (Summerlin, Green Valley, Anthem, Mountain's Edge, Southern Highlands) more than official city lines, so your website and Google Business Profile should use the words locals use. Restaurants that serve both audiences should read our [restaurant marketing pillar](/blog/ai-marketing-for-restaurants) on Maps, menus and direct email lists.

## Marketing in a 24/7 economy

Gaming, hospitality and entertainment run around the clock, and many residents work swing or graveyard shifts. That reshapes the local customer journey.

::chat A 2 a.m. inquiry that turns into a booked appointment | them: Do you take new patients? I work nights at a casino and can only come in late mornings | you: Yes! We have 10:30 a.m. slots Tuesday and Thursday. Want me to hold one? | them: Thursday works. Do you take my union dental plan? | you: Please send the plan name on your card and we'll verify before your visit. You're on the schedule for Thursday at 10:30.

What the 24/7 economy means in practice:

- **Respond after hours.** Web form leads should get an instant auto-reply with a real next step, and a human follow-up first thing in the morning. Our [website contact form best practices](/blog/website-contact-form-best-practices) guide covers what a good auto-reply says.
- **Offer non-standard hours if you can.** Early mornings, late afternoons and weekends are not perks here. They are often the only times shift workers are free. Put those hours on your Google Business Profile and website.
- **Use messaging channels.** Many locals prefer to message rather than call. A shared inbox for WhatsApp, Instagram and Messenger means nobody's message sits unread over a weekend. Growvia includes one, though Meta connections depend on Meta's approval and are not instant.
- **Measure response time.** "Time to first reply" is one of the most useful numbers a local business can track.

Note that union-sponsored health and dental plans are common among hospitality workers in Las Vegas. If you accept a specific plan, say so clearly on a page of its own. If you don't, don't imply that you do.

## Marketing a dental practice in Las Vegas

Las Vegas dentists compete for a population with a lot of transplants, a lot of shift workers and a lot of families in newer master-planned communities. Practical moves:

1. **Lead with access.** Hours, insurance plans accepted, Spanish or Tagalog spoken (if true), and parking or proximity to major roads. These are what a local patient checks first.
2. **Rank where you are.** Summerlin and Henderson are far apart. A Green Valley practice should build its reviews and content around Henderson, not try to rank everywhere.
3. **Keep consumer health data out of the wrong tools.** Nevada's health data law (below) applies to data HIPAA doesn't cover, which can include some website and marketing data. HIPAA-covered PHI stays in your practice software, not in marketing tools.
4. **Reply to reviews without confirming anyone is a patient.** HHS has settled with practices that disclosed patient details in Yelp replies.

## Marketing a law firm in Las Vegas

Las Vegas has a crowded legal market, especially in personal injury, where billboards, taxi tops and TV ads are everywhere. Visitors who are injured on vacation, residents in car accidents on the I-15 and US-95, and workers' comp claims all feed demand.

Nevada has its own advertising rules, and the biggest surprise for firms moving from other states is filing. Under Nevada RPC 7.2A, lawyers must file advertisements disseminated in exchange for something of value with the State Bar of Nevada within 15 days of dissemination, with a $100 fee per ad, according to the [State Bar of Nevada's lawyer advertising page](https://nvbar.org/for-lawyers/ethics-discipline/lawyer-advertising/). The bar states that websites are not considered advertisements subject to filing, but paid social media posts, billboards, mailers, TV and radio are. Non-English ads need an English translation. Rules change, so check the current rule before every campaign, and treat this as general information, not legal advice.

> **Watch out:** a paid Facebook or Instagram ad can count as a filed advertisement in Nevada. Build the filing step into your campaign checklist so a Spanish-language video ad doesn't go live without a translation and a filing.

## Nevada's consumer health data law (SB 370)

Nevada passed SB 370, a consumer health data privacy law, which took effect March 31, 2024. It is similar to Washington's My Health My Data Act, with important differences. Based on summaries from privacy law firms and the bill text:

- It covers **consumer health data that HIPAA does not**, which can include data collected by med spas, fitness studios, wellness apps, supplement sellers and health-related websites.
- Businesses need **opt-in consent** to collect consumer health data beyond what's needed to provide the service the consumer asked for, and **separate consent** to share it.
- Businesses must publish a **consumer health data privacy policy**.
- It bans **geofencing within 1,750 feet** of a medical facility or in-person health care provider to track consumers or send them health-related ads.
- Unlike Washington's law, it has **no private right of action**. The Nevada Attorney General enforces it.

For a med spa or wellness studio, that means: don't run location-based ads around hospitals or clinics, review what your website forms and tracking pixels collect, and get clear consent. The [HIPAA compliant marketing guide](/blog/hipaa-compliant-marketing-guide) and [healthcare tracking pixels guide](/blog/healthcare-tracking-pixels-privacy) explain the practical steps. The [medical practice marketing pillar](/blog/ai-marketing-for-medical-practices) covers med spas and clinics more broadly. Nevada also has a general online privacy notice law for websites that collect personal information, so check with a Nevada privacy attorney on both.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Nevada lawyers have to file ads with the State Bar?
Yes, for many ads. Nevada RPC 7.2A requires filing paid advertisements within 15 days of dissemination with a $100 fee per ad, while websites are exempt. Check the State Bar of Nevada's current rule before launching.

### Does Nevada's SB 370 apply to my business?
It can apply to any business that collects consumer health data not covered by HIPAA, such as med spas, wellness studios and health apps. It requires opt-in consent and a health data privacy policy and is enforced by the Attorney General. Ask a Nevada privacy attorney about your specific setup.

### Should Las Vegas businesses offer after-hours service?
At minimum, answer after-hours inquiries quickly with auto-replies and next-morning follow-up. Many residents work swing or graveyard shifts, so early, late and weekend hours can be a real competitive edge.

### Is it worth targeting tourists?
Only if you serve them. Restaurants, entertainment, med spas and personal injury firms may, but most dentists, contractors and accountants should focus on residents near their location.

### How do I rank in Henderson or Summerlin?
Have a real location or service area there, use the neighborhood names locals use on your pages and profile, and earn steady reviews from customers in that area.

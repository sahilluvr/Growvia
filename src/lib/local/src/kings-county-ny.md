Brooklyn small business marketing works differently from almost anywhere else in the country. Kings County is home to about 2.7 million people ([2020 Census](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/)), packed into neighborhoods that each feel like their own town. A dentist in Bay Ridge and a dentist in Williamsburg are technically in the same borough, but they serve different people, who speak different languages and walk different streets.

That density is the whole game. When a customer in Park Slope searches "dentist near me" on their phone, Google shows practices within a few blocks, not a few miles. If your listing isn't strong in your own neighborhood, ranking "in Brooklyn" means very little.

This guide covers how to win at that block-by-block level: how Brooklyn residents search, how to show up in their language, what New York's rules mean for law firms and anyone holding customer data, and a 90-day plan you can start this week.

## How Brooklyn customers find a local business

Most Brooklyn residents don't drive to a business. They walk, bike or take the subway, so they care about how close you are and whether you are on their route. That changes search behavior in three ways.

**The Maps radius is tiny.** Google ranks local results by relevance, distance and prominence, as explained on [Google's local ranking help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). In a dense borough, distance cuts deep. You may rank first on your own block and fifth or lower ten minutes' walk away. That's normal, and it's why your effort should focus on prominence (reviews, photos, complete info) in your core neighborhood.

**People search with landmarks.** "Pediatric dentist near Prospect Park," "lawyer near Borough Hall." Mention the streets, subway stops and landmarks near you on your site and profile.

**Many searches happen in other languages.** A Russian-speaking family in Brighton Beach may search in Russian, and an older Haitian patient may ask a grandchild to search for "doktè dan" in Creole. Google and AI assistants can match those searches only if your site and listing actually contain that language.

**AI assistants are a growing front door.** People ask ChatGPT "good immigration lawyer in Sunset Park who speaks Mandarin." Our guide on [getting recommended by ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity](/blog/get-recommended-by-chatgpt-gemini-perplexity) walks through how to show up.

## Make your Google Business Profile work at block level

Your Google Business Profile is the single most important marketing asset for most Brooklyn businesses. Start with the full [GBP optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide), then add these Brooklyn-specific moves.

1. **Use your real storefront address and hours.** Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) don't allow virtual offices or mailboxes. If you share a building, list your suite or floor.
2. **Pick the most specific primary category.** "Pediatric dentist" beats "Dentist" if that's what you are. "Personal injury attorney" beats "Lawyer."
3. **Add languages to your description and website.** Write a sentence like "Our team speaks Russian and Ukrainian" in plain words. Then build a short page in that language on your site.
4. **Post photos of your actual entrance and street.** People need to recognize your door between a bodega and a laundromat.
5. **Ask for reviews that mention the neighborhood.** Never script reviews, but you can ask, "If you leave a review, it helps neighbors to know where we are." Follow the rules in our guide on [getting more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews), including the FTC ban on fake or bought reviews.

## Marketing a dental practice in Brooklyn

Brooklyn has a dental office on what seems like every other corner, so patients choose on convenience, trust and language. The full system is in our [AI marketing guide for dentists](/blog/ai-marketing-for-dentists). Here's what to adjust for Brooklyn.

**Win your walking radius first.** A practice in Bensonhurst is unlikely to win patients from Greenpoint, and that's fine. Focus on a strong profile, steady reviews and a website that loads fast on a phone on the subway platform.

**Speak your patients' language on the website, not just in the office.** If your hygienists speak Russian, Chinese or Spanish, say so prominently and give each language its own page about common services, insurance and how to book. Machine-translated pages read badly. Have a staff member or professional translator review them.

**Be clear about insurance.** Many Brooklyn families use Medicaid managed-care plans, union dental plans or employer plans. A plain "Insurance we accept" section answers the first question most new patients have.

**Reply to reviews without confirming anyone is a patient.** HHS has settled with dental practices over review replies that disclosed patient information, including Elite Dental Associates in Dallas ($10,000 in 2019) and New Vision Dental in California ($23,000 in 2022). The safe pattern looks like this.

::chat A HIPAA-safe reply to a negative review | them: Waited 40 minutes past my appointment and nobody explained why. | you: Thank you for the feedback. We work hard to run on time and we're sorry this visit fell short. | you: Please call our office manager at the front desk number so we can learn more and make it right. | them: Appreciate the response, I'll call Monday.

Notice the reply never says "your appointment," "your cleaning" or anything that confirms a treatment relationship. For more scripts, see our guide to [handling dental reviews under HIPAA](/blog/dental-office-google-reviews-hipaa).

## Marketing a law firm in Brooklyn

Kings County has a busy courthouse district around Borough Hall and Court Street, plus a huge share of residents who need help with housing, immigration, family, personal injury and criminal matters. Start with our [AI marketing guide for lawyers](/blog/ai-marketing-for-lawyers), then layer in New York's rules.

### What New York's advertising rules require now

New York rewrote its lawyer advertising rules effective **June 1, 2026**, moving from a long list of detailed requirements to a principle-based approach much closer to the ABA Model Rules. According to the [New York State Bar Association](https://nysba.org/understanding-changes-to-new-york-state-lawyer-advertising-rules/), the headline changes for Brooklyn firms are:

- **No more mandatory "Attorney Advertising" label.** You no longer have to put those words on your website, emails and mailers. If your templates still carry it from the old rules, you can take it off.
- **The 30-day blackout for personal injury solicitation is gone.** Injury lawyers now follow the same solicitation rules as everyone else, which still ban pressure, coercion and contacting someone who has said no.
- **False or misleading is the test.** Every claim, testimonial and past-results mention must be truthful and not create unjustified expectations. Context and clear wording matter more than fixed disclaimers.
- **"Specialist" claims stay limited.** Only claim to be a certified specialist if you're accredited by an approved body. "Focuses on" or "practice limited to" is safer wording.

Record-keeping, solicitation filing and other details changed too, so read the new rules (or the NYSBA summary) before you rebuild your templates.

These rules change and have detailed exceptions, so treat this as an orientation, not legal advice. Check the current text through the [New York State Unified Court System](https://www.nycourts.gov/) and our broader guide to [lawyer advertising rules by state](/blog/lawyer-advertising-rules-by-state).

### Brooklyn-specific moves for firms

- **Multilingual intake.** If you serve Spanish, Russian, Chinese or Haitian Creole speakers, have someone who speaks that language answer calls and forms.
- **Neighborhood-relevant content.** Pages answering housing court, rent-stabilization and tenant questions attract the right clients.
- **Speed to lead.** The first firm to respond usually wins. Our [law firm intake guide](/blog/law-firm-intake-speed-to-lead) has scripts and response-time targets.

## Home services in brownstone and pre-war Brooklyn

Brownstones, pre-war walk-ups and converted lofts mean steam and radiator heat, aging electrical panels and much-patched plumbing.

**Winter is emergency season.** No-heat calls, frozen pipes in unheated back rooms and boiler failures spike when temperatures drop. Make sure your profile hours are accurate, your phone gets answered, and your site has a clear "Emergency boiler repair in Brooklyn" page.

**Coastal neighborhoods remember Sandy.** Hurricane Sandy in October 2012 flooded parts of Red Hook, Coney Island, Gerritsen Beach and other low-lying areas. Waterproofing and backflow prevention still matter there. Speak plainly and avoid scare tactics.

**Landlords are a customer segment.** A short page for small landlords and property managers (response times, invoicing, building access) can bring repeat work.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a separate page for every Brooklyn neighborhood?

No. Build pages only for the neighborhoods you actually serve and can say something specific about. Thin pages with the neighborhood name swapped can hurt more than help.

### Should my Brooklyn website be in more than one language?

If your staff can serve customers in another language, yes. A real page in Russian, Spanish, Chinese, Yiddish or Haitian Creole helps those customers find you and trust you, but have a fluent speaker review it.

### Do New York lawyer websites still need the "Attorney Advertising" label?

No. Since the amended rules took effect on June 1, 2026, the label is no longer required. Your site and emails still must not be false or misleading, so check the new rules before you publish.

### Does the NY SHIELD Act apply to a small Brooklyn business?

It can apply to any business that holds certain private information about New York residents, though small businesses can scale their safeguards to their size. Check the Attorney General's guidance for current details.

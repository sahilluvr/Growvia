Miami small business marketing has one rule that out-of-town agencies keep missing: in much of Miami-Dade County, your customer's first language is Spanish, and their first search happens on a phone, in Google Maps, often in Spanish or in a mix of both. An English-only website with a stock photo of the skyline does not win Hialeah, Doral or Westchester.

Miami-Dade is home to about 2.7 million people (2020 Census, see [Census QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/)), and the Census Bureau reports the county is majority Hispanic or Latino. It is also a hurricane market, an international real estate market and a medical tourism destination, all at once. Each of those changes what "good marketing" looks like.

This guide walks through how Miami-Dade customers search, how to compete in a crowded Google Maps pack, and what dentists, law firms, contractors, med spas and agents need to do differently here. Law firms get the most detail, because The Florida Bar runs one of the strictest advertising filing systems in the country.

## Miami-Dade at a glance: who you are marketing to

Miami-Dade is not one market. It is a patchwork of cities and neighborhoods with different languages, incomes and habits.

- **City of Miami:** Brickell and Downtown (condo towers, young professionals, finance), Little Havana (Cuban-American roots, Spanish-first), Wynwood and Edgewater (creative businesses, restaurants), Little Haiti (Haitian Creole speakers).
- **Hialeah and Miami Lakes:** dense, working-family, strongly Spanish-first. Reviews in Spanish carry real weight here.
- **Doral:** logistics, corporate offices near the airport, and a large Venezuelan community.
- **Coral Gables and Coconut Grove:** affluent, older homes, premium services, University of Miami.
- **Kendall, Westchester and The Hammocks:** suburban families, a lot of dental, pediatric and home service demand.
- **Homestead and Miami Beach:** newer subdivisions and farmland in the south (hit hardest by Hurricane Andrew in 1992), and a review-driven hospitality economy on the Beach.

A single "Miami" landing page is too blunt. If you serve Kendall and Doral, say so on your site and in your Google Business Profile service areas.

## How Miami customers search

### Spanish, English and Spanglish

People in Miami search in whatever language comes first in the moment: "dentista cerca de mí," "abogado de accidentes Miami," "emergency plumber Hialeah," or a mix. Google matches language as well as words. If your site has no Spanish pages, you are invisible to a big share of those searches.

What works:

1. **Real Spanish pages, not a translate widget.** Have a fluent speaker review your top service pages, using the words locals actually say.
2. **A Spanish-speaking contact path.** Say "Hablamos español" near your phone number only if someone who answers actually does.
3. **Spanish in your Google Business Profile.** Your business description, posts and Q&A can include Spanish. Keep the business name as it legally is, with no keyword stuffing.
4. **Spanish reviews.** Ask Spanish-speaking customers to review in whichever language they prefer. Reviews that mention "dentista" or "plomero" help you show for those searches.
5. **Other languages where they fit,** such as Haitian Creole in North Miami and Little Haiti.

### Maps first, then AI assistants

For local services, the Google Maps "3-pack" decides most first clicks. More people also now ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity "who's the best Spanish-speaking dentist in Kendall?" Those assistants lean on your reviews, your website, directory listings and consistent business details. ## Competing in a crowded Miami Maps pack

In categories like dentists, personal injury lawyers, med spas and roofers, Miami's Maps results are packed with established businesses that have hundreds of reviews. You will not beat them on volume in a month. You can beat them on relevance and proximity in your own corner of the county.

- **Pick the right primary category.** It changes which searches you show for. Our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide) explains how to choose.

## Marketing a law firm in Miami (and Florida Bar ad filing)

Miami is one of the most competitive legal markets in the country, especially for personal injury, immigration, family law and property insurance work. It is also governed by The Florida Bar's advertising rules, which go further than most states. Read this section before you spend a dollar on ads. For the full firm growth system, see our [AI marketing guide for lawyers](/blog/ai-marketing-for-lawyers).

### What has to be filed

According to The Florida Bar's [advertising filing requirements page](https://www.floridabar.org/ethics/etad/advertising-filing-requirements/), lawyers must file most ads for review **at least 20 days before first use**:

- Television, radio, print and internet advertisements (banner ads, paid social ads and similar)
- Unsolicited direct mail and direct email

The filing fee is **$250 per advertisement** when filed on time, and **$750** when filed late (less than 20 days before use). The bar says it responds within 15 days of receiving a complete filing.

### What is exempt from filing

The Bar lists several exemptions, including:

- Your law firm website and pop-ups on your own website
- Mailings to other lawyers, current clients and former clients
- Communications a prospective client asked for
- Legal directory listings
- "Tombstone" ads limited to basic information like name, contact details and practice areas

The Bar's FAQ also says social media pages used for professional promotion are subject to the advertising rules but don't have to be filed. Paid social ads are a different story and generally need filing unless the content is limited to basic, presumptively valid information.

> Exempt from filing does not mean exempt from the rules. Your website, Google Business Profile and social posts must still follow Florida's content rules on misleading claims, testimonials and past results. This is general information, not legal advice. Check The Florida Bar's current rules and handbook before you publish.

### Content rules Miami firms trip over

- **Required details.** Every ad must name at least one responsible lawyer or the firm, and give the city, town or county of at least one real office.
- **The "Advertisement" label.** The Bar's FAQ says direct mail needs "advertisement" on the envelope and each enclosure, direct email subject lines must begin with "advertisement," and targeted social or text messages must start with it.
- **The 30-day rule.** Florida bars written solicitations about personal injury or wrongful death within 30 days of the incident. The U.S. Supreme Court upheld that rule in *Florida Bar v. Went For It* (1995). After a crash on the Dolphin Expressway, no letters for a month.
- **Testimonials and results.** Testimonials that mention results need a disclaimer, and past results can't be framed so they mislead.
- **Spanish ads count too.** They are filed just like English ones.

## Marketing a dental practice in Miami

Miami dental practices compete on two fronts: neighbors down the street and patients weighing a trip abroad for cheaper implants or veneers. You beat the second group on trust, convenience and follow-up care, not price.

- **Bilingual treatment pages.** Implants, Invisalign, veneers and emergency care, each with an English and Spanish page.
- **Insurance clarity.** Many Miami patients ask first about insurance. List the plans you accept, in both languages.
- **Reviews that mention specifics.** "Dr. Gómez explained everything in Spanish" helps you show for the patients you want.
- **HIPAA-safe review replies.** HHS settled with Elite Dental Associates in Dallas for $10,000 in 2019, and New Vision Dental in California for $23,000 in 2022, for disclosing patient information in Yelp replies. Never confirm someone is a patient in a reply. Our [dental reviews and HIPAA guide](/blog/dental-office-google-reviews-hipaa) has safe reply templates.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a Spanish website to market in Miami?

You don't need a full duplicate site, but you should have Spanish versions of your most important service pages and a Spanish-speaking way to contact you. In much of Miami-Dade, Spanish searches are a large share of local demand.

### Do Florida lawyers have to file their websites with The Florida Bar?

No. The Florida Bar exempts lawyer and law firm websites from filing, but websites must still follow the advertising content rules. Most TV, radio, print, paid internet ads and unsolicited direct mail or email must be filed at least 20 days before use.

### How much does it cost to file a lawyer ad in Florida?

The Florida Bar charges $250 per advertisement filed at least 20 days before first use and $750 for late filings. Check the Bar's site for current fees.

### When should Miami contractors start hurricane marketing?

Start before the season opens on June 1, with inspection and preparation content in late spring. After a storm, focus on honest response times and helpful information rather than pressure tactics.

### Can a Miami dentist reply to a negative Google review?

Yes, but never confirm the person is a patient or mention any treatment. Keep replies general, invite them to call the office, and take the details offline.

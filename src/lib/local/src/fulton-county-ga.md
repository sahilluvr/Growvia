Atlanta small business marketing is shaped by two things locals talk about constantly: traffic and which side of the Perimeter you're on. A customer in Johns Creek will not drive to East Point for a dental cleaning, and a homeowner in College Park doesn't want a plumber coming from Milton at rush hour. In Fulton County, the business that looks closest and most trustworthy in Google Maps usually gets the call.

Fulton County has about 1.1 million people (2020 Census, see [Census QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/)). It stretches a long way, from Milton and Johns Creek in the north, through Sandy Springs and the city of Atlanta, down to South Fulton, Fairburn and Palmetto. Most of the city of Atlanta sits in Fulton, though part of it extends into DeKalb County.

This guide covers how to plan your service radius, how north and south Fulton differ, what Atlanta dentists and law firms need to know, and a 90-day plan you can start this week.

## North Fulton, intown and South Fulton are different markets

It's tempting to treat "Atlanta" as one audience. Your Maps rankings, reviews and offers will work better if you don't.

::compare North Fulton vs South Fulton | North (Alpharetta, Johns Creek, Milton, Roswell, Sandy Springs): suburban families and corporate offices along GA-400; strong demand for cosmetic and family dentistry, tutoring, home upgrades; heavy research before buying | South (South Fulton, East Point, College Park, Union City, Fairburn, Palmetto): established neighborhoods and new subdivisions near the airport; strong word of mouth, churches and community groups; value and responsiveness win

**Intown Atlanta** is its own mix again: Midtown and Downtown offices and condos, Buckhead's high-end retail and residences, and neighborhoods like Old Fourth Ward, Inman Park, West End and Grant Park, plus DeKalb-side favorites like Kirkwood, where people shop local and expect businesses to know the area.

Other local factors worth knowing:

- **Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport** on the south side drives hotels, logistics, transportation and a large workforce living nearby.
- **The film and TV industry.** Georgia's film tax credit turned the metro into a major production hub, and Tyler Perry Studios sits on the former Fort McPherson in southwest Atlanta. That brings demand for equipment rental, catering, short-term housing, security, transport and trades who can work on production schedules.
- **Universities.** Georgia Tech, Georgia State and the Atlanta University Center colleges (including Morehouse, Spelman and Clark Atlanta) shape intown demand, with student and young-professional customers.
- **Corporate relocations** to Alpharetta, Midtown and Sandy Springs bring newcomers who have no local dentist, lawyer or contractor yet.

## Traffic sets your real service radius

I-285 (the Perimeter), I-75/85 through the Downtown Connector, and GA-400 decide how far customers will travel and how far your crews can reach in a reasonable time. Your Google Business Profile service area and your city pages should match that reality, not wishful thinking.

How to set a realistic radius:

1. **Map your best customers.** Pull addresses of your last 100 paying customers and see where they cluster.
2. **Check drive times at 5 p.m.,** not at noon. A 12-mile trip on GA-400 can be a very different trip at rush hour.
3. **Build pages only for areas you serve well.** A Roswell HVAC company might serve Roswell, Alpharetta, Milton, Sandy Springs and East Cobb (which is in Cobb County, so say so). It probably shouldn't claim College Park.
4. **Track rank by area.** You can rank in the top 3 a mile from your office and not appear five miles away.

## Marketing a dental practice in Atlanta

Atlanta dental marketing looks different depending on where your chairs are.

- **North Fulton practices** often compete on cosmetic work, Invisalign and implants, and patients compare several offices online before booking. Strong treatment pages, real photos of your team and office, and detailed reviews matter.
- **Intown practices** serve professionals who want early, late or lunchtime appointments near work. Say your hours clearly, and add parking and MARTA directions.
- **South Fulton practices** often win on family care, insurance acceptance and being easy to reach. List the plans you accept and make new-patient booking simple.

### Georgia's advertising rules

Georgia lawyer advertising is governed by Rules 7.1 through 7.5 of the Georgia Rules of Professional Conduct, which the State Bar of Georgia publishes on [gabar.org](https://www.gabar.org/). Like the ABA Model Rules they are based on, Georgia's rules prohibit false or misleading communications about a lawyer's services, regulate paying others for recommendations, and limit direct solicitation. Georgia also requires lawyers to keep copies of their advertisements for a period of time (two years under the current rule).

Georgia's approach differs from Florida's filing and fee system, so don't assume a Florida or Texas checklist carries over in either direction. If you advertise in both states, follow each state's rules for the ads that run there.

> Georgia's rules have specific requirements on disclosures, testimonials and written solicitations that we don't reproduce here. Read the current rules on the State Bar of Georgia's site, or ask the Bar's ethics counsel, before you launch a campaign. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Summer heat, spring pollen and home services

Atlanta summers are long, hot and humid, so AC repair is close to an emergency service from late spring into early fall. Spring brings the city's famous yellow coat of pine pollen, which drives demand for HVAC filter changes, pressure washing, gutter cleaning and car detailing. Winters are usually mild, but occasional ice storms and hard freezes cause burst pipes and tree damage, and the January 2014 storm that stranded drivers on Atlanta's highways is still a local reference point.

::chat Example: a June AC call that turns into a maintenance customer (illustrative) | them: Our AC stopped cooling and it's 94 out. Can someone come today in Alpharetta? | you: Yes, we have a tech in Alpharetta this afternoon. Is anyone at home with health concerns in the heat? | them: My mom lives with us, so yes. | you: We'll prioritize you for 2 p.m. After the repair, ask the tech about our spring and fall tune-up plan.

Seasonal plan for home service companies:

- **February–March:** tune-up offers by email to past customers, before the first hot week.
- **April:** pollen-season content (filters, duct cleaning, pressure washing).
- **May–September:** fast response, honest wait times on your Google Business Profile.
- **October–November:** heating checks and maintenance plan renewals.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Atlanta in Fulton County?

Most of the city of Atlanta is in Fulton County, but part of it extends into DeKalb County. Fulton also includes cities like Sandy Springs, Alpharetta, Johns Creek, Roswell, South Fulton, East Point and College Park.

### How big should my service area be in Atlanta?

Base it on drive times at rush hour and where your best customers already are, not on a fixed mile radius. Many businesses rank well near their office and drop off quickly a few miles away.

### Do Georgia lawyers have to file ads with the State Bar?

Georgia's rules are different from Florida's filing and fee system, and Georgia requires lawyers to keep copies of their ads. Check the current Rules 7.1 through 7.5 on the State Bar of Georgia's site or ask the Bar's ethics counsel before launching a campaign.

### When should Atlanta HVAC companies start summer marketing?

Start in February and March with tune-up offers to past customers, before the first hot spell. By May, focus on fast response and honest wait times.

Most happy customers never leave a review. They aren't being rude. Nobody asked them, or someone asked at the wrong moment, or the link took four taps to find. Meanwhile, the one customer who had a bad day always seems to find the review button.

This guide fixes that the honest way. You'll learn where to find your Google review link and QR code, when and how to ask, and what to say on WhatsApp, SMS, email, receipts and at the counter. You'll also get reply templates, and a clear list of what Google, the US FTC and Indian regulators treat as fake or manipulative.

No tricks, no review-buying, and nothing that can get your profile restricted. Just a simple habit your whole team can run.

## Why Google reviews matter more than you think

Reviews do two jobs at once. They help you show up, and they help you get chosen once you do.

### Reviews help your Maps and local ranking

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Prominence is how well-known and trusted your business is. On its [official local ranking page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091), Google states that review count and review score are factored into local search ranking, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help your ranking.

You can't change your distance from a searcher, but reviews are a prominence signal you can grow every week. If you haven't set up the basics yet, start with our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide).

### Reviews decide who gets the call

Search "dentist near me" or "AC repair Mohali" and you see several businesses side by side, with star rating and review count next to each name. A shop with 9 reviews from 2022 looks riskier than one with 140 reviews and three from last week, even if the work is identical.

Reviews also show what you're good at in your customers' words. "Fixed our inverter the same evening" sells better than anything you can write yourself.

## Get your Google review link and QR code

Before you ask anyone, make leaving a review take one tap. Google gives every verified Business Profile a direct review link and a QR code.

### How to find your review link

Google's [help page on review links and QR codes](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815) describes the current steps:

1. Sign in to the Google account that manages your Business Profile.
2. Open your profile (search your business name on Google, or go to business.google.com).
3. Select **Read reviews**, then **Get more reviews**.
4. Click the copy icon to copy your review link.
5. To get the QR code, right-click it and choose **Save image as**.

Google notes that review QR codes can only be generated from a computer browser, not from a phone. Do it once on a laptop and save both the link and the image somewhere your team can find them.

> Tip: Paste your review link into your WhatsApp Business quick replies, your email signature and your billing software's receipt footer today. Then you never have to search for it again.

### Test it like a customer

Open the link on a phone that isn't logged in to your business account. Customers must be signed in to a Google account to post, and Google says they can create one with a non-Gmail email address.

## When to ask: timing beats everything

The best time to ask is when the customer has just felt the value. Not when they've paid, but when they've *experienced* the result.

### Good moments to ask

- **Right after the "wow".** The haircut is done and they're smiling in the mirror. The car is washed and handed back. The website went live.
- **When they thank you.** "Thank you so much" is the perfect cue for "It would really help us if you said that on Google."
- **After delivery is confirmed.** For products, ask a day or two after delivery, once they've actually used it.
- **After a problem is solved well.** A customer whose complaint you fixed quickly is often your most enthusiastic reviewer.

### Bad moments to ask

Don't ask before the job is finished, in the middle of a complaint, or weeks later when they've forgotten the details. One ask plus one gentle reminder is enough.

### Ask everyone, not just the happy ones

Google's policy says businesses must not "selectively solicit positive reviews from customers". So you can't send the link only to people who gave you 9 or 10 on a feedback form, or ask "Were you happy? If yes, review us here."

The fix is simple. Make asking part of your process for every completed job or order. Some reviews will be four stars, and a natural mix looks more believable anyway.

## Where and how to ask: channels that work

Use the channel your customer already talks to you on: often WhatsApp in India, email for B2B and online stores, and the counter for walk-in businesses.

| Channel | Best for | When to send | Key detail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| In person | Salons, clinics, restaurants, retail | At the moment of thanks or handover | Show the QR code on a small stand or card |
| WhatsApp | Services, home repairs, local shops | Same day, within a few hours of completion | Short message with link, from the number they know |
| SMS | Customers not on WhatsApp, appointment businesses | Same day | Keep under 160 characters, include business name |
| Email | Online stores, agencies, B2B, consultants | 1–3 days after delivery or project close | Personal tone, sent from a real person's inbox |
| Receipt or invoice | Retail, restaurants, any billing software | At billing | QR code plus one line of text |
| Packaging insert | E-commerce, home delivery | Inside the box | Thank-you card with QR code |
| Follow-up call | High-value services, B2B | After a successful project | Ask verbally, then send the link right away |

### In person

Spoken requests feel human, so they work. Keep it short: "We're a small team and Google reviews really help new customers find us. Would you mind leaving one? This QR code takes you straight there."

Don't stand over them while they type. Google's policy says businesses must not require customers to leave a rating while on the premises. Ask, share the QR code, and let them do it in their own time.

### WhatsApp

WhatsApp works well because the customer already trusts your number and the link opens in one tap. If you send review requests at scale, use the official WhatsApp Business API and approved templates rather than bulk-sending from a personal phone. Our [WhatsApp Business API guide](/blog/whatsapp-business-api-guide-small-business) explains the setup.

### Email

Email suits longer relationships: agencies, consultants, online stores and B2B suppliers. Send it from a real person, not "noreply", and keep it as short as a note to a friend.

## What's forbidden: Google's rules and the law

### Google's fake engagement rules

Google's [prohibited and restricted content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) covers reviews under "fake engagement". In plain words, you must not:

- **Offer incentives for reviews.** No discounts, freebies, cashback or lucky draws, even if you don't ask for a positive review.
- **Gate reviews.** Don't ask only happy customers, and don't route unhappy ones to a private form while sending happy ones to Google.
- **Discourage negative reviews.** No refunds or favours in exchange for removing criticism.
- **Post reviews with a conflict of interest.** No reviews from owners, current or former staff, family or business partners, and none of your competitors.
- **Buy reviews or use review services.** That includes "review exchange" groups.
- **Set staff review quotas.** Google's policy calls out asking staff to collect a set number of reviews, or reviews that name a staff member.

If Google detects this, it may unpublish existing reviews for a period, stop new reviews for a period, and display a warning telling customers that fake reviews were removed. You'll get an email first, and you can appeal.

> Tip: Google is stricter than some laws. Follow Google's rule and you're usually safe everywhere.

### The US FTC rule on fake reviews

If you serve US customers, the FTC's Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials took effect on 21 October 2024. According to the [FTC's official Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers), it bans:

- Fake reviews, including AI-generated ones, and buying or selling them.
- Incentives conditioned on the review being positive (or negative).
- Undisclosed reviews by company officers, managers and certain relatives.
- Suppressing reviews through groundless legal threats or intimidation.
- Buying or selling fake followers or views.

The rule lets the FTC seek civil penalties for knowing violations. The FTC also notes that asking only happy customers for reviews could violate the broader FTC Act, even though the rule itself doesn't name it.

### India: IS 19000:2022 and consumer protection law

In India, the Bureau of Indian Standards published **IS 19000:2022**, "Online Consumer Reviews — Principles and Requirements for their Collection, Moderation and Publication". It mainly applies to platforms that publish reviews, covering reviewer verification, unbiased publishing and disclosure of incentivised reviews.

The standard is voluntary. In May 2024 the Department of Consumer Affairs held a [stakeholder consultation on fake reviews](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2020665&reg=48&lang=2) and said it would draft a Quality Control Order to make compliance mandatory. At the time of writing that order hasn't been finalised, so check the current status.

Separately, the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 covers misleading unfair trade practices, and the E-Commerce Rules, 2020 bar sellers from posing as consumers to post reviews. The practical message matches Google's: genuine customers only.

## How to respond to reviews (with templates)

Replies show future customers how you treat people. Google says your reply appears publicly under the review, and the reviewer is notified and can still edit their review. A good reply to a bad review can change the rating.

### Replying to positive reviews

Thank them, mention something specific, and keep it short. Don't paste the same line under every review.

> Thank you, [Name]! We're so glad the [specific thing] worked out for your [occasion]. See you next time at [Business].

### Replying to negative reviews

Wait until you're calm. Then follow four steps:

1. Thank them and acknowledge the specific issue.
2. Apologise for their experience without arguing about details in public.
3. Explain briefly what you're doing about it, if relevant.
4. Move the conversation offline with a real name and contact.

> Hi [Name], thank you for telling us about this, and I'm sorry the [issue] wasn't what you expected. That's not the standard we aim for. I'd like to make it right. Please call or WhatsApp me directly on [number], and ask for [Your name]. [Your name], Owner

> Hi [Name], I'm sorry your wait was longer than promised. We were short-staffed that evening, but that's our problem to solve, not yours. We've added a second person on weekend shifts. I'd love the chance to welcome you back. [Your name]

### Response do's and don'ts

- **Do** reply within a day or two when you can.
- **Do** keep replies human and specific.
- **Don't** reveal private details, such as appointment history, medical information or order contents.
- **Don't** offer a refund or gift in exchange for editing or deleting the review.
- **Don't** argue, accuse the reviewer of lying, or threaten legal action in public.

## Handling fake, spammy or defamatory reviews

Sometimes a review truly breaks the rules: spam, hate speech, a competitor, or someone who was never a customer. You can report these.

### How to report a review

Google's [review removal help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773) gives this flow:

1. Open your Business Profile and select **Read reviews**.
2. Next to the review, select the report option.
3. Choose the reason that fits, such as spam, off-topic, conflict of interest, or offensive content.
4. Submit the report.

Evaluation usually takes several days. You can track it in Google's **Reviews Management Tool**, which shows statuses such as "Decision pending" or "Report reviewed – no policy violation". If Google finds no violation, you can submit a one-time appeal through the same tool, and Google emails you the outcome.

Google is clear that you shouldn't report a review just because you disagree with it. Spam, off-topic, hateful or conflict-of-interest reviews can be removed. A harsh but genuine one-star review usually stays.

### When it's defamation or extortion

If a review makes false and damaging factual claims, Google has a separate legal removal request process. For serious cases, talk to a lawyer. If someone threatens bad reviews unless you pay, Google's help pages provide specific guidance for reporting extortion. Keep screenshots and dates of everything.

> Tip: While a report is pending, still post a calm public reply. Future customers will read your response long before Google decides.

## Build a steady review habit

A burst of 40 reviews followed by six months of silence looks odd. A steady flow of fresh reviews looks natural and keeps your profile current.

### Make it part of the workflow

- **Pick one trigger moment** per customer type: "job marked complete", "order delivered", "appointment checked out".
- **Assign one owner.** Someone sends the request the same day, every day.
- **Automate the boring part.** Have your CRM, billing tool or WhatsApp templates send the request when a job is closed.
- **Review weekly.** Spend ten minutes every Monday replying to new reviews and checking whether requests went out.

### Train staff without breaking rules

Teach staff to ask every customer in their own words, using the QR code or link. Celebrate good reviews in team meetings, but skip per-person quotas and never ask customers to name staff.

### Common mistakes to avoid

- Running one big review campaign a year instead of asking every week.
- Letting negative reviews sit unanswered for weeks.
- Offering "a free dessert for a review", which is still an incentive.
- Asking friends and family to "get the ball rolling".

## Track your reviews against competitors

Your review count only means something next to your competitors. Ten new reviews a month is great if rivals get two, and not enough if the leader gets thirty.

Track these numbers monthly for you and your three to five closest competitors:

- Total number of reviews.
- Average star rating.
- New reviews per month (review velocity).
- How often each business replies.

Competitor reviews are also free market research: what customers praise and complain about shows what buyers in your area care about. Our [local competitor analysis guide](/blog/local-competitor-analysis) walks through the full process.

A monthly spreadsheet works fine. If you use Growvia, the competitor tracker shows review counts and new reviews per month for you and your rivals side by side, so you can see whether your review habit is actually closing the gap.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many Google reviews does a small business need?

There's no magic number. What matters is how you compare with the businesses shown next to you in Maps, and whether your reviews are recent. Aim to match your closest competitors, then keep reviews coming monthly.

### Can I offer a discount or freebie for leaving a Google review?

No. Google's policy bans offering payment, discounts, free goods or services in exchange for any review, positive or negative. Even an "honest review" incentive breaks the rule and can lead to reviews being removed or your profile being restricted.

### Is it okay to ask only my happy customers for reviews?

No. Google prohibits selectively soliciting positive reviews, and the FTC says it may violate US consumer protection law. Ask every customer as part of your normal process and let the ratings fall naturally.

### Can I delete a bad review on Google?

You can't delete a review yourself. You can report it if it breaks Google's content policies, such as spam, conflict of interest or offensive content. A genuine negative review usually stays, so a calm, helpful reply is your best response.

### Can my employees or family leave Google reviews for my business?

They shouldn't. Google treats reviews from current or former employees, family members and business partners as conflict-of-interest content. In the US, the FTC rule also restricts undisclosed reviews from company insiders and certain relatives.

### Should I reply to every Google review?

Ideally, yes, and always to negative ones. Replies show future customers that you listen. Keep them short, specific and personal.

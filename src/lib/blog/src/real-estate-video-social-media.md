A buyer who has watched you walk through a neighborhood for three minutes already feels like they know you. A seller who has seen your listing videos already knows what you'll do for their home. That's what real estate video marketing does better than any other channel: it lets people meet you before they call you.

Most agents know this and still don't post video, or post a slideshow of listing photos set to stock music. The usual blockers are time, camera nerves and not knowing what to film. This guide removes all three. You'll get a listing video shot list, a format for neighborhood tours that keep working on YouTube for years, a weekly reels rhythm, how to use AI for scripts without sounding like a robot, and how to keep captions and ads inside Fair Housing rules.

## The four videos every agent should make

You don't need to be everywhere. Four kinds of video cover almost everything a buyer or seller wants to know.

| Video type | Goal | Length | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listing walkthrough | Sell the home and impress future sellers | 2–4 minutes | YouTube, listing page, MLS virtual tour link (if allowed) |
| Neighborhood tour | Rank for area searches, attract relocating buyers | 4–10 minutes | YouTube, neighborhood guide on your site |
| Short answer | Build trust on one question | 30–60 seconds | Reels, Shorts, TikTok, Google Business Profile |
| Market update | Show you know the numbers | 1–3 minutes | YouTube, email, social |

Start with listing walkthroughs and short answers, because they're quickest to make. Add neighborhood tours once you have a routine. They take longer to film, but they have the longest shelf life.

## Listing videos: a shot list that works

A good listing video feels like a showing with a knowledgeable friend. Your voice explains what photos can't: how the rooms connect, where the light comes from, what the trade-offs are.

Before you film, get the seller's written permission, agree on what can be shown, and ask them to put away personal photos, mail, medication, valuables and anything that reveals security systems or kids' names. Never film neighbors or their homes in detail.

::steps Listing video shot list | Exterior and street — 10 seconds showing the home and the block | Entry and flow — walk in and show how main rooms connect | Kitchen and living areas — light, storage and one standout detail | Bedrooms and baths — quick and steady, primary suite last | Outdoor space — yard, patio, garage, views | Close on camera — one line on who to contact and how

Practical tips:

- **Film horizontal for YouTube, vertical for reels.** If you only film once, film horizontal in 4K and crop vertical clips later.
- **Steady beats fancy.** A cheap phone gimbal makes a bigger difference than a new camera.
- **Audio matters more than video.** A wireless lavalier mic costs little and makes you sound professional.
- **Natural light, lights on.** Film mid-morning or early afternoon with every light on and blinds open.
- **Say something real.** "The kitchen faces west, so it's bright in the afternoon. The pantry is small, but the garage has room for shelving." Honesty builds trust with buyers and sellers who watch.

### Drone footage rules

Aerial shots are great for larger lots, waterfront homes and showing a neighborhood's layout. But flying a drone for business is commercial use.

> Warning: in the US, flying a drone for real estate marketing requires an FAA Part 107 remote pilot certificate, and some airspace needs authorization. Hire a certified pilot or get certified yourself. Check the [FAA's guidance for commercial drone operators](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators).

Local rules on flying over neighbors or parks may also apply.

## Neighborhood tours that rank on YouTube

Neighborhood tours are the best long-term video investment for most agents. People relocating search YouTube for "living in [neighborhood]" or "moving to [city]," and a good tour can keep bringing in leads for years.

What to include:

- **The main streets and housing types.** Drive or walk so viewers see what homes actually look like.
- **Daily life.** Coffee shops, grocery stores, parks, trails, gyms, restaurants.
- **Commute reality.** Show the route to the main employment area and say how long it takes at rush hour.
- **New development.** Construction, new shops or roads coming soon.
- **Honest trade-offs.** Train noise, traffic choke points, flood areas, HOA rules.

Keep it about places and amenities. Never describe who lives there. More on Fair Housing below.

### YouTube SEO basics for agents

YouTube is a search engine. A few habits help your tours get found:

- **Title with the place name first.** "Living in Highland Park, Denver: Full Neighborhood Tour (2026)" beats "My Favorite Neighborhood!"
- **Description that's useful.** Two or three paragraphs summarizing the video, the areas covered, and a link to your written neighborhood guide.
- **Chapters.** Add timestamps in your description to create [video chapters](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579), such as "Housing," "Parks," "Commute," "Schools info." Viewers jump to what they care about.
- **Captions.** Upload or correct captions using YouTube's [subtitle tools](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2734796). It helps accessibility and gives YouTube more text to understand the video.
- **A clear thumbnail.** You, a recognizable local landmark and three or four words.
- **Playlists by area.** Group tours, market updates and listings for each neighborhood.

Embed each tour on the matching neighborhood guide on your website. The video keeps visitors on the page longer, and the page gives the video more context. Our guide to [hyperlocal real estate SEO](/blog/hyperlocal-real-estate-seo) explains how to structure those neighborhood pages.

## Reels and short video: a weekly rhythm

Short vertical videos on Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Facebook reach people who don't follow you yet. The goal isn't viral fame. It's familiarity: being the agent people in your area keep seeing.

A sustainable weekly mix for a busy agent:

- **One listing or just-sold clip.** A 30-second highlight, not the full tour.
- **One short answer.** "What's a buyer agreement?" "Should I sell before I buy?" "What does closing cost here?"
- **One local spot.** A new restaurant, a park, a farmers market, with you talking briefly on camera.
- **One personal or behind-the-scenes post.** Prepping for an open house, a client handover (with permission), your day.

::flow Turn one shoot into a week of content | Film one neighborhood tour | Upload the full tour to YouTube | Cut three vertical clips for reels | Post a still and link on your Google profile | Send the video in your monthly email

Batch your filming. Spend one morning a week, or two mornings a month, filming several clips at once. Then edit and schedule them across the week.

### Music, permissions and endorsements

- **Use licensed audio.** Business accounts often have a limited music library on some platforms. Use those tracks or royalty-free music you've licensed.
- **Get permission before filming clients.** Written consent for any client who appears, and don't share their address or personal details.
- **Disclose paid partnerships.** If a lender, stager or builder pays you or gives you something to feature them, disclose it clearly. The FTC's [disclosure guide for influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers) applies to businesses posting sponsored content too.

## Using AI for scripts without sounding like a robot

AI is genuinely useful for video. It's good at structure, hooks and turning rough notes into a script you can read aloud. It's bad at local knowledge and often writes in a generic, overexcited style.

A workflow that keeps your voice:

1. **Write rough notes first.** Five or six bullet points about the home or the area in your own words, including the trade-offs.
2. **Ask AI for structure.** "Turn these notes into a 60-second video script with a hook in the first five seconds and one clear call to action. Plain, conversational tone. Don't add facts that aren't in my notes."
3. **Read it out loud and cut.** Remove anything you wouldn't say to a client in person. Remove anything you can't verify.
4. **Check for Fair Housing problems.** AI often writes phrases like "perfect for growing families." Rewrite those to describe the property.
5. **Film from bullet points, not a teleprompter.** Most agents sound more natural glancing at three key points than reading a full script.

AI is also handy after filming: writing YouTube descriptions, suggesting chapter titles, drafting captions in several lengths, and turning a long video into a short email.

## Fair Housing-safe captions and ads

The federal [Fair Housing Act](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp) bans housing advertising that indicates a preference, limitation or discrimination based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status or disability, and many states and cities add more protected classes. Social posts about listings and neighborhoods count as advertising.

::compare Rewriting a listing caption | Risky: Perfect for young families!; Quiet, safe neighborhood; Walk to St. Luke's church; Ideal bachelor pad | Better: 4 bedrooms and a fenced backyard; Cul-de-sac with little through traffic; Walk to shops, parks and places of worship; Open-plan 1-bedroom with a home office nook

::check Caption check before you post | Describes the home and amenities, not the people | No words about who would fit in or be ideal | No "safe" or "exclusive" claims | School info points to official boundary tools | Required brokerage and license details included per your state | Any paid partnership disclosed

Boosted posts and paid ads have extra rules. Meta requires housing ads to run under a special ad category with limited targeting options, so you can't target by age, gender or narrow ZIP codes the way you might for other businesses. Follow the platform's rules rather than trying to work around them. Your state real estate commission may also require your brokerage name or license number in ads, so check your state's current rules.

## Turning video viewers into clients

Views are nice. Conversations are what pay. Make it easy for viewers to take a next step:

- **Say what to do next.** "If you're thinking about moving to Highland Park this year, message me and I'll send you what's sold here in the last 90 days."
- **Link to a specific offer.** A neighborhood sales list, a buyer guide or a home value check, not just your home page.
- **Answer DMs fast.** A reel can bring in messages at odd hours. A shared inbox for Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp and email helps you or your assistant reply quickly.
- **Move new contacts into follow-up.** Someone who messages about a tour is a lead. Put them in your pipeline and nurture them like any other. Our [real estate lead follow-up system](/blog/real-estate-lead-follow-up-system) covers the scripts and cadence.
- **Post videos on your Google Business Profile.** It adds fresh content to the profile people see when searching for agents. See our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide).

## Measuring video results

Don't judge video by views alone. A tour with 400 views from people relocating to your area can be worth more than a reel with 40,000 views from people across the country.

Track:

- **Watch time and average view duration** on YouTube.
- **Search traffic** to your tours (YouTube Studio shows which search terms bring viewers).
- **Messages and inquiries** that mention a video.
- **Leads from neighborhood pages** where a tour is embedded.
- **Listing appointments** where the seller mentions your videos.

Here's a simple way to judge your video habit after three months:

::scale How is your video habit working? | Good: posting weekly and getting inquiries that mention your videos | Needs work: posting regularly but few messages or calls | Poor: posting rarely or only photo slideshows

If you're posting regularly but getting few inquiries, the problem is usually the call to action or the topic. Make your next step more specific, and film more about the areas and questions your best clients care about.

## Frequently asked questions

### What kind of video is best for real estate marketing?

Listing walkthroughs and short question-answer clips are the quickest wins. Neighborhood tours take more effort but keep attracting viewers on YouTube for years, especially people relocating.

### How long should a real estate listing video be?

Two to four minutes is usually enough for a walkthrough. Cut 30-second highlights for reels and Shorts.

### Do I need a license to use a drone for real estate videos?

Yes. Flying a drone for business in the US requires an FAA Part 107 remote pilot certificate, and some airspace needs authorization, so hire a certified pilot if you're not certified.

### Can I use AI to write real estate video scripts?

Yes, for structure and first drafts. Give it your own notes, tell it not to add facts, then edit for accuracy, your natural voice and Fair Housing issues before filming.

### What should I avoid in real estate social media captions?

Avoid describing who should live in a home or area, such as "perfect for young families" or "ideal for retirees," and avoid subjective claims like "safe." Describe the property and its amenities instead.

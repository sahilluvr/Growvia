## How Singapore customers find a business

Most local purchases follow a short, mobile-first path. Someone searches Google or Google Maps for "aircon servicing Bishan" or "best laksa near me". They compare ratings and photos, glance at Instagram or TikTok, and then message on WhatsApp to ask about price or availability. If you reply quickly and clearly, you get the booking. If not, the next result gets it.

::flow How a typical Singapore customer chooses you | Google or Maps search on a phone | Reviews, photos and Instagram | WhatsApp: "Got slot this Saturday?" | Quote, booking or order | A review after the visit

That path tells you where to put your effort:

- **Google Business Profile and reviews**, because they decide who appears in the map pack.
- **A fast, mobile-friendly website** with clear prices or price ranges, areas served and a way to contact you.
- **WhatsApp**, because that's where enquiries turn into bookings.
- **Instagram, Facebook and TikTok**, to show your work and stay remembered.
- **Email**, for B2B services, repeat customers and newsletters to people who opted in.

## Singapore's marketing rules, in plain English

This is where many SMEs get caught out. Singapore has three overlapping sets of rules for marketing messages.

### The PDPA: consent for personal data

The Personal Data Protection Act requires you to get consent to collect and use someone's personal data for marketing, tell them the purpose, and let them withdraw that consent. A general "I agree to the terms" checkbox isn't enough for marketing. Ask clearly and separately.

### The Do Not Call (DNC) Registry: phone numbers

The DNC provisions cover marketing messages to Singapore phone numbers: voice calls, SMS, MMS, fax, and messages through phone-number-based apps such as WhatsApp. Before sending a marketing message to a Singapore number, you must check the DNC Registry, unless the person has given you **clear and unambiguous consent** to receive marketing on that number.

A few points that matter for SMEs:

- **Consent beats the registry.** If someone clearly opted in to your WhatsApp or SMS marketing, you can message them even if their number is on the DNC Registry. Keep a record of that consent.
- **Pure service messages are different.** Booking confirmations and appointment reminders without promotional content generally aren't "specified messages" under the DNC rules.
- **B2B is mostly excluded.** Marketing messages sent to organisations for business purposes generally fall outside the DNC provisions.
- **Penalties are serious.** Organisations can face financial penalties of up to S$1 million for breaching the DNC provisions.

### The Spam Control Act: bulk email and SMS

The Spam Control Act covers unsolicited commercial email and SMS sent in bulk. Messages need an unsubscribe facility, and unsolicited ones must be labelled "<ADV>" in the subject or opening. The simplest way to stay safe is to email only people who opted in, and include a working unsubscribe link in every message.

::check A PDPA-friendly marketing checklist for Singapore | Ask for marketing consent separately, with an unticked box | Say which channels you'll use: email, WhatsApp, SMS | Record when and where each person agreed | Check the DNC Registry before messaging numbers without clear consent | Put an unsubscribe or "reply STOP" in every marketing message | Act on opt-outs quickly, across every tool

## Common mistakes Singapore SMEs make

1. **Blasting WhatsApp promotions to a contact list.** Without clear consent, that can breach the DNC rules and get your number restricted by Meta.
2. **No prices or price ranges.** Singapore customers compare quickly. "From S$80" beats "contact us for pricing".
3. **Ignoring reviews.** Reply to every review, good or bad, within a few days. Our [Google reviews guide](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) has templates.
4. **A slow mobile site.** Most of your visitors are on phones. Check your [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained).
5. **Too many disconnected tools.** Leads slip through when chats, forms and emails live in different places.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I send WhatsApp marketing messages in Singapore?

Yes, to people who have given you clear consent to receive marketing on WhatsApp, or after checking that their number isn't on the DNC Registry. On the WhatsApp Business Platform you also need approved templates to start conversations, and Meta's own opt-in rules apply.

### Do I need to check the DNC Registry for emails?

No. The DNC Registry covers Singapore phone numbers. Email marketing is covered by the PDPA and the Spam Control Act, so get consent and include an unsubscribe link.

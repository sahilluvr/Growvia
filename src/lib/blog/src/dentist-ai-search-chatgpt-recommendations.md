More patients now ask an AI assistant before they ask Google. "Who's the best emergency dentist near me that takes Aetna?" goes into ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity, and the answer names three or four practices. If yours isn't one of them, that patient may never see your website or your Maps listing.

There's no ad you can buy to appear in those answers, and no secret prompt trick. AI assistants build recommendations from what the web already says about you: your Google Business Profile, your website, your reviews, health and local directories, and news or community mentions. The practices that show up are the ones with clear, consistent, specific information in all those places.

This guide explains how to get your dental practice recommended by ChatGPT and other AI assistants: how they pick dentists, how to make your practice a clear "entity," why the themes in your reviews matter, what service pages to build, which directories count, and how to track prompts like "best emergency dentist near me." It's part of our wider [AI marketing system for dentists](/blog/ai-marketing-for-dentists).

## How AI assistants choose which dentists to recommend

No AI company publishes a ranking formula for local recommendations, so be wary of anyone who claims to know the exact weights. But how these tools work is well understood at a general level.

### They search the web, then summarize

When someone asks a local question, assistants such as ChatGPT (with search), Gemini, Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot typically run web searches, read the results and summarize them. OpenAI described this when it [introduced ChatGPT search](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-search/). Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode draw on Google's own index and local data, and Google explains how site owners can appear in them on its page about [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).

That means the raw material is mostly the same material that drives local SEO: search results, maps data, reviews and directories.

::flow How an AI assistant builds a dentist recommendation | Patient asks a question | Assistant searches the web and maps data | Reads profiles, reviews, directories, sites | Summarizes 3–5 practices with reasons

### What they seem to look for

Watching how assistants answer dental questions, a few patterns stand out:

- **Clear facts they can confirm.** Name, address, phone, hours, services and insurance that match across several sources.
- **Specific evidence for the question asked.** If the question is about emergencies, practices whose site and reviews mention same-day emergency care are easier to recommend.
- **Reputation signals.** Ratings, review counts and what reviewers actually say.
- **Third-party mentions.** Directories, local news, community organizations and "best of" lists.

Assistants often explain their picks ("known for gentle care with anxious patients," "offers same-day emergency visits"). Those reasons usually trace back to review text and website content. You can influence both honestly.

> Key takeaway: AI assistants recommend practices they can describe with confidence. Your job is to make true, specific facts about your practice easy to find and consistent everywhere.

## Step 1: Make your practice a clear, consistent entity

An "entity" is a thing a search engine or AI system recognizes as distinct, with known attributes. For a dental practice, that means one name, one address, one phone number and a clear set of services, described the same way everywhere.

### Fix name, address and phone everywhere

Inconsistent details make an AI less sure that "Smile Dental Care," "Smile Dental of Plano" and "Dr. Kim's Office" are the same practice. Pick one official version and use it everywhere.

| Source | What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Name, address, phone, hours, categories, services | Feeds Google Maps, AI Overviews and Gemini |
| Your website | Footer, contact page, schema markup | The source you fully control |
| Bing Places | Same details as Google | Bing powers Copilot and is a common search source |
| Apple Business Connect | Same details | Apple Maps and Siri |
| Yelp, Healthgrades, Zocdoc | Profile accuracy, dentist names, services | Frequently cited health and local sources |
| Insurance directories | Your listing in each carrier's find-a-dentist tool | Answers "takes my insurance" questions |
| NPI registry | Dentist and practice details | Official federal record of providers |

Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) explain how to name your profile correctly. Don't stuff keywords into your business name ("Best Plano Dentist Implants"). It breaks Google's rules and can get your profile suspended.

### Keep dentist information accurate too

Patients often ask about individual dentists ("Is Dr. Kim good with kids?"). Make sure each dentist's name is spelled the same way on your site, Google, Healthgrades and other directories, with their education and focus areas. The federal [NPPES NPI registry](https://npiregistry.cms.hhs.gov/) holds each provider's official record, and many directories pull from it, so check that it's up to date.

### Add schema markup

Structured data tells search engines exactly what your page describes. Use the Dentist type from schema.org on your site, with your name, address, phone, hours, geo coordinates and links to your official profiles. Google's [local business structured data documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) explains the required and recommended fields, and our [schema markup guide for local businesses](/blog/schema-markup-for-local-business) shows how to add it.

## Step 2: Shape the themes in your reviews (honestly)

When an assistant says a practice is "great with nervous patients" or "fast with emergencies," it's often summarizing what reviewers wrote. You can't write reviews, and you must not pay for them or filter out unhappy patients. But you can make it easy for happy patients to describe their real experience.

### Ask for detail, not just stars

A review that says "Great!" tells an AI nothing. A review that says "They saw me the same day for a cracked tooth and explained everything" gives it a reason to recommend you for emergencies.

When you ask for a review, gently prompt for specifics:

::chat Asking for a detailed review (example wording) | you: Thanks for coming in today! If you have a minute, a Google review helps other people find us. It's most helpful if you mention what you came in for and how it went. | them: Happy to. Is the link in the email? | you: Yes, it's the button at the top. Thank you so much!

Keep the wording neutral. Don't tell patients what to say or ask only patients you know were happy. Google's policies ban review gating, and the FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (effective October 21, 2024) bans fake reviews, buying reviews and suppressing negative ones. The FTC's [guidance on endorsements and reviews](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews) is worth a read.

### Reply without creating a HIPAA problem

Your replies are public text that assistants can read too. Keep them warm and general. Never confirm someone is a patient or mention treatment. HHS settled with Elite Dental Associates in Dallas for $10,000 in 2019 and with New Vision Dental in California for $23,000 in December 2022 after both disclosed patient information in Yelp review replies. Our [dental reviews and HIPAA guide](/blog/dental-office-google-reviews-hipaa) has safe reply templates.

### Check which themes you're missing

Read your last 50 reviews and tally what people mention. Then compare with what you want to be known for.

::bars Example: themes in a practice's last 50 reviews (illustrative) | Friendly staff: 31 | Gentle cleaning: 18 | On time: 12 | Emergency same day: 3 * | Implants: 1 | Kids: 6

In this hypothetical example, the practice offers same-day emergencies, but almost no reviews mention it. An assistant asked about emergency dentists has little to go on. The fix isn't to coach reviews. It's to make sure emergency patients are asked for a review too, and to describe your emergency service clearly on your site and profile.

## Step 3: Build service pages that answer real questions

Assistants pull facts from websites, so your site should state clearly what you do, for whom, and how. Thin pages that say "We offer a full range of dental services" give them nothing to quote.

### Pages that matter most

- **Emergency dentistry:** what you treat, same-day policy, hours, after-hours instructions.
- **Each high-value treatment:** implants, Invisalign or clear aligners, veneers, crowns, root canals, sedation.
- **Family and kids' dentistry:** ages you see, what first visits look like.
- **Insurance and payment:** plans by name, financing, membership plans.
- **Dental anxiety:** what you do for nervous patients.
- **Location and access:** neighborhoods you serve, parking, transit, languages spoken, accessibility.

### Write in a way AI can quote

Answer questions directly, near the top of each section. "Yes, we see dental emergencies the same day when we have openings. Call before 2pm for the best chance." is far more useful to an assistant (and a patient) than a paragraph of general copy.

::check What makes a dental service page easy for AI to cite | Answers the main question in the first two sentences | Names the treatment the way patients say it | States who it's for and who it isn't for | Lists steps, timing and payment options | Names the dentist who provides it and their training | Includes a short FAQ section | Matches what your Google profile says

Google's guidance on [creating helpful, reliable content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) applies here: write for people first, show real experience and be clear about who wrote it. ## Step 4: Get listed where AI looks for dentists

Assistants lean on third-party sources to confirm what your website claims. For dentists, these are the ones worth your time.

| Directory or source | Priority | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Essential | The single most important local listing |
| Bing Places | High | Feeds Microsoft's search and Copilot |
| Apple Business Connect | High | Apple Maps and Siri |
| Healthgrades | High | Commonly appears in health searches |
| Zocdoc | Medium to high | Useful if you offer online booking through it |
| Yelp | Medium to high | Widely cited for local businesses |
| ADA Find-a-Dentist | Medium | For ADA member dentists |
| State dental association directory | Medium | Credible, often member-only |
| Insurance carrier directories | High for insured patients | Check accuracy with each carrier |
| Local chamber of commerce, community sites | Medium | Local credibility and links |

Quality beats quantity. Ten accurate, complete listings are better than fifty half-filled ones with old phone numbers. Remove or fix duplicates, especially listings for dentists who have left the practice.

Local news and community mentions also help: sponsoring a youth sports team, a free screening day, or a dentist quoted in a local story about kids' dental health. These create credible mentions that assistants and search engines can find.

## Step 5: Track the prompts patients actually use

You can't improve AI visibility without checking it. The simplest approach is a monthly prompt test.

### Build your prompt list

Write 10 to 20 prompts that match how your patients ask. Include your city or neighborhood, services and insurance. For example:

- best dentist in [city]
- best emergency dentist near [neighborhood]
- dentist in [city] that takes [insurance plan]
- gentle dentist for anxious patients in [city]
- dental implants [city] reviews
- family dentist accepting new patients [city]
- Invisalign dentist near [neighborhood]

### Test them consistently

Run each prompt in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google (to see AI Overviews) once a month. Record whether you appear, your position, which competitors appear, and the reasons the assistant gives. Results vary by user, location and day, so look for trends over months rather than reacting to one answer.

::steps A monthly AI visibility routine | Week 1 — Run your prompt list across 3–4 assistants | Week 1 — Record mentions, competitors and stated reasons | Week 2 — Compare with last month | Week 2 — Pick one gap to fix, such as missing emergency info | Weeks 3–4 — Update pages, profiles or directories

## A worked example

Here's a hypothetical case. Dr. Rivera runs a 2-dentist practice in Phoenix. She asks ChatGPT for "best emergency dentist in central Phoenix" and doesn't appear. Two rivals do.

What she finds when she looks:

- Her Google profile lists "Dentist" as the only category, with no services filled in. One rival also uses the "Emergency dental service" category.
- Her website has one line about emergencies on the services page. The rival has a full emergency page with hours and a same-day policy.
- Her Yelp listing has an old phone number from before a move.
- Only a handful of her reviews mention emergencies, though she sees several emergency patients a week.

What she changes over 60 days:

1. Adds the emergency category and services on Google, and updates hours.
2. Publishes a dedicated emergency page with direct answers and schema markup.
3. Fixes Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect and Healthgrades.
4. Makes sure emergency patients get the same neutral review request as everyone else.
5. Re-runs her prompt list monthly to watch for changes.

There's no guarantee she'll appear by a certain date. But each change makes a true fact about her practice easier for an assistant to find and confirm, which is the only lever any practice really has.

## Common mistakes

- **Chasing tricks.** Hidden text, fake listings or "AI optimization" schemes don't work and can hurt your Google standing.
- **Inconsistent details.** Old addresses and phone numbers confuse assistants and patients.
- **Thin service pages.** If your site doesn't say you do implants clearly, don't expect an AI to say it for you.
- **Coaching or gating reviews.** Against Google's policies and the FTC rule.
- **Treatment details in review replies.** A HIPAA risk that has already cost dental practices money.
- **Checking once and giving up.** AI answers shift. Track monthly.

## Frequently asked questions

### How does ChatGPT decide which dentists to recommend?

When it searches the web, ChatGPT reads search results, profiles, reviews and directories, then summarizes the practices it can describe with confidence. OpenAI doesn't publish a ranking formula, but consistent details, specific service information and strong reviews all help.

### Can I pay to be recommended by ChatGPT or Gemini?

No paid placement exists for organic AI recommendations today. Visibility comes from accurate, consistent and specific information about your practice across your website, Google profile, reviews and directories.

### Does my Google Business Profile affect AI search?

Yes. Google's AI Overviews and Gemini draw on Google's local data, and other assistants often surface Maps and review information. Accurate categories, services, hours and reviews on your profile are a core part of AI visibility.

### Which directories matter most for dentists?

Start with Google Business Profile, Bing Places and Apple Business Connect, then Healthgrades, Yelp, Zocdoc if you use it, insurance carrier directories and your state dental association. Accuracy matters more than the number of listings.

### How do I check if AI assistants recommend my dental practice?

Write 10 to 20 prompts that match how patients ask, such as "best emergency dentist in [city]," and run them monthly in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google. Record whether you appear and which competitors do, or use a tool that automates these checks.

### How long does it take to show up in AI recommendations?

There's no fixed timeline, because assistants refresh their sources at different speeds and answers vary by user and location. Treat it like local SEO: fix your foundations, then track trends over several months.

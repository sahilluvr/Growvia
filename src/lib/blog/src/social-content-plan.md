Most small business owners don't fail at social media because they lack ideas. They fail because every post starts from a blank screen at 9 pm, after a full day of running the business. You post three times in one week, then nothing for a month. The algorithm forgets you, and so do your customers.

A content plan fixes that. Not a 40-page strategy deck, just a simple system: one or two platforms, a few themes you rotate through, a posting rhythm you can actually keep, and one short session each week where you prepare everything in advance.

This guide walks you through building that system step by step. You'll get content pillars for three different kinds of business, a sustainable weekly cadence, a ready-to-copy 4-week calendar, hook and caption examples, and an honest look at how to use AI without sounding like a robot.

## Start with your customers, not the platform

The first decision is where to show up. The honest answer is: wherever your customers already spend time, and nowhere else for now. Being consistent on one platform beats being invisible on five.

Here's a quick way to choose:

| Platform | Best for | What works there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instagram | Visual businesses: food, beauty, fitness, fashion, interiors, travel | Reels, carousels, Stories, DMs |
| Facebook Page | Local services, older audiences, community groups, events | Photos, short video, event posts, Messenger |
| Google Business Profile | Anyone with a physical location or service area | Updates, offers, events, photos |
| YouTube Shorts | Businesses that can teach or demonstrate | Short vertical how-to and behind-the-scenes clips |
| LinkedIn | B2B services, consultants, agencies, recruiters | Founder posts, case studies, short insights |

Pick **one main platform** and, if you have the energy, **one supporting channel**. For most local businesses, a strong pairing is Instagram or Facebook plus Google Business Profile. Google posts don't build a following, but they show up when people are already searching for you, which makes them very good value. If you haven't set up your profile properly yet, start with our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide).

> If you're unsure, ask your last ten customers where they found you or which app they open most. Their answers beat any trend report.

## Set goals you can actually measure

"Get more followers" is not a useful goal. Followers who never buy don't pay rent. Instead, decide what you want social media to do for the business, then track the signals that point to it.

| Goal | What to watch | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Get discovered by new people | Reach, accounts reached who don't follow you, shares | Shares put your post in front of new people |
| Build trust and interest | Saves, comments, profile visits | Saves show people found it useful enough to keep |
| Generate enquiries | DMs, website link taps, call or direction taps | These are people raising their hand |
| Drive bookings or sales | Bookings or sales you can trace back to social | The number that actually pays the bills |

Instagram has said that what people do with a post (liking, commenting, saving and sharing it) shapes how widely it's shown. Its own [explanation of how ranking works](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained) is worth ten minutes of your time. In plain terms: make posts people want to keep or send to a friend.

Check your numbers once a month, not every hour. Note the three posts that brought the most saves, shares or DMs. Do more of what they had in common.

## Build 3–5 content pillars

Content pillars are the recurring themes you post about. They stop you asking "what should I post today?" because the answer is always "whichever pillar is next".

A good set of pillars covers four jobs:

- **Teach** something useful your customer cares about.
- **Show** your work, results or products.
- **Prove** you're trustworthy with reviews, before-and-afters and real customers.
- **Connect** with the people and story behind the business.

Add a light **Offer** pillar for promotions, but keep it to roughly one post in five. If every post is a sales pitch, people scroll past.

### Example 1: a café or bakery

- **Teach:** how to store sourdough, which coffee suits which milk, the difference between a flat white and a latte.
- **Show:** today's bake coming out of the oven, the new seasonal menu, latte art in slow motion.
- **Prove:** a regular's favourite order, a screenshot of a lovely review, a corporate order you delivered.
- **Connect:** meet the baker, the 5 am routine, where your beans come from.
- **Offer:** weekend combo, festive pre-orders, loyalty card reminder.

### Example 2: a dental or physiotherapy clinic

- **Teach:** three desk stretches, what to do if a filling falls out, how often to replace a toothbrush.
- **Show:** a tour of a treatment room, new equipment explained simply, what happens at a first visit.
- **Prove:** patient stories shared with written permission, team qualifications, Google reviews.
- **Connect:** why the founder started the clinic, staff birthdays, a community health camp.
- **Offer:** free first consultation week, family check-up packages.

Never share patient details or photos without written consent, and avoid promising outcomes.

### Example 3: a B2B agency or consultant

- **Teach:** a short checklist, a common mistake you see in client work, how to brief a supplier.
- **Show:** a project from start to finish, your process in five steps, a tool you use daily.
- **Prove:** a mini case study with the problem, what you did and the result, a client quote.
- **Connect:** lessons from running the business, team culture, an honest failure.
- **Offer:** a free audit, a webinar, a new service package.

> Write your pillars on a sticky note next to your desk. If a post idea doesn't fit any pillar, it probably doesn't need to be posted.

## How often to post: a cadence you can keep

There's no official magic number for how often to post on Instagram or anywhere else. What the platforms reward is steady activity that people engage with. What your business rewards is a rhythm you won't abandon in week three.

Here are three realistic levels. Start at the lowest one you're sure you can sustain for three months.

| Level | Time per week | Main platform | Stories | Google Business Profile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | About 1 hour | 2 posts per week | A few times a week | 1 post per week |
| Steady | About 2 hours | 3 posts per week | Most days | 1 post per week |
| Growth | 3+ hours | 4–5 posts per week | Daily | 1–2 posts per week |

Consistency matters more than volume. Two good posts every week for a year will do more than ten posts in one week followed by silence.

| Week | Monday (Teach) | Wednesday (Show) | Friday (Prove / Connect) | Google post |

Each week, add casual Stories that support the main post: share it, run a quick poll, or show something happening right now.

## Choose the right format for each idea

Different formats do different jobs. Match the format to the idea instead of forcing everything into video.

### Reels and short video

Short vertical video is the main way to reach people who don't follow you yet. In January 2025, Instagram said it would recommend Reels up to three minutes long, and YouTube says videos with a square or vertical ratio up to three minutes count as [Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en). Length limits keep changing, so check in the app before planning anything long.

Longer limits don't mean longer is better. Get to the point fast and stop once it's made. If a second adds nothing, cut it.

Instagram also says it makes some reels less visible, including low-resolution or watermarked reels, reels with borders, reels that are mostly text, and reels that have already been posted. That comes from the same [ranking explainer](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained). So shoot in good light, avoid reposting clips with another app's watermark, and make your own footage.

### Carousels

Carousels are swipeable posts with several images or slides. They suit step-by-step guides, checklists, comparisons and before-and-after sets. They're often saved because people want to come back to them.

Keep one idea per slide, with large text and a first slide that says what the reader will get.

### Single images

A strong single photo still works for products, finished work, team portraits and reviews. Instagram uploads photos at up to 1080 pixels wide, according to its [help centre](https://help.instagram.com/1631821640426723). That's why **1080×1350 pixels** (a 4:5 portrait) is a common choice for feed posts: it takes up more of the screen than a square.

Instagram's profile grid now shows taller, rectangular previews. Keep important text and faces away from the very top and bottom edges so nothing gets cropped awkwardly.

### Stories

Stories disappear after 24 hours, so they suit low-pressure updates: today's special, a poll, a peek behind the counter. Instagram says past interactions with an account shape whose Stories people see first, so regular Stories keep you near the front.

### Originality matters on Meta

Meta has said it is acting against unoriginal content on Facebook: accounts that repeatedly repost other people's material without adding anything can see reduced reach. It treats things like your own commentary, editing or voiceover as meaningful additions. [Search Engine Journal's summary](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/meta-follows-youtube-in-crackdown-on-unoriginal-content/551096/) covers the details. For a small business, the takeaway is simple: post your own photos, your own videos and your own opinions.

## Hooks and captions that get read

The first line of a caption, or the first second of a video, decides whether anyone stays. That opening is your hook.

### Hook examples you can adapt

- "Three things I wish every customer knew before booking a [service]."
- "Don't buy a [product] until you check this."
- "This is what ₹2,000 gets you at our salon." (Use your own currency and real price.)
- "The question we get asked every single day."
- "We almost closed in our first year. Here's what changed."
- "Before and after: 4 hours of work in 15 seconds."

Good hooks are specific. They promise something useful, show a result or spark curiosity, without clickbait.

### A simple caption structure

1. **Hook:** one line that earns the next line.
2. **Value:** two to five short lines with the tip, story or detail.
3. **Proof or personality:** a quick example, a number you actually know, or a bit of your voice.
4. **Call to action:** one clear next step.

Here's an example for a bakery:

*"Your sourdough goes stale in a day? It's probably the fridge. Bread dries out faster in the fridge than on the counter. Keep it cut side down on a board, or wrap it in a cloth. For longer, slice and freeze, then toast from frozen. Want a fresh loaf this weekend? DM us 'LOAF' and we'll keep one aside."*

> Write captions the way you'd talk to a customer at the counter. If you wouldn't say it out loud, rewrite it.

Use a few relevant hashtags and location tags rather than a long block. Hashtag rules and limits change, so keep them simple and focused on what you actually do and where you do it.

## Using AI for social posts, responsibly

AI tools can take a lot of the grind out of social media. They're good at turning rough notes into a first draft, suggesting hooks, writing three caption versions to choose from, resizing images and planning a week of topics from your pillars.

They don't know your customers, your prices or what happened in your shop today. That's still your job.

### A workflow that keeps your voice

1. **Feed it real material.** Give the AI your notes, a voice memo transcript, a customer question or photos from the day.
2. **Ask for options.** Request three hooks or two caption versions instead of one "final" answer.
3. **Edit hard.** Cut generic phrases, add real details, and fix anything that isn't true.
4. **Read it aloud.** If it doesn't sound like you, change it until it does.
5. **Check facts.** Prices, dates, offers and claims must be correct. AI can confidently get these wrong.

Save a short "voice guide" you can paste into any tool: how you greet people, words you use, words you never use, and two or three past captions you're proud of.

### Label realistic AI imagery

Meta adds an "AI info" label when it detects industry-standard signals that content was made with AI, or when people disclose it themselves. Meta also says it requires people to use its disclosure tool when posting organic content with a **photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio** that was digitally created or altered, and that it may apply penalties if they don't. Both points come from Meta's posts on [labelling AI-generated images](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/) and its [approach to labelling AI content](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/).

In practice:

- If you post a realistic AI-generated video or voice, use the platform's AI label when you upload it.
- Never use AI to fake customer photos, reviews, results or before-and-afters. That misleads people, and in many places it may also break advertising or consumer rules.
- Clearly stylised graphics, like illustrated backgrounds or branded templates, carry less risk, but honesty is still the best policy.

## The weekly batching session: 1–2 hours

Batching means making a week's content in one sitting instead of scrambling every day. It's the single habit that keeps small businesses consistent.

::flow One batching session, a whole week of posts | Pick this week's ideas from your pillars | Draft captions (AI helps) | Make or pick the images | Schedule everything | Reply to comments daily

Here's a 90-minute session you can run every Monday morning or Sunday evening:

1. **Review (10 minutes).** Look at last week's posts. Which got saves, shares or DMs? Note one lesson.
2. **Plan (15 minutes).** Pick this week's three topics from your pillars and the calendar above.
3. **Gather (20 minutes).** Pull photos and clips from your phone. Film anything missing, such as a 20-second process clip.
4. **Write (25 minutes).** Draft hooks and captions, with AI help if you like, then edit in your voice.
5. **Design and resize (10 minutes).** Make carousel slides and export the sizes you need.
6. **Schedule (10 minutes).** Schedule the posts, including your Google Business Profile update.

> Film more than you need. Ten extra seconds of footage today can become next week's Story.

### Repurpose one idea five ways

During the week, note every customer question in your phone. One good question can fill a week. Take "How long does a hair colour last?":

- **Reel:** you answering it in 30 seconds while working.
- **Carousel:** five tips to make colour last longer.
- **Story:** a poll asking "How often do you colour your hair?"
- **Google Business Profile update:** a short tip with a booking button.
- **Email or WhatsApp broadcast:** the same tips, sent to existing clients.

Repurposing is not reposting. You're adapting your own idea for each place, which keeps you on the right side of Meta's originality guidance.

## Turn DMs and comments into leads

Social media only pays off when conversations turn into customers. Treat every DM and question in the comments as a possible enquiry.

- **Reply quickly.** Set aside two short slots a day to answer messages. A fast, friendly reply often wins the sale.
- **Move to a clear next step.** Share a booking link, a price list or a time slot. Don't leave people guessing.
- **Use saved replies** for common questions like prices, hours and location, then personalise the first line.
- **Capture the contact.** With permission, move warm leads to WhatsApp or email so you don't lose them if they stop checking DMs.

If WhatsApp is where your customers prefer to talk, our [WhatsApp Business API guide](/blog/whatsapp-business-api-guide-small-business) explains how to set it up properly. For email, a short [welcome sequence](/blog/email-marketing-welcome-sequence) keeps new leads warm without extra effort.

Log where each enquiry came from. After a month, you'll know which posts bring paying customers.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Trying to be everywhere.** Five half-empty profiles look worse than one active one.
- **Selling in every post.** Teach, show and prove first. Sell occasionally.
- **Posting and vanishing.** Reply to comments and DMs, especially in the first hour or so after posting.
- **Publishing unedited AI text.** Generic captions make you sound like everyone else.
- **Ignoring Google Business Profile.** It's often where ready-to-buy customers find you.
- **Chasing vanity numbers.** Followers and likes matter less than saves, shares, DMs and bookings.
- **Giving up after three weeks.** Results usually come from months of steady posting, not one viral hit.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a small business post on Instagram?

There's no official number. Two to three good feed posts a week, plus Stories most days, is a sustainable starting point for most small businesses. Consistency over months matters more than daily posting for a few weeks.

### What should I post on Instagram for my business?

Rotate through content pillars: teach something useful, show your work or products, prove trust with reviews and results, and connect with the people behind the business. Add an offer post occasionally. Every customer question is a potential post.

### What is the best image size for Instagram feed posts?

Instagram uploads photos at up to 1080 pixels wide. A 1080×1350 pixel portrait (4:5) is a popular choice because it fills more of the screen. Keep key text away from the top and bottom edges, as the profile grid crops previews.

### Is it okay to use AI to write social media posts?

Yes, as long as you treat AI output as a first draft. Give it real details, edit the result in your own voice, and check every fact, price and claim. If you post realistic AI-generated video or audio on Meta's apps, use the AI disclosure label.

### Should I post the same content on Facebook and Instagram?

You can cross-post, since Meta lets you link a Facebook Page and Instagram account. Just check that the caption, hashtags and format suit each platform. A caption packed with Instagram hashtags can look out of place on Facebook.

### Do Google Business Profile posts really help?

They help customers who are already searching for you see what's new, such as offers, events and updates. Google [lets you add an action button](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en), such as one for booking, which can turn a search into an enquiry. One post a week is a realistic habit.

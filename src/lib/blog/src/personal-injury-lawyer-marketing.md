Personal injury is the most expensive corner of legal marketing. The same handful of firms buy billboards, TV spots, Search ads and Local Services Ads in every big metro, and the keywords they fight over ("car accident lawyer," "truck accident attorney") are among the costliest in all of Google Ads. A small PI firm can't outspend them.

You can out-work them, though. Personal injury lawyer marketing comes down to a few things done better than the firm down the street: answering the phone at 11 p.m., having a real page for every case type you take, showing up in the language your clients speak, and knowing your cost per signed case by channel. This guide covers each of those, plus the solicitation rules that trip up PI firms after a crash makes the news.

## Why personal injury marketing is different

Most legal practice areas compete on reputation and referrals. PI competes on speed, visibility and trust in the hours after an accident. A few features shape everything:

- **Urgency.** People search from the hospital, the tow yard or their couch with an insurance adjuster's voicemail waiting. They call the first firm that feels credible and answers.
- **Contingency fees.** You carry the cost of acquiring a case until it settles, sometimes for years. Cash flow, not just cost per lead, decides how much you can spend.
- **Uneven case values.** One commercial trucking case can be worth more than a year of soft-tissue cases. Your marketing should aim at the case types you most want.
- **Heavy regulation.** Bar rules on solicitation, testimonials, past results and paid referrals hit PI harder than almost any other area.

::stats Why PI marketing is a speed game | 24/7 | when injured people search | 45 days | federal no-contact window after air crashes | 7 | case types that deserve their own page (see table below)

The 45-day figure is real: federal law bars attorneys from sending unsolicited communications about a potential personal injury or wrongful death action to victims or relatives of an air carrier accident before the 45th day after the crash ([49 U.S.C. § 1136](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/1136)). ## Build a page for every case type

The biggest SEO mistake PI firms make is one "Personal Injury" page that tries to rank for everything. Google and AI assistants both reward specific pages that answer specific questions.

### The case-type architecture

Create one strong page per case type you actually want, and link them from a personal injury hub page:

| Case type page | What searchers want to know | Must include |
|---|---|---|
| Car accident | What to do after a crash, how fault works in your state, dealing with insurers | Your state's fault and deadline rules, steps after a crash, free consult CTA |
| Truck accident | Why trucking cases differ, who's liable, evidence preservation | Federal trucking rules overview, urgency on evidence, your trucking experience |
| Motorcycle accident | Bias against riders, helmet laws, injury types | State helmet law, how you handle bias |
| Slip and fall / premises | Whether the property owner is liable, what to photograph | State premises liability basics, documentation checklist |
| Dog bite | Owner liability, strict liability vs one-bite rule | Your state's rule, reporting steps |
| Wrongful death | Who can file, timelines, how damages work | Compassionate tone, eligible family members under state law |
| Rideshare (Uber/Lyft) | Whose insurance pays | Coverage tiers explained in plain English |

Each page should answer the questions in plain English, name your state's statute of limitations accurately (and link the statute), explain the first steps the reader should take today, and show who will actually handle the case. Then add location pages only where you have a real office or a real reason to serve that area. Our [law firm SEO guide](/blog/law-firm-seo-guide) explains how to structure practice-area and location pages without creating thin, duplicate content.

### What makes a PI page trustworthy

- **Attorney bios** with bar admissions, trial experience and real photos.
- **Past results**, only if your state allows them, with the disclaimers it requires ("past results do not guarantee a similar outcome" or your state's equivalent).
- **Plain fee explanation**: how contingency works, what "no fee unless we win" means, and who pays costs if you lose. Several states have rules about how "no fee" claims must be worded, so check yours.
- **A visible phone number and a short form** that works on mobile. See our [contact form best practices](/blog/website-contact-form-best-practices) for what to ask and what to skip.

## Local Services Ads and Search ads for PI

For many PI firms, Google Local Services Ads are the highest-intent channel available. You pay per lead, not per click, and the "Google Verified" badge helps prospects trust you. But PI is also one of the most expensive LSA categories, and Google's [ad ranking page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305) says ranking weighs your responsiveness and profile quality (including ratings and review count) alongside your bid. Our dedicated guide to [Google Local Services Ads for lawyers](/blog/google-local-services-ads-for-lawyers) covers screening, lead disputes and budgeting.

Search ads give you control LSA doesn't: you choose the keywords and send traffic to a specific case-type page. For a small PI firm, broad terms like "personal injury lawyer" in a major metro are usually a budget trap. Better options:

- **Long-tail, case-specific terms**: "rear-ended by uninsured driver lawyer," "18-wheeler accident attorney [county]."
- **Spanish-language keywords** (more below), which often have less competition.
- **Smaller geographies** inside your metro where you have an office.
- **Negative keywords** for things you don't handle ("workers comp" if you don't take it, "free legal aid," "lawyer jobs").

::flow Where a PI lead comes from and where it should go | Search, LSA or referral | Case-type page or LSA profile | Live answer within minutes | Signed retainer same day

## Intake that runs 24/7

Every PI marketing dollar flows into intake. If the phone goes to voicemail at 9 p.m. on a Saturday, it doesn't matter how good your ads are. Injured people call the next firm.

### Minimum standard for PI intake

1. **Live answer around the clock**, with your own staff, a legal answering service trained on your case types, or both.
2. **A short qualifying script**: what happened, when, where, injuries, treatment so far, whether police were called, insurance details, and whether they've spoken to another lawyer.
3. **Same-day retainer option**, including e-signature, so a qualified prospect doesn't "think about it" and sign elsewhere.
4. **Instant replies to web forms**: an automatic email confirming you received the inquiry and telling them when you'll call (and then calling within minutes).
5. **A follow-up cadence** for leads who don't answer your callback.

::chat Example after-hours intake call (illustrative) | them: I was rear-ended on the highway tonight. My neck is killing me. | you: I'm sorry, that sounds scary. Are you safe right now, and have you been seen by a doctor? | them: I'm home. I'm going to urgent care tomorrow. | you: Good. Can I ask a few quick questions so an attorney can call you first thing in the morning? | them: Yes, please. | you: Thank you. Please don't give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer until you've spoken with us.

The script avoids promises about outcomes, collects facts, and gives one practical next step. Have your intake team practice it. Our [law firm intake and speed-to-lead guide](/blog/law-firm-intake-speed-to-lead) has more scripts and the metrics to track.

## Solicitation rules after an accident

This is where PI firms get disciplined. ABA Model Rule 7.3 bars live person-to-person solicitation (in person, live phone, real-time video) of someone who hasn't asked for your help, when a significant motive is the lawyer's financial gain, with limited exceptions. Every state adopts its own version, and many go further.

- **Waiting periods for written contact.** Several states, including Florida and Texas, ban targeted written solicitation of accident victims for roughly a month after the incident. Rules differ on what counts (letters, emails, texts, social DMs) and what labels or disclaimers are required when contact is allowed.
- **Air crashes.** Federal law sets a 45-day no-contact period for victims and relatives of air carrier accidents.
- **Runners and cappers.** Paying anyone to steer accident victims to you (tow operators, chiropractors, hospital staff, "case runners") is prohibited under bar rules, and in some states, a crime. ABA Model Rule 7.2(b) bars giving anything of value for a recommendation, with narrow exceptions such as paying for ads or a qualified lawyer referral service.
- **Texting.** Even where text outreach is allowed under bar rules, the TCPA requires prior express written consent for marketing texts. See our [SMS marketing and TCPA guide](/blog/sms-marketing-tcpa-10dlc-guide).

> Warning: "We saw your accident on the news, call us" is exactly the kind of message that gets lawyers disciplined. Advertise broadly and let people come to you. Check your state's current solicitation and advertising rules before any targeted outreach, and treat this section as an overview, not legal advice.

Advertising rules also apply to everything else you publish. Testimonials, "past results," "expert" claims and TV spots each have state-specific requirements, and Texas and Florida require filing many ads with the bar (Texas within 10 days of first use under Rule 7.04, Florida at least 20 days before first use). Our [lawyer advertising rules by state](/blog/lawyer-advertising-rules-by-state) guide breaks these down. You can also read the official [ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/), then your own state bar's version.

## Spanish-language marketing

More than 40 million people in the US speak Spanish at home, according to the Census Bureau's [language use data](https://www.census.gov/topics/population/language-use.html). In many PI markets, especially in Texas, Florida, California, Arizona, Nevada and the Chicago area, a large share of injured people would prefer to talk to a firm in Spanish. Many firms advertise "Se habla español" and then send the caller to voicemail or an English-only intake rep.

### Doing it properly

- **Bilingual intake, live.** If you advertise in Spanish, someone must answer in Spanish at every hour you advertise.
- **Real Spanish pages**, written or reviewed by a fluent speaker, not machine translation pasted onto your site. Translate the case-type pages that matter most first: car accident, work injury (if you handle it), truck accident.
- **Spanish Google Business Profile content**: posts and Q&A in Spanish, and reviews from Spanish-speaking clients in their own words.
- **Separate ad campaigns** in Spanish with Spanish keywords ("abogado de accidentes de carro," "abogado de lesiones personales").
- **Bar rules still apply.** Your state's advertising rules cover Spanish ads too, including any required disclaimers, which you'll need in Spanish.

## Referral networks

Referrals still bring some of the best PI cases. Two kinds matter:

1. **Lawyer-to-lawyer referrals.** Family law, criminal defense, estate and business attorneys regularly get calls about injuries. Make it easy for them to send cases to you. Many states allow referral fees between lawyers only if the client agrees in writing, the total fee is reasonable and each lawyer takes responsibility (ABA Model Rule 1.5(e)), and some states add conditions. Check your state's version.
2. **Past clients.** Former clients refer friends and family. A short, respectful email newsletter a few times a year keeps you in mind. Our [email welcome sequence guide](/blog/email-marketing-welcome-sequence) shows how to set one up.

What you can't do is pay non-lawyers for referrals or set up exclusive reciprocal arrangements that your state forbids. ABA Model Rule 7.2(b)(4) allows reciprocal referral agreements with other lawyers or professionals only if they're not exclusive and the client is told about them.

## Reviews and reputation

PI prospects compare star ratings and recent reviews before they call, and reviews feed your Maps and LSA ranking. Ask every client at the right moment (usually at settlement, when they're relieved, not mid-case when they're frustrated with the timeline). Replies must protect confidentiality: never confirm details of a case or that someone was a client in a way your state's rules forbid. ## Measuring cost per signed case

Cost per lead tells you almost nothing in PI. What you need is cost per signed case by channel, and ideally fee revenue by channel once cases resolve.

::funnel Example: one month of paid leads for a 2-attorney PI firm (illustrative) | Leads from all channels: 120 | Answered live: 90 | Qualified: 40 | Consults: 25 | Signed: 10

If that firm spent an illustrative $12,000 across LSA and Search ads, cost per signed case is $1,200. The useful question is which channel produced the signed cases. A channel with expensive leads but a high sign rate can beat a cheap one full of non-cases.

### Set up tracking in one afternoon

- **Tag every lead's source** in your intake or case management tool (LSA, Google Ads, organic, referral, TV, past client).
- **Use call tracking numbers** per channel. CallRail is a common choice for this.
- **Record outcomes**: qualified, signed, declined, referred out.
- **Review monthly**: spend, leads, signed cases and cost per signed case per channel.
- **Review yearly**: fees collected per channel, since PI cases can take a long time to resolve.

| Channel | What to measure | Typical strength |
|---|---|---|
| Local Services Ads | Cost per signed case, answer rate | High intent, pay per lead |
| Google Search ads | Cost per signed case by keyword group | Control over case type and landing page |
| Organic SEO and Maps | Signed cases from organic and GBP | Compounds over time, lower marginal cost |
| Referrals | Cases and fees by referrer | Often the highest case values |
| Past clients | Repeat and referred cases | Cheapest, built on service |

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should a personal injury firm spend on marketing?

There's no reliable universal figure. Work backwards from the number of signed cases you want, your sign rate and your cost per lead by channel, then check that your cash flow can carry costs until cases settle. Review cost per signed case monthly.

### Can personal injury lawyers contact accident victims directly?

Live, in-person or real-time solicitation of people who haven't contacted you is generally prohibited under ABA Model Rule 7.3 and state versions. Many states also restrict written contact for a period after an accident, and federal law bars contact with air crash victims for 45 days. Check your state's current rules.

### Are Local Services Ads worth it for personal injury lawyers?

They can be, because leads are high intent and you pay per lead. They're also among the most expensive LSA categories, so they only pay off if you answer calls live and track cost per signed case.

### Do I need Spanish-language marketing for a PI firm?

If your market has a large Spanish-speaking population, it's often one of the best opportunities available. Only advertise in Spanish if you can answer calls and handle the case in Spanish at every hour your ads run.

### Can I pay for personal injury referrals?

Paying non-lawyers for recommendations is generally prohibited. Fee-sharing with other lawyers is allowed in many states under conditions like written client consent and a reasonable total fee. Check your state's version of Rules 1.5(e) and 7.2.

Dental patient recall and reactivation is the cheapest growth most practices aren't doing well. Somewhere in your practice management system are hundreds of patients who liked you well enough to come in, had a cleaning, and then simply drifted. Nobody was upset. Life got busy, the reminder card went in a drawer, and now they're 18 months overdue.

## Recall vs reactivation: two different jobs

People use these words interchangeably, but they're different problems.

**Recall (or recare)** keeps active patients on schedule. The goal is that every hygiene patient leaves with their next appointment booked, and gets reminders before it.

**Reactivation** brings back patients who have fallen off. They have no future appointment and are past due, sometimes by years.

A practice with strong recall needs less reactivation. So the first fix is always at the front desk: pre-book the next hygiene visit before the patient leaves.

## Step 1: Fix recall at the chair and the front desk

Before sending a single reactivation message, stop the leak.

- **Pre-book every hygiene patient.** The hygienist or assistant should introduce the next visit in the chair ("Dr. Lee wants to see you again in six months, so let's get that on the calendar"), and the front desk confirms it at checkout.
- **Use the interval your dentists set.** Not every patient needs six months. Your dentists set each patient's recall interval based on their risk, so make sure the PMS reflects it, whether that's three, four, six or twelve months.
- **Send reminders before the visit** from your PMS or patient communication platform, by the patient's preferred channel.
- **Fill cancellations from a short list.** Keep an ASAP list of patients who want an earlier slot.

Patients who decline to pre-book ("I'll call when I know my schedule") go on a list for a reminder a few weeks before they're due. These are the people most likely to drift.

## Step 2: Segment overdue patients by last visit

Run a report in your PMS of patients with **no future appointment** and a last hygiene visit beyond their recall interval. Then split them by how long it's been. The tone and the channel should change as the gap grows.

| Segment | How overdue | What they need | Best channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recently due | 0–3 months past due | A friendly reminder and a booking link | Text or email from your PMS |
| Overdue | 3–12 months past due | A warmer nudge, an easy path back | Email, then a phone call |
| Lapsed | 12–24 months past due | Reassurance, no guilt, a reason to come now | Phone call plus email |
| Long-lapsed | 24–36 months | A "we'd love to see you" check-in, update details | Email or mailed letter |
| Inactive | 36+ months | A final check-in, then mark inactive | One email or letter |

Before you send anything, clean the list. Remove patients who have moved away, transferred their records, asked not to be contacted, or are deceased. Update phone numbers and emails where you can. Nothing ends a reactivation campaign faster than a message to a family who lost a parent last year.

::bars Example: overdue patients by segment at a 2-dentist practice (illustrative) | 0–3 months: 140 | 3–12 months: 310 * | 12–24 months: 260 | 24–36 months: 180 | 36+ months: 220

The numbers above are invented to show what your report might look like. Most practices find the 3–12 month group is the biggest opportunity, because those patients still think of you as their dentist.

## Step 3: Send it from the right system

This is where HIPAA comes in. A list of your patients, with dates of their last visits, is protected health information.

Under HIPAA, appointment reminders and recall notices are treatment communications, and HHS's [guidance on marketing](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/marketing/index.html) explains that these generally don't require the written authorization that marketing does. Messages about your own practice's services typically aren't treated as marketing either, as long as no third party is paying you to send them. But "allowed" doesn't mean "send it from anywhere". The data still needs to be handled by systems that are covered by a [business associate agreement](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html) (BAA).

In practice that means sending recall and reactivation from:

- Your **practice management system's** built-in reminders and messaging.
- A **HIPAA-compliant patient communication platform** such as Weave or NexHealth, which integrate with common dental PMS software and sign BAAs.
- Your PMS's mail-merge for **printed letters**, for long-lapsed patients.

### HIPAA-safe wording

Even inside a compliant system, write messages that would be harmless if someone else saw them. Phone lock screens, shared family email accounts and kitchen-counter mail are all real.

- Keep **subject lines and previews generic**: "A reminder from Bright Smile Dental", not "Your periodontal maintenance is overdue."
- Don't name **procedures, conditions or unfinished treatment** in the message body of a reactivation email. Save treatment discussions for the phone or the visit.
- Use the patient's first name and your practice name, and give a clear way to book or call.
- Include an easy way to opt out of non-essential messages, and honor it. The FTC's [CAN-SPAM compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) explains when email rules apply to messages that promote your services.

## Step 4: Templates you can use today

Adapt these in your patient communication platform. Replace bracketed text with your details. If you'd like help adjusting the tone, Growvia's AI content expert can draft variations of a template, as long as you give it the generic wording only and never patient names or details.

### Recently due (text from your PMS)

"Hi [First name], it's [Practice]. You're due for your next visit with us. Book a time that suits you here: [booking link] or call [phone]. Reply STOP to opt out."

### Overdue, email 1 (send at the start of the month)

Subject: A reminder from [Practice]

"Hi [First name],

It's been a while since we've seen you, and we wanted to check in. Regular visits help us catch small things before they become big ones, and our hygiene team would love to see you.

You can book online in under a minute at [booking link], or call us at [phone]. We have early-morning and [Saturday] appointments if weekdays are tough.

See you soon,
[Name] and the team at [Practice]"

### Overdue, email 2 (about 10 days later, if no booking)

Subject: We saved a few times for you

"Hi [First name],

Just a quick follow-up. We've kept a few hygiene appointments open over the next two weeks for patients who are due. If one works, grab it here: [booking link].

If anything has changed, like a new address or insurance, reply to this email or give us a call and we'll update your file."

### Lapsed, email 3 (about three weeks after email 2)

Subject: Still here when you're ready

"Hi [First name],

We know life gets busy. There's no catch-up lecture waiting for you, just a friendly team and a clean slate. When you're ready, book at [booking link] or call [phone].

If you've moved or found a new dentist, we'd be grateful if you let us know so we can update our records."

That final line matters. It gives people a polite way to tell you they've left, which cleans your list and your metrics.

## Step 5: The phone call still wins

For patients 12 months or more overdue, a real phone call from a familiar voice often succeeds where emails don't. Schedule a daily 30-minute "reactivation block" at a quiet time for one team member.

::chat A reactivation phone script | you: Hi, is this Maria? It's Jess from Bright Smile Dental. Do you have a minute? | them: Oh, hi. Yes, sorry, I know I'm overdue. | you: No apology needed at all, it happens to everyone. We just wanted to check in and see if we can get you back on the schedule. | them: Probably, my schedule's just crazy. | you: We have a 7:30 a.m. slot on Thursday or a Saturday morning on the 14th. Would either work?

Keys to a good call:

- Lead with warmth, not guilt.
- Offer two specific times instead of "When would you like to come in?"
- If they've moved or switched dentists, thank them and update the record.
- If you reach voicemail, leave a short, generic message: name, practice, callback number. No details about why.

Texting is useful in between, but it comes with rules. Use your PMS or patient platform's texting from a registered number, respect consent and quiet hours, and read our [SMS marketing, TCPA and 10DLC guide](/blog/sms-marketing-tcpa-10dlc-guide) if you're unsure. ## Step 7: Measure recall and reactivation

Track these monthly from your PMS. Define each one once and keep the definitions stable so the trend means something.

| Metric | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-appointment rate | Hygiene patients who left with a next visit booked ÷ hygiene patients seen | Whether the front desk is stopping the leak |
| Recall rate | Active patients with a future hygiene appointment ÷ active patients | The overall health of your hygiene base |
| Reactivation rate | Overdue patients who booked ÷ overdue patients contacted | Whether your campaigns work |
| Show rate | Reactivated patients who attended ÷ reactivated patients booked | Whether reminders and timing work |

::funnel Example: one month of reactivation (illustrative numbers) | Overdue patients contacted: 450 | Opened or answered: 230 | Clicked or asked to book: 75 | Booked: 48 | Attended: 41

These numbers are illustrative. Your first campaign gives you a baseline, and each month after that you're trying to improve one step of the funnel. If many open but few book, the booking step is the problem. If few open, look at subject lines, sender name and deliverability.

## Common recall and reactivation mistakes

- **Sending the same message to everyone.** A patient one month late and one three years late need different words.
- **Guilt-tripping.** "You haven't been in for 2 years!" makes people avoid you longer.
- **Naming procedures in subject lines.** It's a privacy risk and it often reads as a sales pitch.
- **Running one big campaign and stopping.** Reactivation works as a monthly routine, because new patients become overdue every month.
- **No capacity.** Bookings three months out lose most of the people you just won back.
- **Never cleaning the list.** Inflated lists make your reactivation rate look worse and annoy people who've moved on.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good dental recall system?

A good recall system pre-books every hygiene patient before they leave, sends reminders from your practice management system, and runs a monthly reactivation routine for anyone without a future appointment. Track pre-appointment rate, recall rate and reactivation rate monthly.

### Do I need patient permission under HIPAA to send recall reminders?

Generally no. HHS treats appointment reminders and recall notices as treatment communications that don't need marketing authorization, but they must be sent through systems covered by a business associate agreement. Check with your compliance advisor for anything beyond reminders.

### How often should I contact overdue dental patients?

A common pattern is three or four touches over about a month, mixing email, text where the patient has consented, and a phone call, then a pause. Repeat for each segment every few months, and stop if a patient asks you to.

### What should a dental reactivation email say?

Keep it warm, short and generic: a check-in from the practice, a booking link and phone number, and no procedure names or treatment details. Give people an easy way to say they've moved or switched dentists.

### When should I mark a dental patient inactive?

Many practices treat patients as inactive after about 36 months without a visit, after a final check-in. Pick a definition that suits your practice and use it consistently so your metrics stay comparable.

### Can I use a regular email marketing tool for recall?

Only if the vendor signs a business associate agreement and is set up for PHI. Most general marketing tools, including Growvia, are not, so use your practice management system or a HIPAA-compliant platform such as Weave or NexHealth.

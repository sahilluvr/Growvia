Most small business owners don't fail at marketing because they lack ideas. They fail because marketing is always the thing that gets pushed to tomorrow — after the customers, the staff, the suppliers and the accounts. Then a quiet month arrives and it's suddenly urgent.

This plan fixes that with **30 days of small, specific tasks** — most take 30 to 60 minutes. It's built for businesses with little or no marketing budget: local shops, clinics, restaurants, service providers, consultants, agencies and online sellers. By the end you'll have solid foundations, a steady stream of enquiries, and a simple routine you can repeat every month.

## Before you start: three decisions

### 1. Pick one goal for the month

Write down one number you want to move. Examples: "30 new enquiries", "15 new Google reviews", "10 booked consultations", "₹2 lakh in new sales". One goal keeps every task focused.

### 2. Know who you're talking to

Describe your best customer in two or three sentences: who they are, what problem sends them looking for you, what they worry about, and where they look for answers (Google, Instagram, WhatsApp groups, referrals, AI assistants). Everything you write this month is for that person.

### 3. Block the time

Put 45 minutes in your calendar every working day for the next 30 days. Treat it like a client appointment.

::steps Your 30 days at a glance | Week 1 — foundations: easy to find, easy to trust | Week 2 — get found in search, maps and AI answers | Week 3 — follow up with email and WhatsApp | Week 4 — show up on social and with partners | Days 29–30 — measure and repeat

## Week 1 — Foundations: be easy to find and easy to trust

**Day 1 — Write your one-line pitch.** "We help [who] get [result] without [pain]." Example: "We help busy families in Mohali get their AC fixed the same day without surprise charges." You'll reuse this everywhere.

**Day 2 — Fix your Google Business Profile.** Correct category, hours, phone, website, services with prices, a clear description and at least 10 real photos. For local businesses this is often the highest-return hour of the whole month.

**Day 3 — Audit your website.** Check it on your phone. Does the first screen say what you do, where, and how to contact you? Is there a WhatsApp or call button? Does it load quickly? List every problem. (A free audit tool will find the technical ones for you.)

**Day 4 — Fix the top three website problems.** Usually: a clearer headline, contact buttons visible on mobile, and one page per main service.

**Day 5 — Add proof.** Collect your best testimonials (with permission), before/after photos, client logos, certifications and years in business. Put them on your homepage and service pages.

**Day 6 — Make contacting you effortless.** Add a simple enquiry form, a WhatsApp chat link and a booking link if you take appointments. Test each one yourself.

**Day 7 — Set up tracking.** Connect Google Search Console and check your Google Business Profile insights. Start a simple sheet (or CRM) where every enquiry is logged with where it came from.

## Week 2 — Get found: search, maps and AI answers

**Day 8 — List your keywords.** Write 20 things customers type into Google or ask ChatGPT, e.g. "dentist near me open Sunday", "cost of teeth whitening Chandigarh". Group them by service.

**Day 9 — Improve your top service page.** Use its main keyword in the page title and heading, explain the service, process, price range and who it's for, and add 4–6 FAQs.

**Day 10 — Improve your second service page** the same way.

**Day 11 — Clean up your listings.** Make sure your name, address and phone are identical on Google, Apple Maps, Bing, Facebook, Instagram and the directories that matter in your industry.

**Day 12 — Ask for reviews.** Message your 20 most recent happy customers with a direct link to your Google review form. Keep it short and personal.

**Day 13 — Check your AI visibility.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity five of your customer questions. Are you recommended? Who is, and which websites do they cite? Note the gaps.

**Day 14 — Write one genuinely useful article.** Answer the question your customers ask most, with real specifics: prices, timelines, local details, mistakes to avoid. Publish it on your website.

## Week 3 — Follow up: email and WhatsApp

Most sales don't happen on the first contact. Follow-up is where small businesses quietly lose — or win — the most money.

**Day 15 — Gather your contacts.** Export past customers and enquiries from your phone, invoices, forms and spreadsheets into one list. Only include people who have dealt with you or asked to hear from you.

**Day 16 — Set up email properly.** Connect your business mailbox and make sure SPF, DKIM and DMARC are set on your domain so your emails land in the inbox, not spam.

**Day 17 — Write a new-enquiry follow-up sequence.** Three short emails: a thank-you with the answer they asked for (day 0), a helpful follow-up with a testimonial (day 3), and a friendly last check-in (day 7). Make sure it stops automatically when they reply.

**Day 18 — Win back past customers.** Send a personal note to customers you haven't heard from in six months or more — a useful tip, a reminder that it's service time, or a genuine loyalty offer.

**Day 19 — Set up WhatsApp.** Add your WhatsApp button everywhere, write a greeting and away message, and prepare templates for appointment reminders and order updates.

**Day 20 — Reply faster.** Put all your messages — email, WhatsApp, Instagram DMs — in one place and set a target: reply within one hour during business hours. Fast replies win jobs.

**Day 21 — Review week three.** How many enquiries did follow-ups produce? Which message got the most replies? Keep what works.

## Week 4 — Show up: social, content and partnerships

**Day 22 — Plan two weeks of posts.** Mix: one tip, one behind-the-scenes, one customer story, one offer, one question per week. Three posts a week, done consistently, beats one burst of daily posting.

**Day 23 — Create and schedule the posts.** Batch them in one sitting and schedule them so they go out without you.

**Day 24 — Turn your article into content.** Your Day 14 article can become three social posts, one email and a short video script.

**Day 25 — Make one short video.** 30 seconds: a common customer question and your answer, or a quick before/after. Vertical format for Reels, Shorts and Stories.

**Day 26 — Find two partners.** Businesses that serve the same customers but don't compete (a wedding photographer and a decorator; a physiotherapist and a gym). Offer to recommend each other.

**Day 27 — Get one local mention.** Offer a useful tip to a local blog, association newsletter or community page, or sponsor a small local event that lists sponsors online.

**Day 28 — Make an offer that's easy to say yes to.** A free consultation, a first-visit discount, a starter package. Put it on your website, Google profile and WhatsApp greeting.

## Days 29–30 — Measure and repeat

**Day 29 — Review the numbers.** Count enquiries by source (Google, Maps, website form, WhatsApp, Instagram, email, referrals), reviews gained, and — most importantly — how many became paying customers.

**Day 30 — Plan next month.** Double down on the two channels that produced customers, fix the weakest part of your funnel, and set next month's single goal. Your routine now becomes:

| Every week | Every month |
|---|---|
| Reply to every message within an hour | One new useful article or guide |
| Ask 5 customers for reviews | Improve one service page |
| 3 scheduled social posts | Check keyword rankings and AI visibility |
| Follow up every new enquiry automatically | Re-engage one group of past customers |
| Log where every enquiry came from | Review results and set one goal |

## What to measure (keep it simple)

| Metric | Why it matters | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| New enquiries per week | The clearest sign marketing is working | Your CRM or enquiry sheet |
| Enquiry → customer rate | Shows whether follow-up and offers work | Your CRM |
| Google reviews (count and average) | Drives local rankings and trust | Google Business Profile |
| Search clicks and top queries | Shows what people find you for | Google Search Console |
| Reply time | Faster replies win more jobs | Your inbox |
| AI mentions | Whether assistants recommend you | Manual checks or an AI visibility tracker |

## Common mistakes

- **Trying every channel at once.** Do a few things well; add more later.
- **Posting without a call to action.** Every post, email and page should tell people what to do next.
- **No follow-up.** The fortune is in the follow-up — most enquiries need more than one touch.
- **Measuring likes instead of customers.** Followers don't pay bills; enquiries and sales do.
- **Stopping after one month.** Marketing compounds. Month three is usually where it starts to feel easier.

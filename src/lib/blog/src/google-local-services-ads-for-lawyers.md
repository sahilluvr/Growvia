Search "car accident lawyer near me" or "divorce attorney" in most US cities and the first thing you see is not an ordinary ad. It is a row of attorney cards with a photo, star rating, years in business and a green "Google Verified" badge. Those are Google Local Services Ads (LSA), and for many practice areas they now sit above both the regular Search ads and the Maps results.

Google Local Services Ads for lawyers work differently from pay-per-click. You pay per lead, not per click, and Google decides who shows up partly on how fast you answer the phone. That makes LSA one of the best intake channels a small firm can run, and one of the easiest to waste money on if calls go to voicemail.

This guide covers who is eligible, how Google's screening works, what actually moves your ranking, how to dispute bad leads, how to set a budget, and how to measure the only number that matters: cost per signed case. If you want to see where LSA fits next to SEO, reviews and email, start with our pillar on [AI marketing for lawyers](/blog/ai-marketing-for-lawyers).

## How Google Local Services Ads work for law firms

LSA is a separate product from Google Ads, with its own dashboard (and an app). You build a profile, pass Google's checks, choose the practice areas and locations you serve, and set a weekly budget. When someone searches a matching query, Google may show your card. The searcher can call you, send a message or, in some markets, book an appointment directly from the ad.

::flow How an LSA lead reaches your firm | Prospect searches "DUI lawyer near me" | Google shows verified attorney cards | Prospect calls or messages from the ad | Lead lands in your LSA inbox and is charged

You pay only for leads Google counts as valid (calls, messages or bookings for your selected practice areas), not for profile views. You don't bid on keywords, your profile acts as the landing page, and screening is mandatory.

Google lists LSA categories for many legal practice areas in the US, including personal injury, family, criminal defense, DUI, estate planning, immigration, bankruptcy, business, real estate, employment and more. The exact list and city availability change, so check the [Local Services Ads help center](https://support.google.com/localservices) for your market.

| Feature | Local Services Ads | Google Search ads |
|---|---|---|
| What you pay for | Valid leads (calls, messages, bookings) | Clicks |
| Targeting | Practice areas and service areas | Keywords, locations, audiences |
| Placement | Top of results, in many legal searches | Top and bottom of results |
| Screening | Required (Google Verified) | Standard ad policies only |
| Landing page | Your LSA profile | Your website |
| Biggest lever | Reviews and responsiveness | Keywords, bids, landing page |
| Biggest risk | Missed calls and unanswered messages | Wasted clicks on broad keywords |

## Eligibility and Google's screening for lawyers

Google calls verified providers "Google Verified" (lawyers used to see "Google Screened"). The process is stricter for legal categories than for most others, because people who hire a lawyer are often in a vulnerable spot.

### What Google checks

Requirements vary by practice area, state and whether you're a solo or a firm, so treat this as an overview and confirm the current list in your dashboard. Typical checks for law firms include:

- **Bar license verification** for each attorney who will appear on the profile, checked against the state bar.
- **Background checks** on the firm and on individual attorneys, run through Google's third-party partner.
- **Business registration and address** details that match your public records.
- **Malpractice or other insurance** where Google requires it for the category or region.
- **Reviews.** Google may require a minimum number of reviews before the ad can go live. Your Google Business Profile reviews usually feed the LSA profile once the two are linked.

::steps Typical LSA onboarding timeline for a small firm | Week 1 — create the LSA account, pick practice areas and service area | Week 1 — add attorneys, license numbers and firm details | Weeks 1–3 — background checks and license verification run | Weeks 2–4 — link your Google Business Profile so reviews show | After approval — set budget, hours and turn on ads

Timelines are illustrative, and checks can take longer for larger firms or when names and addresses don't match records exactly. Make sure the firm name on your application, the state bar listing and your Google Business Profile agree before you apply. Our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide) walks through getting the profile clean.

### Your bar rules still apply

Passing Google's screening does not mean your ad complies with your state's rules. An LSA profile is an advertisement. [ABA Model Rule](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/) 7.1 bans false or misleading communications about a lawyer's services, and Rule 7.2 covers paying for advertising and recommendations. Your state's version of these rules governs, and some states add filing or labelling requirements.

- In **Texas**, Disciplinary Rule 7.04 requires filing most ads with the State Bar's Advertising Review Committee within 10 days of first dissemination, with a fee, unless an exemption in Rule 7.05 applies.
- In **Florida**, most ads must be filed with The Florida Bar at least 20 days before first use. Lawyer websites are exempt.
- In **New York**, the rules were rewritten effective June 1, 2026; the old "Attorney Advertising" label is no longer required, but the false-or-misleading standard still applies.

Whether a given LSA profile needs filing or labelling depends on how your state classifies it. Ask your bar's ethics hotline if unsure, and see our plain-English [lawyer advertising rules by state](/blog/lawyer-advertising-rules-by-state) guide for the details. Nothing here is legal advice.

**Watch the "specialist" trap.** In your LSA profile and anywhere else, don't call yourself a "specialist" or "expert" unless your state allows it, which usually means a recognized certification.

## What decides your ranking in LSA

You can't simply bid your way to the top. Google's [About ad rankings](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305) page says ranking depends on your bid plus how likely you are to turn the lead into a customer, which it judges using:

- **Responsiveness.** How quickly you reply to inquiries. Google says missed calls count against you.
- **Search relevance.** How well your practice areas, location and availability match what the searcher needs right now.
- **Communication options.** Accepting message or booking leads, not just calls, gives you more chances to show.
- **Profile quality.** Ratings, review count, response time, photos, completed verification and accurate business information.

Your bid is the most you're willing to pay for a lead, and you may pay less. Business hours matter too, because you only receive leads while you're marked open. A firm with a strong review profile that answers every call can beat a bigger bid that lets leads go to voicemail.

::bars Example: where a small firm usually has the most room to improve (illustrative, not Google data) | Answering calls live: 9 * | Review count and rating: 8 | Message and booking leads turned on: 6 | Profile photos and details: 5 | Raising the bid: 4

The chart above is our way of showing priorities, not a published Google formula. Google doesn't share weights. What it does say is that responsiveness matters, and that is the factor small firms most often fall short on.

### Responsiveness is a ranking factor and an intake factor

If your ads run 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. and nobody answers between noon and 1 p.m., you lose twice. Google sees missed calls, and the prospect calls the next card down. Our guide to [law firm intake and speed to lead](/blog/law-firm-intake-speed-to-lead) covers coverage models in detail, but the short version for LSA:

1. Only mark hours as open when a human will answer.
2. Route LSA calls to a line that rings more than one person.
3. If you use an answering service, give them a script specific to your practice areas and a way to book consults.
4. Reply to LSA messages within minutes, not hours. Turn on mobile notifications for the LSA app for whoever covers intake.
5. Mark leads as booked or archived in the dashboard. It keeps your reporting honest and helps you spot patterns.

### Reviews that help, within the rules

Reviews feed both your ranking and your conversion rate. Ask every satisfied client, not just the happy ones you pick out (Google bans "review gating"), and never offer anything in exchange. The FTC's Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, effective October 21, 2024, also bans fake reviews, buying reviews and suppressing negative ones. Replies need extra care because of client confidentiality. Our guide on [how lawyers get Google reviews ethically](/blog/lawyer-reviews-ethics-google) has templates that don't confirm someone was a client.

## The missed-call trap

Here is the pattern that burns LSA budgets. Leads arrive, but calls hit voicemail at lunch, after 5 p.m. and on Saturdays. The prospect calls another firm instead. Google's ranking page says missed calls hurt your responsiveness, and depending on how a lead is classified you may still be charged. The firm signs nothing and concludes "LSA doesn't work."

::compare The missed-call trap vs a covered intake line | Before: ads run 9–6 but lunch goes to voicemail; messages answered next morning; nobody marks leads in the dashboard; cost per signed case looks terrible | After: hours set only when someone answers; calls ring two people plus a backup service; messages answered within 5 minutes; every lead tagged booked, not a fit or disputed

Before you blame the channel, pull your LSA call log and check how many leads you answered live. If that number is low, fix coverage first and spend more later.

> Key takeaway: in LSA, answering the phone is the cheapest ranking improvement you will ever make. Every missed call is a lead you paid for and also a signal to Google that you're slow.

## Disputing bad leads

Not every charged lead is a real prospect. Google lets you dispute leads that don't qualify, and credits you if the dispute is approved. The steps are on Google's [How to dispute charges](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/15100654) page. Typical valid reasons include:

- The caller needed a practice area you don't offer or didn't select.
- The prospect is outside your service area.
- Spam, robocalls, sales pitches or job seekers.
- Wrong number, or a duplicate of a lead you already received.

Google sets a time window for disputes, so build a weekly habit. Every Monday, open the dashboard, listen to recordings if you've enabled them, and dispute anything that fits Google's criteria. Don't dispute leads that balked at your fee or that you chose not to take. Those were real prospects.

Call recording is a setting you control. Some states require all-party consent to record, so check your state's rules and your confidentiality obligations before enabling it.

## Setting a budget for LSA

Google lets you set a weekly budget and choose between bidding strategies such as maximizing leads within your budget or setting a maximum you're willing to pay per lead. Cost per lead varies widely by practice area and city, with personal injury and criminal defense in big metros among the most expensive. We won't quote averages, because published figures are rarely reliable. Your dashboard will show your real numbers within a few weeks.

A simple way to set a starting budget is to work backwards from cases.

::funnel Example: one month of LSA for a 3-attorney family law firm (illustrative) | Charged leads: 60 | Answered and qualified: 36 | Consults booked: 20 | Retained clients: 8

If that firm paid an illustrative $90 per lead, the month cost $5,400 for 8 retained clients, or $675 per signed case. Whether that is good depends on your average fee and how much it costs you to get a case from other channels. Run the same math with your real numbers:

1. Decide how many new cases you want from LSA per month.
2. Estimate your lead-to-signed rate (start conservatively, around 1 in 8 to 1 in 10, then use real data).
3. Multiply cases by leads-per-case to get the leads you need.
4. Multiply by your expected cost per lead to get a monthly budget, then divide by about 4.3 for weekly.
5. Review after 30 days and adjust using actual cost per signed case, not cost per lead.

Pick practice areas carefully. If you only want contested divorces, don't select every family law job type. If you don't take criminal cases outside your county, keep the service area tight. Narrow targeting means fewer leads, but fewer of the ones you'll pay for and turn away.

## Tracking signed cases, not just leads

The LSA dashboard shows leads and spend. It doesn't know which leads became retained clients. That last step lives in your intake or case management system, and it's where most firms lose sight of what LSA is really worth.

::check Weekly LSA review checklist | Pull charged leads and missed calls from the dashboard | Dispute leads that meet Google's criteria | Mark each lead booked, not a fit, or lost | Match booked consults to retained clients in your intake tool | Calculate cost per signed case for the week | Ask every newly retained client for a review

A few practical ways to connect the data:

- **Use your intake software.** Tools like Clio Grow and Lawmatics are built for legal intake and can tag the source of each matter. If you already use one, add "LSA" as a lead source and make it required.
- **Use a dedicated LSA number.** Calls from the ad come through Google's tracked number. If you use call tracking (for example CallRail) for your other channels, keep LSA separate so you don't double count.
- **Keep a simple pipeline.** If you're a small firm without intake software, a leads pipeline in a marketing tool works. Growvia includes one, so you can log LSA leads next to website form leads and see which turn into consults. It doesn't track phone calls or run your ads, so pair it with the LSA dashboard for call data.

## Is LSA right for your firm?

LSA tends to work well for firms that handle consumer matters people search for urgently or locally: personal injury, DUI and criminal defense, family law, estate planning, immigration, bankruptcy and employment. It works less well if your work is mostly business-to-business, referral-driven or highly specialized, where clients rarely search "lawyer near me."

## Frequently asked questions

### How much do Google Local Services Ads cost for lawyers?

You pay per valid lead, and the price depends on practice area, city and competition. Personal injury and criminal defense in large metros are usually the most expensive. Your LSA dashboard shows your actual cost per lead after a few weeks of running.

### How long does Google Verified screening take for a law firm?

It often takes a few weeks, depending on the number of attorneys and how quickly background checks and license verification clear. Make sure your firm name, address and attorney names match your bar records and Google Business Profile to avoid delays.

### Can I get a refund for bad LSA leads?

Yes. You can dispute leads that don't qualify, such as spam, wrong practice area or out-of-area callers, within the window Google sets. Approved disputes are credited back to your account.

### Do Local Services Ads count as attorney advertising?

They are advertising, so your state's rules on lawyer advertising apply. Some states require filing or labelling for certain ads. Check your state bar's current rules or ethics hotline.

### Do I need Google reviews to run Local Services Ads?

Reviews strongly affect LSA ranking and conversion, and Google may require a minimum before your ad shows. Link your Google Business Profile so your existing reviews appear, and ask every satisfied client for a review without incentives or gating.

### Should a law firm run LSA or Google Search ads?

Many firms run both. LSA usually wins on cost per signed case for urgent local matters, while Search ads give you control over specific keywords and landing pages. Start with LSA if you can answer calls reliably, then add Search ads for case types you want more of.

When someone searches "plumber near me", "best cafe in Sector 17" or "CA for GST filing Mohali", Google shows a map with three businesses — the **local pack** — above almost everything else. Those three spots get a very large share of the calls, direction requests and website visits for that search. Local SEO is the work of earning one of them, and of ranking well in the normal results just below.

The good news: local SEO rewards effort more than budget. A small business that does the basics thoroughly regularly beats bigger competitors who don't. This checklist walks through everything, in the order that usually gives the fastest results. Tick items off as you go.

## How Google ranks local businesses

Google has said publicly that local results are based mainly on three things:

- **Relevance** — how well your profile and website match what the person searched for.
- **Distance** — how far you are from the searcher (or the location in their search).
- **Prominence** — how well-known and trusted your business is: reviews, links, mentions, articles and overall web presence.

You can't move your shop, but you can make yourself far more relevant and prominent. Everything below targets one of those two.

## Part 1: Google Business Profile (the biggest lever)

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the listing that appears on Maps and in the local pack. If you do nothing else, do this section.

- **Claim and verify** your profile at business.google.com. If a profile already exists (customers can create them), request ownership.
- **Business name:** use your real-world name exactly as on your signage. Adding keywords ("Sharma Dental — Best Dentist Chandigarh") is against Google's guidelines and can get the listing suspended.
- **Primary category:** the single most important relevance setting. Pick the most specific category that describes your core business ("Pediatric dentist", not just "Dentist", if that's what you are). Add a few accurate secondary categories.
- **Address or service area:** shop-front businesses show an address; businesses that visit customers (plumbers, cleaners) can hide the address and set a service area instead.
- **Hours:** keep regular hours accurate and add special hours for holidays. Wrong hours are one of the fastest ways to earn angry reviews.
- **Phone and website:** use a local number if you can, and link to the most relevant page (a location page if you have several branches).
- **Services and products:** list each service with a short description and, where possible, a price or starting price.
- **Description:** 750 characters about what you do, who you serve and where. Write for humans; mention your main services and area naturally.
- **Photos:** real photos of your storefront, interior, team and work. Add new ones regularly. Profiles with plenty of genuine photos tend to get more engagement.
- **Attributes:** wheelchair access, women-led, online appointments, Wi-Fi, and so on — tick every one that's true.
- **Posts:** share offers, events and updates. They keep the profile fresh and give searchers a reason to choose you.
- **Q&A and messaging:** answer questions promptly. If you enable messaging, reply quickly or turn it off.

> Tip: search for your main keyword from your phone and look at the three businesses in the map pack. Compare their categories, review counts, photos and descriptions with yours. The gaps are your to-do list.

## Part 2: Reviews

Reviews influence both rankings and whether people actually choose you.

- **Ask every happy customer.** The best time is right after a great experience. Send a short message with a direct link to your review form (find it in your GBP dashboard under "Ask for reviews").
- **Make it easy:** a QR code at the counter, a link in your invoice email, a follow-up WhatsApp message.
- **Reply to every review** — thank people for good ones; respond calmly and helpfully to bad ones, offering to fix the issue offline.
- **Never buy reviews, offer rewards for positive reviews, or post fake ones.** It breaks Google's policies (and consumer protection laws in many countries) and can get reviews removed or your profile restricted.
- **Aim for steady flow over bursts.** Ten reviews a month for a year looks far more natural than 120 in one week.
- **Encourage detail.** Reviews that mention the service and area ("fixed our inverter wiring in Zirakpur within an hour") help relevance.

## Part 3: Your website — on-page basics

Google cross-checks your profile against your website. A clear, local, fast website strengthens everything.

- **Title tags:** each page's title should say what the page is and where, e.g. "AC Repair in Mohali — Same-Day Service | CoolFix". Keep it under about 60 characters.
- **Meta descriptions:** a one-sentence summary with a reason to click. They don't directly affect ranking but improve click-through.
- **One page per service:** "AC repair", "AC installation", "AC gas refill" each deserve their own page with a clear H1 heading, description, process, pricing guidance, photos and FAQs.
- **Location pages (if you serve several areas):** genuinely useful pages for each area — local projects, directions, area-specific details. Avoid copy-pasting the same page with the town name swapped; thin duplicates rarely rank.
- **NAP on every page:** your name, address and phone in the footer, matching your Google profile exactly.
- **Embed a Google Map** on your contact page.
- **Clear calls to action:** "Call now", "WhatsApp us", "Book online" — visible without scrolling on mobile.
- **Local proof:** photos of real local jobs, client logos, case studies with place names, awards and memberships.
- **Internal links:** link from your homepage to each service page and between related services.

## Part 4: Technical health

You don't need to be technical, but a few things must be right. A good audit tool will flag them for you.

- **HTTPS:** your site should load securely (the padlock in the browser).
- **Mobile-friendly:** most local searches happen on phones. Test every page on your own phone.
- **Speed:** check Google PageSpeed Insights. Compress large images, remove unused plugins and heavy sliders. Pay attention to Core Web Vitals — especially how quickly the main content appears (LCP) and whether the layout jumps around (CLS).
- **Indexing:** make sure Google can find your pages. Submit a sitemap in Google Search Console and check the Pages report for errors.
- **No accidental "noindex":** some site builders add a tag that tells Google not to index a page — often left over from when the site was being built.
- **Broken links and 404s:** fix or redirect them.
- **Structured data:** add `LocalBusiness` schema (or a specific type such as `Restaurant`, `Dentist`, `Plumber`) with your name, address, phone, hours and geo-coordinates, and `FAQPage` schema on pages with FAQs.

## Part 5: Citations and NAP consistency

A citation is any mention of your business name, address and phone number on another website — directories, maps apps, social profiles, industry sites.

- **Pick one canonical format** for your name, address and phone, and use it everywhere. "Shop 12, Phase 7" vs "SCO-12, Ph-7" may look similar to you but inconsistent to a machine.
- **Claim the major platforms:** Apple Business Connect (Apple Maps), Bing Places, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn.
- **Add the directories that matter locally and in your industry.** In India that might include Justdial, Sulekha and IndiaMART; for restaurants, Zomato and Swiggy; for clinics, Practo; in the US, Yelp and industry-specific sites.
- **Find and fix old listings,** especially after moving or changing numbers. Search your old phone number and old address.
- **Don't chase hundreds of low-quality directories.** A few dozen accurate, relevant ones beat hundreds of spammy ones.

## Part 6: Local links and mentions (prominence)

Links from other reputable websites remain one of the strongest signals of prominence. For local businesses the best links usually come from real-world relationships:

- Local news coverage of something newsworthy you did (an event, a milestone, a community initiative).
- Sponsoring a local school event, sports team or charity drive that lists sponsors on its website.
- Chambers of commerce, trade associations and business networks.
- Suppliers and partners who list recommended businesses.
- Guest articles or expert quotes for local blogs and publications.
- "Best of [city]" roundups written by local bloggers and publications.

## Part 7: Content that brings local customers

Helpful, specific content answers questions people ask before they buy — and it's increasingly what AI assistants quote.

- **Price guides:** "How much does a website cost for a small business in Chandigarh?" with honest ranges.
- **Local guides:** "Best time to service your AC before a Punjab summer."
- **Before/after case studies** with real details.
- **FAQs** on every service page.
- **Comparison articles** that help people choose between options — including when you're *not* the right fit.

## Part 8: Track what matters

You can't improve what you don't measure. Set up:

- **Google Search Console** (free): which searches show your site, your average position and clicks. Submit your sitemap here.
- **Google Business Profile insights:** calls, direction requests, website clicks and the searches people used to find you.
- **Rank tracking for 10–30 key local searches** ("dentist in Sector 35", "emergency dentist Chandigarh"). Rankings differ by location, so track from your area.
- **Leads, not just traffic.** Track calls, form submissions and WhatsApp enquiries back to where they came from.
- **AI visibility.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity your key questions regularly and note whether you're recommended.

## How long does local SEO take?

Fixing your Google Business Profile and obvious technical problems can show results within a few weeks. Building reviews, links and content compounds over months. Most small businesses that work steadily through this checklist see meaningful improvement within three to six months — and, unlike ads, the results keep working when you stop paying.

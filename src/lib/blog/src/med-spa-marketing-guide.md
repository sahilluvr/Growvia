Med spa marketing looks easy from the outside. Post some before-and-after photos, run a holiday Botox special, and the bookings roll in. In reality, med spas compete in one of the most crowded local markets, with clients who research for weeks on Instagram, TikTok and Google, then book on impulse once they trust you. And because you deliver medical treatments, your marketing answers to rules that a hair salon never thinks about.

Those rules come from several directions. The FDA governs how drugs and devices can be described. The FTC requires evidence for health claims and honest testimonials. State medical boards decide who can perform and supervise treatments. HIPAA or state health data laws can apply to client photos and records.

This guide shows you how to market a med spa that grows bookings and repeat revenue without stepping on those lines. It's part of our [AI marketing system for medical practices](/blog/ai-marketing-for-medical-practices). It isn't legal advice. Rules vary by state, so check with your medical director and a healthcare attorney.

## Get the foundations right before you promote

Before any campaign, make sure the basics of how you describe your business are accurate.

- **Supervision and credentials.** States set different rules for who can inject, who can operate lasers and what a medical director must do. Show who performs each treatment (name and license type) and who your medical director is.
- **Treatment names.** "Botox" is a brand name for one product, Botox Cosmetic from Allergan. If you also use Dysport, Xeomin, Jeuveau or Daxxify, say "neurotoxin injections" or "wrinkle relaxers" and list the brands you carry. Calling every neurotoxin "Botox" is inaccurate and can draw complaints from trademark owners.
- **FDA status.** Describe approved uses accurately. Don't call a product "FDA approved" for a use it isn't approved for, and don't call a device "FDA approved" if it was cleared through a different pathway. The FDA has also warned consumers not to use needle-free devices (sometimes called hyaluron pens) to inject fillers.
- **Compounded drugs.** If you offer medical weight loss, be careful. Compounded drugs aren't FDA approved, and the FDA has issued warnings about compounded GLP-1 products, whose shortage status has changed. Never call a compounded product a "generic" version of a brand-name drug. Check current FDA guidance before marketing any of these services.

> Warning: Every claim in your marketing, including Instagram captions and influencer posts, is your responsibility. If your agency or a creator writes "permanent results" or "no risks", you own that claim.

## What you can claim, and what you can't

The FTC's [Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance) says health-related claims generally need competent and reliable scientific evidence. In practice, that rules out a lot of common med spa language.

::compare Risky claims vs safer wording | Risky: permanent results; no downtime for everyone; guaranteed to look 10 years younger; melts fat forever; FDA approved (for an unapproved use) | Safer: results typically last a few months and vary by person; most clients return to normal activities quickly; softens moderate frown lines; reduces fat in treated areas; FDA approved for (the specific approved indication)

A few more rules of thumb:

- **Results vary, and say so** near any results claim or before-and-after image.
- **Mention downtime and risks** honestly on treatment pages. Clients trust a page that tells them about bruising and swelling more than one that doesn't.
- **Avoid "painless"** unless it's true for nearly everyone. "Most clients describe it as a quick pinch" is safer.
- **Influencers must disclose.** Under the FTC's Endorsement Guides, anyone you give free or discounted treatments to must clearly disclose that connection when they post about you.

## Before-and-after photos: consent and honesty

Before-and-after photos sell more med spa treatments than anything else you can post. They're also the riskiest content you publish.

If your med spa is a HIPAA covered entity (for example because it bills insurance electronically for any services), identifiable client photos are protected health information, and using them in marketing requires a signed HIPAA authorization. Even if HIPAA doesn't apply to you, state privacy and consumer health data laws may, and you need permission to use anyone's image for advertising. Our [HIPAA compliant marketing guide](/blog/hipaa-compliant-marketing-guide) explains what a valid authorization includes.

::check Before-and-after photo checklist | Signed written consent before posting, naming where photos will appear | Same lighting, angle and distance in both photos | No filters, retouching or makeup differences | Treatment, number of sessions and timeframe stated | Results vary note next to the photos | Consent can be revoked and removal process exists | Photo files stored securely, not on personal phones

Be honest in the photos themselves. Different lighting, makeup, posture or filters between before and after images can make results look better than they are, which the FTC treats as deceptive. If a client's results were unusually good, don't present them as typical.

Ad platforms add their own limits. Meta's advertising standards restrict before-and-after images in ads about personal health and appearance, so use them in organic posts and on your website and keep paid ads to educational or brand content. Check the platform's current policy before every campaign.

## Treatment pages that rank and convert

Clients search by treatment and by problem: "lip filler near me", "laser hair removal cost", "how long does Botox last". One strong page per core treatment beats a single services list.

### What each treatment page needs

1. **What it treats**, in plain language, and who is a good candidate.
2. **Who performs it**, with name, credentials and the supervising medical director.
3. **What to expect**: consultation, the appointment itself, how long it takes.
4. **Downtime, risks and side effects**, stated honestly.
5. **How long results typically last**, as a range, with "results vary".
6. **Pricing approach**: per unit, per syringe, per session or package, or "starting at" prices.
7. **Real before-and-after photos** with consent, and a booking button.
8. **A short FAQ** using the questions your front desk hears most.

Start with your highest-revenue treatments, usually neurotoxins, fillers, laser hair removal, microneedling and chemical peels. Our [keyword research guide](/blog/keyword-research-for-small-business) shows how to find the exact phrases people use.

### Google Business Profile for med spas

Choose "Medical spa" as your primary category if it fits, add secondary categories that match real services (for example "Laser hair removal service"), list treatments in the services section, and keep photos fresh. Med spas aren't a listed Google Local Services Ads category, so your organic Maps presence matters even more.

## Instagram and short video: where clients decide

For most med spas, Instagram is where clients get to know you before they ever book. Treat it like a showroom with a front desk.

### Content that works

- **Injector introductions.** Who they are, their training, why they love what they do.
- **Treatment explainers.** "What actually happens at a lip filler appointment", filmed with a team member or consenting client.
- **Myth-busting.** "Will Botox make me look frozen?" answered honestly.
- **Before-and-afters** with consent and context.
- **Aftercare tips** and what's normal after treatment.
- **Behind the scenes**: the space, new equipment, team training days.

### DMs: move health questions to the right place

Instagram DMs are where many bookings start, and where many privacy problems start too. Answer general questions, but move medical history and specific concerns into your booking and intake system.

::chat Turning a DM into a consultation | them: Hi! How much is lip filler? I had it done somewhere else last year and didn't love it. | you: Hi! ## Memberships and packages

Memberships turn one-off clients into recurring revenue. A typical structure is a monthly fee that includes one service (a facial or a set amount toward neurotoxin) plus member pricing on everything else.

### Make memberships work

- **Keep the offer simple.** One or two tiers are easier to sell at the front desk than five.
- **Make value visible.** Show members their savings in each receipt or email.
- **Be clear about cancellation.** State terms, renewal dates and how to cancel before someone signs up. The FTC's "click to cancel" rule was vacated by a federal appeals court in July 2025, but state automatic renewal laws still apply, and California's are among the strictest.
- **Run memberships in your booking software.** Boulevard, Zenoti, Mangomint, Vagaro and similar tools handle recurring billing, credits and membership tracking far better than a marketing tool.

## Email promotions that don't feel spammy

Email is your best owned channel for promotions, but med spa email lists need care.

- **Build your list on purpose.** Website sign-ups, a gift card purchase, an event, or a contest with clear opt-in. Our [email welcome sequence guide](/blog/email-marketing-welcome-sequence) shows how to turn new subscribers into first bookings.
- **Segment by interest, not treatment history,** in general marketing tools. "Interested in skin care" based on a website sign-up choice is fine. "Had filler in March" pulled from treatment records belongs in your HIPAA-aware booking system, if anywhere.
- **Follow CAN-SPAM.** Honest subject lines, your physical address and a working unsubscribe honored within 10 business days.
- **Authenticate your domain** so promotions don't land in spam. See the [email deliverability guide](/blog/email-deliverability-spf-dkim-dmarc-guide).

## Reviews and reputation

Reviews matter as much for med spas as for any local business, and maybe more, because clients worry about who's putting a needle near their face.

- Ask every client after their treatment, at checkout with a QR code or a link from your booking software.
- Don't gate reviews or offer discounts for them. The FTC's [Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers), effective October 21, 2024, bans buying reviews, fake reviews and suppressing negative ones.
- In replies, don't confirm treatment details or discuss outcomes publicly. Thank the reviewer and invite them to contact you directly.

## Paid ads: what to expect

Paid ads can fill a new injector's schedule or launch a new treatment, but they come with platform restrictions.

| Channel | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Meta and Instagram ads | Awareness, event promotions, new treatment launches | Before-and-after image restrictions, health targeting limits |
| Google Search ads | High-intent treatment searches | Google's [Healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) restricts prescription drug promotion, so prescription brand names can cause limited or disapproved ads |
| Local Services Ads | Not available for med spas as a category | Check current categories, dermatologists are eligible |
| Influencer partnerships | Local reach and trust | Clear disclosure of free treatments, and you own their claims |

Keep ad pixels off booking and intake pages. Our [healthcare tracking pixels guide](/blog/healthcare-tracking-pixels-privacy) explains which pages are risky and how to measure without them. In states with consumer health data laws, such as Washington's My Health My Data Act, ad tracking and audience building need extra care.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can med spas post before-and-after photos?

Yes, with written consent from the client, honest photos taken in the same conditions, and a note that results vary. If your med spa is a HIPAA covered entity, you need a signed HIPAA authorization, and paid ad platforms may restrict these images.

### Can I advertise "Botox" if I use other neurotoxins?

Only call it Botox when you're using Botox Cosmetic. If you use several brands, say "neurotoxin injections" or "wrinkle relaxers" and list the brands you offer.

### Does HIPAA apply to med spas?

It depends on whether your med spa is a covered entity, usually because it transmits health information electronically for insurance billing. Even if HIPAA doesn't apply, FTC rules and state consumer health data laws can.

### What's the best marketing channel for a med spa?

For most med spas, Instagram and Google Business Profile drive the most new clients, supported by treatment pages, reviews and email promotions to subscribers. Memberships drive repeat revenue.

### Can med spas use Google Local Services Ads?

Med spas aren't currently a listed Local Services Ads category in the US, although dermatologists are. Check Google's current list for your area, and rely on Maps, Search ads and social in the meantime.

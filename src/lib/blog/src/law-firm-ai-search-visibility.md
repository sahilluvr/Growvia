Ask ChatGPT "who's the best DUI lawyer in Phoenix?" and you'll usually get a short list of firm names, a sentence on each and sometimes links. The person asking may never see a page of Google results. If your firm isn't on that list, you're invisible to that prospect, however good your Maps ranking is.

Getting your law firm recommended by AI assistants is not magic and it isn't a separate discipline from good local SEO. The assistants pull from the same signals that make a firm look credible to a human: consistent business details, reviews, legal directory profiles, clear attorney bios and pages that actually answer questions. The difference is that you can't see the results unless you check them on purpose.

This guide explains how ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity seem to pick lawyers, which prompts to track, what to fix first, and how to measure progress. It's part of our larger guide to [AI marketing for lawyers](/blog/ai-marketing-for-lawyers), which covers the whole growth system for a firm.

## How AI assistants choose which lawyers to mention

None of the AI companies publish a ranking formula for local recommendations, so treat anyone who claims to know the exact weights with suspicion. What we can say with confidence comes from how these tools work and what their makers have said publicly.

- **Most answers about local firms are grounded in web search.** ChatGPT's search feature, Gemini and Perplexity look up current information when you ask about local services, then summarize what they find. Perplexity shows its sources by default, and ChatGPT and Gemini often do too.
- **Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode use Google's own index.** Google's guidance on [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says there are no extra requirements or special optimizations needed to appear. The same helpful, crawlable content that ranks in Search is what gets used.
- **Assistants favor sources they can cross-check.** When several independent sources (your site, your Google Business Profile, Avvo, Justia, the state bar directory, review sites, local news) agree on who you are and what you do, you're an easier recommendation to make.
- **Review themes matter, not just stars.** Answers often include a reason, like "known for responsive communication" or "focuses on DUI defense." Those reasons tend to echo how clients and directories describe you.

::flow How an AI assistant builds a lawyer recommendation | Prospect asks "best DUI lawyer in Phoenix" | Assistant searches the web and maps data | It cross-checks firm sites, directories and reviews | It names firms that appear consistently with clear reasons

## The prompts to track

Start by finding out where you stand. Make a list of 15 to 25 prompts that real prospects would type, across your practice areas and locations. Write them in plain language, the way a stressed person would.

| Prompt type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Best-of, local | "best DUI lawyer in Phoenix," "top divorce attorney in Plano TX" |
| Situation-led | "I got a DUI in Scottsdale last night, who should I call," "my landlord is keeping my deposit, do I need a lawyer in Austin" |
| Specific need | "estate planning attorney near Naperville who does flat fees," "Spanish-speaking car accident lawyer in Houston" |
| Comparison | "Smith Law vs Jones Defense for DUI," "is [your firm] good" |
| Questions | "how does a DUI affect my license in Arizona," "how long does probate take in Florida" |

Run each prompt in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, ideally logged out or in a fresh session, and note:

1. Whether your firm is mentioned at all.
2. Which competitors are mentioned instead.
3. The reason given for each recommendation.
4. Which sources are cited, if any.
5. Whether anything said about you is wrong (old address, wrong practice area, a former attorney).

Answers vary between runs and between users, so look for patterns over several checks rather than reacting to one answer. Doing this by hand monthly is fine for a few prompts. ## Fix your entity basics first: consistent NAP

To an AI system, your firm is an "entity" with a name, address, phone number (NAP), practice areas, attorneys and reputation. If those details conflict across the web, the assistant is less sure who you are, and less likely to recommend you.

Common conflicts at law firms:

- The firm changed its name when a partner left, but old directory listings still use the former name.
- The office moved, and Avvo, Yelp or the bar directory shows the old address.
- Each attorney has their own Google Business Profile with a different phone number from the firm's.
- The website footer says "Suite 200" and the Google Business Profile says "Ste 210."

::check NAP consistency checklist for law firms | Google Business Profile matches the website footer exactly | State bar directory listing is current for every attorney | Avvo, Justia, FindLaw and Martindale profiles claimed and updated | Bing Places and Apple Business Connect listings match | Old firm names and addresses corrected or closed | One main phone number used consistently

## Legal directory citations

Legal directories carry extra weight for law firms because they're trusted, topic-specific and often cited by AI tools. Prioritize these:

- **Your state bar's lawyer directory.** It's the most authoritative source for whether you're licensed. Make sure every attorney's record is current.
- **Avvo, Justia, FindLaw, Martindale-Hubbell and Lawyers.com.** Claim profiles, fill every field, list practice areas accurately and keep them in sync.
- **Super Lawyers, Best Lawyers and similar recognitions**, if you've genuinely earned them. Follow your state's rules on how you describe awards; many require that the basis for the award be explained and that it not imply a comparison you can't back up.
- **Local sources**: chamber of commerce listings, local news coverage, community organization pages and law school alumni features.

Don't pay for low-quality "lawyer directory" listings that exist only to sell links. They rarely help with Google or AI answers, and they can make your firm look less credible.

Be careful about paid placements that look like recommendations. ABA Model Rule 7.2 limits what you can pay for a recommendation, and your state's version governs. ## Write FAQ content that AI tools can use

AI assistants answer questions, so pages that answer questions clearly are easy for them to draw on. The same pages help human readers and rank in Google. The pattern that works:

1. **Pick a real question** your intake team hears every week.
2. **Answer it in the first two sentences**, specifically for your state.
3. **Explain the details**: process, timelines, exceptions, what the reader should do now.
4. **Cite the statute or official court resource.**
5. **Add the attorney byline** and a "reviewed on" date.
6. **Link to the relevant practice-area page** and consult form.

::compare Vague vs AI-friendly FAQ content | Before: "DUI laws are complex and every case is different. Call us for a free consultation to learn your options."; no state named; no author | After: "In Arizona, a first DUI usually triggers a 90-day license suspension through the MVD, separate from the criminal case."; cites the statute; explains next steps; written by a named attorney

The "after" example names a specific rule. Before publishing anything like it, have the responsible attorney confirm the current law in your state. AI assistants repeat what they find, so outdated pages can end up quoted. ### Use FAQ sections and schema sensibly

Add a short FAQ section to each practice-area page with 4 to 6 real questions. Structured data (schema.org types like LegalService or Attorney, plus Organization details) helps search engines understand your business, though Google says no special schema is needed for AI features. See our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-for-local-business) for implementation. Make sure the markup matches what's visible on the page.

## Attorney bios that build trust

When an assistant explains why it recommends a firm, it often mentions an attorney's focus or background. Thin bios ("John is a dedicated advocate for his clients") give it nothing to work with.

A strong bio includes:

- **Bar admissions** with years, and federal courts if relevant.
- **Practice focus** in plain words: "DUI and criminal defense in Maricopa County."
- **Experience that can be checked**: years in practice, former roles (prosecutor, public defender, judicial clerk), publications, speaking.
- **Certifications**, only if recognized under your state's rules. Most states restrict the words "specialist" and "certified" to lawyers certified by an approved organization.
- **Languages spoken.** "Habla español" is a reason an assistant can cite for prompts like "Spanish-speaking DUI lawyer."
- **A real photo** and a link to the attorney's state bar record.

Keep the bio identical in substance across your site, Avvo, Justia and other directories.

## Reviews: what clients say shapes what AI says

AI answers often summarize reputation in a phrase. That phrase comes from somewhere, usually patterns in reviews and directory descriptions. If dozens of reviews mention "returned my call the same day" or "explained everything clearly," that theme can show up in recommendations.

- Ask every satisfied client for a Google review, at the right time and without incentives. The FTC's [Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers), effective October 21, 2024, bans fake and purchased reviews and review suppression, and Google bans asking only happy clients.
- Don't script what clients write. You can tell them what kinds of details help other people ("what the process was like"), but the words must be theirs.
- Reply to reviews without revealing confidential information. ABA Formal Opinion 496 covers responding to online criticism.

## Don't block the crawlers that power AI search

Some firms have blocked AI crawlers in robots.txt without realizing what it affects. There's a difference between crawlers that collect training data and crawlers that power search answers. OpenAI documents separate bots on its [crawler overview page](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots), including OAI-SearchBot, which is used to surface sites in ChatGPT search results. Blocking it can keep your pages out of those answers. Perplexity and others have their own crawlers too.

Decide deliberately: you may be comfortable opting out of model training while still allowing search crawlers. Check your robots.txt, your firewall or CDN bot settings, and any security plugin that blocks "AI bots" by default. ## Measuring AI visibility over time

AI answers change constantly, so a single check is a snapshot. Track a fixed prompt set monthly and record results in a simple table.

::bars Example: firm mentioned across 20 tracked prompts over 4 months (illustrative) | Month 1: 3 | Month 2: 5 | Month 3: 8 | Month 4: 11 *

What to watch:

- **Mention rate**: how many tracked prompts include your firm, per assistant.
- **Competitors**: which firms appear most, and whether that changes.
- **Accuracy**: wrong facts about your firm, which point to an outdated source you need to fix.
- **Cited sources**: which directories and pages the assistants use, so you can prioritize them.
- **Business outcomes**: ask new clients "how did you hear about us?" and include "ChatGPT or another AI assistant" as an option on your intake form.

> Key takeaway: you can't optimize what you don't check. A monthly prompt audit, plus fixing the inconsistencies it uncovers, does more than any "AI SEO" trick.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get my law firm recommended by ChatGPT?

Make your firm easy to verify: consistent name, address and phone everywhere, complete legal directory and state bar profiles, strong reviews, detailed attorney bios and pages that clearly answer common questions for your state. Then track a set of prompts monthly to see whether you appear.

### Can a law firm pay to be recommended by AI assistants?

There's no paid placement for organic AI recommendations in ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity answers. Be wary of vendors promising guaranteed AI rankings, and remember that bar rules limit paying for recommendations.

### Do I need special schema or files for Google AI Overviews?

No. Google says there are no extra requirements or special optimizations needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. Standard SEO, helpful content and accurate structured data are enough.

### Which legal directories matter most for AI search?

Start with your state bar directory, then Avvo, Justia, FindLaw and Martindale-Hubbell, plus your Google Business Profile, Bing Places and Apple Business Connect. Accuracy and consistency matter more than the number of listings.

### How often should a law firm check its AI search visibility?

Monthly is enough for most firms. AI answers vary from run to run, so track the same prompts over time and look for trends rather than reacting to a single answer.

### Is AI search content considered attorney advertising?

Content on your own website and directory profiles is generally subject to your state's lawyer advertising rules, whether a human or an AI assistant reads it. Keep claims accurate and check your state's current rules.

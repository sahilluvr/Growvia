Marketing a small business in Dubai feels different from marketing one almost anywhere else. Your customers might speak Arabic, English, Hindi, Tagalog or Russian. They find you on Google Maps, check you on Instagram, then message you on WhatsApp before they ever call. And the rules on advertising, promotions and data are specific to the UAE, sometimes to a single emirate.

Most marketing advice online is written for the US or the UK. It assumes postcodes, English-only websites and email as the main sales channel. Little of that maps cleanly onto a salon in Al Barsha, a clinic in JLT or a brokerage in Business Bay.

This guide is for owners in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah and the wider UAE. It covers how people here search and buy, how to win on Google Maps, how to do Arabic SEO properly, and how to use Instagram and WhatsApp without breaking local rules. There's a worked example and a 30-day plan you can start this week.

## How UAE customers search and buy

The UAE is one of the most connected markets in the world. [DataReportal's Digital 2026 report for the UAE](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-united-arab-emirates) puts internet use at 99 percent of the population in late 2025. It also counts roughly two mobile connections per resident. Meta's ad tools suggested Instagram ads could reach about 70 percent of the population.

Treat platform figures with care. DataReportal notes they may not represent unique people, because many hold several accounts. But the direction is clear: your customers are online, on their phones, every day.

### What that means in practice

- **Mobile first.** Most people will see your website on a phone, often on the move. Slow pages and tiny buttons cost enquiries.
- **Maps before websites.** For anything local, people search Google Maps directly, typing "dentist JLT" or "salon near me".
- **Instagram as the shop window.** Many people check your Instagram to judge quality, prices and style before contacting you.
- **WhatsApp as the checkout.** Quotes, bookings and payment links happen in chat. A fast reply often beats a higher ranking.
- **Two or more languages.** Arabic and English both matter for many businesses, and other languages may too.

### Which channel does what

Most small businesses need three or four of these channels, not all of them.

| Channel | Main job | Best for | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google Business Profile | Be found on Maps and local search | Clinics, salons, restaurants, retail, services | Low, ongoing |
| Website and SEO | Rank for service searches and build trust | Everyone, especially consultancies and real estate | Medium to high, compounding |
| Instagram | Show quality, style and social proof | Restaurants, salons, beauty, fashion, property | Medium, weekly |
| TikTok | Reach new audiences with short video | Food, beauty, retail, e-commerce | Medium to high |
| WhatsApp Business | Answer, book, follow up, re-engage | Almost every local business | Low to medium, daily |
| Email | Nurture leads and repeat customers | E-commerce, B2B, consultancies | Medium |
| Paid ads | Fast, targeted demand | Launches, seasonal peaks, high-value services | Budget plus weekly checks |

## Local SEO in Dubai: areas, communities and addresses

Dubai is a city of communities. People search "dentist Dubai Marina" or "physio Business Bay" more naturally than "dentist Dubai". Your local SEO needs to speak that language. If you're new to the basics, start with our [local SEO checklist for small businesses](/blog/local-seo-checklist-small-business). Below is the UAE-specific layer.

### Name your area the way customers say it

Use the community names people type: Business Bay, JLT, Al Barsha, Dubai Marina, Downtown, Deira, Al Quoz. In Abu Dhabi it might be Al Reem Island or Khalifa City; in Sharjah, Al Nahda or Al Majaz.

Put the area in your page titles, main heading and contact page. If you serve several communities, write one genuinely useful page per area, with landmarks, parking and metro directions. Thin copies with only the area name swapped do more harm than good.

Arabic place names also have several English spellings, such as Al Barsha and Albarsha. Pick the one Google Maps and your customers use, and keep it consistent everywhere.

### Makani numbers and directions

In Dubai, buildings carry a Makani number. It's a 10-digit code from Dubai Municipality that points to a building's entrance, and the system also covers several other emirates.

Google Business Profile has no Makani field. Add the number to your website contact page, booking confirmations and WhatsApp greeting instead. It helps taxis, couriers and first-time visitors find the right door.

> Put your Makani number, nearest metro station and parking tips in every booking confirmation.

### Free zone or mainland: which address goes on Google

Many UAE companies hold a free zone licence, sometimes with a flexi-desk nobody works from. Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say a virtual office you don't operate from isn't eligible. A co-working office only counts if it has clear signage, receives customers during business hours and is staffed by your team.

So show the place where customers actually visit you or your staff actually work. If you go to customers instead, like cleaners or mobile groomers, set up as a service-area business and hide the address. Also confirm your licence lets you serve the customers you're targeting, since free zone and mainland licences carry different permissions.

## Google Business Profile: the Dubai playbook

For most local businesses here, Google Business Profile is the highest-return marketing task. It's free and puts you where buying decisions start.

### Get the basics exactly right

1. Use your real trading name. Google prohibits adding keywords or locations, so "Smile Dental" is fine and "Smile Dental Best Dentist JLT" is not.
2. Choose the most specific primary category, plus a few accurate secondary ones.
3. Set hours carefully, including Friday prayer closures, Ramadan hours and public holidays.
4. Add a UAE number that is answered, ideally one that also works on WhatsApp.
5. List every service with a short description and a price range where you can.

### Photos, reviews and two languages

Upload real photos of your entrance, signage, team and interior. People here often look for the entrance photo before driving to an unfamiliar tower. Add fresh photos monthly.

Ask every happy customer for a review, ideally by WhatsApp with a direct link. Reply to every review within a few days. Your description and posts can be in English, Arabic or both, but avoid awkward machine translation.

> Reply to Arabic reviews in Arabic and English reviews in English. It shows you serve both.

## Arabic and bilingual SEO done properly

Arabic SEO is not translation. It's a separate market with its own search habits, and many English-only competitors ignore it.

### Research Arabic keywords separately

People searching in Arabic don't simply type a translation of the English phrase. They may use dialect terms, mix in English words or write Arabic in Latin letters. Build your Arabic keyword list from Google autocomplete, Search Console data and conversations with Arabic-speaking staff and customers. Then check who ranks: a results page full of directories is a different opportunity from one with small businesses.

### Use real translation, then review it

Machine translation is a fine first draft, never the final version. Have a fluent Arabic speaker with industry knowledge rewrite and review it. Watch tone, formality and Gulf-specific wording, which can differ from Arabic used elsewhere.

### Get the technical setup right

- **Separate URLs per language**, such as `/en/` and `/ar/` folders. Don't switch language with cookies on one URL.
- **hreflang tags.** Google's [guide to localised versions](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions) explains them. Codes like `ar-AE` and `en-AE` are valid, and each page must link back to the others or Google may ignore the tags.
- **Right-to-left layout.** Arabic pages need `dir="rtl"` and a properly mirrored layout. Test menus, forms and numbers on a real phone.
- **Arabic metadata.** Write separate Arabic titles and meta descriptions.
- **A proper language switcher** that links each page to its equivalent, not the homepage.

AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity increasingly answer local questions too, and clear pages in both languages help there. Our guide to [getting recommended by ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity](/blog/get-recommended-by-chatgpt-gemini-perplexity) goes deeper.

## Instagram and TikTok for UAE businesses

Instagram is often where people decide whether you're worth a message. TikTok is often where they discover you first. Both reward real people, real places and real results.

### Instagram: your second website

Treat your profile like a landing page. Your bio should say what you do, which area you're in and how to book, with a link to booking or WhatsApp. Use Highlights for prices, services, location and reviews. Mix Reels for reach, carousels for explaining services, and Stories for offers and behind-the-scenes moments. Tag your location consistently by community name.

### TikTok: reach and personality

TikTok suits food, beauty, fitness, retail and e-commerce. Simple formats work best: a quick tour, a before-and-after, or an answer to a common question. Honest phone footage often beats polished studio ads.

### Bilingual content without doubling the work

You don't need every post in two languages. Try on-screen text in one language and captions in the other, or alternate across the week. Your insights will show which language your audience engages with.

> Film once, caption twice. One clip can carry Arabic and English captions as two posts.

If you pay or gift creators, check they hold the UAE Media Council advertiser permit first. More on that below.

## WhatsApp: where UAE sales actually close

In the UAE, WhatsApp is often the main way customers talk to businesses. They ask prices, book, check availability and send location pins. A slow reply is often a lost customer.

::flow The typical UAE path to a sale | Instagram, TikTok or Google | Tap to WhatsApp | Questions, prices and photos | Booking or order | Review and repeat visit

### WhatsApp Business app or API

The free WhatsApp Business app suits an owner or small team on one phone, with a profile, catalogue, quick replies and labels. The WhatsApp Business API suits teams that need several agents on one number, automation, CRM links or approved template messages. Our [WhatsApp Business API guide](/blog/whatsapp-business-api-guide-small-business) explains the difference.

### Ask before you message

Meta's [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) is clear. You may only contact people who have given you their number and opted in to hear from you, and you must respect every opt-out.

Collect opt-in on your booking form, at checkout or in the first chat. Say what they'll receive, such as reminders or offers. Broadcasting promotions to your whole contact list is the fastest route to blocks and restrictions.

### Practical WhatsApp habits

- A greeting message that answers the top questions, including location and hours.
- Quick replies for prices, directions and booking links, so every staff member answers consistently.
- Labels by stage: new enquiry, booked, follow-up, repeat.
- A reminder the day before appointments and a review request after.

## The rules: advertising, permits and promotions

UAE advertising rules have tightened in recent years. Most are straightforward once you know they exist. This is a summary, not legal advice.

### Media content standards and the advertiser permit

The UAE media law, Federal Decree-Law No. 55 of 2023, sets content standards that cover advertising and social media. Ads must not mislead or exaggerate, must disclose paid promotion clearly, and must respect the UAE's religious, cultural and national values.

In July 2025 the [UAE Media Council](https://uaemc.gov.ae) introduced an advertiser permit for anyone publishing advertising content on social media, paid, gifted or unpaid. Influencers and creators promoting other businesses need it. According to [Gulf News's explainer](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/telephone-internet/advertiser-permit-uae-who-needs-it-and-how-to-apply-online-1.500278441), people advertising their own products, or those of a company they own, on their personal accounts are exempt. If staff, freelancers or creators front your content, check the current rules with the Media Council.

### Sector rules you can't ignore

- **Real estate in Dubai.** Property ads, including online and social ads, need a permit through the Dubai Land Department's [Trakheesi system](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/eservices/request-a-real-estate-permit). Brokers usually need a marketing contract with the owner.
- **Healthcare.** Dubai Health Authority advertising standards ban absolute or exaggerated claims such as "the best", "100%" or "no side effects". Abu Dhabi and the northern emirates have their own health regulators.
- **Food.** Claims about health benefits, ingredients or halal status must be accurate and provable.

### Sales, promotions and prize draws

In Dubai, the Department of Economy and Tourism (DET) regulates sales promotions, discount campaigns and prize draws, and permits have traditionally been required. Other emirates have their own economic departments. Before announcing a raffle or a store-wide sale, check the current requirement and allow time for approval.

### Marketing calls and SMS

Cabinet Decision No. 56 of 2024, enforced by the TDRA, tightened telemarketing rules. As [Khaleej Times reports](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/tdra-uae-telemarketing-violations-fines-numbers-cut), marketing calls are limited to 9am to 6pm, personal mobile numbers may not be used for promotion, and residents can opt out through the Do Not Call Registry. The rules also cover marketing SMS and messages sent through social apps, and businesses need prior approval to run telemarketing.

> Never cold-call or bulk-text from a staff member's personal number. It's explicitly not allowed.

## Data protection and consent: PDPL, DIFC and ADGM

Every lead form, booking system and WhatsApp list holds personal data, and the UAE has rules on using it.

### The federal PDPL

The Personal Data Protection Law, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021, has been in force since January 2022. It covers most mainland businesses and most free zones. The core idea is familiar: collect data for a clear purpose, keep it secure, and rely on clear, specific consent that's easy to withdraw for most marketing.

At the time of writing, the law's executive regulations still hadn't been published, leaving details such as penalties undefined. Don't treat that as a loophole. Good consent habits also protect your sender reputation and customer trust.

### DIFC and ADGM have their own laws

If you're licensed in the Dubai International Financial Centre or Abu Dhabi Global Market, a separate data protection law and regulator apply. Check which regime covers you before setting up forms and lists.

### Consent habits that work everywhere

- An unticked marketing consent checkbox on every form.
- Plain words on what you'll send and how often.
- A record of when and how each person opted in.
- Easy unsubscribing on email, SMS and WhatsApp, acted on promptly.
- Deleting leads you no longer need.

## Plan around the UAE calendar

The UAE has a distinct marketing rhythm, and planning around it is an easy win.

### Ramadan and Eid

During Ramadan, working hours shorten, evenings get busier and online activity often shifts later at night. Restaurants focus on iftar and suhoor; retail and gifting build towards Eid al-Fitr. Update your posting times and Google hours early, and keep messaging respectful. Both follow the lunar calendar, moving about 10 to 11 days earlier each year. Eid al-Adha is a second major holiday later in the year.

### Winter season and Dubai Shopping Festival

The cooler months, roughly October to April, are peak season for tourism, outdoor dining, events and retail. The Dubai Shopping Festival usually runs from December into January and draws deal-seekers.

### Summer slowdown

Many residents travel in the hot summer months, and footfall drops. Use summer to fix your website, gather reviews, build content and run offers for residents who stay.

### B2B: GITEX and exhibitions

For B2B and tech companies, GITEX Global, usually held in Dubai in October, is a major moment. Plan content, outreach and meetings weeks ahead.

## Worked example: a dental clinic in JLT

Bright Smile Dental is a fictional two-dentist clinic in a Jumeirah Lake Towers tower. It serves JLT, Dubai Marina and JVC residents in English and Arabic.

### Where they started

The Google profile had three photos and a name that included "Best Dentist JLT", which breaks Google's rules. The website was English only. WhatsApp enquiries went to the receptionist's personal phone. Instagram was irregular stock images.

### What they changed

1. **Fixed the profile.** They removed keywords from the name, chose "Dental clinic" as the primary category, and added services with price ranges and photos of the tower entrance and lift lobby.
2. **Added directions everywhere.** The contact page shows cluster, tower, Makani number, nearest metro station and visitor parking.
3. **Built area pages** for JLT, Dubai Marina and JVC, covering travel time, parking and common questions.
4. **Launched Arabic pages**, reviewed by a fluent dental professional, with RTL layout, Arabic titles and hreflang.
5. **Moved WhatsApp to one business number** with quick replies for prices, location and booking.
6. **Cleaned up claims**, removing "painless guaranteed" and "best results" in line with DHA standards.
7. **Started review requests** by WhatsApp after each visit, only to patients who agreed to messages.
8. **Planned for Ramadan**, publishing adjusted hours two weeks early and promoting evening slots.

### What they track

Each month they review calls and direction requests from Google, WhatsApp enquiries, booked appointments, new reviews and rankings for "dentist JLT" in both languages. Follower counts aren't on the list.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need an advertiser permit to post about my own business on Instagram?

Generally, no. People advertising their own products or services, or those of a company they own, on their personal accounts are exempt. Creators promoting other businesses need the UAE Media Council permit.

### Can I use my free zone address on Google Business Profile?

Only if customers visit you there or your staff work there during business hours. Google doesn't allow virtual offices, and co-working offices need signage, staff and customer visits. If you go to customers, set up as a service-area business and hide the address.

### Is Arabic SEO worth it for a small business in Dubai?

For many businesses, yes, especially if Arabic-speaking customers are part of your market. Arabic searches often face less competition from small businesses. The key is reviewed translation and separate keyword research, not machine translation alone.

### How do I get more Google reviews in the UAE?

Ask every satisfied customer soon after the visit, with a direct link by WhatsApp or email, only to people who agreed to hear from you. Reply to every review. Never offer rewards for reviews, as Google's policies prohibit it.

### Can I send WhatsApp promotions to all my customers?

Only to people who opted in to messages from you. WhatsApp requires opt-in and respecting opt-outs, and UAE telemarketing rules also cover marketing messages sent through social apps. Keep promotions relevant, occasional and easy to stop.

### Do I need a permit to run a sale or giveaway in Dubai?

Sales promotions, discounts and prize draws in Dubai fall under the Department of Economy and Tourism, and permits have traditionally been required. Check the current requirement with DET before announcing anything. Other emirates have their own authorities.

### Does the UAE PDPL apply to my business?

The federal PDPL applies to most mainland and free zone businesses handling personal data. Companies in DIFC or ADGM follow their own data protection laws instead. Its executive regulations were still pending at the time of writing, but its consent and security principles already apply.

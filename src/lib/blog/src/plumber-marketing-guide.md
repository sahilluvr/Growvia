Plumbing is the most urgent trade in home services. A homeowner with a burst supply line or a sewage backup doesn't compare five quotes. They search, call the first company that looks trustworthy and hire whoever picks up. That makes **plumber marketing** different from almost any other local business: speed and visibility in the next 30 minutes matter more than brand awareness over the next year.

But emergencies are only half the business. The steadier money is in water heaters, drain maintenance, repiping and the customers who call you every time because you were there when it mattered.

This guide covers both. You'll learn how emergency searches work, how to set up Local Services Ads and your Google Business Profile, which service pages to build first, how to be open about pricing, how to get a review after every job and how to sell a maintenance membership. ## How people search for a plumber

Plumbing searches fall into three groups, and each needs a different response.

| Search type | Examples | What the customer needs | Where you win it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency | "emergency plumber near me", "burst pipe", "sewer backup" | Someone on the phone now and a truck soon | Local Services Ads, Map Pack, call button |
| Urgent but plannable | "water heater not working", "toilet keeps running", "slow drain" | A same-day or next-day slot and a sense of price | Map Pack, service pages, online booking |
| Research | "tankless vs tank water heater", "repipe cost", "what is a backflow test" | Clear answers and a company they trust | Organic content, AI assistants, email follow-up |

Emergency searches are almost all on phones, and the customer calls rather than fills in a form. Research searches happen on laptops in the evening and often lead to the biggest tickets: water heater replacements, repipes and sewer lines.

::flow An emergency plumbing search, start to finish | "Burst pipe" search on a phone | Ad or Map Pack listing | Call answered in 3 rings | Truck dispatched | Review link sent after the job

If any step breaks, you lose the job. The most common break is the third: the call goes to voicemail because the owner is under a sink. ## Set up your Google Business Profile for plumbing

Your Business Profile decides whether you show in the Map Pack. For plumbers, the details matter.

### Pick the right categories

Your primary category should be **Plumber**. Add secondary categories only for services you genuinely offer as a business line. Depending on your work, options can include water heater installation or repair, drainage service, septic system service or emergency services categories if Google shows them in your region. Category names change over time, so search the category list in your profile rather than copying one from a competitor. Google's [help page on business categories](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669) explains how they work.

### Hide your address if customers don't visit

Most plumbers run from a home or a yard. Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say service-area businesses that don't serve customers at their address should hide it and set a service area. Never rent a virtual office to fake a location in a bigger city. Suspensions are common and painful to reverse.

### List your services with real descriptions

Fill in the services section with your actual offerings: water heater repair, tankless installation, drain cleaning, hydro jetting, leak detection, sewer camera inspection, repiping, fixture installation, gas line work if licensed for it. A sentence per service is enough. Add your hours honestly. If you offer 24/7 emergency service, say so only if someone really answers at 2 a.m.

::check Weekly Business Profile routine for a plumbing company | Post 2–3 photos from real jobs (before and after, with no customer addresses visible) | Answer every new review | Add one Google post about a seasonal issue | Check that hours and holiday hours are correct | Answer any questions in the Q&A section

## Local Services Ads for plumbers

Plumbing is one of the core Local Services Ads categories. These ads show at the top of results with a **Google Verified** badge, your rating and a call button. You pay per lead rather than per click.

To qualify, you go through Google's screening, which for plumbers typically includes a business background check, license verification where your state or city licenses plumbers, and proof of insurance. The exact requirements vary by state; Google's [screening and verification page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12174778?hl=en) lists them.

### What makes LSA work for plumbers

- **Answer every lead.** Google lists responsiveness as a ranking factor and says missed calls may hurt it.
- **Choose job types carefully.** Turn off job types you don't want (for example, septic if you don't do it). Paying for leads you'll turn down wastes budget.
- **Set your hours to match your answering.** If you're not answering at night, don't run at night.
- **Dispute bad leads.** Wrong-trade calls, spam and out-of-area calls can be flagged through lead feedback. Google may credit some, but don't count on it.
- **Build reviews.** Your rating and review count affect where you appear.

Our guide to [Google Local Services Ads for home services](/blog/google-local-services-ads-home-services) covers budgets, disputes and how LSA fits with Search ads.

> **Watch out:** Google replaced the old Google Guaranteed badge with Google Verified in late 2025 and discontinued the money-back guarantee, according to its [About Google Verified page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/9376654?hl=en). Remove any "Google Guaranteed" claims from your trucks, website and scripts.

## Build the service pages that bring in money

Many plumbing websites have a home page, an about page and one "Services" page with a list. That's not enough to rank for "water heater replacement" or "hydro jetting" in your town. Each money service needs its own page.

### Which pages to build first

Start with the services that bring the most revenue or the most calls. For most residential plumbers, that order looks something like this:

1. **Water heater repair and replacement** (tank and tankless, gas and electric).
2. **Drain cleaning** (kitchen, bathroom, main line) and **hydro jetting**.
3. **Emergency plumbing** (burst pipes, active leaks, no water).
4. **Sewer line repair and camera inspection.**
5. **Leak detection** (slab leaks if common in your area).
6. **Repiping** (especially where older galvanized or polybutylene pipe is common).

### What goes on each page

A good water heater page answers the questions a homeowner has while standing in a wet garage:

- **Signs it's time:** no hot water, rusty water, rumbling, a leak from the tank.
- **Repair or replace:** your honest rule of thumb based on age, warranty and the problem.
- **Options:** tank vs tankless, gas vs electric, sizing for household size.
- **Price guidance:** a range or the factors that move the price (see below).
- **Permits and code:** many cities require a permit for water heater replacement. Say so; it shows you do things properly.
- **Proof:** photos of your installs, brands you carry, your license number, reviews that mention water heaters.
- **One clear action:** a call button and a short booking form.

## Be open about pricing

Homeowners are nervous about plumbing bills. They've heard stories about $900 drain clears. Being open about how you price is one of the easiest ways to stand out.

You don't have to publish a full price book. Choose the level that fits your business:

- **Flat diagnostic or trip fee,** stated clearly, and whether it goes toward the repair.
- **"Starting at" prices** for common jobs like a standard drain clear or a toilet rebuild.
- **Price factors** for big jobs: "Water heater replacement price depends on tank size, fuel type, venting, code upgrades like expansion tanks and haul-away."
- **Upfront pricing promise:** "You'll get the price before we start, and it won't change unless we find something new and you approve it."

::compare How pricing language changes the call | Before: "Call for a quote"; vague about trip fees; customer fears a surprise bill; more price-shopping calls | After: flat diagnostic fee stated; "starting at" prices for common jobs; written price before work starts; callers arrive already comfortable

If your state or city has rules about advertised prices, emergency surcharges or estimates, follow them. Check your state's current rules and your licensing board's guidance.

## Turn every job into a review

Reviews decide which of the three Map Pack plumbers gets the call. They also feed your LSA ranking. The plumbers with the most reviews almost always have one thing in common: the tech asks, every time.

### The ask, at the end of the job

Train your techs to say something like this (adapt it to your voice):

::chat The review ask that techs actually use | you: All done. The new shut-off valve is in, and I tested every fixture. Any questions? | them: No, that's great. Thanks for coming out so fast. | you: Glad we could help. Would you mind leaving us a quick Google review? I'll send the link to your email in a few minutes. It really helps a small company like ours. | them: Sure, happy to.

Then send the link within an hour, while the relief is fresh. Your field service software (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro) can usually send it by text with the invoice. Growvia can send it by email from your own mailbox and include it in your job follow-up sequence. If you text review requests from a marketing tool, you need proper consent and 10DLC registration; Growvia doesn't send texts.

### Stay inside the rules

Ask every customer, not only the happy ones. Google's policies ban review gating, and the FTC's [Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/rule-consumer-reviews-testimonials) bans fake reviews, buying reviews, and reviews from employees or family without disclosure. Don't offer a discount in exchange for a review. Our guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) has more scripts and templates.

### Reply to every review

A short reply to each review shows future customers you care. For positive reviews, thank them and mention the service ("Glad the new water heater is working well"). For negative ones, stay calm, don't argue facts in public, and offer to call. Avoid sharing details about the customer's home or job in a reply.

## Sell a plumbing maintenance membership

Emergencies are unpredictable. Memberships smooth out the slow months, raise customer lifetime value and make your company the first call when something goes wrong.

### What a plumbing membership can include

There's no standard package. Common ingredients:

- An annual whole-home plumbing inspection.
- A yearly water heater flush and anode check.
- Priority scheduling, especially in emergencies.
- A discount on repairs (state a clear percentage).
- Waived or reduced trip fees.
- Drain maintenance for homes with recurring problems.

Price it so the benefits are obvious and the inspection itself generates repair work you'd otherwise miss.

### How to market it

Offer the membership at three moments: right after an emergency repair ("this is how you avoid the next one"), when you replace a water heater ("we'll flush it every year to protect your warranty") and in a yearly email to past customers.

Email works well because you can explain the value in a few paragraphs. A simple sequence:

1. **Day 1 after the job:** thank you, review link, warranty details.
2. **Day 30:** "3 signs your water heater needs attention," with a soft mention of the membership.
3. **Month 11:** reminder that their yearly water heater flush is due, with a booking link.
4. **Each spring:** a seasonal tip email (EPA runs its [Fix a Leak Week](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/fix-leak-week) every March, a natural hook for a leak-check offer).

Every marketing email needs an honest subject line, your physical address and a working unsubscribe, per the [FTC's CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business).

## Common plumber marketing mistakes

- **Advertising 24/7 service you can't staff.** One unanswered 2 a.m. call is a lost job and a bad review.
- **Keyword-stuffed business names** like "Emergency Plumber Dallas 24 Hour". Google can suspend them.
- **One generic services page** instead of real pages per service.
- **Hiding all pricing,** which makes nervous callers shop around.
- **No follow-up after the invoice.** The customer forgets you until they search again.
- **Stock photos of smiling models in hard hats.** Real job photos build more trust.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best way to market a plumbing business?

Answer every call fast, build a strong Google Business Profile with steady reviews, run Local Services Ads for emergency work and create a dedicated page for each money service. Then email past customers to sell maintenance memberships.

### Are Local Services Ads good for plumbers?

They're often a strong channel because plumbing searches are urgent and phone-first, and you pay per lead. Results depend on answering quickly and keeping a good rating.

### Should plumbers show prices on their website?

You don't need a full price list, but showing your diagnostic fee, "starting at" prices for common jobs and the factors behind big-job pricing builds trust and filters out price shoppers.

### How do plumbers get more Google reviews?

Have the tech ask in person at the end of every job, then send the review link within an hour. Ask every customer, never offer rewards for reviews and reply to each one.

### What should a plumbing membership include?

Most include a yearly inspection, a water heater flush, priority scheduling and a repair discount. Keep the benefits simple enough to explain in one sentence.

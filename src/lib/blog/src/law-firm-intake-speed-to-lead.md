Picture a Tuesday evening. Someone's brother was arrested two hours ago. They search "criminal defense lawyer near me," call the first firm and get voicemail. They call the second firm and get voicemail again. The third firm answers, asks four calm questions and books a consultation for 8 a.m. The first two firms will hear the voicemail tomorrow morning and call back a person who has already hired a lawyer.

That's law firm intake in one scene. Many potential clients contact several firms and hire the first one that responds well. You can spend thousands on SEO and ads and still lose the case in the five minutes after the phone rings.

This guide shows you how to build an intake process that answers fast, qualifies properly, protects you on conflicts and follows up until there's a clear yes or no. ## Why speed to lead matters so much in legal work

Legal problems tend to be urgent, stressful and unfamiliar. A person facing an arrest, an eviction, a serious injury or a custody filing wants reassurance that someone competent is on it, now. That changes how they shop:

- **They contact several firms in a short window.** Comparing three or four firms in one evening is normal.
- **They hire the first firm that feels safe.** Being first and being reassuring usually beats being the most impressive.
- **They rarely wait for callbacks.** Even a few hours can be too long for urgent matters.

You don't need a study to see this in your own firm. Pull last month's inquiries, note how long each one took to get a human response, and compare the response times of the ones that signed with the ones that didn't.

::funnel Example: how slow response leaks cases (illustrative month, 2-attorney firm) | Inquiries received: 120 | Reached by a human the same day: 70 | Consultations booked: 32 | Consultations held: 26 | Signed: 11

In this made-up example, 50 people never spoke to anyone at the firm on the day they reached out. That's the cheapest place to find new cases, because the marketing cost has already been paid.

## Map your intake process first

Before fixing anything, write down what actually happens today. Most firms find gaps in an afternoon.

For each channel, answer:

1. **Phone during business hours.** Who answers? What happens when they're busy or at lunch?
2. **Phone after hours and weekends.** Voicemail, answering service, forwarded cell or nothing?
3. **Web forms.** Where do they go? Who checks them, and how often?
4. **Email to general addresses.** Who owns the info@ inbox?
5. **Chat, WhatsApp, Facebook and Instagram messages.** Is anyone watching them?
6. **Google Business Profile and Local Services Ads leads.** Do they reach the same person?
7. **Referrals from other lawyers.** Are they logged anywhere, or do they live in one attorney's inbox?

Then call your own firm at 12:30 p.m., 5:45 p.m. and 8 p.m., and submit your own web form on a Saturday. What you experience is what your prospects experience.

## Build coverage: phones, forms and messages

### Phone coverage during business hours

The goal is simple: a trained human answers live. Options include a dedicated intake specialist, a receptionist with a clear script, or paralegals on a rotation. If the attorney is in court, the call still gets answered.

### After-hours coverage

Criminal defense, personal injury and family law inquiries often arrive in the evening and on weekends. Options, roughly from least to most coverage:

| Option | How it works | Good fit for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voicemail with a callback promise | Message states when you'll call back | Transactional and business law | Urgent clients won't wait |
| Forwarding to an attorney's cell | Calls ring through after hours | Solo criminal defense | Burnout, inconsistent answers |
| Legal answering service | Live agents take messages or follow a script | Most small firms | Script quality, how leads are handed off |
| Virtual receptionist with intake | Agents qualify and book consultations | PI, criminal, family firms with volume | Cost per call; needs clear rules |
| In-house evening intake shift | Your own staff covers set hours | Larger firms | Staffing cost |

Virtual receptionist services such as Ruby and Smith.ai are popular with small firms, and some legal-specific answering services also exist. Whichever you use, give them a short written script, your conflict-check questions and clear rules on what not to discuss.

### Web forms that answer back

A web form that sends an email to a shared inbox and nothing else is a slow lead. Every submission should trigger:

- **An instant auto-reply** confirming you got it, saying when a human will contact them, and giving a phone number for urgent matters.
- **An internal alert** to the person responsible for intake, not just a shared inbox.
- **A record in your pipeline** with the source, so you can measure it later.

Keep the form short: name, phone, email, a practice-area dropdown, a brief description field and consent wording if you plan to text them. Add a line asking people not to include confidential details until you've spoken. ## Conflict checks and what not to ask yet

Speed can't come at the cost of conflicts. Under ABA Model Rule 1.18, a person who consults you about possibly hiring you is a prospective client, and you owe them duties even if you never take the case. If intake collects detailed information from someone whose interests are adverse to an existing client, the firm can end up with a disqualification problem.

A safe intake sequence:

1. **Collect only what you need for a conflict check first:** the caller's name, the names of other parties (spouse, other driver, employer, business), and the general matter type.
2. **Run the conflict check** in your practice-management system before discussing details.
3. **Only then** gather the facts needed to decide whether to take the matter.

Train intake staff to say, "Before you tell me the details, I need a couple of names so I can make sure we're able to help you." Read the [text of Model Rule 1.18](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/rule_1_18_duties_of_prospective_client/) and your state's version. States vary in how they handle screening and disqualification.

> Never let intake staff or a marketing tool give legal advice. Intake gathers facts, checks conflicts and books consultations. The attorney advises.

## Intake scripts that sound human

Scripts keep quality consistent across staff, answering services and late nights. They shouldn't sound robotic. Here's a structure for a first call.

1. **Greet and reassure.** "Thanks for calling Rivera Law. I'm Ana. I can help you get this started."
2. **Get contact details first,** in case the call drops.
3. **Conflict names.** "Before you share details, who else is involved?"
4. **Basic qualification.** Practice area, location, key dates (such as an arrest date, accident date or court date).
5. **Explain next steps and fees plainly.** Consultation length, cost if any, and who they'll speak with.
6. **Book on the call.** Offer two specific times.
7. **Confirm in writing,** by email or text if they consented.

::chat First call for a hypothetical DUI inquiry | them: My husband was arrested last night for DUI. I don't know what to do. | you: I'm sorry, that's a stressful night. I can help get things started. Can I get your name and best number in case we're disconnected? | them: Sure, it's Priya, 602-555-0148. | you: Thanks, Priya. Before you share details, what's your husband's full name, and was anyone else involved? | them: Just him. His name is Arjun. | you: Thank you. Mr. Cole handles DUI cases and has openings at 9 or 11 tomorrow morning. Which works better?

Notice what's missing: no promises about outcomes, no opinion on the case and no fee quote the attorney hasn't approved.

## Follow-up: most firms quit too early

Many inquiries don't book on the first contact. They're comparing firms, waiting on a spouse or simply busy. A clear cadence fixes this.

| When | Channel | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| Within 5 minutes | Phone, then auto-reply email | Introduce yourself and offer consultation times |
| Same day, 2–3 hours later | Phone and email | Short follow-up with direct booking link or number |
| Next morning | Phone | "Wanted to make sure you got our message" |
| Day 3 | Email | What to expect at a consultation, what to bring |
| Day 7 | Phone and email | Check-in: still need help? |
| Day 14 | Email | "Closing the loop," the door stays open |
| Monthly | Email (if they agreed) | Useful, general content relevant to their matter type |

Two rules keep follow-up compliant. First, email must follow CAN-SPAM, including a physical address and a working unsubscribe, as the [FTC's CAN-SPAM compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) explains. Second, marketing texts require prior express written consent under the TCPA, and texting from a local number needs 10DLC registration. Our [SMS and TCPA guide](/blog/sms-marketing-tcpa-10dlc-guide) has the details. Follow-up should never cross into the kind of pressure your state's solicitation rules prohibit, and stop when someone says no.

::steps A consultation no-show recovery sequence | Within 15 minutes — call, then email with two new times | Same evening — short email acknowledging things come up | Day 2 — final call attempt | Day 7 — email with a direct booking link | Day 30 — add to monthly general newsletter if they agreed

## Common intake mistakes

- **Letting the attorney be the only intake person.** Court days become lost-lead days.
- **A form that only goes to info@.** Route it to a named person with an alert.
- **No after-hours plan.** Evenings and weekends are when many urgent matters start.
- **Gathering the whole story before the conflict check.** Names first, details second.
- **One voicemail and done.** Follow a cadence for at least two weeks.
- **No source tracking.** You can't improve marketing you can't measure.

## Frequently asked questions

### How fast should a law firm respond to a new lead?

As fast as possible: a live answer by phone, and a human follow-up within minutes for web forms during business hours. After hours, an instant auto-reply plus an answering service or a clear next-morning callback is the minimum.

### What questions should intake ask before a conflict check?

Only what's needed to run the check: the caller's name and contact details, the names of other people or businesses involved and the general type of matter. Hold detailed facts until the check is clear.

### Is a legal answering service worth it for a small firm?

For practice areas with urgent, after-hours inquiries, such as criminal defense, PI and family law, it often pays for itself if it saves even a few cases a year. Give the service a written script and clear limits on what it can say.

### How many times should a law firm follow up with a lead?

A reasonable cadence is several touches over about two weeks across phone and email, then a monthly general email if the person agreed to receive it. Stop when someone says no, and keep within your state's solicitation rules.

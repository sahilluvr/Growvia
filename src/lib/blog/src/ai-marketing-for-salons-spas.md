Most salons and spas don't have a marketing problem. They have a leaky bucket. New clients come in from Instagram or Google Maps, love their service, and then drift to another salon because nobody booked their next visit, nobody followed up, and nobody gave them a reason to come back before the urge to try somewhere new kicked in.

This guide lays out an all-in-one AI marketing system for salons and spas that fixes the bucket first and then fills it. It covers rebooking and retention, Google reviews, your Google Business Profile, Instagram, online booking, gift cards, memberships, stylist personal brands, AI search, and the tools that run it all. You'll finish with a 90-day plan and the handful of numbers worth tracking each week.

It's written for hair salons, barbershops, nail studios, lash and brow bars, day spas and massage studios. If you offer injectables, lasers or other medical treatments, read our [med spa marketing guide](/blog/med-spa-marketing-guide) as well, because medical services come with extra rules.

## How a salon actually grows

A salon's revenue is mostly a function of three things: how many new clients you win, how often existing clients come back, and how much they spend per visit. Marketing touches all three, but not equally.

A new client costs time and money to find. A returning client costs almost nothing and usually spends more, because they trust you enough to add a treatment or buy retail. That's why this guide starts with the clients you already have.

::flow The salon growth loop | Get found on Maps and Instagram | Book online in under a minute | Great service plus rebook at checkout | Review and referral | Return visits and memberships

Each step feeds the next. An "AI marketing system" just means using software to keep each step running when you're too busy doing hair to think about marketing.

## Step 1: Fix rebooking and retention first

If you do one thing from this guide, make rebooking at checkout a habit for every service provider. A client who leaves with their next appointment on the calendar is far more likely to return than one who says "I'll text you."

We cover this in depth in our guide to [salon client retention and rebooking](/blog/salon-rebooking-client-retention), including the exact chair-side script, reminder timing by service type and a lapsed-client win-back sequence. Here's the short version.

### The three numbers to know

| Metric | How to calculate it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Rebooking rate | Clients who book their next visit before leaving ÷ total clients served | The single best predictor of a full book next month |
| First-visit retention | New clients who return within 90 days ÷ new clients | Shows whether your first impression works |
| Average visit interval | Average days between visits, by service | Tells you when to send reminders and when a client is "lapsed" |

Most salon booking software (Vagaro, Boulevard, GlossGenius, Mindbody, Square Appointments, Fresha and others) can report rebooking and retention by provider. Pull it monthly. Gaps between stylists are usually about habit, not talent.

The fastest fix is one shared rebooking line that offers two specific times: "To keep this color looking fresh, you'll want to come back in about six weeks. I have Tuesday the 14th at 5 or Thursday the 16th at 6. Which works better?"

> Key takeaway: a 10-point lift in rebooking rate usually does more for revenue than doubling your Instagram followers. Fix retention before you spend a dollar on new-client ads.

## Step 2: Win Google Maps and reviews

When someone searches "hair salon near me," "balayage specialist" or "massage open now," Google shows a map pack of three businesses before any website. Your Google Business Profile (GBP) decides whether you're in it.

Google says local rankings are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and its [local ranking help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) notes that review count and rating are part of prominence. You can't move your salon closer to every searcher, but you can make the profile more relevant and more trusted.

### GBP setup for salons and spas

- **Primary category.** Pick the one that matches most of your revenue: Hair salon, Beauty salon, Nail salon, Day spa, Massage spa, Barber shop, Eyebrow bar or Eyelash salon. Add secondary categories only for services you really offer.
- **Services list.** Add each service with a short description and a "from" price if you're comfortable. Clients compare prices before they call.
- **Booking link.** Add your online booking URL as the appointment link. Many booking platforms also integrate with Reserve with Google, which can put a "Book" button right on your profile. Check your platform's settings.
- **Photos.** Upload real photos of your space, your team and your work, refreshed monthly. Stock photos do nothing for trust.
- **Hours and holiday hours.** Wrong hours on a Saturday morning cost real bookings and generate angry reviews.

Our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide) walks through every field, and the [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-small-business) covers your website and directory listings.

### Getting more reviews, the right way

Salons have a natural review moment: the client is looking in the mirror and loves the result. Ask then, in person, and follow up with a link the same day.

::chat Asking for a review at the mirror | you: How are you feeling about the color? | them: Honestly I love it, it's exactly what I wanted. | you: That makes my day. Would you mind saying that on Google? I'll text you the link with your receipt. | them: Of course, happy to.

Two rules keep you safe. First, ask every client, not only the ones you think are happy. Google's policies ban "review gating," which means filtering who gets asked based on how they feel. Second, don't offer discounts or freebies in exchange for reviews. [Google's Maps content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) prohibits incentives for reviews, and the [FTC's Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers), effective October 21, 2024, bans buying reviews, conditioning incentives on positive reviews, posting fake reviews, and suppressing negative ones.

Our guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) has templates for texts, emails and counter cards.

### Replying to reviews

Reply to every review within a few days. For positive ones, thank the client and mention the service in natural words ("so glad you love your new curtain bangs"). That helps future searchers and reinforces what you're known for.

For negative ones, stay calm, keep it short, and move the conversation offline: "We're sorry your visit didn't feel right. We'd love the chance to fix it. Please call or message us and ask for the manager." Never argue details in public.

## Step 3: Make Instagram book appointments, not just likes

For salons, Instagram is a portfolio, a word-of-mouth engine and a booking channel in one. The trap is treating it like a magazine: beautiful posts, lots of likes, and no clear path to a booked appointment.

Our full [salon Instagram marketing guide](/blog/salon-instagram-marketing) covers content pillars, reels, consent for client photos and turning DMs into bookings. The essentials:

### Five content pillars that work for salons

1. **Transformations.** Before and after, with the client's written consent. This is your proof.
2. **Process.** Short videos of a blowout, a gloss, a lash fill or a massage room being set up. People love watching skilled hands.
3. **Education.** "Why your blonde turns brassy," "how often to get a facial," "gel vs dip." These get saved and shared.
4. **People.** Introduce each stylist or therapist: their specialty, their story, what they love doing.
5. **Offers and availability.** "Two openings Thursday afternoon" posts and stories fill gaps fast.

### Turn followers into bookings

- Put your booking link in your bio and use Instagram's action button for booking if your platform supports it.
- Answer DMs within the hour during business hours. A slow reply sends the client to the next salon in their feed.
- Save quick replies for common questions: price ranges, how long a service takes, whether you need a consultation.
- Tag your location on every post and mention your neighborhood in captions. Local discovery matters more than viral reach.

::check Instagram profile checklist for salons | Booking link in bio and action button | Name field includes your service and city | Highlights for services, prices, team and reviews | Pinned posts showing your best work | Location tagged on every post | DMs answered within an hour

## Step 4: Make online booking frictionless

A client who finds you at 11 p.m. on a Sunday should be able to book without calling. If your only option is "call during business hours," you're losing the people who plan ahead and the people who hate phone calls.

### What good online booking looks like

- **Three taps to a time slot.** Service, provider (or "any"), time. Don't force account creation before showing availability.
- **Clear service names and durations.** "Full balayage with toner, 3 hours" beats "Color service B."
- **Deposits or card-on-file for long services.** A no-show on a four-hour color appointment is expensive. Most booking platforms support deposits and cancellation fees. State your policy clearly at booking.
- **New-client consultations.** For complex color, extensions or corrective work, offer a free 15-minute consult so clients aren't scared off by not knowing what to book.
- **A booking button on every page of your website**, plus a contact form for questions. Our guide to [website contact forms that convert](/blog/website-contact-form-best-practices) covers the form side.

## Step 5: Gift cards and memberships for steady cash flow

Salons and spas are seasonal. December, Mother's Day and Valentine's Day bring gift card rushes, and January and late summer can feel quiet. Gift cards and memberships smooth that out.

### Gift cards

Promote gift cards from early November through December, again before Valentine's Day and Mother's Day, and around graduation season. Make them easy to buy online, at any hour, and deliverable by email.

Know the rules. Under the federal Credit CARD Act, store gift cards generally can't expire within five years of the date they were issued or last loaded, and dormancy or inactivity fees are heavily restricted. The federal rule is in the CFPB's [Regulation E, section 1005.20](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/20/), and many states, including California, add stricter ones. Check your state's current rules before printing an expiration date.

Make sure gift card recipients become clients in your booking system so you can welcome them properly.

### Memberships

Memberships work best for services clients need on a predictable cycle: monthly massages, facials, blowouts, lash fills, men's cuts or brow maintenance.

| Membership design choice | Option A | Option B | Tip |
|---|---|---|---|
| What's included | One set service per month | Credit that can be used on any service | Set services are simpler to explain and price |
| Price | Discount on the regular service price | Same price plus perks (priority booking, retail discount) | Perks protect your margin better than deep discounts |
| Unused visits | Roll over for a limited time | Expire monthly | Rollovers feel fairer and reduce cancellations |
| Cancellation | Online or by email, any time after a minimum term | Requires notice | Make it easy, and follow your state's auto-renewal rules |

Recurring memberships are subscriptions, so state automatic renewal laws apply. California, New York and many other states require clear disclosure of renewal terms, affirmative consent, and an easy way to cancel. Treat cancellation as part of the client experience, not a trap. Check your state's current rules or ask a local attorney.

## Step 6: Build your stylists' personal brands, without losing clients

In salons, clients often follow a person, not a business. That makes some owners nervous about promoting individual stylists. The better approach is to build stylist brands inside your salon brand.

- Give each provider a team page on your website with their specialties, a photo and a booking link that goes straight to their calendar.
- Feature each stylist on your main Instagram account at least monthly, and encourage them to repost to their own accounts.
- Ask clients to mention their stylist by name in reviews. "Ask for Maya" in fifty reviews is powerful social proof, and it ranks your profile for the services Maya is known for.
- Agree in writing, with legal help, on how client lists and social accounts are handled if someone leaves. Booth renters and commission employees have different arrangements, so get this right for your model.

## Step 7: Email and messaging that keep clients coming back

Your booking software sends reminders. Marketing messages do something different: they bring clients back before they think about it, introduce new services, and fill slow days.

### The messages every salon should automate

1. **Welcome after a first visit.** Thank them, introduce their stylist, explain how to rebook and how your loyalty or membership program works.
2. **Rebook reminder.** Timed to the service interval: about 4 weeks for men's cuts and lash fills, 6 to 8 weeks for color, and so on. Adjust to your own data.
3. **Lapsed client win-back.** At 1.5 times their usual interval, a friendly "we miss you" with their stylist's next openings.
4. **Birthday or anniversary note.** A small perk that doesn't require a review or anything else in return.
5. **Seasonal campaigns.** Holiday gift cards, summer hair care, wedding season, back-to-school.

::steps A new client's first 90 days (example sequence) | Day 0 — thank-you email with stylist name and rebooking link | Day 2 — review request with direct Google link | Day 21 — care tips for their service | Day 35 — rebook reminder with two suggested times | Day 75 — "we miss you" note if they haven't returned

Keep marketing emails honest and legal. Under [CAN-SPAM](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business), use a truthful From name and subject line, include your physical address, and honor unsubscribes within 10 business days. Our guides to [building an email welcome sequence](/blog/email-marketing-welcome-sequence) and [email deliverability](/blog/email-deliverability-spf-dkim-dmarc-guide) cover the setup.

Texting works well for salons, but marketing texts need prior express written consent under the TCPA, plus 10DLC registration if you text from a local number. Our [SMS marketing and TCPA guide](/blog/sms-marketing-tcpa-10dlc-guide) explains what's required. Appointment reminders through your booking software are a separate, transactional use.

## Step 8: Show up in AI search

More clients are asking ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity things like "best curly hair salon in Austin" or "relaxing day spa near downtown Chicago for a birthday." These assistants pull from your website, your Google profile, review sites and local articles.

To show up:

- Describe your specialties clearly and consistently on your website, GBP and social profiles. "Curly hair specialists trained in dry cutting" gives an AI something concrete to repeat.
- Build a page for each signature service, with what it is, who it's for, how long it takes and a price range.
- Encourage reviews that mention specific services and stylists.
- Get listed in local "best of" roundups, wedding directories and neighborhood guides where it makes sense.

## Know your local market

Competition varies enormously by area. A salon in a dense, image-conscious market like [Orange County](/local-marketing/orange-county-ca) or [Los Angeles](/local-marketing/los-angeles-county-ca) will face a crowded map pack and high Instagram standards. In [Miami-Dade](/local-marketing/miami-dade-county-fl), Spanish-language posts and replies can be a real advantage. In [Brooklyn](/local-marketing/kings-county-ny), your realistic radius may be a few neighborhoods, so neighborhood names matter in your profile and captions.

## Your 90-day salon marketing plan

::steps 90-day plan at a glance | Weeks 1–2 — fix GBP, booking link, review process | Weeks 3–4 — rebooking script and reminder settings | Month 2 — Instagram pillars, DM replies, welcome and win-back emails | Month 3 — membership or gift card push, AI search pages, review the numbers

### Days 1–14: foundations

- Audit your Google Business Profile: categories, services, hours, booking link, photos.
- Run an SEO audit on your website and fix the top three issues (page speed and mobile layout are common).
- Set up your Google review link and QR code. Put the QR code at each station and the front desk.
- Pull your current rebooking rate and first-visit retention by provider.

### Days 15–30: retention

- Train the whole team on one rebooking script. Role-play it at a team meeting.
- Check your booking software's reminder and confirmation settings.
- Pull a list of lapsed clients and send a simple personal win-back email.
- Start replying to every review, new and old.

### Days 31–60: Instagram and automation

- Plan four weeks of posts across your five content pillars, batch-shoot content, and schedule.
- Set up quick replies for DMs and assign someone to check messages at set times each day.
- Turn on your first-visit welcome email and lapsed-client sequence.
- Collect written photo consent as part of your intake or consultation form.

### Days 61–90: grow revenue per client

- Launch or relaunch a membership for one or two predictable services.
- Plan your next gift card season and build the landing page now.
- Write or improve pages for your three signature services, with FAQs.
- Check whether AI assistants mention your salon for your top services, and note which competitors appear.
- Compare your numbers to day one.

## Common mistakes salons make

- **Discounting to get new clients, then not rebooking them.** Pair any intro offer with a strong rebooking routine.
- **Ignoring the Google profile.** Many salons put everything into Instagram and leave GBP with old photos and wrong hours.
- **Asking only happy clients for reviews.** It breaks Google's rules and makes your rating look less believable.
- **No clear cancellation policy.** Unclear deposits and fees create no-shows and bad reviews.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best marketing strategy for a salon?

Start with retention: rebook clients at checkout and follow up with lapsed clients. Then build your Google Business Profile and reviews, and use Instagram to show your work and drive bookings. New-client ads work best once your rebooking habit is solid.

### How can AI help with salon marketing?

AI tools can draft Instagram captions and images, write review replies, suggest email copy, audit your website and track how you rank on Google Maps and in AI assistants. They save time, but you should still review everything before it goes out, especially anything about prices or specific clients.

### Do I need written consent to post client photos?

Get written consent before posting any recognizable client photo, including before-and-after shots. A simple checkbox or signature on your intake form, with an option to say no, protects you and builds trust.

### Can I offer a discount for Google reviews?

No. Google prohibits incentives for reviews, and the FTC's review rule bans conditioning incentives on positive reviews. Ask every client honestly and make the review link easy to find.

### How do I get more salon clients from Google Maps?

Choose the right primary category, list your services with prices, add a booking link, upload fresh photos, and earn reviews steadily. Reply to every review and keep your hours accurate.

### Should my salon offer memberships?

Memberships work well for services clients need on a regular cycle, such as massages, facials, blowouts and lash fills. Keep the terms simple, make cancellation easy, and follow your state's automatic renewal rules.

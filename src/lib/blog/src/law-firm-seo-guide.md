When someone types "divorce lawyer near me" or "Phoenix DUI attorney," they see a few ads, a map with three firms and then a list of websites. Most callers never scroll past that first screen. Law firm SEO is the work of earning a place on it without paying for every click.

The good news for small firms is that local legal search rewards relevance and trust more than budget. A two-attorney practice with a well-built site, a correct Google Business Profile and steady reviews can outrank a bigger firm that ignores the basics. This guide shows you how to build each piece, in the order that tends to pay off fastest.

## How law firm SEO works: two results, two strategies

Legal searches usually show two kinds of free results, and each one ranks differently.

| | Google Maps (local pack) | Organic results |
|---|---|---|
| What ranks | Your Google Business Profile | Pages on your website |
| Main factors | Relevance, distance, prominence (reviews, links, citations) | Content relevance, page quality, links, technical health |
| Best for | "Near me" and city searches with urgent intent | Questions, practice-area research, nearby towns |
| What you control most | Categories, profile completeness, reviews, photos | Practice-area pages, bios, articles, site speed |
| Time to see movement | Often weeks after fixes | Often months for competitive terms |

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and explains each factor on its [local ranking help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). You can't change where a searcher is standing. You can make Google understand exactly what you do and see clear signals that people trust you.

::flow What Google needs to rank a law firm locally | Understand your practice areas | Confirm your location and details are consistent | See trust signals from reviews and links | Match your pages to the searcher's question

## Step 1: Set up your Google Business Profile correctly

Your profile is the single most important local SEO asset for most firms. Our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide) covers every field. Here is what matters most for lawyers.

### Choose categories that match real practice areas

Your primary category carries the most weight. Choose the one that matches the work you most want, not the broadest option. Google offers specific legal categories such as "Personal injury attorney," "Family law attorney," "Criminal justice attorney," "DUI attorney," "Estate planning attorney," "Bankruptcy attorney," "Immigration attorney" and "Employment attorney," alongside general ones like "Lawyer" and "Law firm."

Add secondary categories only for practice areas you actually handle. Adding ten loosely related categories dilutes relevance and invites edits or suspensions.

### Follow Google's rules for names and addresses

Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) are strict, and law firms are frequent offenders:

- **Use your real firm name.** "Garcia Law Group" is fine. "Garcia Law Group – Car Accident Lawyer Dallas" breaks the rules and is a common reason for suspension.
- **Use a real office.** You need a location where clients can meet you during stated hours. Virtual offices and unstaffed coworking addresses put your profile at risk.
- **Practitioner profiles.** Google allows individual practitioners, such as attorneys, to have their own profiles in some situations. A solo attorney who is the firm's only practitioner should usually keep one profile, under the firm or attorney name.

### Fill everything else in

- Services listed under each category, with short plain-English descriptions.
- Hours, including holiday hours, and whether you take calls after hours.
- Real photos: attorneys, the reception area, the building entrance and parking.
- A short business description that says who you help and where, with no unverifiable claims.
- Posts every week or two: a new article, a firm update, a community event.

::check Weekly Google Business Profile habits for a law firm | Reply to new reviews within a few days without case details | Add one new real photo | Publish a post linking to a useful article | Check for suggested edits from users or Google | Confirm hours are still accurate

## Step 2: Build your site around practice areas and locations

Google ranks pages, not websites. If one page tries to cover personal injury, family law, wills and traffic tickets, it won't rank well for any of them.

### A simple structure that works

- **Home page.** Who you are, where you practice, your main practice areas, a clear phone number and consultation form.
- **One page per practice area.** For example, "Divorce," "Child Custody," "DUI Defense," "Estate Planning."
- **Sub-pages for important case types,** once the main page is solid. A PI firm might add "Truck Accidents" and "Motorcycle Accidents" under its car accident page.
- **One bio page per attorney.**
- **A location page per real office,** with address, map, parking and the courthouses you appear in.
- **A resource section** with articles answering real client questions.

### What a strong practice-area page includes

A thin page says "We handle child custody cases. Call us today." A strong page answers the questions a worried parent actually has:

1. How custody works in your state, in plain English, with the terms the court uses.
2. What the process looks like, step by step, and roughly how long each stage can take.
3. What affects the outcome, and what the client can do now.
4. How your firm approaches these cases and who will handle them.
5. How fees work: consultation cost, flat fee or hourly, retainer.
6. A short FAQ and a clear next step.

That page might run 1,200 to 2,000 words. It's longer because it's useful, not because length itself ranks.

### Location pages without the spam

Location pages work when they contain real local information. If you have one office in Tampa and get clients from Brandon and Plant City, a Brandon page with nothing but the town name swapped in won't help and can hurt.

Better options: mention the areas you serve on your practice-area pages, write about the specific courthouse or local procedures where they differ, and only create a dedicated location page for a real office or an area where you genuinely have something distinct to say.

> A good test for any location page: would a resident of that town learn something they couldn't get from your main practice-area page? If not, don't publish it.

## Step 3: Write attorney bios that prove experience

Google's quality guidelines emphasize experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust (often shortened to E-E-A-T), especially for topics that affect people's money, safety or legal rights. For a law firm, the attorney bio is where that trust is built, and it's also one of the most-visited pages on many firm sites.

A strong bio includes:

- Bar admissions with years, and court admissions where relevant.
- Practice focus, in specific terms. "I represent parents in contested custody cases in Maricopa County" beats "family law."
- Education and relevant prior experience, such as former prosecutor or in-house counsel.
- Professional memberships, published articles and speaking engagements.
- Certifications, stated exactly and only if your state permits the wording you use.
- A real, recent photo and a short personal paragraph.
- Links to articles the attorney wrote on your site.

Every article on your site should name its attorney author and link to the bio. That connects your content to a real, licensed person, which helps readers and search engines.

## Step 4: Get listed in legal directories and citations

Citations are listings of your firm's name, address and phone number on other sites. Consistent citations help Google trust your location data. For law firms, a handful of directories also send real referral traffic and are often cited by AI assistants.

| Directory | Why it matters | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Your state bar's lawyer directory | Official, trusted source of your license status | Check your listing is accurate and complete |
| Avvo | Large consumer-facing lawyer directory | Claim your profile, add practice areas and photo |
| Justia | Lawyer directory and legal information site | Free basic profile |
| Martindale-Hubbell / Lawyers.com | Long-established directory and peer ratings | Claim and update |
| FindLaw | Consumer legal directory | Free basic listing |
| Local bar association | Local referral and credibility | Many offer referral services |
| Chamber of commerce, local business groups | Local relevance and links | Often a membership fee |

Make your name, address and phone match your Google Business Profile exactly, down to "Suite" vs "Ste." Our [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-small-business) explains how to audit and clean up citations.

## Step 5: Earn reviews steadily

Review count, rating and recency influence Maps rankings and, just as importantly, which firm a client calls. Steady reviews beat a burst followed by silence.

Ask every client whose matter has concluded well, by email or in person, with a direct link to your Google review form. Don't offer anything of value in return, don't ask only the clients you expect to be happy and never write reviews on anyone's behalf. Replies must not reveal confidential client information, even when a review is unfair.

## Step 6: Publish content that answers real questions

Your intake team already knows what people ask. Write those questions down for two weeks and you'll have a year of content ideas.

::bars Example: questions an estate planning firm logged in two weeks of calls (illustrative) | Do I need a will or a trust?: 14 * | What happens if I die without a will?: 9 | How much does an estate plan cost?: 8 | Do I need to update my will after moving states?: 5 | What is probate and how long does it take?: 4

Guidelines for legal content that ranks and converts:

- **One question per article,** answered in the first paragraph, then explained.
- **State-specific.** "How is alimony decided in Florida?" beats "How is alimony decided?"
- **Honest about uncertainty.** Say when the answer depends on facts, and invite a consultation.
- **Written or edited by a lawyer,** named as the author.
- **Updated when the law changes,** with a visible "last reviewed" date.

AI tools can draft outlines and first versions quickly. The attorney's job is to add experience, correct anything inaccurate and remove claims your state's rules don't allow. ## Step 7: Handle the technical basics

Most law firm sites don't need advanced technical SEO, but a few problems are common and costly:

- **Slow mobile pages,** often from heavy video headers, chat widgets and tracking scripts. See our guide to [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained).
- **Missing or duplicate title tags,** such as every page titled "Home | Smith Law."
- **Thin or duplicate pages** created by old location-page projects.
- **No structured data.** Schema.org has types such as LegalService and Attorney, plus LocalBusiness properties for address, hours and geo. Our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-for-local-business) shows how to add them.
- **Broken forms.** Test your consultation form from a phone every month.

## The ethics of SEO claims: "expert," "specialist" and "best"

This is where law firm SEO differs most from other industries. Marketing copy that's normal for a plumber can violate a lawyer's professional rules.

- **"Specialist" and "certified."** Under ABA Model Rule 7.2(c), a lawyer shouldn't state or imply certification as a specialist unless certified by an approved organization, which must be named. Some states are stricter. Illinois, for example, generally bars lawyers from describing themselves as "certified," "specialist" or "expert," with narrow exceptions.
- **"Expert."** Many states treat it the same way as "specialist." Avoid it unless your state's rules clearly allow it.
- **"Best," "top" and "#1."** These can be misleading if they can't be factually verified. If you cite an award or ranking, name the source and year, and check whether your state requires a disclaimer.
- **Past results.** Case results can create unjustified expectations without context. Many states require disclaimers, and some restrict how results are presented.
- **Testimonials.** Rules on client testimonials differ widely by state.

AI writing tools and SEO plugins often suggest these words because they're high-volume search terms. You can target "divorce lawyer" without calling yourself the "best divorce lawyer." For the state-by-state detail, see our [lawyer advertising rules by state guide](/blog/lawyer-advertising-rules-by-state).

> Your website is a communication about your services under Rule 7.1 in every state, even where websites are exempt from ad filing. Have a lawyer review every page before it goes live.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does law firm SEO take to work?

Google Business Profile fixes can move Maps rankings within weeks. Organic rankings for competitive practice-area terms often take several months of steady work on pages, reviews and links, longer in crowded markets like PI.

### Should each attorney have their own Google Business Profile?

Google allows individual practitioner profiles in some cases, but a solo attorney who is the firm's only practitioner should usually have one profile. For multi-attorney firms, read Google's practitioner guidelines before creating individual profiles, because duplicates can split reviews and confuse rankings.

### Can a law firm use the word "specialist" on its website?

Only if your state's rules allow it. Under the ABA Model Rules you need certification from an approved organization, named in the communication, and some states such as Illinois are stricter. Check your state bar's current rules.

### Do legal directories like Avvo and Justia help SEO?

They help with citation consistency and can send referral traffic, and AI assistants often draw on them. Claim and complete your profiles, keep details matching your Google Business Profile, and don't expect directories alone to outrank strong practice-area pages.

### Should a law firm create pages for every nearby city?

Only where you have a real office or genuinely distinct local information. Near-identical city pages rarely rank and can lower the perceived quality of your whole site.

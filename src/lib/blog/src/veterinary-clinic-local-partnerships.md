Pet owners rarely choose a vet from a search result alone. They ask their groomer, their dog walker, the trainer at puppy class, the staff at the shelter where they adopted. Those pet businesses see owners every week and get asked "who's a good vet around here?" all the time.

A veterinary clinic that's known and trusted by the local pet businesses gets a steady stream of new clients without paying for each one. This guide covers who to partner with, how to approach them and what to offer.


## Why local pet businesses are the best source of new clients

Pet owners trust people who handle their animals every week. A groomer who has bathed a dog since it was a puppy, a daycare that knows exactly how it plays, a trainer who taught it to sit: these are the people owners ask when they need a vet, move to a new area or feel unhappy with their current clinic. Their recommendations carry more weight than ads because they're based on real experience with the pet.

Pet businesses also notice health problems first. Groomers see skin issues, lumps, ear infections and overgrown nails. Daycares notice limping, coughing or changes in energy. Trainers see behavior changes that can signal pain. When those businesses have a vet they trust, they suggest a visit, and the owner often follows their advice.

Partnerships also help you reach new owners at the right moment. Shelters and rescues place animals who need a first visit, and puppy classes are full of first-time owners choosing a vet for the first time. Being the name those organizations mention means starting relationships that can last a pet's whole life.

Finally, partnerships build your reputation in the pet community. When groomers, trainers and daycare staff speak well of your clinic, that reputation spreads through dog parks, social media groups and neighborhood conversations.

## The first email

> **Subject:** Quick vaccine records for your daycare clients
>
> Hi Jess — we're the vet clinic around the corner from your daycare. Daycares we work with get vaccine records emailed within an hour of a visit, and we keep a few same-day slots for injuries. Would a printed "when to see the vet" sheet for your front desk be useful? Happy to drop some off.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Ignoring the front desk.** Groomers and daycare staff refer to whoever is easy to reach.
- **One-way relationships.** Refer back when clients ask about grooming or boarding.
- **Promotions that break professional rules.** Check your state board's guidance before offering discounts.

## Your first visit to a partner

1. What health issues do you notice most often?
2. How do you handle vaccination records today?
3. What do you tell owners when they ask for a vet recommendation?
4. What has frustrated you about vets before?
5. Would your staff like a short talk on spotting health problems?
6. Can we leave handouts or cards at your front desk?

Listen for frustrations about slow records, hard-to-reach clinics or unfriendly staff. Those become your promises.

## Handling common objections

| What they say | What it usually means | A helpful response |
|---|---|---|
| "We already recommend another vet." | Existing loyalty. | Offer to be a second option for owners who prefer your location or hours. |
| "We don't want to seem pushy." | They worry about recommending. | Suggest they share several names, including yours. |
| "Clients complain vets are expensive." | Price sensitivity. | Explain your pricing transparency and wellness plans if you offer them. |
| "We're too busy for meetings." | Time is tight. | Drop off handouts and a short note instead. |
| "Our clients go to a big hospital." | Preference for larger facilities. | Highlight your personal care, availability and referral options for specialties. |

## Preparing your clinic

Partners and the owners they send will look you up. Make sure your Google Business Profile has current hours, photos of the clinic and team, and replies to reviews. Your website should explain services, new client steps and how to request records. Train your front desk to handle partner referrals warmly and to send records quickly. Keep a few same-day slots for urgent visits where you can, and let partners know how to request them.

## Partnership ideas that build trust

**Front-desk handouts.** A "when to suggest a vet visit" sheet for groomers and daycares, with your contact details.

**Staff training sessions.** A short talk for daycare or grooming staff on spotting injuries, heat stress or common skin problems.

**New adopter support.** Work with shelters on a simple first-visit process for adopted pets, within your professional rules.

**Puppy class talks.** Ten minutes on vaccines, parasite prevention and puppy health at a trainer's class.

**Community events.** Microchip clinics or pet health days at pet stores.

## A worked example

Here's how a partnership might grow. The details are illustrative.

You email a busy dog daycare around the corner offering faster vaccine records and a front-desk guide. The owner replies to your follow-up, frustrated that two clinics nearby take days to send records. You drop off printed guides and introduce yourself to the staff.

A week later, the daycare notices a dog limping and suggests a vet visit; the owner calls you because your name is on the guide. You see the dog that afternoon and email the updated record to the owner, who forwards it to the daycare. The daycare manager mentions your clinic in the next newsletter to clients.

Over the following months, the daycare refers several new clients, and you recommend the daycare to clients looking for boarding. The two-way relationship keeps both businesses growing.

## Keeping partnerships healthy in year two

Check in with each partner every few months. Ask what's changed, whether records are still arriving quickly and whether staff would like another training session. Refer your clients to partners when they ask about grooming, boarding or training. Thank partners for referrals with a note, keeping gestures modest. Partnerships that feel two-way last much longer than those that feel one-sided.

## Welcoming new clients from partners

The first visit from a referred client sets the tone for years. Make booking easy, confirm the appointment promptly and send new client forms ahead of time. Ask how they heard about you and note the partner. During the visit, take time to explain findings and recommendations clearly, and give the owner a written summary. Many owners switching vets are anxious about the change, so warmth and clarity matter.

After the visit, send a short follow-up and, if appropriate, a reminder about vaccines or next checkups. Thank the referring partner with a quick note, without sharing medical details. These small steps turn first visits into long-term clients and keep partners confident about recommending you.

## Supporting partners with practical tools

Partners appreciate tools that make their own work easier. For daycares and boarding, offer a simple way for owners to request vaccine records. For groomers, provide a guide to common skin and ear issues and when to suggest a vet visit. For trainers, share materials on puppy health, socialization and vaccination timing. For shelters, coordinate a straightforward process for new adopters' first visits, following your professional rules. Practical support makes you the obvious vet to recommend.

## Building community reputation

Community events help owners meet your team in a relaxed setting. Host microchip clinics, senior pet evenings or puppy health talks with partners. Sponsor a local dog event or shelter adoption day. Share helpful content on social media and in partner newsletters, such as seasonal safety tips. Over time, your clinic becomes known in the local pet community, which supports referrals from every partner.

## Supporting new puppy and kitten owners

New pet owners are some of the most valuable clients a clinic can win, because the relationship can last the pet's whole life. They also have lots of questions: vaccines, parasite prevention, nutrition, training and spaying or neutering. Partner with trainers, pet stores and shelters to provide clear, friendly guidance. A simple new pet guide, a first-visit checklist and a welcoming first appointment make a big difference. Owners who feel supported early stay loyal and recommend you to other new owners.

## Communicating clearly about costs

Price concerns are a common reason owners delay visits or switch clinics. Be transparent: explain estimates before treatment, discuss options when appropriate and provide written summaries. Consider wellness plans if they fit your clinic. When partners hear that owners felt informed and respected about costs, they're more comfortable recommending you.

## Staying connected with partners year round

Plan partner touchpoints through the year: spring parasite reminders for daycares and groomers, summer heat safety tips, autumn senior pet care and winter holiday hazard guides. Seasonal materials give partners something useful to share and keep your clinic in their minds.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do veterinary clinics get new clients?

Beyond search and reviews, partner with groomers, daycares, trainers, shelters and pet stores. They're asked for vet recommendations constantly.

### What can a vet offer a dog daycare?

Fast vaccine records, same-day slots for injuries when possible and educational material for their front desk.

### Should vets refer clients to groomers and trainers?

Yes. Two-way referrals strengthen partnerships and help clients find trusted local services.

### Are vet clinic events worth it?

Educational events like puppy talks or microchip clinics build trust with owners and partners. Keep them helpful rather than sales-focused.

### How do vets get referrals from groomers?

Make it easy: give groomers a front-desk guide on when to suggest a visit, respond quickly to referred clients and recommend the groomer to your own clients.

### Should vet clinics partner with animal shelters?

Yes. Shelters place pets who need a first visit. Work out a simple process that follows your professional and state rules.

### What can vets offer dog daycares?

Fast vaccine records, staff training on spotting health issues and same-day slots for injuries when possible.

### How do vet clinics track partner referrals?

Ask new clients how they heard about you and note the partner in your records or CRM.

### How do vet clinics get new clients from shelters?

By working with shelters on a simple, welcoming first-visit process for adopted pets, within professional and state rules.

### What should a "when to see the vet" guide include?

Common signs like limping, coughing, vomiting, lumps, ear scratching and changes in appetite or energy.

### Can vet clinics offer discounts to partner clients?

Rules vary. Check your state board and professional guidance before offering discounts tied to partners.

### How do vets thank referral partners?

With a thank-you note or small gesture, keeping it modest and never sharing medical details.

### How can vet clinics speed up vaccine record requests?

By offering owners a simple way to request records and sending them promptly after visits.

### What events attract new pet owners?

Puppy health talks, microchip clinics, senior pet evenings and shelter adoption days.

### What should a new pet guide include?

Vaccine schedules, parasite prevention, nutrition basics, training tips and when to schedule the first visit.

### How do vets discuss costs with owners?

With clear estimates before treatment, options where appropriate and written summaries.

### What seasonal topics can vets share with partners?

Parasite prevention, heat safety, senior pet care and holiday hazards.

### Do wellness plans help vet clinics?

They can help some clinics provide predictable care and costs for owners. Consider what fits your practice.

### How do vets build relationships with trainers?

By offering short puppy health talks at classes and answering trainers' questions about vaccines and socialization timing.

### Can vet clinics share partner businesses with clients?

Yes. Recommending trusted groomers, daycares and trainers strengthens two-way partnerships.

### How do vet clinics introduce themselves to groomers?

With a short email, a front-desk guide on when to suggest a vet visit and a friendly in-person visit to drop off materials.

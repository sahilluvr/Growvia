Search "chiropractor near me" in almost any US city and you'll see a crowded Map Pack, a row of ads and a few practices with hundreds of reviews. The patient who searches at 7 a.m. with a stiff back usually books with one of the first three practices that look trustworthy and can see them today. Chiropractor marketing is mostly about becoming one of those three, then keeping patients through their plan of care.

The good news is that the winning moves aren't expensive. A complete Google Business Profile, steady reviews, clear condition pages, a few strong referral relationships and a reactivation habit will out-earn most paid campaigns. The traps are specific to chiropractic: new-patient offers that clash with Medicare rules, health claims your board or the FTC won't accept, and patient data drifting into the wrong tools.

## Win Google Maps first

Most new chiropractic patients start on Google Maps. Google's [local ranking help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) says results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. You can't move your office, but you can control relevance (what Google thinks you do) and prominence (how established and trusted you look).

### Google Business Profile setup for chiropractors

- **Primary category: Chiropractor.** Add secondary categories only if they're true and you want those searches, for example "Sports massage therapist" if you employ licensed massage therapists.
- **Services.** List the conditions and services patients search for: back pain, neck pain, sciatica, headaches, auto injury care, sports injuries, prenatal chiropractic, if you offer them.
- **Description.** Two or three sentences in patient language. Mention your neighborhood, who you help and what a first visit includes. No cure claims.
- **Hours and same-day availability.** If you see same-day patients, say so in your description and posts.
- **Booking link.** Point to your online scheduler or a short contact page.
- **Photos.** Exterior and entrance (so people find you), front desk, treatment rooms, the team. Never photograph patients without written consent.
- **Posts.** One or two a month: a stretch tip, a new hour, a community event.

## Reviews: the fastest prominence lever

Review count, rating and recency shape both your Maps ranking and the choice a patient makes between you and the office down the road. The best chiropractic practices get reviews steadily, not in bursts.

### How to ask

- **Ask at a high point.** After a visit where the patient says they feel better, or when they finish their initial plan of care.
- **Ask everyone.** Google bans "review gating", which means only asking patients you expect to be happy.
- **Make it one tap.** A QR code at the front desk and a short link sent through your patient communication platform.
- **Never incentivize.** The FTC's [Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers), effective October 21, 2024, bans buying reviews and fake reviews, with civil penalties per violation. A free adjustment for a review counts as buying one.

### How to reply without a HIPAA problem

Never confirm someone is your patient in a reply, and never mention their condition or treatment, even if they did. HHS settled with two dental practices over review replies that disclosed patient information, and the same rules apply to chiropractors who are covered entities. Thank the reviewer, keep it general, and invite them to call. ## Condition pages that rank and convert

Patients search by problem: "sciatica relief near me", "chiropractor for headaches", "car accident chiropractor". A single "Services" page with a bulleted list won't rank for those. One strong page per condition will.

### What a good condition page includes

1. **A clear headline** with the condition and city ("Sciatica Treatment in Round Rock").
2. **Who it's for**, in plain language, including the symptoms that should send someone to urgent care or a physician instead.
3. **What the first visit looks like**: history, exam, any imaging policy, and what happens next.
4. **Your approach**: techniques you use, typical visit frequency in honest ranges, and home exercises.
5. **Insurance and cost**: which plans you accept and self-pay pricing if you publish it.
6. **Who provides it**: the chiropractor's name, credentials and a photo.
7. **A short FAQ** and a booking button.

### Claims to avoid

Health claims need evidence. The FTC's [Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance) says health claims generally need competent and reliable scientific evidence. In 2021 the FTC brought its first case under the COVID-19 Consumer Protection Act against a St. Louis-area chiropractor over unsupported COVID-19 claims. State boards also discipline unprofessional advertising.

Stay away from "cure", "guaranteed relief", claims about treating non-musculoskeletal diseases, and "specialist" titles your state doesn't recognize. Describe what you do and who it helps.

## New-patient offers: what's allowed

Discounted first visits ("$49 exam and adjustment") are common in chiropractic and can work well. They also carry real rules.

| Offer type | Common use | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Discounted first visit for self-pay patients | Attract cash-pay patients | Your state board's advertising rules and any required disclaimers |
| Free consultation or screening | Lower the barrier to a first conversation | Some states require specific disclaimers on free or discounted services ads (Florida, for example, has a statute on this) |
| Offers to Medicare or Medicaid patients | Rarely appropriate | Federal beneficiary inducement rules limit gifts and discounts that influence provider choice |
| Routine copay or deductible waivers | Not recommended | Can create problems with payer contracts and federal fraud and abuse laws |
| Promotional gifts | Branded water bottles, lumbar rolls | OIG's nominal value guidance for Medicare and Medicaid beneficiaries |

For Medicare and Medicaid patients, the federal civil monetary penalties law prohibits offering things of value that are likely to influence a beneficiary's choice of provider. The HHS Office of Inspector General allows gifts of nominal value, which it has defined as no more than $15 per item and $75 in total per patient per year. The [OIG's physician education resources](https://oig.hhs.gov/compliance/physician-education/) explain the fraud and abuse basics.

Also remember Medicare's chiropractic coverage is narrow. According to [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/chiropractic-services), Medicare Part B covers manipulation of the spine to correct a subluxation, when medically necessary, and doesn't cover other services or tests a chiropractor orders. Make that clear in any insurance content so seniors aren't surprised.

> Warning: Many chiropractic marketing programs sell "done-for-you" new-patient offers. Before you run one, check it against your state board's rules and your payer contracts. You're responsible for the ad, not the agency.

## Local Services Ads and Google Search ads

Chiropractor is an eligible Google Local Services Ads category in the US. LSA ads appear above regular search ads, you pay per lead rather than per click, and practices that pass Google's screening show a "Google Verified" badge. Check [Google's Local Services help center](https://support.google.com/localservices/) for availability in your city.

### LSA tips for chiropractors

- **Answer the phone.** Responsiveness affects how often your ad shows. Missed LSA calls are lost money.
- **Keep reviews flowing.** Your rating and review count influence LSA ranking.
- **Dispute bad leads.** Google lets you dispute leads that aren't valid, such as wrong numbers or job seekers.
- **Track to booked visits.** Count booked first visits from LSA in your practice software, not just leads.

### Search ads

Search ads work best on high-intent terms like "chiropractor near me", "sciatica chiropractor" and "chiropractor open Saturday". Send each ad to the matching condition page, not your home page. Keep ad pixels off your booking pages and forms. Our [healthcare tracking pixels guide](/blog/healthcare-tracking-pixels-privacy) explains why and how to measure without them.

## Referral relationships that keep sending patients

The steadiest chiropractic practices have a short list of professionals who refer patients every month. Good referral partners for chiropractors include primary care physicians, massage therapists, personal trainers and gyms, running and cycling clubs, physical therapists (for co-management), prenatal providers and doulas, and employers with physical jobs.

### A simple referral routine

1. **Pick ten targets** within a 15-minute drive.
2. **Bring something useful.** A one-page sheet on which conditions you treat, red flags you refer out, your availability and insurance accepted.
3. **Offer a workshop.** A 20-minute "desk posture" talk for a local employer or a "running injury prevention" session for a running club.
4. **Report back.** With patient authorization, send referring providers a brief update through a secure channel.
5. **Track referrals by source** every month.

Watch two lines. Paying anyone per referral, or giving gifts to induce referrals of federal healthcare program patients, can violate the Anti-Kickback Statute. Many states also restrict direct solicitation of accident victims and the use of paid "runners". Check your state's rules before partnering on auto injury work.

The fastest way to lose a ready patient is to let the call go to voicemail or answer with a vague "we'll call you back". Offer two specific times every time.

## Reactivation and plan-of-care completion

Patients who drop out of care mid-plan are the biggest hidden leak in most chiropractic practices. Reactivating them is cheaper than winning new patients, but it involves PHI, so it belongs in the right system.

- **Use your practice software or HIPAA-compliant patient platform** for missed-appointment follow-ups, recall and "we haven't seen you in a while" messages. Tools like ChiroTouch, Jane and HIPAA-focused messaging platforms handle this under a BAA.
- **Set rules.** For example: a reminder after one missed visit, a personal call after two, and a check-in at 90 days after the last visit.
- **Keep the wording general.** "It's been a while since your last visit. Would you like to schedule a check-in?" works without naming a condition.
- **Measure plan completion** in your practice software. If many patients stop at visit three or four, look at how you explain the plan on day one.

Your marketing tool can still send general content to website subscribers, like a monthly stretch-of-the-month email. Don't export your patient list into it.

## Social media and video

Chiropractic is visual and teachable, which makes it a good fit for short video. The best-performing practice content usually teaches something patients can try at home.

- **Stretch and exercise demos** for common issues (desk neck, low back stiffness after gardening).
- **Myth-busting** in plain language ("Does cracking your knuckles cause arthritis?").
- **Behind the scenes:** the team, a new piece of equipment, a community event.
- **What to expect on a first visit,** filmed with a team member as the "patient".

Rules for video: get written authorization before filming any real patient, avoid cure claims, and add a simple note that videos are educational and not a substitute for an exam. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best marketing for a chiropractor?

For most practices, the highest-return work is a complete Google Business Profile, a steady flow of reviews, strong condition pages and a few active referral partners. Paid ads and social video help once those are in place.

### Can chiropractors advertise free or discounted first visits?

Often yes for self-pay patients, but rules vary by state, and some states require specific disclaimers on free or discounted service ads. Offers to Medicare and Medicaid patients are limited by federal beneficiary inducement rules, so check with your state board and a healthcare attorney.

### Are chiropractors eligible for Google Local Services Ads?

Yes. Chiropractor is an eligible Local Services Ads category in the US, though availability varies by location. You pay per lead and must pass Google's screening to get the Google Verified badge.

### How should a chiropractor respond to a negative review?

Thank the reviewer, avoid confirming they're a patient or mentioning any care, and invite them to call the office. Never argue the details publicly.

### How can chiropractors reactivate inactive patients safely?

Use your practice software or a HIPAA-compliant messaging platform, keep the wording general, and set simple rules for follow-ups after missed visits. Don't load patient lists into general marketing tools.

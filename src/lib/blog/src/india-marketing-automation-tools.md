Marketing in India runs on WhatsApp, Google Maps and Instagram, in that order for most local businesses. A customer finds you on Maps, checks your reviews, looks at a few Instagram posts, and taps the WhatsApp button. Everything after that, the quote, the reminder, the follow-up and the repeat order, happens in chat.

That makes India different from the US or Europe, where email often does the heavy lifting. Tools built for those markets can feel expensive and incomplete here. This guide covers what Indian small businesses really need, what it costs in rupees, the new consent rules under the DPDP Act, and how to pick tools without overpaying.

## What Indian small businesses actually need

Whether you run a clinic in Pune, a coaching institute in Jaipur, a D2C brand in Bengaluru or a salon in Chandigarh, the jobs are the same:

::flow The path most Indian customers take | Google Maps or Instagram | Reviews and a quick look at your site | Tap to WhatsApp | Price, slot or catalogue in chat | Payment, visit and a review

1. **Be found on Google and Maps.** A complete Google Business Profile, a fast mobile site, and pages for each service and area.
2. **Reply fast on WhatsApp**, with a shared inbox when more than one person answers.
3. **Send reminders and offers** to customers who opted in, using approved WhatsApp templates.
4. **Post regularly on Instagram and Facebook**, and YouTube if video suits you.
5. **Follow up by email** for B2B services, higher-value sales and anyone who prefers it.
6. **Stay compliant** with the DPDP Act as its consent rules come into force.

## What WhatsApp marketing really costs in India

WhatsApp is where most of your marketing spend goes, so understand the bill first. On the WhatsApp Business Platform, there are two separate costs.

**Meta's message fees.** Meta charges per delivered template message, by category. In India, marketing templates cost roughly ₹0.86 each, while utility and authentication templates (order updates, reminders, OTPs) cost much less. Replies inside the 24-hour window after a customer messages you are free. Rates change, so check [Meta's WhatsApp pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing) for current numbers.

**The platform's fees.** Most Indian WhatsApp platforms (known as BSPs) charge a monthly subscription, often ₹1,000 to ₹2,500 to start, and many add a markup on each message. AiSensy, for example, lists plans from ₹1,500 a month with marketing messages at ₹1.09 each.

Here's what that means for a business sending 2,000 marketing messages a month:

::bars Approximate monthly cost for 2,000 WhatsApp marketing messages | Typical BSP (₹1,500 plan + ₹1.09 per message): ₹3,680 | Meta's fees only, about ₹0.86 per message: ₹1,720 (what you pay with Growvia's free plan) *

Growvia connects to Meta's official Cloud API using your own WhatsApp Business account, so Meta bills message fees to you directly and Growvia adds no per-message markup. WhatsApp chats, templates and broadcasts are included on the free plan. BSPs can still be the better choice if you need chatbot flow builders, Shopify cart-recovery flows or click-to-WhatsApp ad managers, which are their specialities.

## The DPDP Act: what changes for marketing

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is now being phased in. The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 with an 18-month rollout. Most of the duties that affect marketers, such as notice, consent, withdrawal and data rights, apply in full from around May 2027 under that timeline. The government has consulted on bringing parts of it forward, so it's smart to get ready now.

::stats DPDP Act timeline for marketers | Nov 2025 | DPDP Rules notified; the Data Protection Board is set up | Nov 2026 | consent manager rules switch on | May 2027 | notice, consent and data-rights duties apply in full

In practice, for marketing that means:

::check DPDP-ready marketing, in practice | Ask for marketing consent clearly, with an unticked checkbox | Explain what you'll send, on which channel, and why | Keep a record of when and how each person agreed | Make withdrawing as easy as agreeing, like "reply STOP" or one-click unsubscribe | Delete or stop using data when someone asks | Don't buy or scrape contact lists

## A note on SMS and DLT

Commercial SMS in India must go through TRAI's DLT system: your business, sender IDs (headers) and message templates all need registering with a telecom operator. It works, but it takes setup time, and promotional SMS can't be sent at night. For most small businesses, WhatsApp templates now do the same job with richer messages and read receipts.

## Slow networks and budget phones

Many of your customers browse on budget Android phones and patchy mobile data. That affects your marketing more than you might think. A slow site loses visitors before they see your WhatsApp button. Heavy images in posts take longer to load. Keep your site light, and check your [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained) on mobile, not just on office Wi-Fi.

### How much does the WhatsApp Business API cost in India?

Meta charges per delivered template message, roughly ₹0.86 for marketing and much less for utility and authentication messages, with replies in the 24-hour window free. Platforms then add a monthly fee and sometimes a markup. Check Meta's rate card for current rates.

### Do I need consent to send WhatsApp messages in India?

Yes. Meta requires opt-in before you message people on the WhatsApp Business Platform, and the DPDP Act requires clear, specific consent for marketing as it comes into force. Record when and how each person agreed.

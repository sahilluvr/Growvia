Electrician marketing has a problem most trades don't. Homeowners rarely know what they need. They search "lights flickering," "breaker keeps tripping" or "can I put a Tesla charger in my garage," and the electrician who answers that question clearly, and looks safe to let into the house, usually gets the call.

The work has also shifted. Ten years ago a residential shop lived on service calls and remodels. Today the big tickets are panel upgrades, EV charger installs, whole-home generators and heat pump circuits. Each one has its own search terms, its own buyer and its own seasonal rhythm.

This guide shows you how to market an electrical business around those jobs: how to set up Google Business Profile and Local Services Ads, which pages to build first, how to turn your license into a selling point, and how to get reviews that mention the work you want more of. It's one part of our wider playbook on [AI marketing for home services](/blog/ai-marketing-for-home-services), which covers the full system across trades.

## Residential vs commercial electrician marketing

Before you spend a dollar, decide which customer you're chasing. The two markets search, buy and judge you differently, and mixing them on one generic page usually serves neither.

| | Residential | Commercial |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides | Homeowner, often in a hurry | Facility manager, GC, property manager, owner |
| How they find you | Google Maps, "near me" searches, Nextdoor, neighbors | Referrals, bid lists, LinkedIn, Google organic, repeat relationships |
| What they care about | Safety, price range, showing up on time, clean work | Licensing, insurance limits, safety record, scheduling, invoicing |
| Typical trigger | Something broke, a new EV, a storm outage, a remodel | Tenant buildout, code violation, expansion, preventive maintenance |
| Best channels | GBP, Local Services Ads, reviews, service pages | Relationship selling, case-study pages, email follow-up, GC networking |
| Sales cycle | Hours to days | Weeks to months |

Many shops do both. That's fine, but give each its own section of your site and its own follow-up. A property manager who lands on a cartoon-lightbulb "emergency electrician" page won't trust you with a 40-unit building.

::compare Positioning a mixed shop | Generic: one services page; stock photos; "we do it all"; same follow-up for everyone | Split: separate residential and commercial pages; real job photos for each; license and insurance limits up front; different follow-up for bids vs service calls

## The jobs worth marketing hardest

Not every job deserves the same marketing effort. Put your energy behind work that is high value, searched often, and something you do well. For most residential electricians, that's four categories.

### Panel upgrades and service changes

Panel upgrades come from several triggers: an older home with a fuse box or a panel brand inspectors flag, a renovation, a new EV, a heat pump, or an insurer asking questions. Homeowners search "panel upgrade cost," "200 amp service upgrade," and "do I need a new electrical panel."

A strong panel upgrade page explains warning signs in plain words, what drives price (amperage, meter relocation, utility coordination, permit and inspection), how long the power is off, and what the homeowner needs to do. Don't publish a fixed price unless you mean it. Give the factors and invite a quote.

### EV charger installation

EV owners are some of the most researched buyers you'll meet. They've read forums and often know their car's charging rate. They want to know whether their panel can handle a Level 2 charger, whether they need a load calculation, and whether you'll pull the permit.

The U.S. Department of Energy's [home charging page](https://afdc.energy.gov/fuels/electricity-charging-home) is a good reference for the basics customers ask about, like Level 1 vs Level 2 charging. Your page should go further with your real process: site visit or photo-based quote, load calculation, panel check, permit, install, inspection.

### Generators and storm readiness

Whole-home standby generators and generator interlocks sell after outages, and they sell months later when people remember the outage. Your content should cover sizing, fuel type, transfer switches, permits and maintenance.

Safety content earns trust here. Link to the Consumer Product Safety Commission's [portable generator safety guidance](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Portable-Generators) on carbon monoxide risks, and explain why backfeeding a house through a dryer outlet is dangerous to the family and to utility line workers. A homeowner who reads that from you is more likely to call you for a proper transfer switch.

### Service and troubleshooting

Flickering lights, dead outlets, tripping breakers and burning smells are your entry jobs. They're lower ticket but they create the relationship that leads to the panel or generator job later. Build one strong troubleshooting page and one "emergency electrician" page if you actually answer after hours.

## Set up Google Business Profile for an electrical contractor

For residential work, your Google Business Profile drives most of the calls that don't come from ads. Our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide) covers every field. These are the electrician-specific points.

### Categories

Choose **Electrician** as your primary category. Add secondary categories only for work you really do, such as "Electrical installation service," "Lighting contractor," "Generator shop" or "Electric vehicle charging station contractor" where available. Check what the electricians ranking above you use, and don't add categories just to show up for more searches.

### Name, address and service area

Your profile name must match your real business name. "Bright Line Electric" is fine. "Bright Line Electric EV Charger Install Panel Upgrade Denver" breaks Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) and invites suspension.

If customers don't visit your shop, hide the address and set service areas by city or ZIP code. Our [local SEO guide for home service businesses](/blog/local-seo-home-service-businesses) explains the service-area rules in detail.

### Services, photos and posts

Fill in the Services section with each job type and a short description. Upload photos that show what makes you trustworthy: a clean panel with labeled breakers, a tidy EV charger install with neat conduit, your branded truck, your team in uniform. Electrical work photographs well when it's neat, and neatness is exactly what a homeowner worries about.

Post monthly: a recent EV charger install (no address), a reminder to test GFCI outlets, or a pre-storm generator maintenance note.

## Turn your license into marketing

Electrical work is dangerous, and most states and many cities require licensing for it. That's a marketing advantage for licensed contractors, because unlicensed handymen compete on price and can't compete on proof.

### Where to show credentials

- **Website header or footer:** your state license number on every page, with a link to your state's license lookup if it has one.
- **Service pages:** a short line such as "Permits pulled and inspections scheduled on every panel upgrade."
- **Quotes and invoices:** license number, insurance carrier and a note on permits.
- **Google Business Profile:** mention licensing in your description and show your license on Local Services Ads, where Google checks it.
- **Truck wraps:** many states require license numbers on vehicles anyway. Check yours.

### Talk about permits plainly

Many homeowners see a permit as extra cost and delay. Explain why it protects them: an inspector checks the work, and the record helps when they sell. Keep it factual, no scare tactics.

Mention that work follows the National Electrical Code as adopted in your state. States adopt NEC editions on different schedules, so don't name an edition unless you know which one your jurisdiction enforces.

> Your license is the one thing a cheaper competitor can't copy. Put it everywhere a homeowner decides whether to trust you: profile, website, quote and truck.

## Local Services Ads for electricians

Electricians are an eligible category for Google Local Services Ads in the US. These are the boxed ads at the top of results with a **Google Verified** badge. You pay per lead, not per click.

To qualify, you go through Google's [screening and verification](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12174778), which for electricians typically includes license checks where your state requires them, insurance verification and background checks. Keep your documents current, because an expired insurance certificate can pause your ads.

### Why LSA suits electricians

- Homeowners searching urgent problems tend to call the first verified business they see.
- You can select the job types you want, such as panel upgrades or EV charger installs, and turn off ones you don't.
- Google's [ad ranking page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305) says responsiveness, reviews and proximity affect placement, not just your bid.

### The catch

You pay for leads whether or not they become jobs, and Google counts answered calls, messages and some voicemails as leads. Missed calls also hurt your ranking. If your office lets calls roll to voicemail during busy hours, fix that before you raise your budget. ## Website pages that bring in electrical work

Your site needs fewer, better pages. A homeowner on a phone wants three things fast: can you do this job, are you safe to hire, and how do I reach you.

### The page list to build first

1. **Home page** with your service area, license, click-to-call and top services.
2. **Panel upgrades and service changes.**
3. **EV charger installation**, with a short list of what you need from the customer (panel photo, car model, garage location).
4. **Generators**, covering standby units, interlocks and maintenance.
5. **Electrical troubleshooting and repair.**
6. **Emergency electrician**, only if you answer after hours.
7. **Commercial electrical services**, if you do commercial work.
8. **Areas we serve**, with city pages only where you have real local content.

### What each service page should include

- The problem in the customer's words ("My breaker trips when the dryer and microwave run together").
- How you solve it, step by step.
- What affects the price, without inventing a number you won't honor.
- Photos from real jobs and a review that mentions this service.
- Permit and inspection notes.
- A short quote form and a click-to-call button near the top.

For forms, shorter usually wins. Our guide to [website contact form best practices](/blog/website-contact-form-best-practices) explains which fields to keep.

### Use photo-based quotes for EV chargers

A strong conversion trick for EV installs is letting customers upload photos of their panel and garage in the quote form. You can often give a ballpark range without a site visit, and the customer feels progress right away.

::flow EV charger lead to booked install | Customer searches "EV charger installer" | Lands on your EV page | Uploads panel and garage photos | You send a range within hours | Site visit or install booked

### Be careful with tax credit claims

Many electricians used federal tax credits as a selling point for EV chargers and panel upgrades. Federal legislation signed in July 2025 scheduled both relevant credits to end: the [Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/energy-efficient-home-improvement-credit) for property placed in service after 2025, and the [Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property Credit](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/alternative-fuel-vehicle-refueling-property-credit) for property placed in service after June 30, 2026. Check the IRS pages for current status before mentioning either. Utility and state rebates may still exist in your area, so link to your utility's program rather than promising savings yourself.

## Reviews that sell the right jobs

Reviews do double duty. They help you rank in the Map Pack and Local Services Ads, and they answer the homeowner's real question: will this person do clean, safe work in my house?

### Build the ask into job completion

1. The electrician finishes, tests the work and shows the customer what was done.
2. They explain anything the customer should know, such as how the new breaker panel is labeled.
3. They mention that reviews help a local business.
4. A review link goes out by email or text within the hour.
5. One reminder goes out a few days later if needed.

Ask every customer, not just the happy ones. Google bans review gating, and the [FTC's rule on consumer reviews](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers), effective October 21, 2024, bans buying reviews and suppressing negative ones. No discounts or gift cards for reviews. Our guide on [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) covers the full process within the rules.

### Get reviews that mention the job

You can't script reviews, but you can make the job memorable. Customers naturally mention what you talked about at the end of the visit. If the tech says "Your new 200 amp panel is ready for the EV and the heat pump," the review often mentions both. Those words help you show up for those searches.

### Reply to every review

Thank people specifically. For negative reviews, stay calm, acknowledge the issue, and offer to fix it offline. Our guide to [responding to negative reviews](/blog/professional-services-negative-review-responses) has templates.

## Seasonality and follow-up

Electrical demand has its own seasons, and they differ by region.

- **Storm and outage seasons** drive generator interest. Hurricane areas peak before and after summer storms. Northern areas see it with winter ice storms.
- **New-car seasons** and EV launches bring charger requests. Year-end car purchases often mean January installs.
- **Spring and summer remodels** bring panel upgrades and lighting work.
- **Heat pump and HVAC replacements** often need a new circuit or panel capacity. Partner with HVAC contractors on this. Our [seasonal HVAC marketing plan](/blog/hvac-marketing-seasonal-plan) shows when their busy seasons hit.

### Email the customers you already have

Past customers are your cheapest source of new work. A simple email schedule works well:

- After a generator install: an annual maintenance reminder.
- After an EV charger install: a note when they might be adding a second EV.
- After any service call: a seasonal check, such as testing smoke detectors and GFCIs.
- Before storm season: a generator readiness note for everyone on your list.

Keep it useful, include your physical address and an unsubscribe link to meet CAN-SPAM, and set up email authentication so messages land in inboxes. ## Common electrician marketing mistakes

- **One "Services" page for everything.** Google can't rank you for EV chargers if you never wrote about them.
- **Keyword-stuffed GBP names.** They work briefly, then get reported and suspended.
- **Promising tax credits that may have ended.** Check IRS pages before every campaign.
- **Paying for LSA leads you don't answer.** Missed calls cost you the job and your ranking. Our guide to [missed calls and speed to lead](/blog/missed-calls-speed-to-lead-home-services) covers fixes.
- **Ignoring past customers.** A generator install from three years ago is a maintenance job waiting to be booked.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best marketing for electricians?

For residential electricians, a well-built Google Business Profile, steady reviews and clear service pages for panel upgrades, EV chargers and generators usually bring the most calls. Local Services Ads add fast leads if you can answer every call. Commercial work leans more on relationships and referrals.

### Are electricians eligible for Google Local Services Ads?

Yes. Electricians are an eligible LSA category in the US. You need to pass Google's screening, which typically includes license checks where required, insurance verification and background checks, to earn the Google Verified badge.

### Should electricians list prices on their website?

List what affects price rather than a fixed number, unless you'll honor it. Panel upgrades and EV installs vary with amperage, distance from the panel, permits and utility work. Explaining those factors builds trust and still invites a quote.

### Can I still advertise federal tax credits for EV chargers?

Be careful. Federal legislation in July 2025 scheduled the EV charger credit to end for property placed in service after June 30, 2026, and the home improvement credit after 2025. Check the IRS pages for current status, and point customers to utility or state rebates instead.

### How do electricians get more Google reviews?

Ask every customer at job completion, then send a direct review link within the hour. Don't offer discounts for reviews or ask only happy customers, since both break Google's policies and the FTC review rule.

### Should I market residential and commercial work separately?

Yes. Homeowners and facility managers search and decide differently. Give each its own pages, photos and follow-up, and show insurance and licensing details prominently on the commercial side.

A homeowner with no hot water calls three plumbers. The first goes to voicemail. The second rings out. The third picks up on the second ring and books a visit for this afternoon. Plumbers one and two will never know that call happened, or that it was worth a water heater replacement.

That's the quiet tax on most home services businesses. Missed calls don't show up on a P&L. Nobody complains. The customer just calls the next name on Google, and the money you spent on ads, SEO and truck wraps to make the phone ring goes to a competitor.

This guide shows you how to find out how many calls you're missing, set response targets your team can hit, cover after-hours and busy periods, make web forms answer themselves, and track it all so you know it's fixed. It's part of our broader guide to [AI marketing for home services](/blog/ai-marketing-for-home-services), where speed to lead is the first pillar for a reason.

## Why missed calls cost more than you think

Home service searches are urgent. A homeowner with a leaking ceiling or a dead AC in July isn't collecting quotes for next week. They want someone today, so they call down the list until a human answers. Being first to answer often matters more than being cheapest or best reviewed.

### The cost is double if you run Local Services Ads

If you advertise on Google Local Services Ads, missed calls hurt twice. Google's [ad ranking help page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305) says your responsiveness affects where you show, and that missed calls may hurt it. So a missed call loses today's job and can push you lower tomorrow. Our guide to [Google Local Services Ads for contractors](/blog/google-local-services-ads-home-services) covers how ranking works.

### Run your own math

You don't need an industry statistic to see the damage. Use your own numbers.

::funnel Example: one month of inbound calls for a 3-truck HVAC company (illustrative) | Calls to main line: 400 | Answered live: 300 | New-customer calls among answered: 150 | Booked jobs: 90

In this made-up example, 100 calls were never answered. If even a quarter of those were new customers ready to book, and this shop books about 60% of new-customer calls it answers, that's roughly 15 lost jobs a month. Multiply by your average ticket and the number usually gets an owner's attention fast.

::stats Example: what 100 missed calls might cost (illustrative) | 25 | likely new customers | 15 | jobs lost at a 60% booking rate | $6,000 | lost at a $400 average ticket

Swap in your own call volume, booking rate and average ticket. The point isn't the exact figure. It's that a fix costing a few hundred dollars a month often pays for itself many times over.

## Step 1: Find out how many calls you actually miss

Most owners guess low. The office says "we get almost everything," but nobody counts the calls that ring out while the dispatcher is on another line.

### Where to get the real number

- **Your phone system's call log.** Most VoIP systems (RingCentral, Nextiva, Grasshopper, Google Voice and others) show answered, missed and voicemail counts. Pull the last 30 days.
- **Local Services Ads reports.** LSA shows calls and whether they were answered or missed.
- **Google Business Profile performance.** It shows how many calls came from your profile, though not whether they were answered.
- **Call tracking software.** Tools like CallRail record source, answer status and duration for every call, which is the most complete picture.

### What to look for

Break missed calls down by hour and day of the week. Most shops find clusters: first thing in the morning, lunch, late afternoon when techs call in, and the first hot or cold day of the season. Those clusters tell you where to add coverage, which is cheaper than covering everything.

> Count missed calls before you buy any new marketing. If you're missing a big share of the calls you already get, more ads just means paying for more voicemails.

## Step 2: Set a call-back standard and write it down

Some calls will always be missed. What matters is how fast you get back to them. Set a written standard, often called a service level agreement or SLA, so everyone knows the target.

::scale Callback speed for a missed call during business hours | Good: within 5 minutes, before they reach the next company | Needs work: within 30 minutes | Poor: next morning or never

These bands aren't from a study. They follow the logic of urgent searches: a homeowner with a problem typically keeps calling until someone answers, so the earlier your callback, the more likely they're still free.

### A simple call-back policy

1. Every missed call during business hours gets a callback within 5 minutes.
2. Voicemails are checked every 15 minutes, not at the end of the day.
3. One named person owns the callback list each shift.
4. If the first callback doesn't connect, try again within an hour, then the next morning.
5. Log the outcome: booked, not interested, wrong number, existing customer.

### A callback script that works

::chat Calling back a missed call | you: Hi, this is Dana from Summit Plumbing. We just missed a call from this number, sorry about that. How can we help? | them: Our water heater is leaking. I already called someone else. | you: Understood. Has anyone been scheduled yet? We have a tech nearby who could be there by 2 today. | them: Nobody's coming until tomorrow. 2 works. | you: Great, let me get your address and I'll text the tech's name when he's on the way.

Notice the script opens by owning the miss, asks if they've already booked, and offers a specific time. "We have a tech nearby" only works if it's true, so keep the dispatch board in front of whoever makes callbacks.

## Step 3: Cover the gaps with answering options

You can't put a dispatcher on every hour. You can pick the right mix of coverage for your size and your emergency volume.

| Option | Best for | Strengths | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overflow to a second office line | Busy hours, small offices | Cheap, keeps calls in-house | Only works if someone is free to pick up |
| Owner or tech on-call phone | After hours, 1–3 trucks | No extra cost | Burnout, missed calls on jobs |
| Live answering service | After hours, overflow | A human answers every time, takes details, can dispatch | Quality varies, script and train them |
| Trades-specialist call center | Larger shops, booking-heavy | Can book into your field software | Higher cost, needs integration |
| AI phone answering | Overflow, after-hours intake | Answers instantly, captures details | Test it hard, handle emergencies carefully, check state call-recording consent rules |
| Voicemail only | Nothing | Free | Most urgent callers hang up and call someone else |

### Choosing a setup by size

A one-truck owner-operator can start with a simple rule: calls during jobs roll to an answering service that takes details and promises a callback by a specific time. A 5-to-15-truck shop usually needs a dedicated CSR during business hours plus an answering service or call center for overflow and nights.

### Be honest about your hours

If your Google Business Profile, LSA profile and website say 24/7, you need a human or a well-tested system answering at 2 a.m. If you don't, list real hours and an emergency number only if someone actually picks up. Promising 24/7 and sending calls to voicemail is a fast route to one-star reviews.

## Step 4: Make web forms and messages answer themselves

Phone calls aren't the only leads you miss. A quote request that sits in an inbox until tomorrow is just as lost.

### Send an instant auto-reply

Every form submission should trigger an immediate email confirmation that:

- Confirms you got the request and repeats the key details.
- Says exactly when a human will respond ("within 30 minutes during business hours, by 8 a.m. if after hours").
- Gives your phone number for emergencies.
- Includes your license number and a link to your reviews.

Auto-replies to a request someone just made are transactional messages, but keep them honest and accurate anyway, as the [FTC's CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) expects for any marketing that follows. Make sure your domain has SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up so the reply doesn't land in spam. Our [email deliverability guide](/blog/email-deliverability-spf-dkim-dmarc-guide) explains how.

### Keep forms short

Name, phone, ZIP, service needed and a short description is enough to call back. Every extra field loses people. Our guide to [website contact form best practices](/blog/website-contact-form-best-practices) covers what to cut.

::flow Web lead to booked job in under 30 minutes | Homeowner submits form | Instant email confirmation | Lead lands in pipeline with alert | CSR calls within 15 minutes | Job booked and confirmed

### Missed-call text-back

Many field service and phone tools offer "missed-call text-back," which sends an automatic text when you miss a call. It can work well, because the customer gets a response while still deciding. But texting has rules. The TCPA and carrier requirements apply, including 10DLC registration for business texting from local numbers. Keep any automated text to a direct reply about their inquiry, not marketing. Our [SMS marketing, TCPA and 10DLC guide](/blog/sms-marketing-tcpa-10dlc-guide) covers the details, and the FCC's [guide on unwanted calls and texts](https://www.fcc.gov/consumers/guides/stop-unwanted-robocalls-and-texts) explains the consumer side.

### Don't forget social messages

Homeowners also message you on Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp. Those messages often sit unread for days because nobody owns that inbox. Route them into the same place as your other leads, with the same response target.

## Step 5: Follow up on the leads that didn't book

Not every lead books on the first contact. Quote requests for bigger jobs, like a new HVAC system or a panel upgrade, may take days or weeks. A simple follow-up sequence recovers some of them.

::steps Follow-up cadence for an unbooked quote | Day 0 — call within 15 minutes, email the quote the same day | Day 1 — second call and a short email answering common questions | Day 3 — email with reviews from similar jobs and financing options | Day 7 — check in, ask if timing or budget changed | Day 30 — one last note, then move to seasonal emails

Keep every follow-up useful: answer a question, show a similar job, explain financing. Stop when someone says no. For seasonal trades, our [HVAC seasonal marketing plan](/blog/hvac-marketing-seasonal-plan) shows how to keep past quotes warm until the next season.

## Step 6: Measure it every week

What gets measured gets fixed. Track five numbers weekly and review them in a 15-minute meeting.

### The five metrics

1. **Missed call rate:** missed calls divided by total inbound calls.
2. **Median callback time:** for missed calls and voicemails during business hours.
3. **Form response time:** from submission to first human contact.
4. **Booking rate:** booked jobs divided by new-customer leads.
5. **Lead source:** which channel each booked job came from.

### Where the data lives

Here's how the tools split up. Call tracking software like CallRail is the specialist for calls: it records which ad, page or listing drove each call, whether it was answered, and the recording. ## Common mistakes

- **Counting calls but not answer status.** Volume means nothing if a third went unanswered.
- **Letting techs answer calls on jobs.** They miss details, rush the customer and lose focus on the work. Route calls to a person whose job is answering.
- **Generic voicemail greetings.** If you must use voicemail, say when you'll call back and give an emergency option.
- **No owner for the callback list.** "Whoever's free" means nobody.
- **Promising 24/7 without coverage.** It invites bad reviews and LSA ranking drops.
- **Never testing.** Call your own business from an unknown number once a week, at different times. You'll learn more than any report.

## Putting it together: a fictional example

Summit Plumbing is **fictional**: four trucks in a mid-size Ohio city, one office manager, and the owner on call at night. Here's what a fix would look like.

1. Pull 30 days of call logs and find most missed calls cluster between 7–9 a.m. and after 5 p.m.
2. Add a part-time CSR from 7–10 a.m. and route evening calls to a live answering service with a plumbing script.
3. Set a 5-minute callback rule for daytime misses and assign one owner per shift.
4. Shorten the website form and add an instant email auto-reply with a response time promise.
5. Add call tracking to see which channels drive calls, and log every booked job's source.
6. Review the five metrics every Monday.

## Frequently asked questions

### How fast should a home services business return a missed call?

Aim for within 5 minutes during business hours. Homeowners with urgent problems usually keep calling until someone answers, so the faster you call back, the more likely they haven't booked a competitor yet.

### Do missed calls affect Google Local Services Ads?

Yes. Google says responsiveness is a ranking factor for Local Services Ads and that missed calls may hurt it. Answering more calls helps both your jobs today and your ad placement over time.

### Is an answering service worth it for a small contractor?

Often, yes, especially for after-hours and overflow calls. Compare the monthly cost to the value of the jobs you're currently missing using your own call logs. A trained human who takes details and promises a callback beats voicemail.

### Can I send an automatic text when I miss a call?

Many tools offer missed-call text-back, and it can work well. Keep it a direct reply to the caller's inquiry, register for 10DLC if texting from a local number, and follow TCPA rules before sending any marketing texts.

### How do I track which marketing brings in phone calls?

Use call tracking software like CallRail with dynamic numbers on your website, check LSA and Google Ads call reports, and ask every caller how they found you. Log the source of every booked job, not just every call.

### What should a web form auto-reply say?

Confirm you received the request, state when a person will respond, give a phone number for emergencies, and include trust signals like your license number. Send it instantly from an authenticated domain so it reaches the inbox.

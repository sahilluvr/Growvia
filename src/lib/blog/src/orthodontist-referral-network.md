Orthodontic practices grow two ways: families who find you directly, and families sent by a dentist they already trust. The second group arrives with confidence already built. They've been told "you should see Dr. Patel" by someone who has looked in their child's mouth for years.

Strong referral networks don't happen by accident. They come from practices that make referring easy, communicate quickly and treat the referring dentist as a partner rather than a source. This guide shows how to build that network. For direct-to-family marketing, see [AI marketing for dentists](/blog/ai-marketing-for-dentists).


## Why referral networks matter more than ever for orthodontists

Orthodontics has more competition than it used to. Direct-to-consumer aligner brands advertise heavily, general dentists offer aligners in-house and families compare options online. In that environment, a recommendation from the family's own dentist carries enormous weight. Parents trust the person who has looked after their child's teeth for years, and adults trust the dentist who knows their history.

Referred patients also tend to start treatment at higher rates. They arrive with a clinical reason for the visit, a trusted recommendation and fewer doubts. Their consults are more focused, and their families are often already used to regular dental care, which helps with compliance during treatment.

A strong referral network also protects your practice from swings in advertising costs. When paid channels get expensive, referrals keep arriving. And because referring dentists talk to each other at study clubs and local meetings, a good reputation spreads through the professional community in a way no ad can buy.

The flip side is that referral relationships require care. Dentists will quietly stop referring if communication is slow, if patients don't come back for hygiene or if they feel you're competing with them. Building the network is as much about protecting their relationships as growing your own.

## Who refers orthodontic patients

| Referral source | When they refer | What they want from you |
|---|---|---|
| General dentists | Crowding, bite issues spotted at check-ups | Fast consults, clear updates, patients returned for hygiene |
| Pediatric dentists | Early interceptive treatment questions | Shared philosophy on timing, gentle communication |
| Oral surgeons | Cases needing combined treatment | Coordinated plans and timelines |
| Speech therapists | Tongue thrust, bite-related speech issues | Clear explanations they can pass to parents |
| School nurses and PTAs | Parents asking for recommendations | Educational talks and handouts |

General and pediatric dentists within a reasonable drive are the core of the network.

## What makes referring easy

::check A referral experience dentists remember | Consult within a week | Same-day confirmation that the patient booked | A short written summary after the consult | Patient sent back for cleanings during treatment | A thank-you note, not a gift that raises eyebrows

The summary letter is where many practices fall short. Dentists want to know what you found and what you plan, in three or four sentences, not a twelve-page report.

## The first contact

Introduce yourself as a colleague, not a vendor.

> **Subject:** Orthodontic consults for your patients
>
> Hi Dr. Kim — I'm Dr. Patel at Brightline Orthodontics on Main Street. When your team refers a patient, we book a consult within a week, confirm with your office the same day and send you a short summary afterwards. We also make sure patients keep their hygiene visits with you during treatment. Would a quick coffee or a visit to your office be welcome?

Then follow up in person. A short lunch for the team, with a one-page referral guide, does more than any brochure.

## Keeping the network healthy

- **Track referral sources** in your CRM so you notice when a practice goes quiet.
- **Send a short quarterly note** with something useful: early-treatment timing, aligner case selection, or new technology you've adopted.
- **Host an annual study club or lunch-and-learn** for local dental teams.
- **Return the favor.** Refer families back for general care when they need it.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Keeping patients from their dentist.** Always send them back for hygiene and restorative work.
- **Slow communication.** If a dentist has to chase you for an update, they'll refer elsewhere.
- **Gifts that look like payment.** Professional courtesy is fine; anything that looks like paying for referrals can create ethical and legal issues. Check your state's rules.

## Your first visit to a referring practice

1. How does the team decide when to recommend an orthodontic evaluation?
2. Who in the practice usually brings it up with families: the dentist or the hygienist?
3. What has frustrated you about referring to orthodontists before?
4. How would you like to receive updates: letter, email or portal?
5. How important is it that patients keep hygiene visits with you during treatment?
6. Are there cases you'd prefer to keep in-house?

Respecting the last point builds trust. If a dentist does simple aligner cases, acknowledge it and position yourself for the cases they'd rather refer.

## Handling common objections

| What they say | What it usually means | A helpful response |
|---|---|---|
| "We refer to Dr. X already." | Existing loyalty. | Offer to be a second option for families who prefer a different location or schedule. |
| "We do aligners in-house." | They keep simpler cases. | Position yourself for complex cases and offer case consultations. |
| "Patients don't come back to us." | Past bad experience. | Promise and prove that patients return for hygiene and restorative care. |
| "We never hear what happened." | Poor communication from others. | Show your summary format and commit to a turnaround time. |
| "We're too busy for lunches." | Time is tight. | Offer a five-minute drop-in with materials or a virtual session. |

## Preparing your practice

Referring dentists and the families they send will look you up. Make sure your website explains early evaluation, treatment options and what happens at a first visit, in language parents understand. Keep your Google Business Profile current, with team photos and replies to reviews. Create a simple referral form or pad for partner practices, and make sure your front desk knows to prioritize referred patients and confirm bookings back to the referring office the same day.

## Communication that keeps referrals coming

**Same-day booking confirmation.** A quick note to the referring office that the patient has booked shows the referral was handled.

**Short consult summaries.** Findings, timing, plan and next step, within a couple of days.

**Milestone updates.** Brief notes at the start of treatment, mid-treatment and completion.

**Hygiene reminders.** Encourage patients to keep cleaning visits with their dentist, and coordinate around appliances.

**Thank-you notes.** A personal note after referrals, without anything that could look like payment.

## A worked example

Here's how a referral relationship might grow. The details are illustrative.

You email a family dental practice ten minutes away and follow up with your one-page early evaluation guide for hygienists. The hygiene lead replies asking for printed copies. You drop them off with lunch, meet the dentist briefly and leave referral pads.

Two weeks later, the practice refers an eight-year-old with a crossbite. Your team books the consult within a week, confirms with the referring office the same day and sends a four-line summary afterwards recommending early treatment. The family starts treatment and keeps their cleanings with the dentist.

At the next quarterly check-in, the dentist mentions two more families. Within six months, the practice is one of your most consistent referrers, and the dentist recommends you at the local study club.

## Mistakes to avoid as the network grows

Don't let communication slip when you get busy; it's the first thing referrers notice. Avoid recommending your own general dentist friends to referred families. Be careful with marketing that competes directly with partners, such as heavily promoting services they offer. Track referral sources accurately so you notice when a practice goes quiet, and reach out with genuine interest rather than a sales pitch.

## Making the first consult memorable

Referred families arrive with high expectations because someone they trust recommended you. Make the consult welcoming and clear. Explain findings in plain language, show images or models, outline options and timing, and give a written summary with costs and next steps. Address common concerns directly: discomfort, appointment frequency, school schedules and payment options. Families who leave the consult feeling informed are more likely to start treatment and tell their dentist about the positive experience.

Follow up after the consult with a short note and answers to any questions. If the family isn't ready to start, offer a recall evaluation at the right time. Keep the referring dentist informed, so they can reinforce your recommendation at the next checkup.

## Coordinating care during treatment

Orthodontic treatment lasts a long time, and coordination with the general dentist matters throughout. Encourage patients to keep hygiene visits, share appliance information when relevant and communicate about any restorative needs. Send brief updates at major milestones. When treatment finishes, send a completion note and thank the dentist for the referral. This continuous coordination is what separates orthodontists dentists love from those they tolerate.

## Hosting study clubs and lunch-and-learns

Educational events strengthen professional relationships. Host a study club or lunch-and-learn for local dental teams on topics like early treatment timing, aligner case selection or managing patients with appliances. Invite hygienists and assistants too, since they often spot issues first. Keep sessions practical, provide useful handouts and allow plenty of time for questions. Teams who learn from you remember you when they see patients who need orthodontic care.

## Adult orthodontics and referrals

Adults are a growing part of orthodontic practices, often referred by dentists for crowding, bite problems or before restorative work. Adults care about discretion, scheduling and cost. Explain aligner and appliance options clearly, offer convenient appointment times and coordinate closely with the referring dentist on restorative plans. Dentists who see their adult patients handled well refer more cases, including complex ones.

## Using technology to make referrals easier

Digital referral forms, shared imaging and secure messaging can simplify communication with referring offices. Offer a simple online referral form, accept digital scans and send summaries electronically. Make sure all systems protect patient privacy. Easier referrals mean more referrals.

## Supporting families with financing

Cost is a common concern for families considering treatment. Explain fees clearly, offer payment plans where possible and help families understand any insurance benefits. When families feel the financial side is manageable, they're more likely to start treatment, and referring dentists hear positive feedback.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do orthodontists get more referrals?

Make referring easy: fast consults, same-day booking confirmation, short summary letters and patients returned to the referring dentist for hygiene. Visit practices in person and stay in touch quarterly.

### Should orthodontists send referral reports?

Yes. A brief summary of findings and the treatment plan keeps the referring dentist informed and confident to refer again.

### How far away should referring dentists be?

Most referrals come from practices within a short drive. Focus on dentists 15–20 minutes from your office first.

### Is it okay to give gifts to referring dentists?

Small courtesies are common, but anything that could look like payment for referrals may raise ethical or legal issues. Check your state's rules and keep gestures modest.

### When should orthodontists ask dentists for referrals?

Start by offering something useful, like a hygienist guide or a lunch talk. Referrals follow once dentists trust your communication and approach.

### How fast should an orthodontist send a consult summary?

Within a couple of days. Speed shows respect for the referring dentist and keeps them confident.

### Should orthodontists market directly to parents too?

Yes, but in a way that doesn't compete with partner dentists. Emphasize collaboration and returning patients for hygiene.

### How do I find out why a practice stopped referring?

Ask directly and kindly during a check-in. Often it's a communication issue that's easy to fix.

### How do orthodontists keep referring dentists informed?

With short consult summaries, milestone updates and completion notes, sent promptly.

### Should orthodontists offer free consultations?

Many do, especially for referred patients, as it lowers barriers for families.

### What should be in an orthodontic consult summary for families?

Findings, recommended timing, treatment options, costs, payment plans and next steps.

### How can orthodontists support hygienists?

With practical guides on signs that suggest an orthodontic evaluation, and by answering their questions.

### What if a referring dentist offers aligners too?

Collaborate on case selection and focus on complex cases they prefer to refer.

### How often should orthodontists visit referring practices?

Quarterly visits or check-ins are common, with more frequent contact for new relationships.

### Do dentists refer adult orthodontic patients?

Yes, often for crowding, bite issues or before restorative treatment.

### How can orthodontists make referrals easier?

With simple referral forms, digital scan acceptance and fast electronic summaries.

### What do adult orthodontic patients care about most?

Discretion, convenient scheduling, clear costs and treatment length.

### Do orthodontists offer payment plans?

Many do. Clear plans help families start treatment with confidence.

### How do orthodontists welcome referred families?

With a prompt booking, a warm first visit, clear explanations and a written summary of options and costs.

### Should orthodontists send holiday notes to referring offices?

A short thank-you note is a common courtesy; keep gestures modest and professional.

### How do orthodontists track referral trends?

Record the referring practice for every new patient, then review the numbers each quarter. A practice that suddenly sends fewer patients deserves a friendly check-in to ask how things are going and whether anything could be improved.

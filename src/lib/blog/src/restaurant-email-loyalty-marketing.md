Restaurant email marketing is the cheapest way to turn a first visit into a habit, and most restaurants barely use it. A guest has a great meal, pays, leaves, and you never hear from them again unless they happen to think of you. Meanwhile the delivery apps and social platforms you rely on decide who sees your name and charge you for the privilege.

An email list changes that. It's a group of people who asked to hear from you, and nobody can raise its price or change its algorithm. On a slow Tuesday you can send one email and see guests walk in with it on their phones.

This guide shows you how to build that list with proper consent, what to send (a welcome, a birthday club, slow-night offers, win-backs), how often, and how to measure whether it's working. It's part of our complete guide to [AI marketing for restaurants](/blog/ai-marketing-for-restaurants), which covers Google Maps, reviews, social, delivery apps and a 90-day plan.

## Why a list you own matters

Every other channel you use is rented. Google decides your Maps position. Instagram decides how many followers see your post. Delivery apps own the customer relationship and take a commission on every repeat order. Those channels are still worth using, especially for new guests, but they're not yours.

Email works differently in three ways:

- **You control the timing.** Send a slow-night offer at 3 p.m. on the day you need covers.
- **You own the relationship.** If you switch POS or reservation systems, your list comes with you, as long as you export it.
- **It brings back guests you already earned.** Bringing back a regular usually costs far less than finding a new guest.

::flow How a guest becomes a regular | Gives email with consent at the table or online | Gets a welcome with a reason to return | Joins the birthday club | Receives monthly news and slow-night offers | Gets a win-back if they lapse

## Capture emails with clear consent

The best lists are built in the restaurant, at the moment a guest is enjoying themselves. The key word is consent. People must know they're joining a marketing list and agree to it.

### Wi-Fi login

A guest Wi-Fi login page that asks for an email is one of the easiest capture points. Do it properly:

- **Make marketing opt-in separate and clear.** A checkbox that says "Send me news, specials and my birthday treat" works. Don't bury it in terms of service.
- **Don't pre-check the box** or make Wi-Fi depend on joining your list. Let people connect either way.
- **Say how often you'll email.** "About twice a month" sets expectations and reduces unsubscribes.

### QR codes on tables and checks

A small table tent or check presenter card with a QR code works well, especially with a clear reason to join: "Join our birthday club and get a free dessert on your birthday." The code opens a short signup form on your website with name, email, birth month and day, and an optional favorite location if you have more than one.

Ask for birth month and day only, not year. You don't need it, and avoiding birth years and age questions keeps you away from collecting children's data. The FTC's [COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions) explains the rules if your site or program could attract kids under 13. Our guide to [contact forms that convert](/blog/website-contact-form-best-practices) has tips on keeping signup forms short.

### Online orders and reservations

Your direct ordering and reservation systems can add a marketing opt-in at checkout. Use them, and make sure the opt-in is a real choice, not automatic. Check how each platform lets you export subscribers, because a list stuck inside one vendor isn't fully yours.

### Your website and social profiles

Add a simple "Join our list" form to your homepage, menu page and events page. Link it in your Instagram bio and Business Profile posts.

::chat A server inviting a table to join | them: This was amazing, we'll definitely be back. | you: So glad you enjoyed it. If you scan the code on the check, you'll get a free dessert on your birthday and hear about our wine dinners first. | them: Oh nice, just did it. | you: Perfect, you'll get a welcome email in a minute.

::funnel Example: one month of in-restaurant list building (illustrative) | Guests served: 4,000 | Saw the QR card or Wi-Fi offer: 2,400 | Scanned or logged in: 500 | Joined the list: 260

Those numbers are made up, but the lesson is real: the offer and the server's mention matter more than the card itself.

## Follow the rules from day one

You don't need a lawyer to email guests, but a few rules apply to every restaurant.

- **CAN-SPAM** applies to all commercial email in the US. Use honest From names and subject lines, include your physical postal address, and give a working unsubscribe that you honor within 10 business days. The FTC's [CAN-SPAM compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) covers it.
- **Mailbox provider rules.** Google's [email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126) require authentication, easy unsubscribes and low spam complaint rates, with stricter rules for bulk senders. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC before you send. Our [email deliverability guide](/blog/email-deliverability-spf-dkim-dmarc-guide) explains each in plain English.
- **State privacy laws.** If your business meets the thresholds of a state privacy law, such as California's [CCPA](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa), you may have notice duties, including a notice of financial incentive for some loyalty programs. Check whether your business is covered.
- **International guests.** If you're in a tourist area and email guests from abroad, other countries' laws may apply. Our guide to [email marketing laws by country](/blog/email-marketing-laws-by-country) covers GDPR and CASL.
- **Text messages are different.** Marketing texts need prior express written consent under the TCPA, plus quiet hours and 10DLC registration. Our [SMS marketing guide](/blog/sms-marketing-tcpa-10dlc-guide) explains the details.

Alcohol offers deserve a second look. Some states restrict drink discounts and happy-hour promotions. Massachusetts, for example, has banned happy-hour drink discounts since the 1980s. Check with your state alcohol beverage control agency before emailing a drink special.

## The five emails every restaurant should automate

You don't need a complicated program. Five automated emails, set up once, do most of the work. These run on their own, triggered by a signup, a date or inactivity.

| Email | Trigger | Timing | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome | Joins the list | Within minutes | Thanks them, sets expectations, gives one reason to come back |
| Second visit nudge | No visit recorded or redeemed | About 2 weeks later | Highlights a dish or night they haven't tried |
| Birthday | Birth month and day | About a week before | Delivers the birthday treat with a clear window to redeem |
| Anniversary | One year on the list | On the date | Thanks them for a year, with a small surprise |
| Win-back | No opens or redemptions for months | After a set gap, such as 90 days | Asks if they still want to hear from you, with an offer |

::steps The first 30 days after a guest joins | Minute 1 — welcome email with a thank-you and what to expect | Day 3 — your story, your best dishes and a photo of the team | Day 14 — second-visit nudge featuring a night or dish | Day 30 — first monthly newsletter with news and an event

### Template: welcome email

**Subject:** Welcome to the [Restaurant] family

Hi [First name],

Thanks for joining us. You'll hear from us about twice a month with new dishes, events and the occasional treat. Nothing more.

Next time you're in, try [signature dish]. It's what our regulars order on their first visit, and most of them order it on their tenth too.

See you soon,
[Owner name], [Restaurant name]
[Postal address] · [Unsubscribe]

### Template: birthday email

**Subject:** Your birthday dessert is waiting

Hi [First name],

Happy almost-birthday from all of us at [Restaurant]. Your free dessert is good any day from [date] to [date]. Just show this email to your server.

Bring a friend, bring the whole family, or come by for a quiet treat at the bar. We'd love to celebrate with you.

[Owner name] · [Postal address] · [Unsubscribe]

A birthday window of a week or two works better than a single day, because people plan birthday meals around weekends.

## Fill slow nights with targeted offers

Slow-night emails are where the list pays for itself. The idea is simple: send a specific, time-limited reason to come in on the nights you have empty tables.

### Choose the right offer

Discounts work, but they train guests to wait for them. Value-led offers often work just as well and protect your margins.

::compare Discount-heavy vs value-led slow-night offers | Discount-heavy: 20% off everything; guests wait for the next coupon; margin shrinks; attracts deal hunters | Value-led: kids eat free on Tuesdays; chef's off-menu special; a new dish preview for subscribers; first look at event tickets

Good slow-night ideas for many restaurants:

- **A recurring night** guests can plan around: family night, trivia, a three-course prix fixe.
- **Subscriber-only dishes** or an early taste of a new menu.
- **Event invitations:** wine dinners, cooking demos, tasting menus with limited seats.
- **A weather-based offer:** soup and grilled cheese on the first cold day.

### Time it right

Send slow-night emails the day of, or the day before, early enough for people to plan. Mid-afternoon often works for same-night dinner offers. Test two send times over a month and keep the one with more redemptions.

### Template: slow-night email

**Subject:** Tuesday is family night (kids eat free)

Hi [First name],

Every Tuesday from 4 to 8 p.m., kids 12 and under eat free from our kids' menu with each adult entrée. No code needed, just mention this email.

Tables fill up around 6, so come early or book here: [link].

[Restaurant name] · [Postal address] · [Unsubscribe]

### Template: win-back email

**Subject:** We miss you at [Restaurant]

Hi [First name],

It's been a while. We've added [new dish] and [new thing] since your last visit, and we'd love for you to try them. Here's [small offer] to say welcome back, good until [date].

If you'd rather not hear from us anymore, no hard feelings. You can unsubscribe below.

[Owner name] · [Postal address] · [Unsubscribe]

## How often to email

Most restaurants do well with two to four emails a month: one monthly newsletter, one or two offers or event invitations, plus the automated emails above. Send more and unsubscribes climb. Send less and people forget they joined.

Keep each email short. One main message, one photo and one clear action. Guests read these on their phones between other things.

> Never send an offer you can't honor. If your kitchen can't handle a rush, cap the offer by time or seats. A disappointed guest who came in for a deal does more harm than one who never came at all.

## Measure redemptions, not just opens

Open rates are a weak signal, partly because some email apps load images automatically and inflate opens. For restaurants, the measure that matters is whether guests came in and spent money.

| Metric | How to track it | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| List growth | New subscribers per week by source | Whether capture is working |
| Click rate | Clicks on menu, booking or ordering links | Whether the email interested people |
| Redemptions | Unique codes, a POS button for each offer or servers noting "email offer" | Whether the email drove visits |
| Revenue per redemption | Average check on redeemed offers | Whether the offer pays for itself |
| Unsubscribe rate | Unsubscribes per email | Whether you're sending too much or the wrong thing |
| Repeat visits | Loyalty or POS data, where available | Whether the program builds habits |

The simplest redemption tracking is a dedicated button on your POS for each email offer, or a unique code per campaign that servers enter. At the end of the month, you'll know which emails brought people in.

::bars Example: redemptions by email type over one quarter (illustrative) | Birthday: 62 * | Family night: 48 | Wine dinner invite: 30 | New dish preview: 24 | General newsletter: 9

In this made-up example, the birthday email wins, as it often does, and the general newsletter barely moves anyone. That's normal. Newsletters keep you in mind; targeted offers drive visits.

## Common restaurant email mistakes

- **Adding guests without consent,** like importing every reservation email into your newsletter. It breaks trust and can break the law.
- **Only sending discounts,** which trains guests to wait for deals.
- **No birthday club.** It's the easiest automated email to set up and often the best performer.
- **Measuring opens instead of redemptions.**
- **Skipping domain authentication,** so your emails land in spam.
- **Letting the list sit in one vendor's system** with no export.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it legal to collect emails through restaurant Wi-Fi?

Yes, if guests clearly agree to join your marketing list, ideally with a separate, unchecked opt-in box. Follow CAN-SPAM for every email and check whether state privacy laws apply to your business.

### What should a restaurant birthday club offer?

A free dessert, appetizer or drink (where alcohol rules allow) with a redemption window of one to two weeks works for most restaurants. Ask only for birth month and day, not year.

### How often should a restaurant send marketing emails?

Two to four emails a month works for most restaurants: a monthly newsletter, one or two offers or event invitations, plus automated welcome and birthday emails.

### How do I track whether restaurant emails bring in guests?

Use a dedicated POS button or a unique code for each email offer, then count redemptions and the average check at the end of the month. Redemptions matter more than open rates.

### Should restaurants use email or text messages?

Both can work. Email is cheaper and easier to stay compliant with, while texting needs prior express written consent, quiet hours and 10DLC registration. Many restaurants start with email and add texting through their POS later.

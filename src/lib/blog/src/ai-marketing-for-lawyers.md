## How people actually choose a lawyer in 2026

Someone who needs a lawyer is usually stressed, short on time and comparing two or three firms in a single evening. The path looks roughly the same across practice areas:

::flow How a potential client finds and hires a law firm | Searches Google or asks an AI assistant | Compares 3–4 firms by reviews, Maps position and website | Calls or submits a form to the first two or three | Signs with the firm that responds well first

Three things decide who wins: **visibility** (Maps, organic results, Local Services Ads and AI assistants' short lists), **trust** (reviews, attorney bios, practice-area pages) and **response** (answering quickly and booking a consultation before they call the next firm).

Most firms over-invest in the first and ignore the third. The cheapest growth in legal marketing is almost always better intake.

## Step 1: Fix intake before you buy more leads

If you only change one thing this quarter, change how fast and how well you respond. A potential client with an urgent problem, an arrest, an injury, a custody dispute, rarely waits a day for a callback. They keep calling down the list.

Our [law firm intake guide](/blog/law-firm-intake-speed-to-lead) covers scripts and cadences in detail. The short version:

- **Answer live during business hours.** If the attorney is in court, a receptionist, paralegal or answering service picks up.
- **Cover after-hours.** Criminal defense, PI and family law clients often reach out at night and on weekends.
- **Auto-reply to every web form within a minute.** Confirm you received it, say when a human will call, and give a phone number for urgent matters.
- **Follow up on a schedule.** Same day, next morning, day three, day seven. Most firms stop after one voicemail.
- **Run a basic conflict check before any substantive conversation,** and avoid collecting more detail than you need before you know you can take the matter.

::scale How fast does your firm respond to new inquiries? | Good: live answer by phone; forms replied to within 5 minutes; after-hours coverage | Needs work: same-day callbacks; forms checked a few times a day | Poor: next-day callbacks; forms checked when someone remembers

It does not answer or track phone calls, so pair it with an answering service and a call tracking tool if phones are your main channel.

## Step 2: Win Google Maps and local search

For most practice areas, the Google Maps results decide who gets the call. Google says local ranking is based on relevance, distance and prominence, and explains these factors on its [official local ranking help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091). You can't move your office closer to every searcher, but you control relevance and a lot of prominence.

### Google Business Profile basics for law firms

::check Google Business Profile checklist for a law firm | Primary category that matches your main practice, such as "Personal injury attorney" or "Family law attorney" | Secondary categories only for real practice areas | Exact firm name with no keyword stuffing | Address, phone and hours that match your website | Services listed by practice area with short descriptions | Real photos of attorneys, office and building entrance | A post or update at least every two weeks

Two law-firm-specific traps to avoid:

- **Keyword-stuffed names.** "Smith Law – Best Car Accident Lawyer Houston" breaks Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) and can get your profile suspended. Use your real-world firm name.
- **Virtual offices.** Google requires a real location where you meet clients during stated hours. A mailbox or a coworking address you never staff is a suspension risk.

Our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide) walks through every field, and the [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-small-business) covers citations and NAP consistency.

### Practice-area and location pages

Your website should have one strong page per practice area you want to rank for, not one "Practice Areas" page with a bulleted list. A DUI defense page should answer what happens after an arrest in your state, the administrative license process, typical timelines, what you do differently and how fees work. That is what earns rankings and trust.

Location pages work only when they are genuinely useful: the courthouse you appear in, local procedures, directions and parking. Twenty near-identical "Car Accident Lawyer in [Suburb]" pages tend to be ignored by Google and can drag the site down.

## Step 3: Stay inside your state bar's advertising rules

Lawyers can advertise, but every state regulates how. The ABA's [Model Rules of Professional Conduct](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/model_rules_of_professional_conduct_table_of_contents/) set the template: Rule 7.1 bans false or misleading communications, Rule 7.2 covers advertising and payments for recommendations, and Rule 7.3 limits solicitation. Each state adopts its own version, and some add filing requirements.

A few examples of how much states differ:

- **Texas** requires most advertisements to be filed with the State Bar's Advertising Review Committee within 10 days of first dissemination, with a fee, under Rule 7.04.
- **Florida** requires most ads to be filed with The Florida Bar at least 20 days before first use, with a $250 fee per ad filed on time. Lawyer websites are exempt.
- **Illinois** bars lawyers from describing themselves as "certified," "specialist" or "expert" except in narrow circumstances, under its Rule 7.4.
- **New York** overhauled its rules effective June 1, 2026, dropping the old "Attorney Advertising" label requirement and moving closer to the ABA model.

Our [lawyer advertising rules by state guide](/blog/lawyer-advertising-rules-by-state) has a state-by-state table and a pre-publish checklist. Build that review into your workflow so every new ad, landing page and social campaign gets checked before it goes live.

The words that cause the most trouble are "expert," "specialist," "best," "guaranteed" and any mention of past results without context. AI writing tools love these words. If you use AI to draft marketing copy, and you should, because it saves hours, have a lawyer review every claim before publishing.

## Step 4: Reviews, the right way

Reviews are both a ranking signal and the main thing a stressed client reads before calling. Two sets of rules apply to law firms.

First, everyone's rules. The FTC's [Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers), effective October 21, 2024, bans fake reviews, buying positive or negative reviews, undisclosed insider reviews and suppressing reviews through threats. Google's policies also ban "review gating," which means asking only the happy clients.

Second, lawyer-specific rules. Confidentiality under Rule 1.6 applies to review replies. In [ABA Formal Opinion 496](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/administrative/professional_responsibility/aba-formal-opinion-496.pdf) (January 2021), the ABA's ethics committee concluded that a negative online review alone doesn't allow a lawyer to reveal client information in a reply, and suggested that often the best response is no response, or a short one such as "Professional obligations do not allow me to respond as I would wish."

In practice: ask every client whose matter ended well, never offer anything of value for a review (that runs into the FTC rule and Rule 7.2), thank positive reviewers without case details, and reply to negative reviews carefully or not at all. Full templates live in our guide on [how lawyers get Google reviews ethically](/blog/lawyer-reviews-ethics-google), and the cross-industry [negative review response guide](/blog/professional-services-negative-review-responses) covers fake reviews and when to flag them.

## Step 5: Local Services Ads vs Google Search ads

Google Local Services Ads (LSA) are available in the US for many legal categories, including personal injury, family, criminal, DUI, estate, immigration, bankruptcy, business and real estate law. You pay per lead rather than per click, and firms must pass Google's screening and verification to show the "Google Verified" badge.

| | Local Services Ads | Google Search ads (PPC) |
|---|---|---|
| You pay for | Each lead (call, message or booking) | Each click |
| Placement | Top of results, with badge | Top and bottom of results |
| Setup | License and background screening by Google | Account, keywords, landing pages |
| Ranking inputs | Reviews, responsiveness, proximity, hours, budget | Bid, ad quality, landing page experience |
| Biggest risk | Missed calls weaken your ranking and waste leads | Paying for clicks that never contact you |
| Best for | Firms that can answer the phone fast | Firms with strong landing pages and a manager |

LSA rewards responsiveness, which brings us back to intake. A firm that misses LSA calls pays for the clicks it does answer and loses ground in placement. ## Step 6: Content that answers real legal questions

People search questions long before they search for a lawyer. "Do I need a lawyer for a minor car accident?" "How is child support calculated in Georgia?" "What happens at an arraignment?" Good answers bring in people early and make your firm the obvious next call.

What works: answer one question per article, specific to your state; write from experience; name the attorney author and link their bio; end with a clear next step; and never promise outcomes or give advice for a specific person's facts.

## Step 8: Email nurture and referrals

Many legal decisions take weeks or months. An estate plan gets postponed. A business owner thinks about a contract dispute for a quarter. A divorce consultation happens long before a filing. Email keeps you present without chasing.

Three sequences cover most firms: a consultation follow-up (same day, day 3, day 10, day 30), a monthly nurture email for prospects who haven't hired yet, and a quarterly update to former clients and referral partners such as CPAs, financial planners and other lawyers.

Keep emails general and never reference a specific client's matter in a marketing email. Follow CAN-SPAM: honest From and subject lines, your physical address and a working unsubscribe honored within 10 business days. Our [email marketing laws guide](/blog/email-marketing-laws-by-country) covers the details, and the [email deliverability guide](/blog/email-deliverability-spf-dkim-dmarc-guide) explains why your firm's emails may be landing in spam.

Referrals still drive a big share of new matters for many firms. Most state rules let you build reciprocal referral relationships with other lawyers within limits, but paying for referrals is restricted. Check your state's version of Rule 7.2 before setting anything up.

## KPIs: measuring cost per signed case

Leads and clicks are inputs. Signed cases are the output. The core metric is simple:

**Cost per signed case = marketing spend for a source ÷ signed cases from that source.**

To calculate it, you need every lead tagged with its source, and every signed case traced back to that lead. That's tedious without a pipeline, which is why most firms guess.

::funnel Example: one month for a 3-attorney family law firm (illustrative) | Inquiries from all sources: 180 | Qualified after intake: 70 | Consultations held: 35 | Signed engagements: 14

In this made-up example, if the firm spent $7,000 on marketing that month, its blended cost per signed case would be $500. The more useful view splits it by source: LSA, organic search, referrals, directories and so on. You'll often find one source signing cases at a fraction of the cost of another.

Track these monthly:

| KPI | How to calculate | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Speed to first response | Minutes from inquiry to first human contact | The strongest predictor of who signs |
| Consultation rate | Consults ÷ qualified inquiries | Shows whether intake is converting |
| Signed rate | Signed ÷ consults | Shows fit, pricing and consult quality |
| Cost per signed case | Spend ÷ signed cases, by source | Tells you where to put the next dollar |
| Review velocity | New reviews per month vs top 3 competitors | Predicts Maps movement |
| AI and Maps visibility | Position for your main searches and prompts | Early warning before leads drop |

## Common mistakes law firms make

- **Buying more leads while missing calls.** Fix response first. It's cheaper.
- **Copying another state's ad template.** A disclaimer that works in Georgia may miss a required element in Texas or Florida.
- **Letting AI write unreviewed claims.** "Top-rated expert trial lawyer" might be four rule problems in four words.
- **No source tracking.** Without it, every budget decision is a guess.

> The firms that grow fastest usually aren't the ones with the biggest ad budgets. They answer first, follow up longer and know their cost per signed case.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best all-in-one AI marketing tool for lawyers?

There isn't one tool that does everything. Legal intake CRMs like Clio Grow or Lawmatics are strongest for intake workflows, while a marketing platform like Growvia covers SEO, AI-search visibility, competitor tracking, reviews monitoring, web leads and email. Most firms pair one marketing tool with their practice-management system.

### How much should a small law firm spend on marketing?

It depends on practice area, market and growth goals, and there's no universal percentage. Start from a target cost per signed case and the number of new cases you can handle, then work backward. Our guide to [marketing budgets by industry](/blog/marketing-budget-by-industry-local-business) shows the math.

### Can lawyers use AI to write marketing content?

Yes, and it saves time, but a lawyer must review every piece for accuracy and compliance with your state's advertising rules. Watch for words like "expert," "specialist," "best" and unqualified past results, which AI tools often add.

### Are Google Local Services Ads worth it for law firms?

They can be, especially for practice areas where clients call urgently, such as criminal defense, PI and family law. They work best when you answer leads quickly, because responsiveness affects placement and missed calls waste spend.

### Is it ethical for lawyers to ask clients for Google reviews?

Generally yes, as long as you ask without offering anything of value, don't ask only happy clients and don't write or edit reviews for clients. Replies must protect client confidentiality, as ABA Formal Opinion 496 explains. Check your state bar's guidance too.

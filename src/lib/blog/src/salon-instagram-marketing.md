Salon Instagram marketing has one job: fill your appointment book. Followers and likes feel good, but a salon with 2,000 local followers who book is doing better than one with 40,000 followers scattered around the world who never will.

This guide shows you how to run Instagram as a booking channel. You'll get five content pillars, reel ideas you can film between clients, the consent rules for before-and-after photos, how to use location and hashtags for local reach, and a simple process for turning DMs into confirmed appointments.

## Set up your profile to convert

Before you post anything, make your profile do the selling. Someone who lands there from a reel or a friend's tag should know in five seconds what you do, where you are and how to book.

### Profile essentials

- **Account type.** Use a professional (business) account so you get insights, contact buttons and booking options.
- **Name field.** This field is searchable. Use your salon name plus your main service and city, such as "Willow Room | Balayage & Curls Austin."
- **Bio.** One line on your specialty, one on your location or neighborhood, one call to action. "Lived-in color and curly cuts. South Congress, Austin. Book below."
- **Booking button and link.** Connect your booking platform if Instagram supports it, or add a direct booking link. Avoid link pages with fifteen options. Booking should be the first link.
- **Contact options.** Add your address, phone and email so people can get directions or call.
- **Highlights.** Services and prices, Team, Reviews, Before and after, FAQ, Location and parking.

## Five content pillars for salons

Content pillars stop you from staring at a blank screen. Rotate through five themes and you'll always have something useful to post.

| Pillar | What it shows | Example posts | Best format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transformations | Proof of skill | Before and after color, extensions, lash sets | Carousel or reel |
| Process | Craft and care | Blowout in 30 seconds, toner reveal, nail art time-lapse | Reel |
| Education | Expertise | "Why your blonde turns brassy," "gel vs dip," "how often to facial" | Reel or carousel |
| People | Trust and personality | Meet the stylist, a day behind the chair, team training | Reel or photo |
| Availability and offers | Bookings now | "Two openings Thursday," new service launch, gift card reminder | Story |

A useful weekly rhythm is three to five feed posts or reels plus daily stories. Consistency matters more than volume. A small salon posting three solid reels a week will usually beat one that posts daily for a month and then disappears.

## Reels that get local reach

Reels are how new people find you on Instagram. They're shown to non-followers, which makes them your main discovery tool.

### Reel ideas you can film between clients

1. **The reveal.** Start with the "before" for one second, then the turn to camera. Hook in the first two seconds.
2. **Mistakes to avoid.** "Three things that make your color fade faster." Talk to camera while working.
3. **Satisfying process.** Sectioning, foils, a scalp massage, a nail being shaped. Quiet, close-up and calming.
4. **Answer a real question.** "Can I go from box black to blonde in one visit?" Honest answers build trust.
5. **Price transparency.** "What $180 gets you at our salon." Clients appreciate knowing.
6. **A day in the life.** Opening the salon, coffee, first client, cleanup. Shows your culture.
7. **Stylist intros.** Fifteen seconds per person: specialty, years of experience, favorite service.
8. **Seasonal trends.** Fall color palettes, wedding-season updos, summer hair protection.

### Filming tips

- Film vertically, near a window or ring light, with a clean background.
- Put the result or the hook in the first two seconds.
- Add on-screen text, because many people watch with sound off.
- Keep most reels short. You can go longer for education, but earn every second.
- Use audio carefully. Business accounts often have limited access to popular music for licensing reasons, so use Instagram's commercial-use audio library or your own voice.

## Before-and-after photos: consent first

Transformation posts are your strongest proof, but the photo is of a real person. Get permission before you post.

### What good consent looks like

- **Written, not verbal.** Add a photo consent section to your intake form or consultation card. A signature or checkbox is far better than "Is it okay if I post this?" in the chair.
- **Specific.** Say where you might post (Instagram, Facebook, your website, ads) and whether the face will be shown.
- **Optional.** Make it clear the service doesn't depend on saying yes. Offer a "hair only, no face" option.
- **Revocable.** Tell clients they can ask you to remove a post at any time, and honor it quickly.
- **Stored.** Keep consent with the client record in your booking software.

Tag clients only if they want to be tagged. Some love it, others don't want their account linked to your salon.

> Warning: if you offer medical aesthetic treatments such as Botox, fillers or laser, before-and-after posts carry extra rules on claims, consent and patient privacy. Read our [med spa marketing guide](/blog/med-spa-marketing-guide) before posting those.

### Keep results honest

Don't over-edit transformation photos. Heavy filters, smoothing or color correction that misrepresents the result can mislead clients and set expectations you can't meet. Shoot in the same light for before and after, and note when a result took more than one session.

## Influencers, collaborations and the FTC

Working with local creators can be great for salons. A creator gets a free service, you get content and reach. But the FTC treats a free service as a material connection.

The [FTC's Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) say that when someone promotes your business and has received something of value, the connection must be clearly disclosed. The FTC's [Disclosures 101 for social media influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers) recommends simple, clear labels like "ad" or "sponsored" placed where people will see them, not buried in hashtags. Instagram's paid partnership label helps, but it's good practice to make the disclosure clear in the post too.

Two more rules to keep straight:

- Don't ask creators or clients to post reviews on Google in exchange for free services. [Google's Maps content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) bans incentives for reviews.
- The [FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) bans fake social-media influence, such as buying followers or fake engagement. Don't buy followers. Besides the legal risk, fake followers make your real reach worse.

## Local hashtags, location tags and keywords

Instagram works more like a search engine than it used to. Captions, on-screen text, your name field and location tags all help people nearby find you.

### What to do on every post

- **Tag your location.** Use your salon's location, or your neighborhood for content shot elsewhere.
- **Use searchable words in captions.** "Curly cut in Austin" or "balayage specialist in South Congress" in natural sentences helps.
- **Use a few relevant hashtags.** Instagram has reduced how much hashtags matter and has limited how many you can use, so check the current limit. Three to five specific tags beat thirty generic ones.
- **Mix hashtag types.** One for your city or neighborhood (#austinhair, #southcongress), one for your service (#curlycut, #lashextensions), and optionally your own salon tag.

| Hashtag type | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| City or neighborhood | #brooklynhairsalon, #parkslopenails | Reaches people near you |
| Service | #balayage, #curlyhaircut, #browlamination | Matches what people search |
| Specialty plus place | #austincurlyhair | Small but highly relevant |
| Branded | #willowroomhair | Collects client posts in one place |

Avoid huge generic tags like #hair or #beauty. They're crowded and attract people who will never visit.

## DMs to bookings: the process that matters most

Most salons lose Instagram bookings in the DMs. A client asks "How much for highlights?" at 8 p.m., gets a reply the next afternoon, and has already booked elsewhere.

### Set up a DM process

1. **Assign an owner.** One person checks DMs at set times (for example 10 a.m., 1 p.m., 4 p.m. and 7 p.m.) or whenever the front desk is free.
2. **Save quick replies.** For prices, consultations, parking, cancellation policy and your booking link.
3. **Reply with a range and a next step.** Never just a price.
4. **Move to booking fast.** Send the link, or offer two times and book it for them.
5. **Follow up once.** If they go quiet, one friendly nudge the next day.

## Measure what matters

Instagram insights show plenty of numbers. Focus on the ones tied to bookings.

- **Profile visits and link or button taps.** Shows whether content drives action.
- **Reach to non-followers.** Shows whether reels are bringing in new people.
- **Saves and shares.** Good signals of useful content.
- **DMs that became bookings.** Track it manually with a tally, or ask "How did you hear about us?" at booking.
- **Response time to DMs.** Aim for under an hour during open hours.

Also connect Instagram to the rest of your growth system. New clients from Instagram still need to be rebooked at the chair. ## Common salon Instagram mistakes

- **No clear booking path.** A beautiful grid with no booking button wastes attention.
- **Slow DM replies.** Speed wins clients.
- **Posting without consent.** It damages trust and can cost you a client.
- **Buying followers.** Banned by the FTC rule and bad for reach.
- **Only posting finished looks.** Process and education build expertise.
- **Ignoring stories.** Stories are where you fill last-minute openings.
- **Using generic hashtags.** Local and specific beats big and broad.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a salon post on Instagram?

Three to five feed posts or reels per week plus daily stories is a realistic target for most salons. Consistency over months matters more than posting every day for a few weeks.

### Do I need client permission to post before-and-after photos?

Yes. Get written consent, specify where you might post and whether faces will show, and let clients withdraw consent at any time. Keep the consent with their client record.

### What hashtags should a hair salon use?

Use a few specific tags: your city or neighborhood, the service shown, and a branded tag. Check Instagram's current hashtag limit, and skip generic tags like #hair.

### How do I get bookings from Instagram DMs?

Reply within the hour, answer price questions with a range plus a next step, and offer two specific times or a direct booking link. Saved quick replies and a shared inbox help a busy team keep up.

### Do influencers have to disclose free salon services?

Yes. The FTC treats a free service as a material connection, so creators should clearly label posts as an ad or sponsored. Instagram's paid partnership label helps.

### Should my salon use reels or regular posts?

Use both. Reels are best for reaching new local people, while carousels and photos work well for detailed transformations and education your followers save.

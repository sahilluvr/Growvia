Home buyers often meet their lender through someone else: the agent showing them houses, the builder selling the lot, the accountant reviewing their income. Mortgage brokers who build strong relationships with those professionals get a steady flow of pre-approvals without bidding for every online lead.

This guide covers which partners matter, how to approach them and how to stay on the right side of settlement-service rules while you do. For agents' side of the funnel, see our [real estate lead follow-up system](/blog/real-estate-lead-follow-up-system).


## Why referral partners matter more than online leads

Online mortgage leads are expensive and crowded. The same borrower fills in a form and hears from several lenders within minutes, and the conversation quickly becomes a race to the lowest quoted rate. Conversion is low, and the borrowers who do close often have little loyalty.

Referred borrowers are different. When a real estate agent tells a buyer "call Maria, she's the one I trust", the buyer arrives already inclined to work with you. They're further along in their decision, often under contract or about to start searching seriously, and they want the agent's recommendation to work out. That trust makes the conversation about the right loan rather than only the lowest rate.

Referral relationships also repeat. An active agent works with many buyers a year, and if every one of them has a smooth experience with you, the agent sends the next one too. Builders sell homes in phases and need lenders who can manage longer timelines. CPAs see self-employed clients every year who struggle to qualify with lenders who don't understand their income.

Finally, referral networks protect you when the market shifts. When rates rise and purchase volume drops, agents and builders still close deals, and they send them to the lenders they trust most. Brokers with strong relationships tend to keep a steadier pipeline through slow markets.

## Know the rules

Federal rules on settlement services generally prohibit giving or accepting anything of value for referrals of mortgage business. Co-marketing is allowed only when costs are split fairly according to each party's share. State licensing rules add more. Keep relationships based on service quality, and run co-marketing past your compliance team.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Anything that looks like a referral payment.** Including gifts tied to referrals.
- **Silence during the loan process.** Agents judge you on communication.
- **Overpromising rates.** Quote accurately and explain what changes them.

## Your first meeting with an agent

1. What slows down closings most often for your buyers?
2. How quickly do you need pre-approvals when buyers start searching?
3. How do you like updates during the loan process?
4. What types of buyers do you work with most?
5. What's gone wrong with lenders in the past?
6. Would a first-time buyer workshop be useful for your clients?

Bring your milestone update messages, a sample pre-approval letter and the first-time buyer guide. Agents want to see how you'll make them look good to their clients.

## Handling common objections

| What they say | What it usually means | A helpful response |
|---|---|---|
| "I have a preferred lender." | Existing loyalty. | Offer to be the backup for files that don't fit or busy weeks. |
| "Online lenders are cheaper." | Rate comparison. | Explain how local service and on-time closings protect deals. |
| "My buyers choose their lender." | They influence, not decide. | Offer materials they can share when buyers ask. |
| "I don't want to be pushy." | Concern about recommending. | Suggest they share a few names including yours. |
| "Send me your rate sheet." | Price focus. | Share what affects rates and offer a scenario for a typical buyer. |

## Preparing your business

Agents and buyers will look you up. Make sure your website explains loan programs, the pre-approval process and how to apply. Keep licensing information visible. Prepare a first-time buyer guide, a self-employed borrower checklist and sample milestone updates. Make sure you or your team can answer calls in the evening and on weekends, when buyers are house hunting. Review all marketing and co-marketing with your compliance team.

## Programs that open partner doors

**Fast pre-approvals.** A written pre-approval within a day or two for most buyers gives agents confidence.

**First-time buyer education.** Workshops and guides help agents serve newer buyers who need more support.

**Self-employed borrower expertise.** CPAs refer clients to lenders who understand complex income documentation.

**New construction financing.** Builders need lenders who can manage longer timelines and rate considerations.

**Relocation support.** Remote-friendly processes for transferring employees and their relocation providers.

## A worked example

Here's how an agent relationship might develop. The details are illustrative.

You email an agent who works with many first-time buyers, offering a buyer guide. She replies to your follow-up about milestone updates because her current lender rarely communicates. A week later, she asks you to pre-approve a nervous first-time buyer.

You issue a written pre-approval the next day and walk the buyer through closing costs. Once they're under contract, you text the agent and buyer at each milestone. The loan closes on time, and the buyer leaves a glowing review mentioning both of you.

The agent sends two more buyers in the next month and invites you to co-host a first-time buyer workshop, with costs split according to each party's share. Several workshop attendees become clients, and other agents in her office start sending files.

## Keeping relationships strong

After each closing, thank the agent and share how it went. Check in regularly with useful market updates, without overwhelming them. Offer workshops or training a couple of times a year. Keep careful records of co-marketing costs and make sure arrangements follow settlement-service rules. Above all, keep closing on time and communicating proactively, which is what agents remember.

## Making the borrower experience referral-worthy

Agents and builders refer lenders based on what their clients tell them afterwards. A smooth borrower experience is your best marketing. Explain the process at the start, set expectations about documents and timelines, and provide a checklist so borrowers know exactly what to send. Respond to questions quickly, especially in the evening when buyers are anxious. Warn borrowers early about common mistakes, such as opening new credit, changing jobs or making large unexplained deposits before closing.

After closing, thank the borrower, ask for a review and stay in touch with useful updates, such as annual mortgage reviews. Happy borrowers refer friends and family, and they tell their agent about the experience, which strengthens the agent relationship.

## Working with builders on new construction

New construction loans often involve longer timelines, rate decisions and coordination with the builder's schedule. Builders want lenders who understand those timelines, communicate with their sales teams and keep buyers informed about rate options and deadlines. Offer builders a simple update process, educational sessions for their sales staff and clear guidance for buyers on what to expect. Builders with multiple communities can become significant referral partners when buyers have consistently good experiences.

## Staying compliant while building relationships

Mortgage marketing is regulated, and referral relationships must follow settlement-service rules. Keep co-marketing arrangements documented, split costs fairly, avoid gifts tied to referrals and review marketing materials with your compliance team. Include required licensing information in communications. Building relationships on service quality, rather than incentives, keeps you on the right side of the rules and earns more durable trust.

## Educating first-time buyers

First-time buyers often feel overwhelmed by the mortgage process. Offer clear education: how pre-approval works, what affects rates, what closing costs include and what to avoid during the process. Use short guides, checklists and simple examples. Hosting educational workshops with agents helps buyers feel confident and introduces you to buyers before they choose a lender. Educated buyers have smoother loans, and agents love lenders who help their clients understand the process.

## Working with self-employed borrowers

Self-employed borrowers often struggle with lenders who don't understand variable income or business deductions. Learn the documentation requirements for these borrowers and explain them clearly. Coordinate with their accountants when appropriate and with consent. Brokers who handle self-employed files well earn referrals from CPAs and from the borrowers themselves, who often know other business owners in the same position.

## Responding fast when buyers are ready

Buyers often decide to act on evenings and weekends, after a showing or an open house. Agents need lenders who respond then. Set up a simple system for after-hours inquiries: a dedicated line, quick acknowledgment messages and next-morning follow-up at the latest. Speed at the moment a buyer is ready to make an offer is what agents remember most, and it often decides which lender they recommend next time.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do mortgage brokers get referrals from realtors?

Offer fast pre-approvals, proactive updates and on-time closings. Start as a backup lender on one buyer, then earn a place on the agent's preferred list.

### Can mortgage brokers pay agents for referrals?

Generally no. Federal settlement rules prohibit paying for referrals of mortgage business. Co-marketing must be split fairly; check with compliance.

### Why partner with CPAs as a mortgage broker?

Self-employed buyers often need a lender who understands complex income, and CPAs are their trusted advisor.

### What events help mortgage brokers find clients?

Educational first-time buyer workshops and self-employed borrower sessions, co-hosted with agents or CPAs.

### How do mortgage brokers stand out to agents?

Fast pre-approvals, proactive milestone updates, on-time closings and help educating first-time buyers.

### Can mortgage brokers co-market with agents?

Co-marketing is possible when costs are split fairly according to each party's share and arrangements follow settlement-service rules. Check with your compliance team.

### Why do self-employed borrowers need specialized lenders?

Their income documentation is often more complex, and lenders experienced with it can guide them through qualification more smoothly.

### How often should mortgage brokers contact agents?

Regularly but lightly: after each closing, plus useful updates or events every month or two.

### How long does a mortgage pre-approval take?

It depends on the borrower and documents. Many brokers can issue a written pre-approval quickly once documents are received.

### What should buyers avoid before closing?

Opening new credit, large unexplained deposits, changing jobs and big purchases on credit, unless discussed with the lender first.

### How do mortgage brokers work with new construction buyers?

By explaining timelines, rate options and deadlines, and coordinating closely with the builder.

### Can mortgage brokers sponsor agent events?

Joint events must follow settlement-service rules on fair cost sharing. Check with your compliance team.

### How do brokers keep in touch with past borrowers?

With occasional useful updates, such as annual mortgage reviews, without overwhelming them.

### What makes agents stop referring a lender?

Missed closings, poor communication and borrowers who complain about the experience.

### What documents do self-employed borrowers usually need?

Often tax returns, business financial statements and bank statements, depending on the loan program.

### How can first-time buyers prepare for a mortgage?

By checking credit, saving for down payment and closing costs, gathering documents and avoiding new debt.

### Should mortgage brokers host workshops?

Educational workshops help buyers and build relationships with agents, following co-marketing rules.

### How do brokers work with accountants?

By coordinating on income documentation with the borrower's consent.

### What affects mortgage rates?

Market conditions, credit, down payment, loan type and other factors specific to each borrower.

### Do mortgage brokers need to work weekends?

Being reachable on evenings and weekends helps, because buyers often act after showings.

### How quickly should brokers respond to agent inquiries?

As quickly as possible, ideally the same day, especially when a buyer is preparing an offer.

### What do builders want from mortgage brokers?

Reliable financing for long timelines, clear buyer communication and coordination with the sales team.

### How do brokers ask agents for feedback?

After each closing, ask what went well and what could improve, and act on the answers.

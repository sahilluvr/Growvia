Google Local Services Ads can be the fastest way for a contractor to make the phone ring. They sit at the very top of Google results with a verification badge, a star rating and a call button, and you pay per lead instead of per click. For plumbers, HVAC techs, electricians and roofers, that's an easy pitch.

It's also easy to waste money on them. Contractors sign up, set a budget, and then watch leads arrive for jobs they don't do, in towns they don't serve, at hours nobody answers. Others never get through screening because a document is out of date.

This guide walks you through how Local Services Ads (LSA) work for home services in 2026: getting verified, picking job types and service areas, what Google counts as a lead and how credits work, what decides your ranking, how to plan a budget, and how LSA fits with your Business Profile and regular Search ads. It's one part of our full guide to [AI marketing for home services](/blog/ai-marketing-for-home-services).

## How Local Services Ads work

LSA is a separate ad product from regular Google Ads. You don't pick keywords or write ad copy. Google builds your ad from your profile: business name, rating, review count, years in business, hours, service area and verification status. When a homeowner searches something like "water heater repair near me" or "roofer in Plano," Google shows a few LSA advertisers at the top.

The homeowner can call you, send a message, or in some categories request a booking. Each of those can count as a lead, and that's what you pay for.

::flow How an LSA lead reaches you | Homeowner searches a service | Google shows verified pros | Homeowner calls, messages or books | You're charged for a valid lead | You respond and win or lose the job

### Which home services are eligible

Most home service trades are eligible in the US, including plumbers, electricians, HVAC contractors, roofers, pest control, cleaning, locksmiths, garage door, lawn care and many more. Availability varies by city and category, so check eligibility for your ZIP codes when you sign up.

### The badge is now "Google Verified"

If you remember the green "Google Guaranteed" checkmark, it's gone. In late 2025, Google merged its older badges (Google Guaranteed, Google Screened and License verified by Google) into one badge called **Google Verified**, according to Google's [About Google Verified page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/9376654). Google also discontinued the Money Back Guarantee tied to the old badge. If a homeowner asks whether you're "Google Guaranteed," the accurate answer is that you're Google Verified.

## Getting verified: screening for contractors

To run LSA, your business has to pass Google's screening. The checks depend on your category and state. Google's [screening and verification page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12174778) lists the requirements, which for home services typically include:

::check Documents to have ready before you apply | Trade license for each state or city that requires one | Certificate of general liability insurance | Owner details for the business background check | Field technician details if your category requires tech checks | Business registration documents where required | A verified Google Business Profile to link

### Tips to get through faster

- **Match names exactly.** The business name on your license, insurance certificate and Business Profile should match. Mismatches are a common cause of delays.
- **Check insurance requirements.** Make sure your coverage meets Google's minimums for your category and that the certificate is current.
- **Respond quickly to requests.** Google or its screening partners may ask for more information. Slow replies stretch the process.
- **Set renewal reminders.** An expired license or insurance certificate can pause your ads. Put renewal dates in your calendar and upload new documents before they lapse.

Screening usually takes a few weeks, so start before your busy season, not during it.

> Start LSA screening at least a month before your peak season. An HVAC company that applies during the first heat wave may not be live until it's over.

## Job types and service areas

Once you're verified, setup decisions control which leads you get. This is where most wasted spend comes from.

### Pick job types carefully

LSA lets you choose the specific job types you accept within your category. A plumber might accept water heater repair, drain cleaning and leak repair but turn off sewer line replacement if they don't do it. An electrician might accept panel upgrades and EV charger installation but switch off low-voltage work.

Turn off anything you don't do or don't want. Every lead for a job you can't take is money spent and time lost. Review your job types every few months as your crew and equipment change.

### Set realistic service areas

Choose the cities or ZIP codes you can actually reach profitably. If your techs won't drive 50 minutes for a $150 service call, don't include towns that far out. You can often get more out of a tighter area where you're closer to customers, since proximity is a ranking factor.

### Match your hours to reality

Set business hours when someone actually answers. You can choose to receive leads only during those hours. If you promise 24/7 and send night calls to voicemail, you'll pay for leads you can't convert and damage your responsiveness score.

## What counts as a lead, and how credits work

You pay per lead, not per click or impression. Google's [How leads work page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7195435) explains what counts. In the US, a lead can be:

- A phone call you answer.
- A message sent through your ad.
- A voicemail, or a missed call you return that connects.
- A booking request, in categories where booking is available.

### Invalid leads and credits

Google's systems aim not to charge for leads they judge invalid, such as spam, wrong category or out-of-area requests. Charged leads can also be reassessed and credited later if found to be low quality. You can rate leads and give feedback in your dashboard, which may lead to more credits.

Treat credits as a bonus, not part of your budget. Review leads weekly so feedback is timely, and keep notes on patterns. If you keep getting leads for a job type you don't do, the fix is turning that job type off, not disputing leads forever.

### What a lead costs

Cost per lead varies widely by trade, job type, city and competition. A roofing replacement lead in a competitive metro may cost much more than a pest control lead in a small town. Google shows estimated lead costs in your account when you set up, which is the most reliable guide for your market. Be wary of any blog or salesperson quoting a national "average cost per lead" without a source.

## What decides who shows first

LSA ranking isn't only about how much you bid. Google's [About ad rankings page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305) lists several factors, including:

- **Proximity** to the customer's location.
- **Review score and number of reviews.**
- **Responsiveness** to customer inquiries and requests. Google notes that missed calls may hurt it.
- **Business hours**, whether you're open when the search happens.
- Whether you've set up **messaging and booking**.
- Your **bid** and budget.
- Completed **verification checks**, photos and profile details.

::compare What helps vs hurts your LSA placement | Helps: answering calls live; steady new reviews; real hours; messaging turned on; tight service area | Hurts: missed calls and slow replies; few or old reviews; 24/7 hours with nobody answering; job types you never accept; expired documents

### Responsiveness is the factor you control most

You can't move closer to every customer, and reviews take time. But you can answer more calls starting tomorrow. If you're missing calls during busy hours, fix that before raising your bid. ### Reviews feed LSA and Maps together

Reviews on your Google Business Profile count toward LSA ranking. A steady review process helps both your ads and your Map Pack position. ## Bidding and budget planning

LSA gives you two levers: your budget and your bidding approach.

### Budget

You set an average weekly budget. Google tries to get you as many leads as it can within that. Some weeks you'll spend a bit more, some a bit less.

### Bidding options

You can typically let Google maximize leads within your budget, or set a maximum amount you're willing to pay per lead. Maximizing leads is simpler for new accounts. Setting a per-lead cap gives more control once you know which leads turn into profitable jobs. Check your account for the options available in your category.

### Plan around your season

Most trades have seasons. Plan budgets to match demand and your crew's capacity, not a flat number all year.

::bars Example: weekly LSA budget plan for an HVAC company in a hot climate (illustrative) | Jan: 300 | Mar: 400 | May: 700 | Jul: 900 * | Sep: 500 | Nov: 450

These numbers are made up to show the shape of a seasonal plan. Your real budget depends on your lead costs, capacity and close rate. Our [HVAC seasonal marketing plan](/blog/hvac-marketing-seasonal-plan) and [roofing storm season guide](/blog/roofing-marketing-storm-season) show how demand moves for those trades.

### Do the math on booked jobs, not leads

The number that matters is cost per booked job. Work it out like this:

::funnel Example: from LSA spend to booked jobs in one month (illustrative) | Leads charged: 60 | Leads contacted: 54 | Quotes or visits: 36 | Booked jobs: 24

If those 60 leads cost $2,400, your cost per lead is $40 but your cost per booked job is $100. Compare that to your average job value and margin. If the math works, you can raise your budget. If it doesn't, look at where leads drop off, often unanswered calls or slow quotes, before cutting spend.

## How LSA fits with GBP and Search ads

LSA is one layer of the search results page. The Map Pack, powered by your Google Business Profile, sits below it, and regular Search ads and organic results sit around it. Most successful contractors use more than one.

| | Local Services Ads | Google Search Ads | Google Business Profile (Map Pack) |
|---|---|---|---|
| How you pay | Per lead | Per click | Free, costs time |
| Control | Job types, areas, hours, budget | Keywords, ad copy, landing pages, bids | Categories, services, photos, posts, reviews |
| Trust signal | Google Verified badge | "Sponsored" label | Reviews and photos |
| Ranking drivers | Proximity, reviews, responsiveness, bid | Bid, ad quality, landing page | Relevance, distance, prominence |
| Speed | Live after screening, often weeks | Days | Builds over months |
| Best for | Steady calls in eligible job types | High-value jobs, specific services, extra coverage | Long-term, lower-cost leads |

### A common setup

- **LSA** for core service calls in your main area.
- **Search ads** for high-value work where you want control over keywords and landing pages, such as "tankless water heater installation" or "whole home generator."
- **Google Business Profile** working constantly underneath, with reviews and photos that also help LSA.

Your Business Profile should be in good shape before you start LSA, since it's linked to your account and its reviews feed your ranking. ## Tracking what LSA actually earns you

LSA's dashboard shows leads, calls and spend. It doesn't know which leads became paying jobs. You need to connect the two.

- **Log every LSA lead in your field service software** (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro) with the source marked as LSA.
- **Mark the outcome:** booked, quoted, lost, not a fit.
- **Compare monthly** cost per booked job across LSA, Search ads and organic.
- **Use call tracking** like CallRail for your website and Search ads, but keep your LSA calls in LSA's own reporting, since Google provides the number on the ad.

## Common LSA mistakes contractors make

- **Accepting every job type.** You'll pay for leads you can't serve.
- **Service areas that are too wide.** Long drives eat profit, and proximity affects ranking anyway.
- **Missing calls.** It loses the job and hurts responsiveness.
- **Letting documents expire.** Ads pause until you upload new ones.
- **Judging LSA on cost per lead alone.** Cost per booked job is what pays the bills.
- **Setting it and forgetting it.** Review leads weekly, rate them, and adjust job types and budgets with the season.
- **Ignoring reviews.** A competitor with more recent reviews can outrank a higher bid.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much do Google Local Services Ads cost for contractors?

You pay per lead, and the cost varies by trade, job type, city and competition. Google shows estimated lead costs in your account when you set up. Judge LSA on cost per booked job, not just cost per lead.

### Is Google Guaranteed still available?

No. In late 2025 Google replaced Google Guaranteed, Google Screened and License verified by Google with a single Google Verified badge, and discontinued the Money Back Guarantee tied to the old badge. You still pass screening to earn the new badge.

### How long does LSA verification take?

Google says screening usually takes a few weeks, depending on your category, state and how quickly you respond to requests. Have your license, insurance and owner details ready before you apply.

### Can I dispute a bad Local Services Ads lead?

Google aims not to charge for invalid leads and may credit low-quality leads automatically after review. You can also rate leads and give feedback in your dashboard, which may lead to more credits. Turn off job types that keep producing bad leads.

### What affects my ranking in Local Services Ads?

Google lists proximity, review score and count, responsiveness, business hours, messaging and booking setup, your bid and budget, and completed verification checks. Missed calls can hurt responsiveness, so answering coverage matters.

### Should I run LSA and Google Search ads at the same time?

Many contractors do. LSA works well for core service calls, while Search ads give keyword and landing page control for high-value jobs. Your Google Business Profile supports both through reviews and the Map Pack.

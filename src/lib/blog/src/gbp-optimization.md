When someone nearby searches "dentist near me" or "best biryani in Sector 70", Google rarely shows them a website first. It shows a map and three business listings. Those listings come from Google Business Profile, the free profile that used to be called Google My Business. For most local businesses, it is the most valuable online asset they own.

A well-kept profile brings calls, direction requests and walk-ins without a single ad. A neglected one quietly sends those customers to the competitor down the road. The difference is rarely budget. It is usually a wrong category, missing hours, five blurry photos and a pile of unanswered reviews.

This guide takes you through every part of the profile in the order you should fix it, including recent feature changes, suspension risks and a full checklist.

How does Google pick those three listings? Its own page on [how local ranking works](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) names three factors.

- **Relevance:** how well your profile matches the search. Complete, accurate information helps Google judge this.
- **Distance:** how far your business is from the searcher, or from the location they typed.
- **Prominence:** how well known you are. Reviews, ratings and links to your website all feed into it.

You can't change distance without moving. Almost everything below improves relevance or prominence. Google also says there is no way to pay for a better local ranking, so ignore anyone selling "guaranteed top 3 on Maps".

> Think of your profile as a set of answers: what is this, where is it, when is it open, and is it any good?

## Step 1: Claim, verify and fix your basics

An unverified profile is a weak profile. You can't see performance data, many edits won't go live, and anyone can suggest changes to your listing.

### Find or create your listing

Search your business name on Google Maps first. Google may already have a listing built from public data. If it exists, choose "Own this business?" and claim it. If not, create one at business.google.com. Never create a second profile for a business that already has one.

### Verification options

Google assigns the verification method; you can't choose it. The [official verification help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242) lists these options:

1. **Phone or SMS:** a code sent to your business number. Automated phone menus usually can't receive it.
2. **Email:** a link sent to an address linked to the profile.
3. **Video:** you show your location, signage and proof that you manage the business, often on a live call with Google support.
4. **Postcard:** a code mailed to your address. Most arrive within 14 days and expire after 30 days.

Some businesses must complete more than one method. Reviews can take up to five business days.

### Passing video verification

Video is where many owners get stuck. Plan it before you start.

- Start outside. Show your permanent signage, the street and a nearby landmark in one continuous shot.
- Walk inside and show equipment, stock or a workspace that proves you operate there.
- Show proof of management, such as unlocking the door or logging into your billing software.
- Service-area businesses can show a branded vehicle, tools and a document like a GST certificate or trade licence.

> Don't edit your name, address or category while verification is pending. It can reset the process.

### Name, address and phone (NAP)

NAP stands for Name, Address and Phone. These must match your real business and stay consistent everywhere online.

### Business name rules

Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say your name must match your real-world branding: signage, stationery, invoices. You may not add taglines, keywords, locations, phone numbers, hours or ALL CAPS words. For a physiotherapy clinic in Mohali:

| Wrong | Right |
| --- | --- |
| Best Physiotherapy Clinic Mohali – Back Pain Specialist | Align Physio Clinic |
| ALIGN PHYSIO 24x7 | Align Physio Clinic |
| Align Physio Clinic, Near Sector 70 Market | Align Physio Clinic |

Keyword-stuffed names are a common reason genuine businesses get suspended. Any short-term bump isn't worth the risk.

### Address and phone

Use your exact physical address, including shop or floor number. P.O. boxes and remote mailboxes aren't allowed. Use a local or mobile number you actually answer.

### Consistency across the web

Google compares your profile with your website, directories like Justdial, Practo, Zomato or Yelp, and your social pages. Small differences ("Shop 12, Phase 3B2" versus "SCO 12, 3B2") weaken trust over time. Choose one exact format and use it everywhere. ## Step 2: Choose categories carefully

Categories are one of the strongest relevance signals you control. You get one primary category and can add up to nine more.

### Primary category

Google's rule: pick the category that completes "This business **is** a...", not "This business **has** a...". A bakery in Pune that also serves coffee is a "Bakery", not a "Coffee shop". Be as specific as the list allows. "Physiotherapist" beats "Health consultant".

### Secondary categories

Add these only for genuine core parts of your business. The Pune bakery might add "Cake shop" and "Dessert shop". It shouldn't add "Wedding planner" just because it bakes wedding cakes.

### Research before you choose

Search your main service on Google Maps and open the top three competitors. Their primary category appears under the business name. Use this as research, never as a reason to add categories that don't describe you.

> If your main service changes, change your primary category. A salon that is now mostly a bridal makeup studio should say so.

## Step 3: Write a description and fill in every field

You get 750 characters. Google's help pages say not to include URLs or HTML, and not to focus on promotions, prices or sales.

### What to include

- What you do and who you serve, in the first sentence.
- What makes you different: years in business, qualifications, specialities, languages spoken.
- The main areas you serve, mentioned once, naturally.
- Practical details like parking, home visits or same-day service.

### Worked example

A weak description for a plumber in Austin: "We are the best plumbers in Austin. Plumbing, plumber Austin, drain cleaning Austin, cheap plumber. Call now for 20% off!"

A better one: "Family-run plumbing company serving Austin, Round Rock and Pflugerville since 2011. We handle leak repairs, water heater installs, drain cleaning and repiping for homes and small businesses. Every plumber is licensed and background-checked, and we give a written price before starting. Same-day appointments are usually available on weekdays."

The second holds more useful search terms, yet reads like a person wrote it.

### Services and products

Depending on your category, Google may suggest predefined services. Tick every one that applies, then add custom ones with short descriptions. A gym in Chandigarh might list "Personal training", "Group HIIT classes" and "Nutrition consultation".

Products suit retail, restaurants and D2C brands. Add a name, photo, price or range, and a one-line description. A clinic can list packages too, such as "Post-surgery rehab, 10 sessions".

### Attributes

Attributes are tags like "Wheelchair-accessible entrance", "Free Wi-Fi", "Women-owned" or "Appointment required". The options depend on your category and country. Some you set, others come from customer feedback. Set every one that is true and check back every few months, because Google adds new ones.

## Step 4: Set hours you actually keep

Wrong hours lose trust fast. If someone drives to your salon at 7pm because Google said you were open, expect a one-star review.

- **Regular hours:** enter customer-facing hours. If you close for lunch, split the day into two slots.
- **More hours:** use this for services on a different schedule, such as delivery hours or lab collection times.
- **Special hours:** use these for short exceptions like Diwali, Eid, Christmas or a local festival. Confirm holiday hours when Google prompts you, even if nothing changes.
- **Temporarily closed:** use this for closures of a week or longer, instead of a long run of special hours.

> Never mark yourself "Permanently closed" by accident. It is far harder to undo than it is to click.

## Step 5: Add photos and videos that sell

Google lists photos and videos as a way to improve your profile. Quality matters more than volume, but a steady flow of new images shows you are active.

### Technical requirements

Google's [photo and video guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862) recommend:

- **Photos:** JPG or PNG, between 10 KB and 5 MB, ideally 720 x 720 pixels or larger.
- **Videos:** up to 30 seconds, up to 75 MB, 720p or higher.
- **Quality:** in focus, well lit, without heavy filters or major edits.

### What to upload

- **Logo and cover:** your logo on a clean background, and a cover that shows your best side.
- **Exterior:** your shopfront in daylight, so first-time visitors recognise it.
- **Interior:** reception, seating, treatment rooms or a clean kitchen.
- **Work and products:** finished haircuts, plated dishes, completed renovations.
- **Team:** real faces build trust, especially for clinics, coaches and home services.

Aim for three to five new photos a month, shot on a phone in natural light. Real photos beat stock images, which Google may remove anyway.

## Step 6: Use posts, and know what has changed

Several features changed after 2024, and many older guides haven't caught up.

### Posts

Posts come in three types: **Updates**, **Offers** and **Events**. Each can include a photo or video and a button such as "Book" or "Call". Google archives posts older than six months unless you set a date range, and you can now schedule them ahead.

Google doesn't list posts as a ranking factor. They help people browsing your profile choose you. A gym announcing new evening batches or a restaurant promoting a festive menu are good examples. One or two posts a month is enough.

### Q&A has been replaced by AI answers

The old Questions and Answers section is gone. Google retired the Q&A API in November 2025. Its replacement, a Gemini-powered Google Maps feature called Ask Maps, began rolling out in March 2026, starting with mobile users in the US and India. [BrightLocal's summary of the change](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-business-profile-qa) notes that answers draw on your profile, reviews, website and other sources.

So the information you control must answer common questions directly. Put parking, booking rules and languages spoken into your description, services, attributes and website FAQ. Our guide to [getting recommended by ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity](/blog/get-recommended-by-chatgpt-gemini-perplexity) explains how AI systems pick up this information.

### Chat has ended

Google [discontinued Business Profile chat and call history](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14919056) on July 31, 2024. Quote requests through the profile ended at the same time. Make sure your phone number is right and your website contact form works. Google points eligible businesses to alternatives such as WhatsApp; our [WhatsApp Business API guide](/blog/whatsapp-business-api-guide-small-business) explains the setup.

## Step 7: Build and respond to reviews

Google names reviews as part of prominence and lists replying to them as a way to improve your profile. They also do your selling, because customers read them before choosing.

### Do

- Ask every happy customer, right after a good experience, using the short review link from your dashboard.
- Reply to every review within a few days, positive or negative.
- Be specific: "Thanks, Priya, glad your knee feels stronger" beats "Thanks for your feedback".
- Answer complaints calmly, move the conversation offline and never share private details.

### Don't

- Buy reviews, swap them with other businesses or write them yourself.
- Offer discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews. Google's policies prohibit this.
- Filter unhappy customers away from your review link.

Fake review activity can lead to removed reviews, warnings on your profile or suspension. For scripts and timing that work, read [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews).

## Step 8: Read your Performance insights

Performance data is only available for verified profiles. Google's performance help page lists these main metrics:

- **Views:** people who viewed your profile on Search and Maps, each counted once a day.
- **Searches:** the terms people used to find you, updated at the start of each month.
- **Calls, directions and website clicks:** the actions that usually become customers.
- **Bookings, menu clicks, product views and offers**, where they apply.

### Turning data into action

Check monthly and compare with the same month last year, since seasonal businesses swing a lot. The search terms report is the most useful part. If a dental clinic sees people finding it through "teeth aligners", it should add aligners as a service, post aligner photos and mention them in its description.

If calls are high but direction requests are low, check your map pin sits exactly on your door.

> Ask new callers "How did you find us?" It is the simplest way to know which calls really came from Google.

## Suspensions: what triggers them and how to recover

### Common triggers

- **Keyword stuffing in the business name.**
- **Virtual offices and co-working addresses.** The guidelines say these aren't eligible unless the space has permanent signage, is staffed, and receives customers during business hours.
- **P.O. boxes, or a home address shown** when customers don't visit you there.
- **Duplicate profiles** for the same business.
- **Fake or incentivised reviews.**
- **Big edits all at once** to name, address and category, especially right after verification.

### How to appeal

Use Google's Business Profile appeals tool, select the profile and submit evidence. Good evidence includes a business registration, licence, tax certificate or utility bill. The name and address on these documents should match your profile. Don't create a new profile while your appeal is pending.

## Service-area and multi-location businesses

### Service-area businesses

Plumbers, cleaners, electricians, home-visit physios and pest control firms serve customers at the customer's address.

- Hide your address if customers don't visit you. You still enter it for verification.
- Set service areas by city or postal code. The guidelines say they shouldn't extend beyond about two hours' drive from your base.
- Keep it realistic. The Austin plumber should list Austin and nearby cities, not all of Texas.
- Build a genuine page on your website for each main area you serve.

> Hiding your address doesn't stop you ranking. Google simply leans more on relevance and prominence, so reviews matter even more.

### Multi-location businesses

Each real location with its own staff and customer-facing hours can have its own profile.

- Use the same naming pattern for every branch, and the same primary category, as Google's guidelines require for chains.
- Link each profile to its own location page on your website, not the homepage.
- Give each branch its own local phone number where possible.
- Manage branches together in a business group, so holiday hours can be updated in one place.
- Add local business schema markup to each location page so Google can tie website and profile together.

## The complete GBP checklist

Work through this top to bottom. Most businesses can finish the setup in one focused afternoon.

| Area | What to check | How often |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verification | Verified, no duplicate listings | Once, then yearly |
| Name | Matches signage, no keywords or locations | Once |
| Address and pin | Exact address and pin, or hidden for service-area businesses | When you move |
| Phone and website | Number you answer, link to the right location page | Quarterly |
| Categories | Specific primary, only core secondaries | Quarterly |
| Description | Under 750 characters, useful, no links or sales pitch | Twice a year |
| Services and products | Every main service listed and described | Quarterly |
| Attributes | Every true attribute set | Quarterly |
| Hours | Regular, more hours and holiday hours confirmed | Before each holiday |
| Photos and videos | Logo, cover, exterior, interior, team, work | 3–5 photos a month |
| Posts | One or two updates, offers or events | Monthly |
| Reviews | Ask consistently, reply to every one | Weekly |
| Performance | Views, searches, calls and directions versus last year | Monthly |
| NAP consistency | Same details on website, directories and social pages | Twice a year |

### Keeping it up

The biggest gains come from the first proper setup. After that it is maintenance: new photos, holiday hours, a monthly post and review replies. Block 20 minutes on the same day each week.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to rank higher on Google Maps?

There's no fixed timeline. Fixing your category, name and hours can change visibility within weeks, because they are strong relevance signals. Prominence from reviews and links builds over months. Distance never changes, so you will always rank best close to your location.

### Is Google My Business the same as Google Business Profile?

Yes. Google renamed Google My Business to Google Business Profile in 2021. It is the same listing and the same features. You manage it from Google Search, Google Maps or business.google.com.

### Can I add keywords to my business name to rank better?

No. Google's guidelines forbid keywords, locations or taglines that aren't part of your real-world name. It is a common cause of suspension. Anyone can also suggest an edit removing them, which may trigger a review of your profile.

### Can I use a virtual office or co-working address?

Usually not. Google allows it only if the space has permanent signage, is staffed and receives customers during your hours. If you work from home and visit customers, set up as a service-area business and hide your address.

### What happened to Q&A and chat on Google Business Profile?

Chat and call history ended on July 31, 2024. The Q&A section has been phased out and replaced by Ask Maps, an AI feature that answers questions using your profile, reviews and website. Keep those sources accurate, because they now shape the answers customers see.

### How often should I update my Google Business Profile?

Review everything quarterly and make small updates weekly. Add photos monthly, post once or twice a month, reply to reviews within a few days and confirm holiday hours early. Regular, genuine activity keeps your information fresh for customers and for Google.

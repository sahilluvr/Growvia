In India, Brazil, Indonesia, much of Europe, the Middle East and Africa, WhatsApp isn't just a messaging app — it's where customers expect to ask questions, get quotes, confirm bookings and receive updates. A reply on WhatsApp often gets read within minutes, when an email might sit for days.

But there are rules. Send the wrong kind of message, message people who never agreed to hear from you, or blast too many at once, and your number can be restricted or banned. This guide explains how WhatsApp for business actually works — so you can use it to grow without risking the number your customers know.

## Three ways to use WhatsApp for business

| | WhatsApp (personal) | WhatsApp Business app | WhatsApp Business Platform (API) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who it's for | Friends and family | Solo owners and very small teams | Growing businesses and teams |
| Devices | Your phone | Your phone plus a few linked devices | Any number of team members through software |
| Business profile, catalogue, quick replies | No | Yes | Yes (through your software) |
| Automation and integrations | No | Basic (greeting and away messages) | Full — CRM, chatbots, order updates |
| Messaging many customers | Not appropriate | Broadcast lists (only people who saved your number receive them) | Approved templates to opted-in contacts |
| Cost | Free | Free | Charged per template message by Meta; replies in the 24-hour window are free |

**The WhatsApp Business app** is perfect for starting out: free, with a business profile, catalogue, labels and quick replies. Its limits appear as you grow — one phone number, a handful of devices, and no proper way to share conversations across a team or connect to your CRM.

**The WhatsApp Business Platform** (commonly called the WhatsApp Business API, or the Cloud API when hosted by Meta) lets software like Growvia send and receive messages for you. Your whole team can reply from one shared inbox, conversations are linked to leads, and you can send order confirmations, reminders and approved marketing messages at scale.

> One important detail: a phone number used on the API can't be used at the same time in the regular WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business app. Many businesses use a new or secondary number for the API, or migrate their existing business number deliberately.

## The 24-hour customer service window

This is the single most important rule to understand on the API.

- When a customer messages you, a **24-hour window** opens. Inside it, you can reply with **any message** — free text, photos, documents, voice notes — just like a normal chat.
- Every time the customer messages again, the window resets for another 24 hours.
- Once 24 hours pass without a message from the customer, you can only contact them with an **approved message template**.

This protects users from unwanted messages. For you, it means two things: reply fast (within the window replies are simple and free), and prepare templates for everything you'll need to send outside it.

## Message templates, explained

A template is a pre-approved message format. You write it once, submit it to Meta for review (usually approved within minutes to a day), and then send it to many people with personalised variables such as their name, order number or appointment time.

Templates fall into three categories:

- **Marketing** — offers, announcements, new products, re-engagement. ("Hi {{1}}, our Diwali menu is here — 15% off family meals this week.")
- **Utility** — updates about something the customer already did or asked for: order confirmations, delivery updates, appointment reminders, payment receipts. ("Hi {{1}}, your appointment is confirmed for {{2}} at {{3}}.")
- **Authentication** — one-time passcodes for logging in or verifying an account.

Choose the correct category. Meta reviews templates and may re-categorise or reject ones that look like marketing disguised as utility. Pricing also differs by category, so it matters.

### Writing templates that get approved

- Be clear who you are and why you're messaging.
- Use variables (`{{1}}`, `{{2}}`) for the parts that change and give realistic sample values when submitting.
- Avoid threatening or misleading language, and don't ask for sensitive data like card numbers.
- Add buttons where helpful: quick replies ("Yes, confirm" / "Reschedule"), a call button, or a link to your website.
- For marketing templates, give people an easy way to stop — a "Stop promotions" quick-reply button is common.
- Keep it short. WhatsApp is a chat, not a newsletter.

## Opt-in: the non-negotiable rule

WhatsApp requires that people **agree to receive messages from your business** before you send them templates. Good ways to collect opt-in:

- A checkbox on your website form: "Send me updates on WhatsApp" (unticked by default).
- A "Message us on WhatsApp" button or QR code — when customers message first, you're clearly welcome to reply.
- At checkout or booking: "Get your confirmation and reminders on WhatsApp?"
- In person, with clear wording, recorded in your CRM.

Keep a record of who opted in and when. Never import a purchased list of phone numbers or message people who only gave you their number for a different purpose.

## What it costs

On the API, Meta charges for **template messages**, with rates depending on the **category** (marketing, utility, authentication) and the **country** of the recipient. Replies you send within the 24-hour customer service window are free, and Meta has made utility templates sent inside an open window free as well. Rates change from time to time, so check Meta's current pricing page for your market before planning a large campaign. Your software provider may also charge its own fee.

The practical takeaway: conversations your customers start are cheap or free, reminders and updates are inexpensive, and marketing messages cost the most — so use them thoughtfully.

## Limits, quality rating and the green tick

- **Messaging limits.** New numbers can only start conversations with a limited number of people per day. The limit rises automatically as you send messages that people engage with and after you verify your business with Meta.
- **Quality rating.** Meta tracks how people react to your messages. Lots of blocks and reports lower your rating, which can reduce your limits or pause templates. High engagement keeps it healthy.
- **Business verification.** Verifying your business in Meta Business Manager unlocks higher limits and is required for some features.
- **The verified badge.** Notable, well-known businesses can apply for a verified badge; most small businesses don't need it to succeed.

## What gets numbers restricted

- Messaging people who didn't opt in.
- Sending the same promotional message to a large list all at once from a new number.
- Frequent marketing messages with no easy way to opt out.
- High block and report rates.
- Using unofficial "WhatsApp blaster" tools or modified apps that automate the regular WhatsApp app. These break WhatsApp's terms and are a common cause of permanent bans. The official API is the safe route.

## A simple WhatsApp playbook for small businesses

1. **Add WhatsApp everywhere customers look:** a "Chat on WhatsApp" button on your website, Google Business Profile, Instagram bio and printed material. Inbound chats are free, high-intent and open the 24-hour window.
2. **Reply fast.** Aim for minutes, not hours. Set a friendly greeting and away message for out-of-hours.
3. **Create your core templates:** booking confirmation, reminder the day before, "we tried to reach you", order shipped, review request, and one or two seasonal offers.
4. **Automate the boring parts:** reminders and confirmations should go out on their own.
5. **Segment before you broadcast.** Send offers only to people for whom they're relevant — past customers of that service, a specific area, a specific interest.
6. **Measure replies and bookings,** not just messages sent.
7. **Keep everything in one place.** When WhatsApp, email and Instagram conversations all land in one shared inbox linked to the lead, nobody on your team misses a customer or asks the same question twice.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use my existing number?** Yes, but it can't stay active in the WhatsApp Business app at the same time. Plan the move, or use a new number for the API.

**Do I need a website?** It helps with business verification and gives customers a place to opt in, but it isn't strictly required to start.

**Can I send a message to someone who hasn't messaged me?** Only with an approved template, and only if they've opted in.

**How long does template approval take?** Often minutes, sometimes up to a day. Rejected templates can be edited and resubmitted.

**Is WhatsApp better than email?** They do different jobs. WhatsApp is best for quick, conversational, time-sensitive messages; email is better for longer content, documents and people who prefer it. Most businesses do best using both.

Used well, WhatsApp is one of the most effective channels a small business has: fast replies, high open rates and customers who feel looked after. Used carelessly, it can cost you the number your customers rely on.

Most dental practices that try paid search end up frustrated in one of two ways. Either they pay for clicks from people who wanted a free cleaning two towns away, or they sign up for Local Services Ads, get a burst of calls, and then can't tell which ones turned into patients.

Both problems come from the same mistake: picking an ad product before deciding what job it should do. Google Ads (Search) and Local Services Ads (LSA) look similar on the results page, but they charge differently, target differently and reward different habits inside your practice.

This guide compares Google Ads vs Local Services Ads for dentists in plain terms. You'll see how LSA pay-per-lead works, how to get the Google Verified badge, how to dispute bad leads, which Search keywords are worth paying for, how to set an illustrative starting budget, and why the person answering your phone matters more than any bid. It's one part of our larger [AI marketing system for dentists](/blog/ai-marketing-for-dentists), which covers the free channels you should have in place first.

## The short answer: what each ad type is good at

If you only read one section, read this one.

**Local Services Ads** sit at the very top of many dental searches, above regular ads, with your rating, review count and a "Google Verified" check. You pay per lead (a call, message or booking request), not per click. They are best for broad, high-intent local searches like "dentist near me" or "emergency dentist," and they reward practices with strong reviews and fast responses.

**Google Ads Search campaigns** show text ads for keywords you choose, and you pay per click. They are best when you want control: specific treatments (implants, Invisalign, sedation), specific towns, specific hours, and your own landing pages. They reward practices that write tight keyword lists and send traffic to pages built to convert.

| Factor | Local Services Ads | Google Ads (Search) |
|---|---|---|
| You pay for | A lead (call, message, booking) | A click |
| Placement | Top of page, above text ads, on many local searches | Text ads at top and bottom of results |
| Targeting | Service categories and job types, service area | Keywords, locations, schedule, devices, audiences |
| Landing page | Your LSA profile (no website needed) | Any page on your site you choose |
| Trust signal | Google Verified badge, reviews | Ad copy, extensions, your site |
| Setup effort | Verification, profile, budget | Keyword research, ad copy, landing pages, tracking |
| Biggest risk | Paying for unqualified or missed calls | Wasted clicks from broad keywords |
| Best for | "Dentist near me," emergency, general new patients | High-value treatments, niche services, specific towns |

> Key takeaway: LSA is usually the faster test for a general practice with good reviews and someone reliably answering the phone. Search ads are usually the better tool for high-value treatments where you need your own page to make the case.

Neither replaces your free foundation. If your profile is thin and your review count lags rivals, fix that first with our [dental SEO guide](/blog/dental-seo-guide) and the [dental reviews and HIPAA guide](/blog/dental-office-google-reviews-hipaa). Ads amplify what you have; they don't fix a weak profile.

## How Local Services Ads work for dentists

Google lists "dentist" among the health categories available in Local Services Ads in the US, alongside categories like orthodontist and chiropractor. The details live in [Google's Local Services Ads help center](https://support.google.com/localservices/), which is worth bookmarking because the rules and job types change.

### Pay per lead, not per click

With LSA you are charged when a potential patient contacts you through the ad: a phone call, a message or a booking request (depending on what you enable). Someone who looks at your profile and leaves costs nothing. That is the main appeal for dental offices that have been burned by click costs.

The catch is that "lead" doesn't mean "patient." A lead can be a price shopper, someone whose insurance you don't take, or an existing patient who used the ad to find your number. Some are chargeable and some are disputable.

::flow How an LSA lead reaches your chair | Searcher taps your LSA profile | Call, message or booking request | Front desk answers and qualifies | Appointment booked in your PMS

### Getting the Google Verified badge

Before your ads run, Google screens your business. For health providers this centers on license verification, and Google may also check business registration and other details depending on the category and state. Once you pass, your ad shows the Google Verified badge.

Plan for this to take time. Make sure dentist names and license details match your state dental board records exactly, use the same name, address and phone as your Google Business Profile, and keep hours accurate. Showing as open when nobody answers wastes leads and hurts your ranking.

### What decides your LSA ranking

Google's help center describes several factors that affect where your LSA appears, including your proximity to the searcher, your review score and number of reviews, how responsive you are to customer inquiries, your business hours, and whether Google has received serious complaints about your business. Your budget and bidding settings also affect how often you show.

Three of those are inside your control every day: reviews, responsiveness and hours. A practice that answers every LSA call and keeps a steady flow of honest reviews tends to do better than one that just raises its budget.

::bars What you can control in LSA ranking (illustrative weighting of effort) | Answering every lead fast: 9 * | Steady flow of reviews: 8 | Accurate hours: 6 | Budget and bid: 5 | Profile photos and bio: 3

That chart is an illustration of where your team's effort pays off, not Google's published weighting. Google does not publish exact weights.

### Disputing bad leads

You will get leads you shouldn't pay for. Common examples for dentists: wrong numbers, spam and robocalls, sales calls, or a caller asking for a service you didn't select (for instance a request for a specialist procedure you don't offer).

Google's process lets you review leads in your LSA dashboard and request credit for ones that don't qualify, within the time window and rules Google sets. Google has also moved toward automatically crediting some invalid leads. Check the current policy in the help center, because the rules on what qualifies have changed over the years.

A simple weekly routine keeps this under control:

1. Every Monday, open the LSA lead list for the previous week.
2. Listen to or read each lead. Mark it booked, not booked or invalid.
3. Dispute anything that looks like spam, a wrong number or a service you don't offer.
4. Note why non-booked leads didn't book (insurance, price, timing, no answer).
5. Fix the pattern. If many callers ask about an insurance plan you don't take, say so on your profile.

One caution: if you record calls to review them, check your state's consent rules first, because some states require every party to agree to recording.

## How Google Ads Search works for dental practices

Search campaigns give you control over exactly which searches trigger your ad and which page the visitor lands on. That control is why they still matter even if LSA works well.

### Keyword strategy that doesn't waste money

Dental keywords fall into rough intent groups. Your budget should flow to the top of this list first.

| Intent group | Example searches | Typical value | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urgent | emergency dentist, tooth pain dentist open now, broken tooth dentist | High, books fast | Only run when someone can answer and you can see them soon |
| High-value treatment | dental implants near me, Invisalign dentist, all-on-4, veneers | Very high per case | Needs a dedicated landing page with clear next steps |
| New patient, general | dentist accepting new patients, family dentist, dentist that takes [plan] | Medium, long lifetime value | Often overlaps with LSA |
| Research | how much do implants cost, is Invisalign worth it | Low immediate, useful later | Better served by content and SEO |
| Wrong intent | dental school, dental assistant jobs, free dental clinic, DIY | None for you | Add as negative keywords |

Start narrow. Use phrase match or exact match for your core terms, set your location targeting to the area patients actually travel from, and build a negative keyword list on day one. Common negatives for a private practice include "jobs," "salary," "school," "course," "free," "DIY," "Medicaid" (only if you don't accept it), and the names of dental schools or community clinics nearby.

Review your search terms report weekly for the first two months. It shows the real searches that triggered your ads, and it is where most wasted spend hides.

### Healthcare ad policies to know

Dental practices can advertise on Google, but health is a sensitive area in ad policy. Two things matter most:

- Google's [Healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031) governs what health advertisers can promote and where.
- Google's [Personalized advertising policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465) restricts targeting people based on health conditions. That limits remarketing for health services, so read it before building audience lists from treatment pages.

There is also a HIPAA angle. Ad pixels and tags that send data from patient-facing pages to ad platforms have drawn HHS attention. Our guide to [healthcare tracking pixels](/blog/healthcare-tracking-pixels-privacy) covers what is still risky (patient portals and appointment flows especially) and safer ways to measure conversions.

### Write ads that set expectations

Good dental ad copy filters out the wrong patients before they click. Say what you offer, who it's for and what happens next. For example:

- "Same-Day Emergency Dentist in Tampa | Call Now, Seen Today When Possible"
- "Dental Implants Consultation | 3D Scan, Clear Pricing Options, Financing Available"
- "New Patients Welcome | Most PPO Plans Accepted | Evening Appointments"

Only promise what your schedule and policies can deliver. If "seen today" is true only on some days, say "when possible" or describe your actual hours.

## Budgets: an illustrative way to plan

We won't quote "average" cost per click or cost per lead for dentists, because these vary hugely by city, season, competition and how your account is set up, and published averages are often unreliable. Instead, work backward from what a new patient is worth to you.

::stats Example: planning math for a 2-dentist practice (illustrative) | $1,500 | monthly test budget | 25% | lead-to-patient rate target | 3 months | minimum test length

Here's how to use numbers like those:

1. **Estimate the value of a new patient** from your own data, such as average first-year production.
2. **Decide the most you'll pay to acquire one.**
3. **Set a test budget you can afford to lose.** Run LSA first if your reviews are strong.
4. **Run it for three months.** One month is noisy.
5. **Measure cost per booked patient, not cost per lead.** A cheap lead that never books is expensive.

Dr. Patel's 2-chair practice in Tampa (a hypothetical example) might start with LSA at a modest weekly budget, add a small Search campaign only for implant consultations with its own landing page, and compare the two by cost per booked new patient after 90 days. ## Landing pages: where Search ads win or lose

LSA sends people to your Google profile. Search ads send people to your website, which means your page has to do the selling.

Never send ad traffic to your home page if you can avoid it. Build one page per campaign:

- **Emergency page:** click-to-call button at the top, your real hours, what counts as an emergency, what to do while waiting, and a map.
- **Implants page:** who is a candidate, the steps from consult to final crown, financing options, the dentist's training, and a short consult request form.
- **New patient page:** insurance plans accepted, new patient process, first visit expectations, online booking or a request form.

::check Dental ad landing page checklist | Headline matches the ad's promise | Phone number tappable on mobile | Insurance and financing info visible | Short form with name, phone, preferred time | Real photos of the team and office | Loads fast on a phone | No treatment history fields on marketing forms

That last item matters. Your marketing form should ask only for contact details and a preferred time. Medical history belongs in your HIPAA-compliant intake system after the appointment is booked. ## Call answering: the part most practices skip

Ads generate demand. Your phone converts it. For both LSA and Search, a missed or rushed call is where most ad money disappears.

### The leaks to look for

Calls ringing out during lunch or busy check-in times, voicemail after hours while ads keep running, long holds, no script for price questions, and no follow-up when a caller says "I'll think about it."

### A simple new-patient call script

::chat New patient call from an ad (example script) | them: Hi, do you take Delta Dental? And how much is a cleaning? | you: Great question. We're in network with several Delta Dental plans. Can I grab your name so I can check your plan for you? | them: Sure, it's Maria. | you: Thanks, Maria. Most new patients start with an exam, X-rays and cleaning. I have Thursday at 4 or Saturday at 9. Which works better? | them: Thursday at 4. | you: Perfect, you're booked. I'll send a confirmation with our address and new patient forms.

Notice what the script does: it answers the question, gets a name, checks coverage and offers two specific times. It doesn't quote a price it can't stand behind, and it doesn't let the call end without a next step.

LSA has its own lead reporting in the dashboard. Your practice-management software tells you who actually became a patient. The goal is to connect those three so you can see cost per booked patient by channel.

## Running both: a practical sequence

::steps A 90-day paid search rollout for a dental practice | Weeks 1–2 — Fix GBP, hours, reviews, phone coverage | Weeks 3–4 — Launch LSA with a modest budget and dispute routine | Weeks 5–8 — Add one Search campaign for your highest-value treatment | Weeks 9–10 — Compare cost per booked patient by channel | Weeks 11–12 — Shift budget to the winner, cut what isn't working

When both run, one searcher may see your LSA profile and your text ad. That takes more space on the page, but it muddies attribution, so tag form sources and ask every new patient "How did you hear about us?" at booking. If LSA already captures "dentist near me" well, point your Search budget at treatment terms instead.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Broad match with no negatives.** You'll pay for job seekers, students and people looking for free clinics.
- **Running emergency ads when nobody answers.** Schedule emergency campaigns only for hours you can actually take the call.
- **Ignoring disputes.** Unchallenged spam leads quietly raise your cost per patient.
- **Neglecting reviews.** Reviews affect LSA ranking and click-through on every ad type. Our [Google reviews guide](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) covers asking within the rules.
- **Copying a competitor's claims.** Ads must be truthful, and some states restrict how general dentists describe specialty services. Check your state dental board's advertising rules.

## Measuring what matters

Track these monthly, by channel:

| Metric | Where it comes from | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Leads | LSA dashboard, Google Ads, form submissions | Volume indicator only |
| Answered rate | Call tracking or phone system | Shows where calls leak |
| Booked new patients | Practice-management software | The real outcome |
| Cost per booked new patient | Spend divided by booked patients | Your main decision number |
| Show rate | Practice-management software | Catches no-show problems from certain sources |
| Disputed lead credits | LSA dashboard | Keeps LSA costs honest |

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Local Services Ads better than Google Ads for dentists?

Neither is better for every practice. LSA is often the quicker win for general "dentist near me" and emergency searches if you have strong reviews and answer every call, while Search ads give you more control for high-value treatments like implants and Invisalign.

### How do dentists get the Google Verified badge?

You apply through Local Services Ads and pass Google's screening, which for health providers centers on license verification. Make sure your license details, practice name, address and phone match official records and your Business Profile.

### Can I get refunded for bad Local Services Ads leads?

Google lets you request credit for leads that don't qualify, such as spam, wrong numbers or services you don't offer, within the time window and rules in its help center. Google also credits some invalid leads automatically, so review your lead list weekly.

### How much should a dental practice spend on Google Ads?

Work backward from the value of a new patient and the most you'll pay to acquire one, then set a test budget you can afford for at least three months. Judge results by cost per booked new patient, not cost per click or per lead.

### Can dentists use remarketing ads?

Google's personalized advertising policy restricts targeting based on health, which limits remarketing for health services, and ad pixels on patient-facing pages raise HIPAA concerns. Read Google's policy and keep tracking off patient portals and appointment flows.

### Do I need a website to run Local Services Ads?

No. LSA sends people to your LSA profile, and they contact you from there. You still need a good website for Search ads and for patients who research you before calling.

HVAC companies live on two peaks and two valleys. In the first heat wave, the phones ring nonstop and you turn away work. In April and October, techs sit idle and the owner wonders where the calls went. Most shops market hardest during the peaks, when they need it least, and go quiet in the valleys, when they need it most.

A good **HVAC marketing plan** flips that. You fill the shoulder months with tune-ups, maintenance agreements and replacement quotes, so you go into each peak with a full schedule of members who call you first. When the extreme weather hits, you switch from selling to answering.

This guide gives you a month-by-month calendar, a weather-triggered content kit, a maintenance agreement playbook, a way to adjust Local Services Ads by season and email templates you can use this week. ## Why HVAC marketing has to follow the weather

Demand for HVAC work is driven by temperature more than by anything you do. A mild June in Atlanta means fewer AC calls than a scorching one, regardless of your ad budget. That has three consequences for your plan.

First, **you can't create emergency demand, only capture it.** When it's 105°F in Phoenix, everyone with a weak compressor will call someone. Your job is to be found and to answer.

Second, **you can create planned demand.** Tune-ups, maintenance agreements, indoor air quality work and replacements of aging systems can be sold in any month. That's where marketing actually moves the numbers.

Third, **timing beats volume.** ENERGY STAR's [maintenance checklist](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling/maintenance-checklist) advises checking the cooling system in spring and the heating system in fall, before contractors get busy. That's your marketing calendar in one sentence.

::compare Peak-only marketing vs a seasonal plan | Before: ads ramp up during heat waves; turn away calls in July; idle techs in April and October; members are an afterthought | After: tune-up campaigns 4–6 weeks before each season; agreement members booked in shoulder months; ads tuned to capacity; techs busy year-round

## The month-by-month HVAC marketing calendar

This calendar assumes a climate with a real summer and a real winter. If you're in Florida or the Desert Southwest, stretch the cooling season and shrink heating. If you're in Minnesota or Michigan, do the opposite. Shift dates by your local weather, not the calendar.

| Month | Main focus | Campaigns and content | Ads posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | Heating emergencies, agreement renewals | "No heat" content, renewal emails | Full during cold snaps if you have capacity |
| February | Heating tail, replacement quotes | "Repair or replace your furnace?" guide, financing page | Steady |
| March | Pre-season cooling tune-ups | AC tune-up email to all past customers, first social posts | Moderate, tune-up focused |
| April | Tune-up push, agreement sales | Reminder email, agreement offer, IAQ content | Moderate |
| May | Last tune-up window, replacement planning | "Is your AC ready for summer?" posts, final reminder | Build up |
| June | Cooling peak begins | Heat wave content ready, quick-reply templates | Full when capacity allows |
| July | Peak | Answer, book, ask for reviews | Adjust daily to capacity |
| August | Peak tail, replacement leads | Follow up on "repair now, replace later" quotes | Steady |
| September | Pre-season heating tune-ups | Furnace tune-up email, carbon monoxide safety content | Moderate |
| October | Heating tune-ups, agreement sales | Reminder email, agreement offer | Moderate |
| November | Last heating tune-ups, holiday scheduling | "Before the first freeze" posts, holiday hours | Build up |
| December | Heating peak begins, year-end replacements | Cold snap content, year-end financing offers | Full during cold snaps |

Print this, put it on the office wall and assign an owner for each month's tasks. The plan only works if someone sends the March email in March.

## Pre-season tune-up campaigns

The tune-up campaign is the backbone of the plan. You send it 4 to 6 weeks before the season starts, when techs have capacity and customers still have time to plan.

### Who to send it to

- Every customer you've served in the last three years.
- Agreement members (they're due anyway; make booking easy).
- Customers who got a "repair now, replace later" recommendation.
- Leads who asked for a quote and didn't book.

### What to say

Keep it short, practical and specific. Here's a sample email you can adapt:

**Subject:** Book your AC tune-up before the first heat wave

Hi Jordan,

Summer is about six weeks away, and once the first hot week hits, every HVAC company in town gets booked solid. A spring tune-up now means we check refrigerant charge, clean the condenser, test the capacitor and catch small problems before they become a breakdown on the hottest day of the year.

We have openings over the next three weeks. Book a time here, or reply to this email and we'll call you.

Thanks,
Lisa at Valley Comfort Heating & Air

That email (a hypothetical example) works because it gives a reason to act now, says what's included and offers two easy ways to book.

Send a reminder 10 days later to anyone who didn't book. Then stop. Two emails are enough; more starts to feel like pressure.

## Heat waves and cold snaps: the weather-triggered content kit

When extreme weather arrives, you won't have time to write. Build a kit in the quiet months, then publish when the forecast turns.

::check Weather-triggered content kit (prepare in advance) | "AC not cooling? 6 things to check before you call" page | "Furnace not turning on? Safe checks for homeowners" page | Heat wave and cold snap social posts, scheduled as drafts | A short email to members with priority scheduling details | Holiday and after-hours schedule posts | Quick-reply templates for "how soon can you come?"

### What good heat wave content looks like

Useful content during a heat wave helps the homeowner first: check the thermostat and breaker, replace a clogged filter, make sure the outdoor unit isn't blocked, don't keep resetting a tripping breaker. It ends with a clear "if that doesn't work, call us" and your real availability. People share helpful posts, and Google rewards pages that answer the actual question.

Include basic safety advice and link to a trusted source. The National Weather Service's [heat safety page](https://www.weather.gov/safety/heat) is a good one for heat waves. For winter, remind people about carbon monoxide alarms and never using ovens or grills for heat.

### Responding when demand spikes

During peak weather, your marketing problem becomes an answering problem. Calls stack up, forms pile in and messages go unread.

::chat Handling a peak-day inquiry honestly | them: AC died this morning and it's 98 in the house. Can you come today? | you: I'm sorry, that's miserable. Today is full, but I can get a tech there first thing tomorrow at 8. Meanwhile, close the blinds and run fans. If anyone in the home is elderly or unwell, please consider a cooler place to wait. | them: Tomorrow at 8 works. Thank you. | you: You're booked. You'll get a confirmation email shortly.

Honest timing keeps the job and avoids the one-star review that comes from overpromising. Our guide to [missed calls and speed to lead](/blog/missed-calls-speed-to-lead-home-services) covers overflow answering and call-back rules for peak days.

## Maintenance agreements: the engine of the plan

A maintenance agreement (some shops call it a club or membership) turns a one-time customer into a yearly relationship. Members get two tune-ups a year, and you get predictable work in the shoulder months plus first call when something breaks.

### What to include

Common benefits:

- Spring cooling and fall heating tune-ups.
- Priority scheduling during peak weather.
- A repair discount (state the percentage).
- Waived or reduced diagnostic fees.
- Filter reminders or filter delivery.

Keep it simple enough to explain in one sentence: "Two tune-ups a year, you jump the line when it's hot or cold, and 15% off repairs." (That percentage is an example; set your own.)

### When to sell it

- **Right after a repair:** "This is how we keep it from happening again."
- **During every tune-up:** the tech mentions it and leaves a one-page summary.
- **After a new system install:** many manufacturers require regular maintenance for warranty coverage; check the specific warranty terms.
- **In the April and October campaigns** to non-members.

### Renewals

Track renewal dates and send a renewal email 30 days before expiry, with a second reminder at 7 days. Your field service software (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro) usually handles billing and renewal tracking. ## Adjusting Local Services Ads and Search ads by season

HVAC is one of the core Local Services Ads categories. You pay per lead, appear at the top of results with a Google Verified badge and compete on rating, responsiveness and budget. Google's [ad ranking page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en) lists responsiveness among the factors and notes missed calls may hurt it.

Seasonal adjustments that make sense:

- **Peak weeks:** keep LSA running only for hours and job types you can actually take. Paying for leads you can't serve hurts twice: wasted money and a hit to responsiveness.
- **Shoulder months:** this is when you may want to push budget, because you have capacity and fewer competitors are spending. Focus on tune-up, maintenance and replacement job types.
- **Search ads:** use them for high-value searches like "AC replacement" and "heat pump installation," with landing pages that offer a free in-home estimate.

For screening, lead disputes and budget planning, read [Google Local Services Ads for home services](/blog/google-local-services-ads-home-services).

> **Key takeaway:** In peak weeks, cap your ads to your capacity. In shoulder months, spend to fill the schedule. The calendar should drive the budget, not the other way around.

## Rebates, tax credits and the replacement conversation

Replacement jobs are your biggest tickets, and money questions come up every time. Be careful with what you promise.

The federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (section 25C), which covered qualifying heat pumps, central AC and furnaces, applied to property placed in service through 2025, per the [IRS credit page](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/energy-efficient-home-improvement-credit). Don't advertise it for 2026 installs. For anything else, tell customers to **check current federal, state and utility rebates**, and point them to tools like the ENERGY STAR [rebate finder](https://www.energystar.gov/rebate-finder). Programs change often, funding runs out and eligibility varies, so never quote a rebate amount as guaranteed.

Two other topics come up in replacement conversations:

- **Refrigerant changes.** New residential systems are moving to lower-GWP refrigerants under EPA's [technology transitions program](https://www.epa.gov/climate-hfcs-reduction/technology-transitions). A plain-English page explaining what that means for repair vs replace is useful content in the shoulder months.
- **Financing.** If you offer financing, put it on a dedicated page with clear terms, and follow your lender's advertising rules.

## Turn peak season into reviews

Peak season is your best review season. You're fixing urgent problems, and grateful customers are happy to say so. Make the ask part of every job: the tech asks in person, and the link goes out within an hour by email or through your field service app.

Ask every customer, not only the happy ones. Google bans review gating, and the FTC's [Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/rule-consumer-reviews-testimonials) bans fake or paid reviews. ## Common HVAC marketing mistakes

- **Starting tune-up campaigns when it's already hot.** By then, you're too busy to do them.
- **Running ads around the clock in July** when you can't take another call.
- **Promising rebates or tax credits** that have expired or that the customer may not qualify for.
- **Selling agreements without tracking renewals,** so members quietly lapse.
- **Writing heat wave content during the heat wave,** when nobody has time.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should HVAC companies market tune-ups?

Send cooling tune-up campaigns about 4 to 6 weeks before summer and heating campaigns about 4 to 6 weeks before winter. ENERGY STAR recommends checking cooling in spring and heating in fall.

### How do HVAC companies get more maintenance agreement members?

Offer the agreement after every repair, during every tune-up and after new installs, and run a dedicated campaign in the spring and fall shoulder months. Keep the benefits simple and track renewals.

### Should I lower my ad budget during peak season?

Cap ads to what you can actually answer and serve. Paying for leads you can't take wastes money and can hurt your Local Services Ads ranking if calls go unanswered.

### Can I still advertise the federal HVAC tax credit in 2026?

The 25C Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit applied to property placed in service through 2025, according to the IRS. Tell customers to check current federal, state and utility rebates instead.

### What content should an HVAC company post in a heat wave?

Practical troubleshooting (thermostat, breaker, filter, outdoor unit), basic heat safety and your real availability. Prepare it in advance so you only need to publish.

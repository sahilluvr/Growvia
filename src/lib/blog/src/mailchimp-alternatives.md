For years Mailchimp was the default answer to "how do I start an email newsletter?" It's still a polished product. But in 2026 a lot of small businesses are taking a second look. The free plan has shrunk, prices rise with every contact on your list, and many owners realise the emails that actually bring in business aren't newsletters at all.

This guide explains what changed, the two very different kinds of email marketing, the best alternatives for each, and a safe way to switch without hurting your deliverability or breaking any laws.

## What changed with Mailchimp

Three things push people to look around:

1. **The free plan got much smaller.** In 2022 it covered 2,000 contacts. Today it covers 250 contacts and 500 sends a month, with Mailchimp branding.
2. **You pay for your audience, not your sending.** Plans are priced by how many contacts you store. At 5,000 contacts, Essentials is about $75 a month and Standard about $100, even in a month you send nothing.
3. **Automation sits higher up.** Multi-step customer journeys need the Standard plan or above.

::stats Mailchimp in 2026 (US list prices) | 250 | contacts on the free plan, down from 2,000 in 2022 | $75 | a month for Essentials at 5,000 contacts | $100 | a month for Standard at 5,000 contacts

None of this makes Mailchimp bad. It just means the math changes as your list grows, and it's worth checking whether you're paying for the right kind of tool. Current numbers are on [Mailchimp's pricing page](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/).

## First, decide which kind of email you send

This is the question most comparison articles skip. There are two kinds of email marketing, and they need different tools.

**Broadcast email** is one message to a big list: a monthly newsletter, a sale announcement, a new collection. Design matters, volume is high, and replies are rare. Mailchimp, Brevo, Zoho Campaigns and MailerLite are built for this.

**Relationship email** is personal and two-way: following up an enquiry, a quote reminder, a welcome series for new leads, a "how did it go?" after a visit. Volume is lower, but each email is worth far more because it's a step toward a sale. These emails work best when they come from a real person's mailbox and stop the moment someone replies.

Many small businesses pay for a broadcast tool when most of their revenue comes from relationship email. Others need both.

## The best Mailchimp alternatives in 2026

### 1. Brevo: best for big lists you email occasionally

Brevo charges by emails sent, not contacts stored, so a large list doesn't cost much to keep. The free plan stores up to 100,000 contacts and sends up to 300 emails a day. Starter is $9 a month for 5,000 emails, and higher tiers add automation, A/B testing and landing pages. It also does SMS and WhatsApp campaigns.

**Best for:** newsletters to large lists, shops with transactional email.
**Watch out for:** the 300-a-day cap on the free plan, and Brevo's logo on Starter unless you pay to remove it. See [Brevo pricing](https://www.brevo.com/pricing/).

### 2. Zoho Campaigns: best value, especially in India and Asia

Zoho Campaigns has one of the most generous free plans: up to 2,000 contacts and 6,000 emails a month. Paid plans start at a few dollars a month and link neatly to Zoho CRM.

**Best for:** cost-conscious businesses, and anyone already using Zoho.

## How to switch from Mailchimp safely

Switching email tools is easy to get wrong in two ways: you email people who unsubscribed, or your new sending setup lands in spam. This plan avoids both.

::steps Switch from Mailchimp in six steps | Export everything — subscribed, unsubscribed and cleaned contacts, as separate files | Protect your suppression list — make sure unsubscribed and bounced addresses can never be emailed again | Authenticate your domain — set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC for the new sender | Rebuild what earns money — your signup form and welcome sequence first, everything else later | Start gently — send to your most engaged contacts first, then widen over a week or two | Cancel — once a full cycle has gone out cleanly from the new tool

### Step 1: Export all three lists

In Mailchimp, export your audience. You'll get subscribed, unsubscribed and cleaned (bounced) contacts. Keep all three. The unsubscribed and cleaned files are just as important as the subscribed one.

### Step 2: Never email people who opted out

Email laws like the US CAN-SPAM Act, GDPR in Europe, Canada's CASL and India's DPDP Act all require you to honour opt-outs. Import only subscribed contacts into your new list, and keep the opt-out file so nobody gets re-added by accident. Our [email marketing laws guide](/blog/email-marketing-laws-by-country) covers each country.

### Step 3: Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC

Gmail, Yahoo and Outlook now check that bulk senders authenticate their domain. Your new tool will show the records to add. If you send through Growvia from your own Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 or Zoho mailbox, add the records your mail provider gives you; Growvia's sending health page checks your SPF, DKIM and DMARC and shows what's missing. Our guide to [SPF, DKIM and DMARC](/blog/email-deliverability-spf-dkim-dmarc-guide) explains each record in plain English.

### Step 4: Rebuild what earns money first

Don't try to recreate every old campaign. Start with your website signup form and your welcome sequence, because new subscribers are the most likely to buy. Our [welcome sequence guide](/blog/email-marketing-welcome-sequence) has a complete five-email example you can adapt.

### Step 5: Start with your most engaged contacts

A new sending setup builds trust with inbox providers over time. Send first to people who opened or clicked recently, then widen the audience over a week or two. In Growvia you also set a daily limit per mailbox, and bigger sends spread over the following days automatically.

### Step 6: Cancel the old account

Once a full campaign cycle has gone out cleanly, downgrade or close Mailchimp. Keep your exported files somewhere safe.

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the best free alternative to Mailchimp?

For large lists, Brevo's free plan (up to 100,000 contacts, 300 emails a day) or Zoho Campaigns' free plan (2,000 contacts, 6,000 emails a month) are the most generous. For enquiry follow-ups from your own mailbox, Growvia's free plan includes 300 emails a month plus forms, a pipeline and SEO tools.

### Is Brevo cheaper than Mailchimp?

Usually, when you have a large list and send occasionally, because Brevo charges per email sent rather than per contact stored. If you email a small list very often, the difference is smaller.

### Will switching from Mailchimp hurt my deliverability?

Not if you authenticate your domain, import only people who opted in, and start with your most engaged contacts. Sudden big sends to old, unengaged lists are what cause spam problems.

### Can I send newsletters with Growvia?

Yes, you can send one email to a tag, a pipeline stage or everyone, now or scheduled, with unsubscribes and bounces skipped automatically. It's designed for personal, relationship email from your own mailbox, up to 5,000 emails a month on Pro. For very large weekly newsletters, a volume tool like Brevo is the better fit.

### Do I need to tell my subscribers I'm switching tools?

No. Your subscribers agreed to hear from your business, not from a particular tool. Just keep your sender name and address the same, and make sure the unsubscribe link works in every email.

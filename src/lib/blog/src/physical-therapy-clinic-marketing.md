Physical therapy clinic marketing has an unusual shape. For years, most PT patients arrived with a prescription from a physician, so marketing meant keeping referral sources happy. That still matters. But patients can now see a physical therapist without a referral in every state in some form, more of them search Google and AI assistants for help with a sore knee or a stiff back, and many clinics lose a large share of patients before they finish their plan of care.

So a modern PT marketing plan has three parts: earn referrals from physicians, win the patients who search directly, and keep the patients you already have through to discharge. Each part is cheap to improve compared with buying ads.

This guide covers all three, with scripts, a referral routine and a 60-day plan. It's part of our [AI marketing system for medical practices](/blog/ai-marketing-for-medical-practices). Direct access and insurance rules vary by state and payer, so check your state's practice act and your payer contracts before you change how you market.

## Direct access: the opportunity and the limits

Direct access means a patient can be evaluated and treated by a physical therapist without a physician's referral. According to the American Physical Therapy Association, all 50 states and DC now allow some form of [direct access to physical therapists](https://www.apta.org/advocacy/issues/direct-access-advocacy). But many states still attach limits.

| Type of direct access | What it typically means | Marketing implication |
|---|---|---|
| Unrestricted | Patients can be evaluated and treated without a referral, with no added limits | You can market directly to patients with confidence |
| Provisional or limited | Treatment without a referral is allowed but capped, for example by number of visits or days, or limited for certain conditions | Tell patients they can start without a referral, and explain when a physician visit may be needed |
| Payer-driven limits | The law allows direct access, but insurers or Medicare impose their own requirements | Explain insurance clearly, and verify benefits before the first visit |

Insurance is often the bigger constraint. Some plans require a referral or prior authorization for PT even where state law doesn't, and Medicare requires a physician or other qualified practitioner to certify the plan of care. Your front desk should check each patient's benefits, and your website should say "Many patients can start PT without a referral. We'll check your insurance for you" rather than promising everyone can.

::check Direct access marketing checklist | Know your state's exact direct access rules and limits | Know which of your main payers require a referral or authorization | Website explains direct access in plain language | Front desk script for "Do I need a referral?" | Process to coordinate with the patient's physician when needed | Red-flag screening so patients who need a physician get one

### How to talk about direct access

Most people still believe they need a doctor's note to see a PT. That belief sends them to urgent care, a primary care wait list, or a chiropractor first. Correcting it is one of your best marketing messages.

- Add a short "Do I need a referral?" section to your home page and every condition page.
- Publish a dedicated page explaining direct access in your state, with honest notes on limits and insurance.
- Train your front desk to answer the question in one sentence and offer an appointment.

## Physician referral marketing that actually works

Referral sources still drive a big share of new patients for most outpatient clinics. Orthopedic surgeons, primary care physicians, sports medicine doctors, podiatrists, OB/GYNs (for pelvic health) and neurologists (for vestibular and neuro rehab) can each send patients for years.

What physicians want from a PT clinic is simple: fast access for their patients, good outcomes, clear communication and no hassle for their staff.

| Referral source | What they value most | What to offer |
|---|---|---|
| Orthopedic surgeons | Post-op protocols followed, fast starts after surgery | Protocol-specific programs, appointment within days of surgery |
| Primary care physicians | Patients seen quickly, fewer return visits for the same complaint | Same-week evaluations, brief progress notes |
| Sports medicine and team physicians | Return-to-sport outcomes | Return-to-sport testing, communication with coaches (with patient authorization) |
| OB/GYNs and midwives | Pelvic health expertise | Clear description of pelvic health services and therapist training |
| Neurologists and ENTs | Vestibular and balance expertise | Vestibular programs and fall-risk assessments |

### A monthly referral routine

1. **Rank your top 20 referrers** from your practice software, plus 10 targets who should refer but don't.
2. **Visit or call two to three offices a week.** Bring a one-page summary: services, availability for new evaluations, insurance accepted, locations and how to refer.
3. **Make referring easy.** A fax number, e-referral option and direct line that someone answers.
4. **Close the loop fast.** Send the initial evaluation and progress notes through your secure clinical channel, on time, every time.
5. **Share outcomes.** Aggregate outcome data (for example average improvement on a standard functional score) is more persuasive than any brochure.
6. **Thank top referrers** in person.

Keep referral marketing compliant. The federal Anti-Kickback Statute and the Stark Law restrict giving anything of value to induce referrals of federal healthcare program patients, and physician self-referral rules affect some ownership arrangements. Lunches, gifts and sponsorships for referral sources should get a compliance check. The [HHS Office of Inspector General's physician education resources](https://oig.hhs.gov/compliance/physician-education/) explain the basics.

## Win the patients who search directly

Patients who search directly type things like "physical therapy near me", "PT for knee pain", "vertigo physical therapy" and "physical therapy after knee replacement". To win them, you need a strong Google Business Profile, condition pages and reviews.

### Google Business Profile for PT clinics

- **Primary category: Physical therapy clinic** (or the closest available). Add secondary categories only when accurate, for example "Sports medicine clinic" if that fits your services.
- **Services:** list conditions and programs (post-surgical rehab, sports injuries, low back pain, vestibular rehab, pelvic health, dry needling if your state allows it).
- **Hours:** early morning and evening slots are a selling point for working patients. Show them.
- **Photos:** gym floor, treatment rooms, parking and entrance, the team.
- **Each location gets its own profile** with its own reviews and photos. If you run several clinics, our [multi-location marketing playbook](/blog/multi-location-marketing-playbook) covers location pages and reporting.

Google's [local ranking help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) explains that results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. Our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide) covers the details, and the [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-small-business) covers citations and directories.

### Condition pages

Write one page per major condition or program. Each should explain who it helps, what the evaluation includes, how many visits are typical (as an honest range), what patients do at home, whether a referral is needed, insurance information, and which therapists lead the program, with credentials.

Good starting pages for most clinics: low back pain, knee replacement rehab, rotator cuff and shoulder pain, ACL rehab, sports injuries, vertigo and balance, pelvic health and neck pain. Use the words patients use, then explain the clinical terms.

### Local Services Ads

Physical therapist is an eligible Google Local Services Ads category in the US. You pay per lead, and clinics that pass Google's screening show a "Google Verified" badge. LSA works best when your phones are answered quickly and your reviews are strong. Check [Google's Local Services help center](https://support.google.com/localservices/) for availability in your area. If you run Search ads too, keep ad pixels off booking pages and intake forms. Our [healthcare tracking pixels guide](/blog/healthcare-tracking-pixels-privacy) explains why.

## Plan-of-care completion: the growth lever most clinics miss

A patient who drops out after three visits doesn't get the outcome, doesn't leave a glowing review and doesn't tell friends. Improving plan-of-care completion improves outcomes, revenue and reputation at the same time, without any new patients.

::funnel Example: visits completed across a 10-visit plan of care (illustrative) | Evaluations: 100 | Completed visit 3: 85 | Completed visit 6: 65 | Completed full plan: 50

### Ways to keep patients through discharge

- **Set expectations on day one.** Explain the plan, how many visits, why each phase matters and what progress should feel like.
- **Book the whole plan up front** where possible, not one visit at a time.
- **Give a clear home exercise program** through your EMR's patient app or printouts.
- **Follow up after a missed visit** within a day, by phone or through your HIPAA-compliant patient messaging.
- **Mark milestones.** Tell patients when they hit a goal ("You're walking without the cane").
- **Track arrival rate and drop-off by therapist** in your practice software, and coach where needed.

All of this involves PHI, so it runs through your EMR and patient engagement tools, which handle reminders and follow-ups under a business associate agreement. A general marketing tool isn't the place for it.

## Reviews: ask at graduation

Discharge is the perfect moment to ask for a review. The patient has just reached their goal and usually feels grateful. Some clinics celebrate "graduation" with a certificate, a photo (with consent) and a review request card with a QR code.

Rules to keep:

- **Ask everyone** who completes care. Google bans selectively asking only happy patients.
- **No incentives.** The FTC's [Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers), effective October 21, 2024, bans buying reviews and fake reviews, with civil penalties.
- **Reply without confirming care.** Don't mention conditions, surgeries or visits in replies, even if the patient did. Our [HIPAA compliant marketing guide](/blog/hipaa-compliant-marketing-guide) has templates, and [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews) covers review links and QR codes.

Patient success stories make great website content, but only with a signed HIPAA authorization that names how the story will be used.

## Reactivation and community marketing

### Bring back discharged patients the right way

Former patients may come back for a new injury, a flare-up or a "tune-up". Reach them through your EMR's patient engagement tools or HIPAA-compliant messaging, with general wording like "If you have a new ache or pain, we're here. Many patients can start without a referral." Keep the patient list in the BAA-covered system rather than exporting it to a marketing tool.

### Get known in the community

PT is a natural fit for community education, which builds direct access demand:

- **Fall prevention workshops** at senior centers and community centers.
- **Running injury clinics** with local running clubs and shoe stores.
- **Ergonomics talks** for local employers.
- **Sports injury screenings** with high school athletic programs (coordinate with the school).
- **Pelvic health talks** with prenatal and postpartum groups.

Collect sign-ups at these events with a clear opt-in for your newsletter. That list belongs to people who chose to hear from you, so a marketing tool can handle it. A monthly email with one exercise tip, one clinic update and an event invite keeps you top of mind. See the [email welcome sequence guide](/blog/email-marketing-welcome-sequence) for a simple setup.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can patients see a physical therapist without a referral?

According to the APTA, all 50 states and DC allow some form of direct access to physical therapists. Many states still have limits, such as visit or time caps, and some insurers require a referral, so check your state's rules and the patient's plan.

### What is the best way to market a physical therapy clinic?

Combine physician referral marketing, a strong Google Business Profile with condition pages, and a review routine at discharge. Then improve plan-of-care completion so more patients reach their goals and recommend you.

### How do PT clinics get more physician referrals?

Offer fast evaluations, follow surgeons' protocols, send progress notes promptly through a secure channel, and visit referral offices regularly with useful information. Avoid gifts or payments that could violate the Anti-Kickback Statute.

### Are physical therapists eligible for Google Local Services Ads?

Yes. Physical therapist is an eligible Local Services Ads category in the US, though availability varies by location. You pay per lead and must pass Google's screening.

### When should a PT clinic ask for reviews?

At discharge or "graduation", when the patient has reached their goal. Ask every patient who completes care, never offer incentives, and never confirm care details in your replies.

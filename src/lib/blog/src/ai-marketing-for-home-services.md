## What the home services growth system looks like

Every booked job travels the same path. A homeowner has a problem, searches on a phone, picks from a short list, calls or fills in a form, and decides within minutes whether you're the one. After the job, they either become a repeat customer and a review, or they forget your name.

::flow The home services growth loop | Search and Maps visibility | Fast answer and booking | Job done well | Review and photo | Maintenance plan and repeat work

Most owners pour money into the first box and ignore the rest. That's backwards. If you only answer half your calls, doubling your ad budget doubles your waste. Work the loop from the middle out: response first, then reviews, then visibility, then retention.

Here's how the parts map to the trades:

| Trade | Typical urgent search | Biggest leak | Best repeat-revenue play |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plumbing | "emergency plumber near me", "water heater leaking" | After-hours calls going to voicemail | Water heater flushes, drain memberships |
| HVAC | "AC not cooling", "furnace repair near me" | Peak-season call overflow | Maintenance agreements with two tune-ups a year |
| Roofing | "roof leak repair", "hail damage roof inspection" | Slow quotes and weak trust signals after storms | Annual inspections, gutter work, referrals |
| Electrical | "electrician near me", "panel upgrade cost" | Quote requests that never get followed up | Generator service, EV charger and surge protection add-ons |

## Step 1: Fix speed to lead and missed calls

Home service buyers rarely wait. Someone with water on the floor calls the first plumber, and if nobody picks up, they call the second. Response time is the cheapest growth lever you have, and it also affects your paid results. Google's [Local Services Ads ranking page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en) lists responsiveness as a factor and notes that missed calls may hurt it.

### Set a response standard

Write down a standard your whole team can see. A reasonable start: live answer during business hours with a backup person, missed calls returned within 5 to 10 minutes, web forms answered with an instant auto-reply plus a human follow-up within 15 minutes, and a clear after-hours plan (answering service, on-call tech or an honest "we'll call you at 7 a.m." message).

### Measure it before you change it

You can't fix what you don't see. A call tracking tool like [CallRail](https://www.callrail.com/) shows missed calls by source. Your field service software (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro) shows which calls became booked jobs. Growvia doesn't track phone calls, but it handles the web side: lead forms with spam protection, an instant email reply, a leads pipeline so no form sits unclaimed, and a shared inbox for email, WhatsApp, Instagram and Messenger messages.

::chat A 9 p.m. web form, handled well | them: Water heater is leaking from the bottom, how soon can someone come? | you: Thanks Maria, this is Dave at Northside Plumbing. Please shut the cold supply valve on top of the heater. We can be there by 8 a.m., does that work? | them: Yes, 8 works. What will it cost to look at it? | you: Our diagnostic is a flat fee that goes toward the repair if you go ahead. See you at 8.

That exchange (a hypothetical example) does three things: it gives a safety step, a firm time and a straight answer on price. It wins jobs that a voicemail never would. ## Step 2: Own your Google Business Profile

For a home service company, the Map Pack is the most valuable free real estate on the internet. You can't buy a spot in it. Google ranks listings on relevance, distance and prominence, per its [guide to improving local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091).

### Service-area business rules

Most trades don't have customers visit their shop. Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say that if you serve customers at their location and don't receive them at your address, you should hide the address and set a service area instead. Don't use a virtual office or a UPS box to fake a location in a city you want to rank in. That's one of the most common causes of suspension in the trades.

### The profile checklist

::check Google Business Profile essentials for contractors | Primary category matches your main money service (Plumber, HVAC contractor, Roofing contractor, Electrician) | Secondary categories for real services you offer | Service area set to the cities and ZIP codes you actually cover | Services list with short descriptions and prices where you're comfortable | Hours that match when someone actually answers | Weekly photos of real jobs, trucks and crew | Every review answered within a few days

Your business name should be your real name, with no stuffed keywords like "Best Plumber Phoenix 24/7". Google can suspend listings for that, and competitors report it. Our [Google Business Profile optimization guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide) walks through each field. ## Step 3: Decide how to use Local Services Ads and Search ads

Local Services Ads (LSA) sit at the very top of results for most home service categories. You pay per lead, not per click, and you need to pass Google's screening. In late 2025, Google replaced its old Google Guaranteed and Google Screened badges with a single **Google Verified** badge, as explained on its [About Google Verified page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/9376654?hl=en). The old money-back guarantee was discontinued, so don't promise it to customers.

Screening varies by trade and state but typically includes background checks, license verification and proof of insurance. Google's [screening and verification page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12174778?hl=en) lists the requirements.

### LSA vs Search ads vs organic

| Channel | How you pay | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local Services Ads | Per lead | Top placement, trust badge, phone-first | Less control over keywords; ranking depends on reviews and responsiveness |
| Google Search ads | Per click | Precise keyword and ZIP targeting, landing pages you control | Clicks cost money even if nobody calls; needs ongoing management |
| Map Pack (GBP) | Free | High trust, steady volume once you rank | Slow to build; distance limits your reach |
| Organic website | Free | Captures research searches ("panel upgrade cost") | Takes months; needs good service and area pages |

Many shops start with LSA because it's simple and phone-first, then add Search ads for high-value jobs like water heater replacement, new HVAC systems or full roof replacements. For budget planning, lead disputes and how to combine the channels, read [Google Local Services Ads for home services](/blog/google-local-services-ads-home-services). ## Step 4: Build service pages and service-area pages that earn rankings

Your website has two jobs. It has to convert a stressed homeowner in under ten seconds, and it has to give Google enough relevant content to rank you for each service and city.

### Service pages first

Build one page per money service, not one "Services" page with a bullet list. A plumber might have separate pages for water heater repair, water heater replacement, drain cleaning, sewer line repair, leak detection and repiping. Each page should answer the questions people actually ask:

- What the problem looks like and when it's an emergency.
- What you do, step by step, in plain language.
- Price ranges or what drives the price, if you're comfortable sharing.
- Brands you service, warranties and licensing.
- Real photos from jobs, a few reviews that mention this service, and one clear call button.

Use our [keyword research guide](/blog/keyword-research-for-small-business) to find the exact phrases customers use, and add LocalBusiness or a more specific type (Plumber, HVACBusiness, RoofingContractor, Electrician) with the help of our [schema markup guide](/blog/schema-markup-for-local-business).

### Service-area pages without the spam

City pages work when they're genuinely about that city. They fail when you copy one page 40 times and swap the town name. A good service-area page includes:

- Which neighborhoods and ZIP codes you cover, and typical arrival times.
- Local conditions that matter (hard water, older housing, hail exposure, freeze risk).
- Two or three local jobs with photos, plus reviews from that area.
- Permit notes where relevant.

## Step 5: Turn every finished job into a review

Reviews feed your Map Pack prominence, your LSA ranking and your close rate. They're also where most contractors are weakest, simply because nobody asks consistently.

### Make asking part of job completion

The best moment is right after the work is done and the customer has seen the result. A simple flow:

::steps A review flow techs actually follow | Finish the job — walk the customer through what you did | Ask in person — "Would you mind leaving a quick Google review? It really helps us." | Send the link — email or text from your field service app within the hour | Reply to the review — thank them by first name and mention the service | Share the best ones — add them to the matching service page

Growvia can send the review request by email from your own mailbox and put the link in your follow-up sequences. For texting, use your field service software or a texting tool with proper consent and 10DLC registration, since Growvia doesn't send SMS. Our [SMS marketing and TCPA guide](/blog/sms-marketing-tcpa-10dlc-guide) explains the consent rules.

### Stay inside the rules

The FTC's [Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/rule-consumer-reviews-testimonials), effective October 21, 2024, bans fake reviews, buying reviews, undisclosed reviews from employees or relatives and suppressing negative reviews through threats. Google's own policies also ban "review gating," which means only sending the review link to customers you think are happy. Ask everyone. A steady trickle of honest four- and five-star reviews, with a few fair complaints answered politely, looks far more trustworthy than a wall of perfection.

## Step 6: Plan around the seasons

Home services is a seasonal business, and the seasons are different by trade and by region. AC calls spike in the first heat wave. Furnace calls spike in the first cold snap. Frozen pipes hit northern cities in January and caught Texas off guard during the February 2021 winter storm. Roofers see surges after hail and hurricanes. Electricians get generator inquiries after outages.

The mistake is marketing hardest when you're already slammed. The smart move is to market *before* the season, when you have capacity, and book tune-ups, inspections and maintenance work that keeps techs busy in the shoulder months.

::bars Example: where an HVAC company's marketing effort could go by season (illustrative, share of yearly effort) | Spring pre-season tune-ups: 30 * | Summer peak: 15 | Fall heating tune-ups: 30 | Winter peak: 10 | Shoulder months (agreements, IAQ, replacements): 15

That example is not a benchmark. It shows the idea: spend effort ahead of demand, then switch to answering and booking. ## Step 7: Use email to sell maintenance plans and repeat work

Your past customers are your cheapest source of new jobs. They already trust you. Most contractors never contact them again unless something breaks.

Email is the right tool here because it's low cost, it doesn't need the strict text-message consent rules, and it lets you explain things (why a tune-up matters, what a membership includes). It still has to follow CAN-SPAM: honest From and subject lines, your physical postal address and a working unsubscribe that you honor within 10 business days. The [FTC's CAN-SPAM compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) has the details.

### Four emails every home service company should automate

1. **Job follow-up (day 1):** thank you, review link, how to reach you if anything's off.
2. **Maintenance reminder (6 or 12 months):** tune-up, water heater flush, roof inspection or electrical safety check, with a booking link.
3. **Membership offer (after second job):** what the plan includes, priority scheduling, the price, one button.
4. **Pre-season reminder:** sent to everyone a few weeks before your busy season starts.

## Step 8: Show up in AI search

More homeowners now ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity things like "who's a reliable HVAC company in Mesa?" These assistants pull from your website, Business Profile, review sites and directories. You can't buy your way in, but you can be easy to recommend:

- Keep your name, address (or service area) and phone identical across Google, Yelp, BBB, Angi, Nextdoor and your trade associations.
- Write pages that answer real questions in plain language, with clear headings and specific facts.
- Earn reviews that mention specific services and towns, because assistants often summarize review themes.
- Get listed where your trade is listed: manufacturer dealer locators, trade associations and the local chamber.

## The 90-day plan

This plan assumes a small shop with an owner, an office person and a few techs. Adjust the pace to your capacity.

::steps 90 days to a working growth system | Days 1–14 — Measure missed calls, set a response standard, add form auto-replies | Days 15–30 — Clean up your Business Profile, fix categories, start weekly job photos | Days 31–45 — Launch the review flow with every tech and reply to all reviews | Days 46–60 — Build or rewrite your top 5 service pages and top 3 city pages | Days 61–75 — Set up job follow-up and maintenance reminder emails | Days 76–90 — Review LSA and ads, check competitors and AI visibility, plan next season

### KPIs that matter

Track a handful of numbers, not a dashboard of 40:

- **Answer rate:** share of inbound calls answered live or returned within your standard.
- **Booked jobs per month by source:** LSA, Maps, organic, referral, repeat customer.
- **Cost per booked job:** marketing spend divided by booked jobs, by channel.
- **Review count and monthly new reviews,** compared with your top competitors.
- **Maintenance plan members** and renewal rate.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Raising ad budgets while missing calls.**
- **Faking a location** with a virtual office to rank in another city.
- **Marketing only during peak season,** when you're too busy to take the work.
- **Texting customers without consent.** TCPA penalties are per message.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best marketing for home service businesses?

The best results usually come from fast answering, a strong Google Business Profile, steady reviews and Local Services Ads, in that order. Add service pages and email to past customers once those basics work.

### Are Local Services Ads worth it for contractors?

For most trades they're worth testing because you pay per lead and appear at the top of results. They work best when you answer every call quickly and have a strong rating, since both affect your ranking.

### How many Google reviews does a home service company need?

There's no magic number. Aim to match or beat the top three companies in your Map Pack and keep new reviews coming every month, since recent reviews matter to customers.

### Does Growvia replace ServiceTitan or Jobber?

No. Growvia is a marketing tool, not a field service system. Use ServiceTitan, Jobber or Housecall Pro for scheduling, dispatch and invoicing, and Growvia for SEO, reviews, email follow-up, competitor tracking and AI-search visibility.

### Should I create a page for every city I serve?

Only for cities where you can write genuinely useful, specific content. Start with your top five cities and add more as you collect local jobs, photos and reviews for each.

### How do I market my home service business in the slow season?

Sell maintenance plans, tune-ups and inspections to past customers by email, publish content for the coming season and use the quieter weeks to build service pages and collect reviews.
